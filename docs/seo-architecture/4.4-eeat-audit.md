# EEAT Signal Audit for CycleHub Utilities

Because CycleHub falls under YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) due to its health nature, EEAT (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) is paramount. Google will suppress programmatic tools in this space if they appear spammy or unsafe.

## Site-Level Trust Signals (Must have on all pages)
1. **Privacy Guarantee:** A prominent badge stating "100% Private, Local-Storage Only" (already in Hero, needs to be on every tool page).
2. **Standard Policies:** Working links in footer to Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, and About Us.
3. **Medical Disclaimer:** A footer or sub-header note clarifying this is an estimation tool, not medical advice.

## Page-Level Expertise Signals
When generating programmatic tools via `pSeoData.json`, ensure each includes:

1. **"How this calculation works" section:** Explicitly show the math.
   - *Example:* "This calculator uses the standard Naegele's rule: LMP + 7 days - 3 months + 1 year." Showing the methodology proves expertise.
2. **Accuracy Disclosures:** Be honest about limitations.
   - *Example:* "Calendar methods are roughly 75% accurate and should not replace hormonal or barrier birth control."
3. **Author/Reviewer Entity:** Even if programmatic, attribute the mathematical formulas to standard medical consensus.

## Schema Types to Reinforce EEAT
- `SoftwareApplication` (Subtype: `HealthApplication`)
- Include the `author` or `publisher` property pointing to the CycleHub Organization schema.
- Include `applicationCategory: "Health & Fitness"`.

## Audit Checklist for New Tools
- [ ] Does the tool clearly explain its mathematical methodology?
- [ ] Does it contain a medical disclaimer?
- [ ] Is the Privacy badge visible near the input fields?
- [ ] Are there 0 spelling/grammar errors in the generated copy?
