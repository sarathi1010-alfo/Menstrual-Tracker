## The Technical Foundation: Cycle Phases and Hormones

To track your cycle effectively, it is essential to understand its four primary phases.

### 1. Menstruation (Days 1-5)
The cycle begins on the first day of your period. During this phase, the uterine lining sheds due to low levels of estrogen and progesterone. You may experience fatigue, cramping, and lower back pain.

### 2. The Follicular Phase (Days 1-13)
Overlapping with menstruation, this phase is marked by the pituitary gland releasing Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to prepare an egg. As the egg matures, estrogen levels rise, often bringing increased energy and improved mood.

### 3. Ovulation (Around Day 14)
A surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) triggers the release of the mature egg from the ovary. This is your most fertile time. Some may notice changes in basal body temperature and cervical mucus.

### 4. The Luteal Phase (Days 15-28)
After ovulation, the empty follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone to prepare the uterus for potential pregnancy. If fertilization does not occur, hormone levels drop, leading to premenstrual symptoms (PMS) and the start of a new cycle.

## Top Tracking Strategies

### 1. Calendar Tracking
The most common method involves simply logging the start and end dates of your period. This helps establish your baseline cycle length and predict future periods.

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT)
Tracking your resting body temperature every morning can help pinpoint ovulation, as BBT typically spikes slightly after the egg is released.

### 3. Cervical Mucus Monitoring
Observing changes in cervical discharge throughout your cycle is another indicator of fertility, with "egg white" consistency indicating peak fertility.

### 4. Symptom Logging
Tracking physical and emotional symptoms (like headaches, mood swings, or acne) helps you recognize personal patterns linked to hormonal shifts.
