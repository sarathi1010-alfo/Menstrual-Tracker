## What is a Menstrual Cycle?

The **menstrual cycle** is the monthly series of biological changes a woman's body undergoes to prepare for a potential pregnancy. Driven by fluctuating hormones, it regulates ovulation and the thickening and shedding of the uterine lining (menstruation).

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is governed by a complex interaction of hormones, primarily from the brain (hypothalamus and pituitary gland) and the ovaries. These hormones coordinate the four main phases:

1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the unneeded uterine lining.
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The preparation of a new egg for release, accompanied by rising estrogen.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of the mature egg.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy by producing progesterone. If pregnancy does not occur, hormone levels drop, triggering the next menstruation.

## Menstrual Cycle vs. Period

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Period (Menstruation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process of hormonal changes and egg maturation. | The specific phase where bleeding occurs. |
| **Duration** | Typically 21 to 35 days. | Typically 3 to 7 days. |
| **Function** | Preparation for potential pregnancy. | Shedding of the unneeded uterine lining. |
