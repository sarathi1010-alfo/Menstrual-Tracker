The menstrual cycle is a monthly series of physiological changes that occur in the female reproductive system. It is nature's way of preparing the body for a potential pregnancy. If pregnancy does not occur, the cycle culminates in menstruation, or a period.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by a complex interplay of hormones, primarily estrogen and progesterone, produced by the ovaries, as well as hormones from the brain (FSH and LH). These hormones signal the body to grow an egg, thicken the uterine lining, release the egg (ovulation), and eventually shed the lining if the egg is unfertilized.

## The Menstrual Cycle vs. A Period

While often used interchangeably, they are not the same.

| Concept | Definition | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Period (Menstruation)** | The shedding of the uterine lining (bleeding). | 3-7 days typically. |
| **Menstrual Cycle** | The entire monthly process, including the period, follicular phase, ovulation, and luteal phase. | 21-35 days typically. |

Understanding the full cycle, rather than just focusing on the period, provides a much broader view of your reproductive and overall health.
