## Definition

The **menstrual cycle** is the monthly series of hormonal and physiological changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for the possibility of pregnancy. Each month, an ovary releases an egg (ovulation), and the uterus thickens its lining. If pregnancy does not occur, the lining is shed through menstruation (a period).

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is controlled by a complex interaction of hormones, primarily estrogen and progesterone, produced by the ovaries, and Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH) and Luteinizing Hormone (LH), produced by the pituitary gland.

It is divided into four main phases:

1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the unneeded uterine lining.
2. **Follicular Phase:** The preparation of a new egg for release.
3. **Ovulation:** The release of the mature egg.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The preparation of the uterus for a potential fertilized egg.

## Menstrual Cycle vs. Period

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Period (Menstruation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process | The bleeding phase only |
| **Duration** | Typically 21-35 days | Typically 3-7 days |
| **Purpose** | Prepare body for pregnancy | Shed the unneeded uterine lining |
| **Hormones involved** | Estrogen, Progesterone, FSH, LH | Triggered by a drop in Estrogen & Progesterone |
