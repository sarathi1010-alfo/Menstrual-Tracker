## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The menstrual cycle is a continuous loop of biological processes governed by a delicate balance of hormones. Its primary biological purpose is to prepare the body for a potential pregnancy. If a pregnancy does not occur, the cycle resets, leading to menstruation (a period).

The process involves complex communication between the brain (specifically the hypothalamus and pituitary gland) and the ovaries. These organs release hormones—mainly Estrogen, Progesterone, Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), and Luteinizing Hormone (LH)—that act as chemical messengers.

These messengers instruct the ovaries to mature and release an egg (ovulation) and signal the uterus to thicken its lining (the endometrium). If the egg is not fertilized, hormone levels drop, causing the thickened lining to shed, which results in menstrual bleeding.

## The Four Phases of the Cycle

Understanding the cycle requires looking at its four distinct phases:

1.  **Menstrual Phase (Days 1-5 approx.):** The shedding of the uterine lining. Estrogen and progesterone are low.
2.  **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13 approx.):** Overlaps with menstruation. The pituitary gland releases FSH to stimulate follicle growth in the ovaries. Estrogen rises, thickening the uterine lining.
3.  **Ovulatory Phase (Day 14 approx.):** A surge in LH triggers the release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4.  **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28 approx.):** The ruptured follicle becomes the corpus luteum, which produces progesterone to maintain the uterine lining. If no pregnancy occurs, the corpus luteum degrades, hormones drop, and a new cycle begins.

## Menstrual Cycle vs. Period

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Period (Menstruation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly hormonal process. | The bleeding phase of the cycle. |
| **Duration** | Typically 21 to 35 days. | Typically 3 to 7 days. |
| **Function** | Prepares the body for potential pregnancy. | Sheds the unneeded uterine lining. |
| **Hormone State** | Fluctuates significantly throughout. | Estrogen and progesterone are at their lowest point. |
