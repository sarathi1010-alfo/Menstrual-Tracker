The menstrual cycle is the monthly process in which a woman's body prepares for a potential pregnancy, involving the shedding of the uterine lining (menstruation) and the release of an egg (ovulation).

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

It is regulated by a complex interplay of hormones—primarily estrogen and progesterone. The cycle is divided into phases that prepare the ovaries to release an egg and the uterus to receive a fertilized egg. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the lining of the uterus is shed, which is your period.

## Real-World Example of the Menstrual Cycle

In a typical 28-day cycle, Day 1 is the start of bleeding. By Day 14, ovulation occurs. If no pregnancy happens by Day 28, the cycle begins again with a new period.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a key indicator of reproductive health and general wellness. Regular cycles usually suggest that the body is ovulating and hormones are balanced.

## Menstrual Cycle vs. Period

| Term | Scope | Definition |
|------|-------|------------|
| Period | 3–7 Days | The actual bleeding (menstruation). |
| Menstrual Cycle | 21–35 Days | The entire process from one period to the next. |
