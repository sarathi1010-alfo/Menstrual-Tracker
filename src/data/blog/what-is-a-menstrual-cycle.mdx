The menstrual cycle is a complex, beautifully orchestrated series of changes that the female reproductive system undergoes each month to prepare for a potential pregnancy. It is driven by fluctuations in key hormones, primarily estrogen and progesterone, which govern the development and release of an egg, as well as the thickening and subsequent shedding of the uterine lining.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is typically divided into four distinct phases:

1.  **Menstruation (The Period):** This is the shedding of the uterine lining, which occurs when a pregnancy has not taken place. It marks the beginning of the cycle.
2.  **Follicular Phase:** Overlapping with menstruation, this phase involves the maturation of follicles in the ovaries, one of which will eventually release an egg. Estrogen levels rise during this time.
3.  **Ovulation:** This is the release of a mature egg from the ovary, usually occurring midway through the cycle. It is triggered by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH).
4.  **Luteal Phase:** After ovulation, the ruptured follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone to maintain the thickened uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, hormone levels drop, leading back to menstruation.

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

When you use [LunaCycle](/) to log the first day of your period, you are marking Day 1 of your menstrual cycle. Over time, as you log consecutive periods, the app calculates the average number of days between Day 1 of one cycle and Day 1 of the next. This allows the algorithm to predict when your next period (and your fertile window) is most likely to occur, giving you actionable insights into your unique rhythm.
