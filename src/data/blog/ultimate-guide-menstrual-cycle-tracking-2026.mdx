## Introduction/Evolution

Understanding your menstrual cycle is more than just knowing when your next period will arrive. It's about gaining insight into your overall health, hormonal fluctuations, and emotional well-being. In recent years, cycle tracking has evolved from simple calendar marking to sophisticated digital tools, but with this convenience comes a growing concern for data privacy.

## Technical Foundation (Cycle Phases, Hormones)

The menstrual cycle is a complex interplay of hormones, primarily estrogen and progesterone. It can be divided into four main phases:

1.  **Menstrual Phase (Days 1-5):** The shedding of the uterine lining, commonly known as your period.
2.  **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** Overlaps with the menstrual phase. The pituitary gland releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to prepare an egg. Estrogen levels rise.
3.  **Ovulation (Around Day 14):** A surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) triggers the release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4.  **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** The empty follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone to prepare the uterine lining for a potential pregnancy. If pregnancy doesn't occur, hormone levels drop, leading to the next menstrual phase.

## Top Tracking Strategies

### 1. Calendar Method
The most basic form of tracking. You log the first day of your period and calculate the average length between cycles. [LunaCycle](/) automates this process securely on your device.

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT) Tracking
Taking your temperature every morning before getting out of bed. A slight rise in temperature indicates that ovulation has occurred.

### 3. Cervical Mucus Monitoring
Observing the changes in vaginal discharge throughout the cycle. Clear, stretchy mucus often indicates peak fertility.

## Future Trends: AI in Cycle Tracking

Artificial Intelligence is increasingly being used to analyze cycle data and provide personalized insights. However, the future of AI in health tracking must prioritize user privacy. Applications that process data locally, without sending sensitive information to external servers, will lead the way in secure, intelligent cycle management.
