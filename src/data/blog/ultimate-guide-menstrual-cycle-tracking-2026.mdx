## Introduction

Understanding your menstrual cycle is a powerful tool for taking charge of your overall health and well-being. Far beyond just knowing when your next period will arrive, a menstrual cycle tracker provides a window into your hormonal health, energy levels, and fertility.

## The Technical Foundation: Cycle Phases and Hormones

The menstrual cycle is a complex, beautifully orchestrated series of hormonal changes designed to prepare the body for potential pregnancy. It is generally divided into four main phases:

### 1. The Menstrual Phase
This is the phase most people are familiar with. It marks the beginning of your cycle (Day 1) when the uterine lining sheds, resulting in menstrual bleeding. This phase typically lasts between 3 to 7 days.

### 2. The Follicular Phase
Overlapping with the menstrual phase and continuing until ovulation, the follicular phase is when your body prepares to release an egg. The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to produce follicles. As estrogen levels rise, the uterine lining begins to thicken.

### 3. The Ovulatory Phase
Ovulation is the release of a mature egg from the ovary. Triggered by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), this usually occurs around the middle of your cycle. This is your fertile window.

### 4. The Luteal Phase
Following ovulation, the ruptured follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone stabilizes the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, progesterone and estrogen levels drop, leading back to the menstrual phase.

## Top Tracking Strategies

1. **Calendar Method:** The simplest approach. Log the first and last days of your period to calculate average cycle length. [LunaCycle](/) provides an excellent privacy-first visual calendar for this.
2. **Basal Body Temperature (BBT):** Taking your temperature first thing in the morning can help identify the slight temperature spike that occurs after ovulation.
3. **Cervical Mucus Monitoring:** Observing changes in your cervical fluid (which becomes clear and stretchy around ovulation) provides clues about your fertile window.
