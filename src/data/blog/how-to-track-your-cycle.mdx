Tracking your cycle is one of the most powerful things you can do for your health. It moves you from "guessing" to "knowing." Here is how to do it effectively.

## Step 1: Choose Your Tool
While paper calendars work, digital tools like LunaCycle offer automatic calculations. The key is choosing a tool that respects your privacy. LunaCycle uses local storage, so your data stays on your phone or computer, not on a server.

## Step 2: Identify Day 1
The first day of your period—full flow, not just spotting—is Day 1. Mark this in your tracker immediately.

## Step 3: Track the Duration
Keep logging every day that you have a period until the bleeding stops. This helps determine your "period length," which is different from your "cycle length."

## Step 4: Add Context (Optional but Recommended)
Your cycle affects your whole body. Try logging:
  - "**Energy levels:** Do you feel like a superhero or a sloth?"
  - "**Mood:** Are you feeling social or do you want to hide?"
  - "**Physical symptoms:** Bloating, headaches, or cramps."

## Step 5: Observe the Patterns
After 2-3 months, look at your tracker. You'll start to see your average cycle length. LunaCycle will automatically calculate this for you and start predicting your next period.
