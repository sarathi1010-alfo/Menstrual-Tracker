## Why Hormonal Awareness Matters

Understanding the hormones that drive your menstrual cycle—estrogen, progesterone, luteinizing hormone (LH), and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)—helps demystify the physical and emotional changes you experience each month. This awareness allows you to better manage your energy, mood, and reproductive health.

## How to Track Hormonal Changes

While blood tests are the only way to measure exact hormone levels, you can track their effects on your body. By logging symptoms, energy levels, mood changes, and physical signs (like cervical mucus or basal body temperature) in [LunaCycle](/), you can recognize patterns that correlate with hormonal fluctuations.

## Top 5 Hormonal Fluctuation Strategies

### 1. Menstrual Phase (Low Hormones)
Focus on rest and gentle activities like stretching or walking. Nourish your body with iron-rich foods to replenish what is lost during bleeding.

### 2. Follicular Phase (Rising Estrogen)
As estrogen rises, you may feel more energetic. This is a great time for higher-intensity workouts, brainstorming, and taking on new projects.

### 3. Ovulatory Phase (Estrogen Peak, LH Surge)
Your energy and communication skills are often at their highest. Socialize, have important conversations, and enjoy peak physical performance.

### 4. Early Luteal Phase (Rising Progesterone)
As progesterone increases, you might feel more focused but less energetic. Shift toward steady-state cardio or strength training, and focus on completing tasks.

### 5. Late Luteal Phase (Dropping Hormones)
If pregnancy hasn't occurred, hormones drop, which can cause PMS. Prioritize self-care, sleep, and gentle movement like yoga. Reduce caffeine and sugar intake.

## Common Hormonal Imbalance Signs

| Symptom | Potential Cause |
| :--- | :--- |
| Very irregular or absent periods | PCOS, thyroid issues, high stress |
| Severe PMS or PMDD | Estrogen dominance or low progesterone |
| Hormonal acne (especially jawline) | Excess androgens |
| Unexplained weight gain or loss | Thyroid issues or insulin resistance |
