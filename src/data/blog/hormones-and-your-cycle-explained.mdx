Estrogen, progesterone, luteinizing hormone, and follicle-stimulating hormone orchestrate the menstrual cycle. Learning their roles demystifies your body's signals.

## Why Hormonal Awareness Matters

Your hormones do much more than regulate your period; they influence your mood, energy levels, sleep, and overall reproductive health. By understanding which hormones are dominant during different parts of your cycle, you can make sense of why you feel the way you do. This awareness allows you to practice self-compassion and adapt your lifestyle to better support your body's needs.

## How to Track Hormonal Changes

While you can't measure exact hormone levels at home without specialized tests, you can track their effects. Using a tool like [LunaCycle](/), log daily symptoms such as energy levels, mood, sleep quality, and physical signs like acne or breast tenderness. Over time, these logs will create a map of your hormonal fluctuations, showing you when estrogen is likely peaking or when progesterone is dominant.

## Top 5 Hormonal Fluctuation Strategies

### 1. Menstrual Phase Strategy
Focus on restorative activities. Your hormone levels are at their lowest, so prioritize rest, gentle movement, and nutrient-dense foods.

### 2. Follicular Phase Strategy
Harness rising estrogen. This is a great time for starting new projects, intense workouts, and socializing.

### 3. Ovulation Strategy
Capitalize on peak energy. Estrogen and testosterone peak, making you feel confident and energetic. It's the ideal time for important meetings or challenging tasks.

### 4. Early Luteal Phase Strategy
Lean into productivity. Progesterone starts to rise, providing a calming effect that's great for focused, detail-oriented work.

### 5. Late Luteal Phase Strategy
Practice self-care. As hormones drop, you may experience PMS. Focus on stress management, light exercise, and avoiding excess sugar or caffeine.

## Common Hormonal Imbalance Signs

| Symptom | Potential Imbalance |
| :--- | :--- |
| Irregular or missing periods | PCOS, thyroid issues, or extreme stress. |
| Severe acne or facial hair | High androgens (often associated with PCOS). |
| Extreme fatigue and heavy bleeding | Low thyroid function or estrogen dominance. |
