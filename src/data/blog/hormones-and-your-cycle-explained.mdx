Estrogen, progesterone, luteinizing hormone, and follicle-stimulating hormone orchestrate the menstrual cycle. Learning their roles demystifies your body's signals.

## Why Hormonal Awareness Matters

Being aware of your hormones and how they fluctuate is crucial for understanding your mood, energy, and reproductive health. When you track your cycle with an app like [LunaCycle](/), you're not just logging dates; you are mapping the hormonal ebb and flow that dictates much of your physical and emotional well-being. Recognizing that you might feel more outgoing and energetic when estrogen peaks, or more reflective and potentially irritable when progesterone rises, allows you to practice better self-compassion and planning. This knowledge empowers you to work with your body rather than against it. To connect this with the broader picture, review [Cycle Phases Explained](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal), [Menstrual Cycle 101](/blog/menstrual-cycle-101-beginners-guide), or the [Ultimate Cycle Tracking Guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide).

## How to Track Hormonal Changes

While you can't see your hormones directly, you can track their effects. By logging your symptoms, mood, energy levels, and physical signs like cervical mucus and basal body temperature, you can create a reliable proxy for your hormonal fluctuations. Consistent tracking over several months will reveal patterns that help you anticipate these changes.

## Top 5 Hormonal Fluctuation Strategies

Understanding how your hormones fluctuate allows you to implement strategies to support your body through each phase.

### 1. Menstruation (Low Hormones)

During your period, hormone levels are at their lowest. Focus on rest, hydration, and gentle movements like walking or yoga. Nourish your body with iron-rich foods to replenish what is lost.

### 2. Follicular Phase (Rising Estrogen)

As estrogen rises, you may feel an increase in energy and creativity. This is a great time to start new projects, engage in more intense workouts, and socialize.

### 3. Ovulation (Estrogen Peak, LH Surge)

Estrogen peaks and luteinizing hormone surges to trigger ovulation. You likely feel your best, most energetic, and communicative. Capitalize on this peak energy for important tasks or challenging workouts.

### 4. Early Luteal Phase (Rising Progesterone)

After ovulation, progesterone begins to rise, which has a calming effect but can also slow digestion. Focus on complex carbohydrates and fiber, and transition to moderate exercise.

### 5. Late Luteal Phase (Dropping Hormones)

As both estrogen and progesterone drop before your period, you might experience PMS. Prioritize self-care, prioritize sleep, manage stress, and be gentle with yourself.

## Common Hormonal Imbalance Signs

Sometimes, the intricate dance of hormones gets out of step. Recognizing the signs of imbalance is important for seeking appropriate care.

| Sign | Potential Imbalance | What You Might Feel |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Severe PMS/PMDD | Progesterone/Estrogen imbalance | Extreme mood swings, severe breast tenderness, debilitating cramps. |
| Irregular or Missed Periods | Various, including PCOS | Unpredictable cycle lengths, skipping cycles entirely. |
| Hormonal Acne | High Androgens | Deep, cystic breakouts, typically along the jawline and chin. |
| Unexplained Weight Gain | Thyroid or Insulin issues | Difficulty losing weight despite diet and exercise, especially around the midsection. |
| Chronic Fatigue | Various | Feeling constantly tired, regardless of how much you sleep. |
