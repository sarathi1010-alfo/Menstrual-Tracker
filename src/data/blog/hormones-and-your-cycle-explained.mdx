## Why Hormonal Awareness Matters
Your menstrual cycle is a complex symphony orchestrated by four main hormones: estrogen, progesterone, luteinizing hormone (LH), and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). Being aware of how these hormones rise and fall throughout the month gives you profound insight into your body. It explains why you might feel energetic one week and sluggish the next, helping you be more compassionate with yourself and proactive about your health.

## How to Track Hormonal Changes
While you can't measure exact hormone levels without a blood test, you can infer their fluctuations by tracking your symptoms. Using a tool like [LunaCycle](/tracker), you can log your mood, energy levels, skin changes, and physical symptoms daily. Over time, you'll see patterns emerge that correlate with the hormonal shifts of each cycle phase.

## Top 5 Hormonal Fluctuation Strategies
Here's how to support your body through its hormonal shifts:

### 1. Menstruation (Low Hormones)
Focus on rest, hydration, and gentle movement like stretching or yoga. Your body is working hard to shed the uterine lining.

### 2. Follicular Phase (Rising Estrogen)
Capitalize on rising energy! This is a great time for challenging workouts, starting new projects, and socializing.

### 3. Ovulation (Peak Estrogen & LH)
You're likely at your most energetic and communicative. Enjoy high-intensity workouts and important meetings.

### 4. Early Luteal Phase (Rising Progesterone)
As progesterone rises, you may naturally want to slow down. Shift to moderate exercise and focus on completing tasks rather than starting new ones.

### 5. Late Luteal Phase (Dropping Hormones)
This is when PMS can occur. Prioritize self-care, eat nutrient-dense foods to stabilize blood sugar, and get plenty of sleep.

## Common Hormonal Imbalance Signs
Look out for these potential signs of hormonal imbalance:

| Symptom | Potential Indication |
|---|---|
| Very heavy or painful periods | Excess estrogen or other conditions |
| Missing periods (Amenorrhea) | Low hormones, stress, or PCOS |
| Severe mood swings/PMDD | Extreme sensitivity to hormonal shifts |
| Hormonal acne | Androgen imbalance |
