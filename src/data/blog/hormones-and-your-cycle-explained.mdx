Estrogen, progesterone, luteinizing hormone (LH), and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) orchestrate the menstrual cycle. Learning their roles demystifies your body's signals and helps you understand why you feel the way you do at different times of the month.

## Why Hormonal Awareness Matters

Your hormones do much more than just control your reproductive system. They have a profound impact on your mood, energy levels, metabolism, and even your cognitive function. By becoming aware of how these hormones fluctuate, you can better manage your overall health, anticipate changes in your emotional state, and recognize when something might be out of balance.

## How to Track Hormonal Changes

While you can't measure your exact hormone levels at home without specialized kits, you can track the *effects* of these hormones. By logging your physical and emotional symptoms daily in an app like [LunaCycle](/), you can infer where you are in your hormonal cycle. For instance, a spike in energy and clear skin often indicates rising estrogen, while breast tenderness and moodiness can point to the dominance of progesterone in the luteal phase.

## Top 5 Hormonal Fluctuation Strategies

Here are strategies to align your lifestyle with your hormonal shifts during the [cycle phases](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal).

### 1. Menstrual Phase (Low Hormones)
*   **Strategy:** Rest and reflect. Your hormones are at their lowest. Focus on gentle movements like yoga or walking, prioritize sleep, and eat warming, iron-rich foods.

### 2. Follicular Phase (Rising Estrogen)
*   **Strategy:** Brainstorm and initiate. As estrogen rises, so does your energy and cognitive sharpness. This is a great time to start new projects, schedule challenging workouts, and socialize.

### 3. Ovulation (Peak Estrogen and LH)
*   **Strategy:** Communicate and connect. You are likely feeling your most energetic and outgoing. It is an ideal time for important conversations, presentations, and high-intensity exercise.

### 4. Early Luteal Phase (High Progesterone)
*   **Strategy:** Organize and complete tasks. Progesterone has a calming effect. Focus on detail-oriented work, organizing your space, and transitioning to moderate exercise like strength training.

### 5. Late Luteal Phase (Dropping Hormones)
*   **Strategy:** Nurture and prepare. As hormones crash before your period, you may feel fatigued or irritable. Practice self-care, reduce social obligations if needed, and focus on stress management.

## Common Hormonal Imbalance Signs

If your hormones are consistently out of balance, you might experience several disruptive symptoms.

| Symptom | Potential Hormonal Imbalance |
| :--- | :--- |
| Irregular or Missing Periods | Could indicate anovulation, PCOS, or thyroid issues affecting estrogen/progesterone balance. |
| Severe PMS or PMDD | Often linked to an exaggerated response to normal hormonal shifts, particularly the drop in progesterone. |
| Unexplained Weight Gain | May be related to insulin resistance or thyroid hormone imbalances, which affect sex hormones. |
| Hormonal Acne | Persistent acne, especially around the jawline, can be a sign of excess androgens (like testosterone). |

If you notice persistent signs of imbalance, consult a healthcare provider. For more tips on managing your tracking routine, explore our [ultimate cycle tracking guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide).
