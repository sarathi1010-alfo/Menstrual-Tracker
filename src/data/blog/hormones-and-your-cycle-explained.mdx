Estrogen, progesterone, luteinizing hormone, and follicle-stimulating hormone orchestrate the menstrual cycle. Learning their roles demystifies your body's signals.

## Why Hormonal Awareness Matters

Being aware of your hormones is crucial for understanding your mood, energy levels, and reproductive health. When you know which hormone is dominant, you can make sense of why you feel a certain way. This is a key part of understanding your [cycle phases](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal).

## How to Track Hormonal Changes

The easiest way to track hormonal changes without daily blood tests is by using symptoms and cycle logging. Noticing changes in cervical fluid, basal body temperature, and mood swings gives you clues about your hormone levels. [LunaCycle](/) makes this easy and private. For a deeper dive, read our [Ultimate Guide to Tracking](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide).

## Top 5 Hormonal Fluctuation Strategies

### 1. Menstrual Phase (Low hormones)
Focus on rest, gentle movement, and iron-rich foods to replenish lost blood.

### 2. Follicular Phase (Rising estrogen)
Capitalize on rising energy with higher-intensity workouts and social activities.

### 3. Ovulation (Peak estrogen & LH)
This is your peak energy phase. Great for communication and challenging tasks.

### 4. Early Luteal (Rising progesterone)
Transition to more steady-state exercises and focus on completing tasks.

### 5. Late Luteal (Dropping hormones)
Focus on self-care, complex carbohydrates to manage cravings, and stress reduction.

## Common Hormonal Imbalance Signs

| Symptom | Potential Imbalance |
| --- | --- |
| Severe PMS/PMDD | Progesterone deficiency or estrogen dominance |
| Very heavy periods | High estrogen |
| Irregular or missing periods | PCOS or hypothalamic amenorrhea |
| Persistent acne | High androgens |

If you notice ongoing signs of imbalance, consider consulting a doctor. It's also helpful to understand [what a normal period looks like](/blog/understanding-your-period-normal-vs-not-normal).
