Estrogen, progesterone, luteinizing hormone, and follicle-stimulating hormone orchestrate the menstrual cycle. Learning their roles demystifies your body's signals.

## Why Hormonal Awareness Matters

Your reproductive hormones don't just stay in your reproductive organs; they circulate throughout your entire body, affecting your brain, bones, skin, and metabolism. Understanding what these hormones do and when they peak can explain why you might feel energetic and outgoing one week, and introspective and tired the next.

## How to Track Hormonal Changes

The most practical way to track hormonal changes is to track your symptoms. The physical and emotional shifts you experience are direct results of hormonal fluctuations. By using an app like [LunaCycle](/), you can log symptoms like acne, mood changes, and breast tenderness, which provide clues about your estrogen and progesterone levels.

## Top 5 Hormonal Fluctuation Strategies

### 1. Menstruation (Low Hormones)
Focus on rest and replenishment. Your body is losing iron, so eat iron-rich foods and prioritize gentle movement like yoga or walking.

### 2. Follicular Phase (Rising Estrogen)
Take advantage of rising energy and brain power. This is a great time to start new projects, socialize, and engage in higher-intensity workouts.

### 3. Ovulation (Peak Estrogen & LH)
You may feel most confident and energetic. Support your liver in metabolizing estrogen by eating cruciferous vegetables.

### 4. Early Luteal Phase (Rising Progesterone)
As progesterone rises, you may feel calmer but your metabolism also increases slightly. Focus on complex carbohydrates and strength training.

### 5. Late Luteal Phase (Dropping Hormones)
PMS symptoms may appear. Prioritize self-care, reduce caffeine and sugar intake, and practice stress-reduction techniques.

## Common Hormonal Imbalance Signs

| Sign | Potential Imbalance |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Severe PMS or PMDD** | Estrogen dominance or low progesterone |
| **Irregular or missing periods** | PCOS, stress, or thyroid issues |
| **Heavy, painful periods** | High estrogen or conditions like endometriosis |
| **Acne and excess hair growth** | Elevated androgens (like testosterone) |
