## The Technical Foundation: Understanding Cycle Phases

To track effectively, you need to understand the four primary phases of the menstrual cycle:

1. **Menstrual Phase:** This is when your period occurs. The uterus sheds its lining, which can result in bleeding, cramping, and fatigue.
2. **Follicular Phase:** Overlapping with the menstrual phase, this phase begins on the first day of your period and ends with ovulation. Estrogen levels rise as your body prepares an egg for release.
3. **Ovulation Phase:** Usually occurring around the middle of your cycle, ovulation is when a mature egg is released from the ovary. This is your most fertile time.
4. **Luteal Phase:** Following ovulation, progesterone levels peak to support a potential pregnancy. If no pregnancy occurs, hormone levels drop, triggering your next period.

## Top Tracking Strategies

### 1. Consistency is Key

The most important aspect of tracking is consistency. Log the start and end dates of your period every month. Over time, apps like [LunaCycle](/tracker) will calculate your average cycle length and provide increasingly accurate predictions.

### 2. Monitor Symptoms

Don't just track bleeding. Record symptoms like headaches, cramping, bloating, and breast tenderness. Noting your mood and energy levels can also help you practice cycle syncing—adjusting your activities based on your cycle phase.

### 3. Observe Physical Signs

For deeper insights, especially if you are tracking fertility, you might also monitor basal body temperature (BBT) and changes in cervical mucus.
