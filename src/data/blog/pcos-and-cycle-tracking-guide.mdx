## What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder that affects roughly 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by three main features:

1.  **Irregular Periods:** Often due to infrequent or absent ovulation.
2.  **Excess Androgens:** High levels of "male" hormones like testosterone, which can cause physical signs like excess facial or body hair and severe acne.
3.  **Polycystic Ovaries:** Ovaries may be enlarged and contain numerous small fluid-filled sacs (follicles) that surround the eggs. (Note: Not everyone with PCOS has cysts, and having cysts doesn't automatically mean you have PCOS).

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you suspect you have PCOS, or have already been diagnosed, [cycle tracking](/blog/how-to-track-your-cycle-step-by-step) is one of the most powerful tools at your disposal.

Because PCOS makes cycles unpredictable, relying on memory isn't enough. Using a secure tool like [LunaCycle](/) allows you to:

*   **Log Cycle Length:** Accurately record the number of days between periods.
*   **Track Specific Symptoms:** Document occurrences of acne, hair growth changes, or sudden weight shifts alongside your cycle data.
*   **Monitor Ovulation:** If you track Basal Body Temperature (BBT) or use ovulation kits, you can confirm if and when ovulation is actually occurring.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your tracking history, look for these specific patterns that are common with PCOS:

*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   **Oligomenorrhea:** Having fewer than nine periods in a year.
*   **Amenorrhea:** Missing periods for three or more consecutive months (when not pregnant).
*   **Spotting:** Frequent spotting between periods or prolonged, light bleeding instead of a distinct period.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle data is most valuable when shared with a medical professional. You should consult a healthcare provider if:

*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You experience sudden or severe changes in your cycle regularity.
*   You have physical symptoms of excess androgens (severe acne, hirsutism).
*   You are struggling to conceive.

Bring your [LunaCycle](/) data to your appointment to provide a clear, factual history of your symptoms.
