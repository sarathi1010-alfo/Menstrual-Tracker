## Understanding PCOS

PCOS, or Polycystic Ovary Syndrome, is a common hormonal disorder affecting approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by three main features:
1. **Irregular periods:** Which means your ovaries do not regularly release eggs (ovulate).
2. **Excess androgen:** High levels of "male" hormones in your body, which may cause physical signs such as excess facial or body hair.
3. **Polycystic ovaries:** Your ovaries become enlarged and contain many fluid-filled sacs (follicles) that surround the eggs.

Because the hormonal imbalance prevents the follicles from developing normally, ovulation often doesn't occur, leading to missed or highly irregular periods.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

When you have PCOS, standard calendar predictions might not be accurate. However, cycle tracking remains incredibly valuable.

By logging your data in a secure app like [LunaCycle](/), you create a detailed medical history. You should diligently track:
*   **Cycle Length:** How many days between periods?
*   **Symptoms:** Log occurrences of acne, unusual hair growth, weight changes, or pelvic pain.
*   **Bleeding Patterns:** Note if you experience spotting instead of a full flow.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your [LunaCycle](/) history, look for these common PCOS patterns:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going months without menstruating.
*   **Anovulatory Cycles:** Bleeding that occurs without prior ovulation, which can sometimes be lighter or heavier than a normal period.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

If you notice that your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days, if you miss three or more periods in a row, or if you are experiencing severe symptoms like sudden weight gain or excess hair growth, you should consult a healthcare provider.

Bring your [LunaCycle](/) data with you to the appointment. Showing a doctor exactly how long your cycles are and when symptoms occur provides them with the concrete evidence they need to make an accurate diagnosis.
