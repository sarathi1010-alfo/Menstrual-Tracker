Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder affecting roughly 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by irregular menstrual cycles, elevated androgen (male hormone) levels, and often the presence of small cysts on the ovaries.

## How PCOS Affects the Menstrual Cycle

The defining feature of PCOS regarding the menstrual cycle is usually *anovulation* or *oligo-ovulation*—meaning a woman rarely or irregularly releases an egg.

In a typical cycle, the [follicular phase](/what-is-follicular-phase) matures an egg, leading to [ovulation](/what-is-ovulation). In PCOS, many small follicles may develop, but none mature enough to trigger ovulation. Because ovulation doesn't occur, the body doesn't produce the progesterone needed in the [luteal phase](/what-is-luteal-phase) to trigger a regular period. This results in prolonged cycles, missed periods, and sometimes heavy or prolonged bleeding when a period finally does occur.

For a baseline on normal cycles, review our [Menstrual Cycle 101](/blog/menstrual-cycle-101-beginners-guide).

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

When you have PCOS, standard app predictions based on a 28-day average will be useless. However, tracking your actual data is more important than ever.

1.  **Identify Patterns in Irregularity:** Even irregular cycles have patterns. Tracking helps you see if your cycles are consistently 45 days, or if they fluctuate wildly between 30 and 90 days.
2.  **Monitor Androgen Symptoms:** Log symptoms related to high androgens, such as acne flare-ups (especially along the jawline), unusual hair growth (hirsutism), or hair thinning.
3.  **Track Metabolic Symptoms:** PCOS is often linked to insulin resistance. Note any significant weight changes, sugar cravings, or energy crashes.
4.  **Provide Data to Doctors:** A 6-month log of your actual bleeding days and symptoms is the most valuable tool you can give your endocrinologist or gynecologist to help them adjust your treatment plan.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

If you are using [LunaCycle](/) to manage PCOS, pay special attention to:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Any cycle lasting longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going 60, 90, or more days without bleeding.
*   **Spotting:** Frequent, light bleeding between full periods.
*   **Lack of Ovulation Signs:** An absence of typical fertile cervical mucus or BBT temperature shifts.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

PCOS requires medical management. You should consult a doctor if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You experience heavy bleeding that soaks a pad every hour.
*   You have fewer than 8 periods a year.
*   You are experiencing distressing symptoms like severe acne or hair growth.
*   You are trying to conceive and have irregular cycles.

For general guidelines on normal vs abnormal bleeding, read [understanding your period](/blog/understanding-your-period-normal-vs-not-normal). If you are younger, read our specific [guide for teens](/use-cases/cycle-tracking-teens-guide).

- **Condition:** Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS).
- **Cycle Impact:** Causes anovulation, leading to prolonged (>35 days) or highly irregular cycles, and sometimes missed periods.
- **Symptoms to Track:** Cycle length, acne, unusual hair growth, weight changes, and spotting.
- **When to See a Doctor:** If cycles are consistently >35 days, if you miss periods for months at a time, or if symptoms affect your quality of life.
