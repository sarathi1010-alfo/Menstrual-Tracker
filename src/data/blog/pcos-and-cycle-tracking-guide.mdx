## Definition: What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder affecting approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by three main features, though not all must be present for a diagnosis:
1.  **Irregular or absent menstrual cycles** (due to a lack of regular ovulation).
2.  **Elevated androgen levels** (male hormones), leading to physical signs like excess facial/body hair (hirsutism) or severe acne.
3.  **Polycystic ovaries** (enlarged ovaries containing numerous small fluid-filled sacs surrounding the eggs).

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

While tracking cannot cure PCOS, it is an essential tool for management and diagnosis. Because PCOS often causes unpredictable periods, relying on memory is insufficient. By rigorously logging cycle length and accompanying symptoms, you create a concrete data record. This data is invaluable when consulting a doctor, as it clearly illustrates the extent of the irregularity.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When tracking with a tool like [LunaCycle](/), users with PCOS should specifically monitor for:

*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles consistently lasting longer than 35 days (oligomenorrhea).
*   **Missed Periods:** Going 3-4 months without a cycle (amenorrhea).
*   **Anovulatory Cycles:** Cycles where bleeding occurs, but ovulation did not (often characterized by unpredictable spotting or very light bleeding).
*   **Symptom Correlation:** Tracking if/when acne flares up, hair growth increases, or weight fluctuations occur in relation to cycle phases.

For teens experiencing these symptoms, early tracking can be crucial, as detailed in our [Cycle Tracking for Teens](/use-cases/cycle-tracking-teens-guide) guide.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

PCOS management requires medical support. You should consult a healthcare provider if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You have missed three or more periods in a row.
*   You are experiencing severe symptoms like heavy bleeding, significant pelvic pain, or rapid weight gain.
*   You have been trying to conceive for over a year (or 6 months if over 35) without success.
