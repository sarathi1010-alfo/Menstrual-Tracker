## Understanding PCOS

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder affecting roughly 10% of reproductive-aged women. It is characterized by three main features: irregular menstrual cycles, elevated levels of male hormones (androgens), and polycystic ovaries (enlarged ovaries containing small fluid collections).

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you have or suspect you have PCOS, cycle tracking is a crucial management tool. By consistently logging your cycle length, flow intensity, and daily symptoms (such as acne, unexpected hair growth, or weight changes), you can identify patterns. This data is invaluable when discussing your health with a doctor, as it provides a concrete history rather than relying on memory.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When tracking with [LunaCycle](/), watch for these specific patterns often associated with PCOS:

- **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently stretch beyond 35 days.
- **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period (oligomenorrhea or amenorrhea).
- **Spotting:** Irregular bleeding between periods without full flow.
- **Inconsistent Ovulation:** Basal body temperature tracking or ovulation strips may not show a clear, consistent ovulatory pattern.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking provides data, but a doctor provides a diagnosis. You should consult a healthcare professional if:
- Your cycles are consistently irregular or absent.
- You experience severe symptoms like rapid weight gain, severe acne, or excessive hair growth.
- You are trying to conceive and have been unable to do so after a year of unprotected intercourse (or 6 months if over 35).
