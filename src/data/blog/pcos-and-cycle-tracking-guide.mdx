Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder that affects approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by irregular menstrual cycles, elevated androgen (male hormone) levels, and sometimes the presence of small cysts on the ovaries.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

For individuals with PCOS, the menstrual cycle is often unpredictable, making it difficult to know when a period will arrive or if ovulation is occurring at all. Cycle tracking transitions this unpredictability into manageable data. By meticulously logging cycle dates and associated symptoms, you create a personalized health record that can guide lifestyle choices and medical treatment. For a general overview of tracking, see our [Ultimate Cycle Tracking Guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide).

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When tracking with PCOS, pay attention to the following patterns:

*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days (oligomenorrhea) are a hallmark of PCOS.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going 90 days or more without a period (amenorrhea).
*   **Irregular Bleeding:** Spotting or unpredictable bleeding patterns.
*   **Androgen Symptoms:** Log symptoms like sudden outbreaks of severe acne, unusual hair growth (hirsutism) on the face or body, or unexplained weight gain.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle data is most powerful when shared with a doctor. You should consult a healthcare provider if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You miss three or more periods in a row.
*   You experience severe symptoms like heavy bleeding or debilitating pelvic pain.
*   You are trying to conceive and have irregular cycles.
