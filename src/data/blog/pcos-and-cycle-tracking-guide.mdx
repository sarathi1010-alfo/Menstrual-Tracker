## Understanding PCOS

PCOS affects roughly 10% of women and is one of the most common causes of female infertility. It can cause irregular cycles, elevated androgen (male hormone) levels, and small cysts on the ovaries. Because the symptoms can vary wildly from person to person, it is often undiagnosed for years.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

Cycle tracking is an essential tool for managing PCOS. By keeping a detailed record, you can log cycle length and specific symptoms (e.g., acne breakouts, unwanted hair growth, weight changes) to identify patterns. This concrete data is incredibly useful when you speak to a healthcare provider.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

If you suspect you have PCOS, pay close attention to:
- **Prolonged cycles**: Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
- **Missed periods**: Going months without a period.
- **Spotting**: Unexplained spotting between periods.
- **Symptom correlation**: Noting when acne or mood swings peak.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

You should consult a doctor if your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days, if you miss three or more periods in a row, or if you experience severe symptoms like rapid weight gain or excessive hair growth. Early diagnosis can help manage the condition and prevent long-term complications like type 2 diabetes.
