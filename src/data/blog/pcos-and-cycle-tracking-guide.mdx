## Definition

PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) is a prevalent hormonal disorder that affects approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is typically characterized by a combination of irregular menstrual cycles, elevated androgen (male hormone) levels, and sometimes small cysts on the ovaries. These hormonal imbalances often disrupt regular ovulation, leading to unpredictable periods and various physical symptoms.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

While tracking cannot cure PCOS, it is an indispensable tool for managing it. By diligently logging your cycle length and associated symptoms (such as acne flare-ups, unusual hair growth, or weight changes), you can identify patterns that might otherwise go unnoticed. This comprehensive data record is invaluable when consulting with healthcare providers, helping them tailor treatments to your specific needs.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your logged data, pay close attention to:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going several months without a period.
*   **Spotting:** Unexplained bleeding between expected periods.
*   **Symptom Correlation:** Noting if symptoms like acne worsen during specific times of your elongated cycles.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking empowers you to know when it's time to seek professional medical advice. You should consult a healthcare provider if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You frequently miss periods entirely.
*   You experience severe, unmanageable symptoms related to your cycle.

| Condition | Symptoms | Cycle Impact | When to See a Doctor |
|---|---|---|---|
| PCOS | Acne, weight changes, hair growth | Irregular, prolonged, or missed periods | Cycles >35 days consistently |
