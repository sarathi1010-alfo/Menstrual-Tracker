# The Complete Guide to PCOS and Cycle Tracking

PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) affects ~10% of women, causing irregular cycles, high androgen levels, and ovarian cysts.

<MedicalDisclaimer />

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

PCOS is fundamentally a hormonal imbalance. Because it often disrupts ovulation, the most common symptom is irregular periods. Cycle tracking is one of the most effective non-invasive ways to monitor the condition. By logging cycle length and associated symptoms (e.g., acne, hair growth, weight changes), you can identify patterns that might indicate PCOS or track whether current management strategies are working.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your tracking history, look for these common PCOS indicators:

*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles consistently lasting longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period (amenorrhea).
*   **Frequent Spotting:** Irregular bleeding without a distinct period flow.
*   **Unpredictability:** A severe lack of pattern (e.g., a 25-day cycle followed by a 60-day cycle).

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

You should share your tracking data with a healthcare provider if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You have fewer than 8 periods a year.
*   You experience severe symptoms like rapid weight gain, severe acne, or significant pelvic pain.
