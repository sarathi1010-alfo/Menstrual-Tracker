PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) is a common hormonal disorder that affects approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by three main features: irregular periods, excess androgen levels (male hormones), and polycystic ovaries (enlarged ovaries containing numerous small fluid collections).

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you have PCOS or suspect you might, cycle tracking is one of the most powerful tools at your disposal. Because PCOS directly impacts ovulation, relying on memory to track when your last period occurred is often insufficient.

By using a tool like [LunaCycle](/tracker), you create an objective, long-term record of your reproductive health. Tracking helps you:
*   Identify whether you are ovulating regularly.
*   Document exactly how many days pass between periods.
*   Correlate physical symptoms (like acne breakouts, unexpected weight gain, or excess hair growth) with specific phases of your cycle.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your tracking history, look for the following patterns that are common in individuals with PCOS:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going 60, 90, or more days without a period.
*   **Unpredictability:** A cycle history that jumps wildly (e.g., 30 days one month, 65 days the next).
*   **Spotting:** Frequent spotting or light bleeding that doesn't develop into a full period.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking empowers you with data for your doctor's appointments. You should consult a healthcare provider if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You miss three or more periods in a row.
*   You experience severe, debilitating pain.
*   You have heavy bleeding that lasts more than a week.

PCOS is highly manageable, but early diagnosis is key. Tracking provides the foundation for that diagnosis.
