## Understanding PCOS

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal condition affecting approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is typically characterized by three main features, and a diagnosis usually requires at least two of them:
1.  **Irregular Periods:** Which means your ovaries do not regularly release eggs (ovulate).
2.  **Excess Androgen:** High levels of "male" hormones in your body, which may cause physical signs such as excess facial or body hair.
3.  **Polycystic Ovaries:** Your ovaries become enlarged and contain many fluid-filled sacs (follicles) that surround the eggs.

Because PCOS disrupts the normal [hormonal cycle](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained), understanding and monitoring your [menstrual cycle](/what-is-menstrual-cycle) is a crucial step in managing the condition.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you have or suspect you have PCOS, cycle tracking is one of the most proactive things you can do. Using a privacy-focused tool like [LunaCycle](/), you can gather concrete data about your body. This data transforms vague feelings into a clear record that you can share with your doctor.

When tracking, pay special attention to:
*   **Cycle Length:** Note if your cycles consistently last longer than 35 days.
*   **Irregularity:** Notice if the length of your cycle varies wildly from month to month.
*   **Symptoms:** Log physical symptoms associated with high androgens, such as acne breakouts, unusual hair growth, or unexplained weight changes.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

A "normal" period usually falls within a specific range, as discussed in [Understanding Your Period](/blog/understanding-your-period-normal-vs-not-normal). With PCOS, you might observe:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Going months between periods.
*   **Spotting:** Occasional light bleeding without a full period.
*   **Heavy Bleeding:** When a period finally arrives after a long absence, it may be unusually heavy because the uterine lining has built up over a longer time.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking provides the evidence you need to seek medical advice confidently. You should consult a healthcare provider if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You go 90 days or more without a period.
*   You experience severe symptoms like pelvic pain or heavy bleeding.
*   You are distressed by physical symptoms like severe acne or excess hair growth.

Early diagnosis and management are important, whether you are an adult or a [teenager just starting to track](/use-cases/cycle-tracking-teens-guide).
