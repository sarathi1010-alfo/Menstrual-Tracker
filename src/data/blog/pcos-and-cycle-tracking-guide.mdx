Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder that affects approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is often characterized by irregular menstrual cycles, elevated levels of male hormones (androgens), and sometimes, small fluid-filled sacs (cysts) on the ovaries.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

When you have PCOS, your cycle may lack the predictability of a standard 28-day cycle. Cycle tracking is one of the most effective tools for managing the condition. By meticulously logging your cycle length and symptoms (such as acne flare-ups, unusual hair growth, or weight fluctuations) in an app like [LunaCycle](/), you provide your healthcare provider with a vital timeline of your hormonal health.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your tracked data, pay close attention to:

- **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days (oligomenorrhea).
- **Missed Periods:** Going 90 days or more without a period (amenorrhea).
- **Anovulatory Cycles:** Cycles where bleeding occurs, but ovulation did not. (Tracking Basal Body Temperature can help detect this).
- **Symptom Clusters:** Noticing that acne or mood swings align with specific times, even if your cycle is irregular.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking empowers you to have informed conversations with your doctor. You should consult a healthcare provider if:

- Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
- You miss periods frequently.
- Your periods are excessively heavy.
- You have severe symptoms of high androgens (excessive body hair, severe acne, male-pattern baldness).

| Condition | Symptoms | Cycle Impact | When to See a Doctor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **PCOS** | Acne, excess hair, weight changes | Irregular, prolonged, or absent cycles | Consistently >35 day cycles |
