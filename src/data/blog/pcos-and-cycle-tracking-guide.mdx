## Definition: What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a hormonal disorder that affects approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by an imbalance of reproductive hormones, which creates problems in the ovaries.

The three main features of PCOS are:
1.  **Irregular periods:** Which means the ovaries do not regularly release eggs (ovulation).
2.  **Excess androgen:** High levels of "male" hormones in the body, which may cause physical signs such as excess facial or body hair.
3.  **Polycystic ovaries:** The ovaries become enlarged and contain many fluid-filled sacs (follicles) that surround the eggs.

*Note: You do not need to have all three features to be diagnosed with PCOS.*

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you have PCOS or suspect you might, tracking your cycle is one of the most powerful tools you have.

By meticulously logging your cycle length and symptoms (e.g., acne flare-ups, hair growth, weight changes), you create a historical record. This record helps your healthcare provider identify patterns, make an accurate diagnosis, and monitor if treatments (like dietary changes or medication) are working.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When tracking with a tool like [LunaCycle](/), keep an eye out for these PCOS-related patterns:

*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Consistently having cycles that last longer than 35 days.
*   **Oligomenorrhea:** Having fewer than eight periods in a year.
*   **Amenorrhea:** Missing periods for three months or more.
*   **Heavy Bleeding:** Because the uterine lining builds up for a longer time before shedding, periods can be unusually heavy.
*   **Symptom Correlation:** Notice if acne or hair growth correlates with longer cycles.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

If your cycles are consistently greater than 35 days, if you miss multiple periods, or if you are experiencing severe symptoms like sudden weight gain or disruptive hair growth, you should consult a doctor.
