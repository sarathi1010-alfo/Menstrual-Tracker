PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) affects ~10% of women, causing irregular cycles, high androgen levels, and ovarian cysts.

## Definition

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a hormonal disorder common among women of reproductive age. Women with PCOS may have infrequent or prolonged menstrual periods or excess male hormone (androgen) levels. The ovaries may develop numerous small collections of fluid (follicles) and fail to regularly release eggs. Understanding this condition is a vital part of reproductive health, a topic covered broadly in our [Ultimate Cycle Tracking Guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide).

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

When you have PCOS, your cycle might feel entirely unpredictable. However, diligent cycle tracking can reveal hidden patterns. By logging cycle length, flow, and various symptoms (e.g., acne, unusual hair growth, weight changes), you gather objective data. This data is invaluable when you visit a healthcare provider, as it provides a clear picture of your condition over time, rather than relying on memory. For younger individuals managing this, refer to our [Cycle Tracking for Teens](/use-cases/cycle-tracking-teens-guide).

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When analyzing your tracking data with PCOS, watch for these key indicators:
- **Prolonged cycles:** Cycles consistently longer than 35 days.
- **Missed periods:** Going months without a menstrual bleed.
- **Spotting:** Unexplained light bleeding between periods.
- **Symptom clusters:** Noting if acne flare-ups or mood changes correlate with specific days in your extended cycle.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

While tracking empowers you, PCOS is a medical condition that requires professional management. You should consult a healthcare provider if:
- Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
- You miss your period for three or more consecutive months.
- You experience severe symptoms like debilitating pain or heavy, prolonged bleeding.
- You are trying to conceive and having difficulty.
