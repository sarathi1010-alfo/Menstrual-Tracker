## What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder affecting roughly 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by three main features (two of which are required for diagnosis):
1. **Irregular or absent menstrual periods** (due to a lack of ovulation).
2. **High levels of androgens** (male hormones), leading to symptoms like acne or excess body hair.
3. **Polycystic ovaries** (enlarged ovaries containing numerous small cysts visible on an ultrasound).

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you suspect you have PCOS, or if you have already been diagnosed, cycle tracking is one of the most effective tools for managing the condition.

### Identifying Patterns
By logging your cycle start dates consistently in [LunaCycle](/tracker), you can easily identify if your cycles are chronically longer than 35 days. The visual calendar and Patterns chart make it simple to see these irregularities at a glance.

### Monitoring Symptoms
PCOS isn't just about periods; it impacts your entire body. By using the daily log to track symptoms like acne breakouts, sudden weight changes, or unusual hair growth, you can monitor how your hormone levels are fluctuating month to month.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your tracking history, watch for:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period.
*   **Unpredictable Spotting:** Light bleeding that doesn't follow a regular pattern.
*   **Lack of Ovulation Signs:** If you track temperature or mucus, an absence of the typical shifts that indicate ovulation.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking data is incredibly empowering when you visit a doctor. You should consult a healthcare provider if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You miss three or more periods in a row.
*   You experience severe PCOS symptoms (severe acne, rapid weight gain, excessive hair growth) alongside irregular cycles.
