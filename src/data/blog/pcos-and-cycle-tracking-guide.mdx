## Definition

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder affecting approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by an imbalance of reproductive hormones, which creates problems in the ovaries. Individuals with PCOS often experience irregular or prolonged menstrual cycles, elevated levels of male hormones (androgens), and small collections of fluid (follicles) in the ovaries, which can disrupt normal ovulation.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

For those managing PCOS, consistent cycle tracking is an essential tool for communicating with healthcare providers. By meticulously logging cycle length and bleeding patterns, you can provide concrete evidence of irregularities. Furthermore, tracking associated symptoms—such as unexplained weight changes, acne flare-ups, or excess hair growth—helps paint a comprehensive picture of how the condition is affecting your body on a daily basis.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your cycle data in [LunaCycle](/), keep an eye out for hallmark signs of PCOS. The most prominent indicator is a consistently prolonged cycle, typically defined as lasting longer than 35 days. Other red flags include entirely missed periods (amenorrhea) for several consecutive months, or frequent, unpredictable spotting without a true menstrual flow.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

If your tracking reveals consistent irregularities, it is vital to consult a healthcare provider for a proper evaluation. Specifically, seek medical advice if you regularly experience cycles longer than 35 days, if you miss three or more periods in a row, or if you struggle with severe physical symptoms like debilitating cramps, sudden weight gain, or significant emotional distress related to your cycle.
