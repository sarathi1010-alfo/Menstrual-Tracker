## What is PCOS?

PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) is a common hormonal disorder that affects approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by a combination of signs, including irregular or missing menstrual cycles, high levels of male hormones (androgens), and often, the presence of many small cysts on the ovaries.

Because PCOS disrupts the normal hormonal signals needed for ovulation, women with the condition often experience highly unpredictable periods.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

When your cycle is irregular, tracking might seem pointless because predictions are often inaccurate. However, tracking is actually *more* important if you have PCOS.

Instead of focusing on predictions, use cycle tracking to gather a historical record. By logging your cycle length and symptoms in [LunaCycle](/), you can begin to identify patterns. Track specific PCOS-related symptoms such as:
- Hormonal acne flare-ups
- Unusual hair growth
- Sudden weight changes
- Pelvic pain

This data creates a concrete record of your lived experience.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your tracking history, keep an eye out for these patterns:

- **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
- **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period (amenorrhea).
- **Frequent Spotting:** Unexplained bleeding between periods.
- **Anovulation Signs:** A lack of typical ovulation signs (like changes in cervical mucus or temperature) if you track them.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking empowers you to have productive conversations with your doctor. You should consult a healthcare provider if:
- Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
- You have missed three or more periods in a row.
- You are experiencing severe symptoms like debilitating pain or rapid weight gain.
- You are trying to conceive and have irregular cycles.

Bring your [LunaCycle](/) data history to your appointment. It provides your doctor with exact timelines rather than relying on estimates, making diagnosis and treatment much more effective.
