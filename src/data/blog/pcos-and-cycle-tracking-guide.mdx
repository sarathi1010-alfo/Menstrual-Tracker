## Definition

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common endocrine system disorder affecting approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is typically characterized by a combination of three main features: irregular or absent menstrual periods, elevated levels of male hormones (androgens), and multiple small cysts on the ovaries (visible via ultrasound). The exact cause is unknown, but insulin resistance and low-grade inflammation are frequently involved.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you have or suspect you have PCOS, cycle tracking is not just about period prediction; it is a vital disease management tool. Because PCOS makes cycles unpredictable, a digital record is essential for communicating with your endocrinologist or gynecologist.

By rigorously logging your cycle, you create a map of your hormonal health. You should log:
*   **Cycle Length:** The exact number of days between periods.
*   **Symptoms:** Note the occurrence of acne breakouts, unusual hair growth (hirsutism), or unexplained weight changes.
*   **Ovulation Signs:** Because ovulation is often delayed or absent, tracking basal body temperature (BBT) or cervical mucus can help you determine *if* you are ovulating at all.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your tracking history in a tool like [LunaCycle](/), look for these specific PCOS indicators:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days (Oligomenorrhea).
*   **Missed Periods:** Going 3 or more months without a period (Amenorrhea).
*   **Spotting:** Frequent, light bleeding between periods without a full flow.
*   **Symptom Correlation:** Notice if spikes in acne or mood changes correspond with delayed periods.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking empowers you to know when it is time to seek professional medical advice. You should consult a doctor if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You miss three or more periods in a row.
*   You experience severe pelvic pain.
*   You are trying to conceive and have not been successful after 6-12 months of tracking.
*   You experience distressing symptoms of high androgens (severe acne, male-pattern hair thinning, excessive body hair).
