## Definition

PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) affects approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is a hormonal disorder characterized by infrequent or prolonged menstrual periods, elevated androgen (male hormone) levels, and sometimes small collections of fluid (cysts) developing on the ovaries.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you suspect you have PCOS, or have been diagnosed, tracking is your best tool for management. By consistently logging your cycle length and daily symptoms (like acne breakouts, unexpected hair growth, or sudden weight changes), you and your healthcare provider can identify patterns. To understand how this fits into a broader tracking strategy, read the [Ultimate Cycle Tracking Guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide) and for specific audiences, see [Cycle Tracking for Teens](/use-cases/cycle-tracking-teens-guide).

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When tracking with [LunaCycle](/), women with PCOS should look out for:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going 90 days or more without a period.
*   **Irregular Spotting:** Bleeding unpredictably throughout the month.
*   **Lack of Ovulation Signs:** Not noticing the typical cervical mucus changes associated with the fertile window.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking provides the evidence you need. You should consult a doctor if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You have fewer than 8 periods a year.
*   You experience severe symptoms of high androgens (severe acne, excessive facial/body hair).
