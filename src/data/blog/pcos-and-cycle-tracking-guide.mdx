## What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a hormonal disorder common among women of reproductive age. It affects approximately 10% of women. It is characterized by infrequent or prolonged menstrual periods, elevated levels of male hormones (androgens), and the potential development of small collections of fluid (follicles) in the ovaries, which may fail to regularly release eggs.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you suspect you have PCOS, cycle tracking is one of the most powerful tools you have. By logging your cycle length and symptoms in [LunaCycle](/tracker), you create a concrete health record. Track physical symptoms such as unexpected weight changes, severe acne, or unusual hair growth, as these are clinical signs of elevated androgens.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your data, look for:
- **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
- **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period (oligomenorrhea).
- **Spotting:** Light, irregular bleeding instead of a full period.
- **Lack of Ovulation Signs:** If you track BBT or cervical mucus and never see a shift, it may indicate anovulatory cycles.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

You should consult a healthcare provider if your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days, if you miss your period for three consecutive months, or if you are experiencing severe symptoms that impact your quality of life. Bring your tracking data with you; it will provide your doctor with invaluable insight.
