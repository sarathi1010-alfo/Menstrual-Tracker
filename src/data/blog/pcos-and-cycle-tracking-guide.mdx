## What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal condition that affects roughly 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by an imbalance of reproductive hormones, leading to issues with the ovaries. Women with PCOS may not ovulate regularly, which results in irregular, prolonged, or missed periods.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

When you have PCOS, your cycle is rarely textbook. This makes tracking not just helpful, but necessary. Detailed logging creates a historical record of your hormonal patterns, which is invaluable for doctors trying to diagnose the condition or monitor the effectiveness of treatments (like lifestyle changes or medication).

By using a tool like [LunaCycle](/), you can safely log your data without worrying about your sensitive medical information being shared.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

If you suspect you have PCOS, or have been diagnosed, pay close attention to:
- **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
- **Missed Periods:** Going 90 days or more without a period.
- **Symptom Patterns:** Documenting the timing and severity of symptoms like severe acne, excessive hair growth (hirsutism), unexplained weight gain, or mood swings.
- **Ovulation Signs:** Tracking basal body temperature (BBT) or cervical mucus to see if ovulation is actually occurring, even if bleeding happens.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

| Scenario | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Your cycles are consistently over 35 days. | Schedule a consultation with a gynecologist or endocrinologist. |
| You miss 3 or more periods in a row. | Seek medical advice to induce a withdrawal bleed and prevent complications. |
| You experience severe pelvic pain. | Contact a doctor immediately, as this could be an ovarian cyst issue. |
| You are trying to conceive without success. | Discuss ovulation induction or other fertility treatments. |
