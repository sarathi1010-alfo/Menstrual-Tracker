Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal condition that affects roughly 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by irregular cycles, elevated androgen levels, and sometimes small cysts on the ovaries.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help with PCOS

For individuals with PCOS, the menstrual cycle is often unpredictable, making it difficult to know what to expect and when. Cycle tracking transforms this unpredictability into manageable data. By meticulously logging cycle length and daily symptoms (such as acne flare-ups, excessive hair growth, or weight fluctuations), you can identify underlying patterns. This data empowers you to understand your unique manifestation of the syndrome and evaluate how well lifestyle changes or medical treatments are working.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When tracking with a tool like [LunaCycle](/), pay close attention to these key indicators if you suspect or have PCOS:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going for months without a period (oligomenorrhea or amenorrhea).
*   **Spotting:** Unpredictable light bleeding between expected periods.
*   **Symptom Correlation:** Notice if symptoms like pelvic pain or mood swings align with specific times, even if a period doesn't arrive.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking provides the concrete evidence you need to have a productive conversation with your doctor. You should consult a healthcare provider if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You miss three or more periods in a row.
*   You experience severe symptoms that interfere with your daily life.
*   You are trying to conceive and are struggling due to irregular ovulation.
