## Understanding PCOS

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a hormonal disorder common among women of reproductive age. It is characterized by infrequent or prolonged menstrual periods, excess male hormone (androgen) levels, and the ovaries may develop numerous small collections of fluid (follicles) and fail to regularly release eggs.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

When you have PCOS, your cycle might feel like a total mystery. Cycle tracking turns that mystery into data.

By consistently logging your period dates and symptoms, you can:
*   Identify just how irregular your cycles are (e.g., varying from 30 to 60 days).
*   Track symptom flare-ups, such as hormonal acne breakouts, unusual hair growth, or sudden weight changes.
*   Monitor if lifestyle changes (diet, exercise) or medications are helping regulate your cycle over time.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

If you suspect you might have PCOS, or are actively managing it, use [LunaCycle](/tracker) to watch for these specific patterns:

*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles consistently lasting longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period.
*   **Spotting:** Frequent mid-cycle bleeding without a full period.
*   **Lack of Ovulation Signs:** Absence of the typical basal body temperature shift or fertile cervical mucus.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking data is your best advocate in the doctor's office. You should consult a healthcare provider if:

*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You miss three or more periods in a row.
*   You experience severe pelvic pain.
*   You are trying to conceive and have been unsuccessful for over a year (or six months if over 35).

Bring your [LunaCycle](/tracker) history to your appointment. Being able to show exactly how long your cycles are is much more helpful to a doctor than estimating from memory.
