## What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder affecting approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by three main features:
1.  **Irregular Periods**: Infrequent, irregular, or prolonged menstrual cycles.
2.  **Excess Androgens**: High levels of male hormones, leading to physical signs like severe acne or excess facial/body hair (hirsutism).
3.  **Polycystic Ovaries**: Ovaries may be enlarged and contain follicles that surround the eggs, leading to a failure to regularly release eggs (anovulation).

You do not need to have all three features to be diagnosed, but irregular cycles are a hallmark symptom.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

When managing or seeking a diagnosis for PCOS, your cycle data is your strongest advocate. Tracking allows you to move beyond vague feelings of irregularity to concrete data.
*   **Log Cycle Length**: Record the exact number of days between periods. PCOS often results in cycles longer than 35 days.
*   **Monitor Symptoms**: Track acne flare-ups, unexplained weight gain, and unusual hair growth alongside your cycle data.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

If you suspect PCOS, analyze your tracking history for these patterns:
*   **Prolonged Cycles**: Consistently having cycles that last longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods**: Going months without a period (amenorrhea).
*   **Heavy Bleeding**: When a period finally arrives after a long delay, the uterine lining may have built up significantly, resulting in a very heavy flow.
*   **Lack of Ovulation Signs**: If you track basal body temperature (BBT) or cervical mucus and never see the typical signs of ovulation.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle tracking provides the evidence you need to have a productive conversation with your doctor. You should consult a healthcare provider if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You miss three or more periods in a row.
*   You experience severe symptoms of high androgens (sudden severe acne, male-pattern baldness).
