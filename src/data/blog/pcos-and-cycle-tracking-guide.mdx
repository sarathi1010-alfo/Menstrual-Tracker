PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) affects approximately 10% of women, causing irregular cycles, high androgen levels, and ovarian cysts. If you suspect you have PCOS, reading our [ultimate cycle tracking guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide) is a great place to start understanding how to monitor your health.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

Cycle tracking cannot cure PCOS, but it is an indispensable tool for managing it. By meticulously logging cycle length and associated symptoms (like acne, abnormal hair growth, or weight changes), you can identify patterns and evaluate if lifestyle changes or treatments are working. It is especially helpful for those who start tracking early, as outlined in our [teens guide](/use-cases/cycle-tracking-teens-guide).

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When tracking with [LunaCycle](/), individuals with PCOS should pay close attention to:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles consistently lasting longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period (amenorrhea).
*   **Spotting:** Irregular bleeding between periods.
*   **Lack of Ovulation Signs:** Absence of basal body temperature shifts or fertile cervical mucus.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

If your cycle data consistently shows cycles longer than 35 days, if you miss three or more periods in a row, or if you experience severe symptoms like debilitating pain or rapid weight gain, you should consult a doctor. Bring your tracking data to the appointment; it is invaluable for the diagnostic process.

*   PCOS is a common hormonal disorder.
