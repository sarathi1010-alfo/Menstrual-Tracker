## Understanding PCOS

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder that affects approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by irregular menstrual cycles, elevated levels of androgen (male hormones), and sometimes polycystic ovaries (ovaries that develop small collections of fluid). These hormonal imbalances can interfere with the growth and release of eggs during ovulation.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you have PCOS, or suspect you might, tracking your cycle is an essential management tool. Logging your cycle length and daily symptoms helps you and your healthcare provider identify patterns. By tracking symptoms such as acne, excess hair growth, weight changes, and mood swings alongside your bleeding dates, you create a comprehensive record of your hormonal health. This data is crucial for diagnosis and monitoring the effectiveness of any treatments. [LunaCycle](/) offers a private way to keep this detailed log.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your tracking history, look for these common PCOS indicators:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles consistently longer than 35 days.
*   **Irregularity:** Cycles that vary wildly in length from month to month.
*   **Missed Periods:** Frequently skipping periods altogether.
*   **Spotting:** Bleeding lightly between expected periods.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

You should consult a doctor if you consistently experience:
*   Cycles longer than 35 days or fewer than 8 cycles a year.
*   Severe acne or unusual hair growth.
*   Difficulty getting pregnant.
*   Sudden weight gain that is hard to lose.
