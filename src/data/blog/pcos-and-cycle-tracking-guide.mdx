PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) affects ~10% of women, causing irregular cycles, high androgen levels, and ovarian cysts.

## Definition of PCOS

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome is a common hormonal disorder among women of reproductive age. It is typically diagnosed when a woman has at least two of the following: irregular or missing periods, elevated levels of male hormones (androgens), or polycystic ovaries visible on an ultrasound. The exact cause is unknown, but it often involves insulin resistance and low-grade inflammation.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you suspect you have PCOS, or if you have already been diagnosed, cycle tracking is one of the most effective management tools available. By diligently logging your cycle length and symptoms, you create a concrete record of your body's patterns. This data is invaluable. It helps you recognize triggers for your symptoms (like acne or fatigue) and provides your doctor with the evidence needed to tailor your treatment plan.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When tracking with an app like [LunaCycle](/), pay close attention to:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going 90 days or more without a period.
*   **Spotting:** Irregular bleeding between periods.
*   **Androgen Symptoms:** Track when you experience severe acne flare-ups or notice excess hair growth.
*   **Weight Fluctuations:** Note any rapid weight gain, as PCOS often impacts metabolism.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

You should not try to diagnose or treat PCOS on your own. Consult a healthcare provider if:
*   Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days or highly irregular.
*   You experience severe symptoms like debilitating pelvic pain or very heavy bleeding.
*   You have missed your period for three consecutive months.
