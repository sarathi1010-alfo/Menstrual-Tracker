Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder that affects approximately 10% of reproductive-age women. It is characterized by irregular menstrual cycles, elevated levels of male hormones (androgens), and often, small cysts on the ovaries.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you have PCOS, or suspect you might, cycle tracking is one of the most powerful tools at your disposal. While tracking cannot cure PCOS, it provides the essential data you and your healthcare provider need to manage it effectively.

Tracking helps you:
1.  **Identify Patterns:** Even irregular cycles have patterns. Tracking helps you see if your cycles are consistently long or if you are skipping periods entirely.
2.  **Monitor Symptoms:** Logging symptoms like acne breakouts, unusual hair growth, or weight fluctuations alongside your bleeding patterns can help pinpoint hormonal surges.
3.  **Evaluate Treatment:** If you start a new treatment (like dietary changes or medication), tracking shows whether your cycle is becoming more regular over time.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When tracking with a tool like [LunaCycle](/), pay close attention to:

*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period.
*   **Anovulatory Cycles:** Cycles where you bleed but do not ovulate (often indicated by a lack of temperature shift if you track BBT, or very unpredictable bleeding).
*   **Spotting:** Frequent spotting between periods.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

You should schedule an appointment with a doctor if:

*   Your menstrual cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You have missed three or more periods in a row.
*   Your periods are extremely heavy (soaking through a pad/tampon every hour).
*   You experience severe pelvic pain.
*   You have symptoms of elevated androgens, such as severe acne or excessive facial/body hair.
