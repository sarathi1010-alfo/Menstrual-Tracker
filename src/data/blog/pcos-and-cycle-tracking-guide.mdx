Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most common endocrine disorders, affecting approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by hormonal imbalances, specifically elevated androgen levels, which can disrupt the [menstrual cycle](/what-is-menstrual-cycle) and cause a range of systemic symptoms.

## Understanding PCOS

Despite its name, PCOS is not strictly about cysts on the ovaries; it's a metabolic and hormonal issue. The defining characteristic of PCOS for many is anovulation—the failure to release an egg regularly. Without regular [ovulation](/what-is-ovulation), the cycle becomes unpredictable, often leading to very long gaps between periods or entirely absent periods.

Other common symptoms include excessive facial or body hair (hirsutism), severe acne, weight gain or difficulty losing weight, and insulin resistance. Because the symptoms are varied and mimic other conditions, proper diagnosis by a healthcare provider is essential.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you suspect you have PCOS, or if you have already been diagnosed, tracking your cycle with a tool like [LunaCycle](/) is one of the most powerful ways you can participate in your care.

Doctors rely heavily on your reported cycle history to make a diagnosis and assess whether treatments are working. By diligently tracking your [cycle phases](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal), you provide concrete data rather than relying on memory. Tracking helps you identify if lifestyle changes or medications are successfully regulating your cycle or mitigating specific symptoms.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When you are tracking with PCOS in mind, pay particular attention to these data points:

*   **Cycle Length:** Look for patterns of prolonged cycles. Cycles consistently lasting more than 35 days are a primary indicator of infrequent ovulation (oligoovulation).
*   **Missing Periods:** Note any cycles that simply do not arrive for months at a time.
*   **Symptom Correlation:** Track non-menstrual symptoms like acne flare-ups, changes in hair growth, or significant shifts in weight or energy, and see if they correlate with specific times in your (even irregular) cycle.
*   **Spotting:** Note any spotting that occurs outside of a normal menstrual flow, as hormonal imbalances can cause the uterine lining to shed irregularly.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

While tracking is a valuable tool, it is not a substitute for medical care. You should consult a healthcare provider if you experience:

*   Cycles that are consistently longer than 35 days.
*   Complete absence of a period for 3 months or more (and you are not pregnant).
*   Periods that are excessively heavy when they do occur.
*   Distressing physical symptoms like sudden, severe acne or unwanted hair growth.

By combining consistent tracking habits with professional medical guidance, you can effectively manage PCOS and improve your quality of life.
