## Definition
Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder that affects approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by a combination of symptoms, primarily driven by an imbalance of reproductive hormones. Women with PCOS often have higher than normal levels of androgens (male hormones), which can interfere with the development and release of eggs during ovulation. As a result, the ovaries may develop numerous small collections of fluid (follicles) and fail to regularly release eggs. The three main features of PCOS are irregular periods, excess androgen, and polycystic ovaries. A diagnosis usually requires the presence of at least two of these features.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help
Living with PCOS can be challenging due to the unpredictability of symptoms, but cycle tracking is an incredibly powerful tool for management. When you track your cycle, you move from guessing about your symptoms to gathering concrete data. This data is invaluable for both you and your healthcare provider.

By logging your cycle length, you can establish a clear record of how often you are (or aren't) menstruating. But tracking should go beyond just dates. Logging daily symptoms—such as acne flare-ups, changes in facial or body hair growth, fluctuations in weight, and energy levels—can help you identify personal triggers and patterns. Over time, this detailed record allows you to see if lifestyle interventions, dietary changes, or prescribed medications are having a positive effect on regulating your cycle and mitigating symptoms.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data
When reviewing your tracking data, specific patterns may point toward PCOS or indicate that current management strategies need adjustment:

- **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days (oligomenorrhea) are a hallmark of PCOS.
- **Missed Periods:** Going 90 days or more without a period (amenorrhea) when not pregnant.
- **Unpredictable Bleeding:** Episodes of heavy or prolonged bleeding, often occurring after a long stretch of missed periods.
- **Symptom Clusters:** Noticing that acne flare-ups or mood changes correlate with specific, prolonged phases of an irregular cycle.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider
If you suspect you have PCOS, or if you have already been diagnosed and your symptoms are worsening, it is crucial to seek medical guidance. You should consult a healthcare provider if:

- Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days or highly unpredictable.
- You have missed your period for three or more consecutive months.
- You are experiencing severe symptoms of excess androgens, such as rapid, excessive hair growth on your face or body, or severe acne.
- You are trying to conceive and have not been successful, as PCOS is one of the most common, but treatable, causes of female infertility.

Armed with months of tracked cycle data, your conversations with your doctor will be much more productive, leading to a more tailored and effective treatment plan.
