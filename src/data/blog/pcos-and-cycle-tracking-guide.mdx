PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) affects approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is an endocrine disorder characterized by irregular cycles, elevated androgen levels, and often, small cysts on the ovaries.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

While tracking a highly irregular cycle can be frustrating, the data is incredibly valuable. Logging your cycle length and associated symptoms (like acne, unwanted hair growth, or weight changes) helps identify long-term patterns. This concrete data is crucial when you speak to a healthcare provider.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

If you have or suspect PCOS, pay close attention to:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles consistently lasting longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going several months without a period.
*   **Spotting:** Unpredictable light bleeding between expected periods.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

You should consult a doctor if your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days, if you miss three or more periods in a row, or if you experience severe symptoms like debilitating pain or rapid weight gain.

| Condition | Symptoms | Cycle Impact | When to See a Doctor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| PCOS | Acne, hair growth, weight changes | Irregular, >35 days | Consistently irregular cycles |
