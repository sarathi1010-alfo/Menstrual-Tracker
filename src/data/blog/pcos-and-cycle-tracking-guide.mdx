## Definition

PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) affects roughly 10% of women of reproductive age. It is a hormonal disorder that can cause irregular menstrual cycles, elevated androgen (male hormone) levels, and small cysts on the ovaries. The irregular cycles are primarily due to a lack of regular ovulation.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you suspect you have PCOS or have been diagnosed with it, cycle tracking is one of the most powerful tools at your disposal. Tracking helps you move from vague feelings of irregularity to concrete data. By logging your cycle length and symptoms—such as acne breakouts, unusual hair growth, or weight fluctuations—you can identify clear patterns. Tools like [LunaCycle](/) allow you to securely store this sensitive data and present a clear history to your healthcare provider, which is invaluable for diagnosis and management.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your tracking data, pay special attention to:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period (amenorrhea).
*   **Unusual Spotting:** Bleeding between periods or very light, infrequent periods.
*   **Symptom Correlation:** Notice if symptoms like acne or fatigue flare up at specific times in your (irregular) cycle.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

You should consult a doctor if your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days, if you miss multiple periods in a row, or if you experience severe symptoms like debilitating pain, heavy bleeding, or noticeable physical changes (like sudden weight gain or excess facial hair).
