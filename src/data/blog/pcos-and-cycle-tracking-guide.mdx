PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) affects ~10% of women, causing irregular cycles, high androgen levels, and ovarian cysts.

## What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a hormonal disorder common among women of reproductive age. The exact cause is unknown, but it often involves insulin resistance and elevated levels of androgens (male hormones). Diagnosis typically requires two of the following three criteria:
1. Irregular periods or no periods (anovulation).
2. Higher levels of androgens (detected via blood tests or physical signs like acne/excess hair).
3. Polycystic ovaries visible on an ultrasound.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you suspect you have PCOS, or have been diagnosed, tracking your cycle with an app like [LunaCycle](/) is a critical management tool.
- **Identify Patterns:** Logging cycle length helps you see just how irregular your periods are.
- **Track Symptoms:** Log symptoms like acne flare-ups, unusual hair growth, or weight changes alongside your cycle data to provide a comprehensive picture to your doctor.
- **Monitor Treatment:** If you are trying lifestyle changes or medications to manage PCOS, tracking helps verify if your cycles are becoming more regular.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When reviewing your tracking data, pay special attention to:
- **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles consistently lasting longer than 35 days.
- **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period.
- **Spotting:** Frequent spotting between periods without a full flow.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

You should consult a healthcare provider if:
- Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
- You have missed three or more periods in a row.
- You are experiencing severe symptoms like heavy bleeding, debilitating pain, or distressing physical changes (excess hair, severe acne).
