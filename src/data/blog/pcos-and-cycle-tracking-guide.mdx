## What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder affecting approximately 10% of women of reproductive age. It is typically characterized by a combination of three main features: irregular menstrual cycles, elevated levels of androgens (male hormones), and polycystic ovaries (ovaries that contain numerous small collections of fluid). These hormonal imbalances can interfere with ovulation, making the menstrual cycle unpredictable.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you have PCOS, or suspect you might, cycle tracking is one of the most powerful tools at your disposal. While a tracker cannot diagnose PCOS, it provides essential data. By diligently logging your cycle length and associated symptoms, you can identify patterns that might otherwise go unnoticed. This objective data is crucial when communicating with a healthcare provider, moving the conversation from "my periods are irregular" to "my last three cycles were 45, 60, and 42 days long."

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When tracking with [LunaCycle](/), specifically look for these patterns often associated with PCOS:
*   **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently last longer than 35 days.
*   **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period (oligomenorrhea or amenorrhea).
*   **Spotting:** Unpredictable light bleeding between periods.
*   **Physical Symptoms:** Note the occurrence of acne (especially along the jawline), excess hair growth (hirsutism), or unexplained weight changes.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

You should consult a doctor if you experience:
*   Cycles consistently longer than 35 days.
*   You have gone three or more months without a period (and are not pregnant).
*   You experience severe pelvic pain.
*   You have symptoms of elevated androgens, such as severe acne or excessive facial/body hair.

Early diagnosis and management of PCOS can help prevent long-term complications. Tracking can also be a helpful tool for younger individuals; see our [guide for teens](/use-cases/cycle-tracking-teens-guide) for more context.
