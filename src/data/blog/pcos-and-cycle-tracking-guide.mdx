## What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal disorder affecting roughly 10% of women of reproductive age. It is characterized by three main features: irregular menstrual cycles, excess androgen (male hormone) levels, and polycystic ovaries (ovaries that become enlarged and contain numerous small fluid-filled sacs). Because it disrupts ovulation, PCOS is one of the leading causes of female infertility and can cause significant metabolic and emotional distress.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you have PCOS, cycle tracking is one of the most powerful tools in your management arsenal. It transforms vague feelings of irregularity into concrete data. By diligently logging cycle lengths and daily symptoms, you can identify patterns that might otherwise go unnoticed. For instance, you might correlate periods of high stress with unusually long cycles, or track how dietary changes affect your symptoms over several months.

Using a private tool like [LunaCycle](/tracker), you can safely log sensitive data such as weight fluctuations, unusual hair growth, or acne flare-ups—all common symptoms of PCOS.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When analyzing your tracking history, keep an eye out for these PCOS indicators:
* **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles that consistently stretch beyond 35 days.
* **Missed Periods:** Going 90 days or more without a period.
* **Frequent Spotting:** Unpredictable bleeding or spotting between expected periods.
* **Lack of Ovulation Signs:** An absence of typical ovulation indicators, such as a mid-cycle basal body temperature shift or fertile cervical mucus.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

| Symptom | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Consistently >35 day cycles | Schedule an evaluation with a gynecologist or endocrinologist. |
| Severe acne or unexpected hair growth | Discuss with a doctor; these are signs of high androgens. |
| Difficulty losing weight | Consult a specialist for metabolic support. |
| Trying to conceive without success for >6 months | Seek fertility consultation, as PCOS often requires medical assistance for ovulation. |

Bring your tracking data with you to your appointment; it will be invaluable to your doctor.
