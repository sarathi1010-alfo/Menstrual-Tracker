## 1. Technical Foundation

### The Four Phases of the Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle is a complex interplay of hormones that prepares the body for a potential pregnancy each month. It is generally divided into four phases:

1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining, marking the start of your cycle.
2. **Follicular Phase:** The pituitary gland releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to prepare an egg. Estrogen levels rise.
3. **Ovulation:** A surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) triggers the release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The ruptured follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone to thicken the uterine lining in preparation for a fertilized egg.

### Key Hormones

- **Estrogen:** Rebuilds the uterine lining and peaks just before ovulation.
- **Progesterone:** Maintains the uterine lining after ovulation. A drop in progesterone triggers menstruation.
- **Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH):** Stimulates follicle growth in the ovaries.
- **Luteinizing Hormone (LH):** Triggers ovulation.

## 2. Top 10 Tracking Strategies

1. **Use a Privacy-First App:** Tools like [LunaCycle](/) keep your data on your device, ensuring maximum privacy.
2. **Track Basal Body Temperature (BBT):** A slight rise in BBT can indicate that ovulation has occurred.
3. **Monitor Cervical Mucus:** Changes in consistency can signal your fertile window.
4. **Log Symptoms Daily:** Track mood, energy, and physical symptoms to identify patterns.
5. **Use Ovulation Predictor Kits (OPKs):** OPKs detect the LH surge that precedes ovulation.
6. **Note Cycle Length:** Record the start date of each period to calculate your average cycle length.
7. **Track Flow Intensity:** Monitoring changes in flow can help identify conditions like menorrhagia.
8. **Observe Changes in Libido:** Hormonal fluctuations can affect sexual desire throughout your cycle.
9. **Record Sleep Patterns:** Hormones can influence sleep quality, especially during the luteal phase.
10. **Consult a Healthcare Provider:** Always discuss significant changes or concerns with a medical professional.
