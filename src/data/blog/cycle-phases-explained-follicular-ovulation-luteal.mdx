The menstrual cycle is divided into four phases, each with distinct hormonal changes and physical effects. Knowing them helps you understand your body's rhythm. By tracking these phases with [LunaCycle](/), you can optimize your lifestyle and health decisions.

## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Your cycle isn't just about the days you bleed. The entire month is a fluctuating landscape of hormones. Understanding these phases—Menstruation, Follicular, Ovulation, and Luteal—is essential for health tracking, managing fertility, and even optimizing your workout and diet plans. You can start by reading the [Menstrual Cycle 101](/blog/menstrual-cycle-101-beginners-guide) to get a foundational understanding, and then read more deeply into the [Ultimate Cycle Tracking Guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide) for comprehensive tracking strategies.

## How to Identify Each Phase

1.  **Menstruation (Days 1-5 approx.):** The shedding of the uterine lining. Symptoms include bleeding and cramping.
2.  **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13 approx.):** Overlaps with menstruation. Estrogen rises as follicles grow in the ovaries. You might notice increased energy.
3.  **Ovulation (Day 14 approx.):** The release of an egg. Look for clear, stretchy cervical mucus and a slight temp rise.
4.  **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28 approx.):** Progesterone dominates to prep for pregnancy. If none occurs, hormones crash, leading to PMS. To understand the hormonal mechanics better, see [Hormones and Your Cycle](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained).

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar
The simplest method. Mark your start dates and estimate phases based on average cycle length.

### 2. BBT (Basal Body Temperature)
Taking your temperature every morning before getting out of bed can help pinpoint ovulation (temperature rises slightly after ovulation).

### 3. Cervical Mucus Tracking
Observing the changes in your vaginal discharge throughout the month. It becomes slippery and wet near ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Over-the-counter ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) test for luteinizing hormone surges.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
|---|---|
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14 | Ovulation day varies greatly between individuals and cycles. |
| The cycle is only about the period | Hormonal changes affect your whole body all month long. |
| You can't get pregnant on your period | Sperm can live up to 5 days, so early ovulation could result in pregnancy. |
