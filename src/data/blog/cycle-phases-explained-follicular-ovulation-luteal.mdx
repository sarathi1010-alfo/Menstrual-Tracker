The menstrual cycle is divided into four phases, each with distinct hormonal changes and physical effects. Knowing them helps you understand your body's rhythm.

## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Your cycle is more than just the days you bleed. It is a continuous loop of hormonal shifts that influence everything from your skin and energy to your mood and metabolism. By understanding the four phases, you can adapt your lifestyle to better support your body's changing needs, whether that means adjusting your workout intensity or knowing when you're most fertile.

## How to Identify Each Phase

### Menstruation (Days 1-5, approximately)
This is when you get your period. The uterine lining sheds because an egg was not fertilized. Estrogen and progesterone levels are low. You might experience cramps, fatigue, and lower energy.

### Follicular Phase (Days 1-13)
This phase starts on the first day of your period and overlaps with menstruation. Your brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting your ovaries to produce follicles. Estrogen rises, which thickens the uterine lining and boosts your energy and mood.

### Ovulation (Around Day 14)
A surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) causes the dominant follicle to release a mature egg. This is your peak fertile window. You might notice egg-white consistency cervical mucus and a slight rise in basal body temperature.

### Luteal Phase (Days 15-28)
The ruptured follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, producing progesterone to maintain the uterine lining. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone and estrogen levels plummet, leading to PMS symptoms and eventually, your next period.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar Tracking
The simplest method, tracking the start and end dates of your period to estimate phase lengths.

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT)
Tracking your body's resting temperature every morning to confirm when ovulation has occurred (temperature rises slightly after ovulation).

### 3. Cervical Mucus Observation
Monitoring changes in discharge to identify the fertile window (mucus becomes clear and stretchy around ovulation).

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Using ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) to detect the LH surge that precedes ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ovulation always happens on Day 14.** | Ovulation day varies greatly from person to person and cycle to cycle. |
| **You can get pregnant any day of your cycle.** | You are only fertile during a small window around ovulation. |
| **PMS is just "in your head."** | PMS is a real physical response to dropping hormone levels in the luteal phase. |
