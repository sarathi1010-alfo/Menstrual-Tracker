Your menstrual cycle is much more than just the days you bleed. It's a continuous loop composed of four distinct phases, each orchestrated by shifting hormones.

## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Understanding which phase you are in empowers you to anticipate changes in your body and mind. This knowledge is key for comprehensive health tracking, managing fertility, and optimizing your lifestyle (like adjusting your workouts or work schedule).

## How to Identify Each Phase

The typical cycle is broken down as follows:

1. **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** The shedding of the uterine lining. Estrogen and progesterone are low.
2. **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** Overlaps with menstruation. The pituitary gland releases FSH, stimulating follicles in the ovaries to mature. Estrogen rises.
3. **Ovulation (Day 14):** A surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) triggers the release of a mature egg. This is your peak fertile window.
4. **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** The empty follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, secreting progesterone to prepare the uterus for potential pregnancy. If no pregnancy occurs, hormone levels drop, triggering menstruation.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar Tracking
The simplest method. Use [LunaCycle](/) to log your cycle length and predict future phases based on averages.

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT)
Tracking your resting temperature daily can confirm ovulation, as temperature spikes slightly after the egg is released.

### 3. Cervical Mucus Observation
Monitoring changes in vaginal discharge helps identify approaching ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) measure LH surges in urine to pinpoint impending ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
|---|---|
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14. | Ovulation timing varies greatly between women and even cycle to cycle. |
| You can get pregnant any day of the month. | Pregnancy is only possible during the fertile window (the days leading up to and including ovulation). |
| The cycle is just "having a period." | The period is just one part of a complex monthly hormonal cycle. |
