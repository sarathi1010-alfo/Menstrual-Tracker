The menstrual cycle is divided into four phases, each with distinct hormonal changes and physical effects. Knowing them helps you understand your body's rhythm.

## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Being aware of your phases is essential for health tracking, fertility management, and lifestyle optimization. It explains why your energy, mood, and even appetite fluctuate throughout the month. Read our [Beginner's Guide](/blog/menstrual-cycle-101-beginners-guide) for an overview of tracking basics.

## How to Identify Each Phase

- **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** The shedding of the uterine lining. Symptoms include bleeding and cramping.
- **Follicular Phase (Days 1-14):** Overlaps with menstruation. Estrogen rises to prepare an egg. You may notice increasing energy.
- **Ovulation (Day 14):** The release of the egg. Look for clear, stretchy cervical mucus and a slight temperature dip followed by a rise.
- **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** Progesterone rises. If not pregnant, hormones drop, leading to PMS symptoms before menstruation begins again. Learn more about [how hormones orchestrate this](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained).

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar
Tracking the start and end dates of your period to estimate phases based on averages. Good for regular cycles. See our [ultimate tracking guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide) for details.

### 2. BBT (Basal Body Temperature)
Taking your temperature every morning before getting out of bed. A sustained rise confirms ovulation has occurred.

### 3. Cervical mucus
Observing changes in vaginal discharge. Stretchy, egg-white consistency indicates high fertility.

### 4. Hormonal tests
Using at-home ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) to detect the LH surge right before ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
| --- | --- |
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14. | Ovulation day varies greatly among individuals and cycles. |
| You can get pregnant any day of the month. | Pregnancy is only possible during the fertile window around ovulation. |
| The cycle is just about the period. | The period is just one phase; complex hormonal changes happen all month long. |
