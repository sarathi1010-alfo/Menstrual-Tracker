## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Understanding the four phases of your menstrual cycle is key to health tracking, fertility, and lifestyle optimization. Each phase brings different hormonal levels that can affect everything from your energy and mood to your appetite and sleep.

## How to Identify Each Phase

- **Menstruation:** Marked by bleeding as the uterine lining sheds. Typically lasts 3-7 days.
- **Follicular Phase:** Starts on the first day of your period and ends at ovulation. Estrogen rises, often bringing increased energy.
- **Ovulation:** The release of an egg. Look for clear, stretchy cervical mucus and sometimes mild pelvic pain.
- **Luteal Phase:** Post-ovulation. Progesterone rises, which can lead to PMS symptoms like bloating or mood changes.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar Tracking
Simply marking the days of your period on a calendar to estimate future phases.

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT)
Tracking your resting temperature, which slightly rises after ovulation.

### 3. Cervical Mucus Observation
Monitoring changes in vaginal discharge, which becomes clear and stretchy around ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Using ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) to detect luteinizing hormone surges.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14. | Ovulation timing varies greatly between individuals and cycles. |
| The luteal phase varies in length. | The luteal phase is usually relatively constant (12-14 days). |
