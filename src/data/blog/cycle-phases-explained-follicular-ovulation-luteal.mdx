The menstrual cycle is divided into four phases, each with distinct hormonal changes and physical effects. Knowing them helps you understand your body's rhythm and optimize your lifestyle. If you want a more comprehensive look, check out our [ultimate cycle tracking guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide).

## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Understanding your cycle phases is crucial for health tracking, fertility planning, and lifestyle optimization. Each phase brings different energy levels, moods, and physical needs. By aligning your diet, exercise, and work with these phases, you can work with your body rather than against it.

## How to Identify Each Phase

*   **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** This is your period. Symptoms often include cramping, fatigue, and lower back pain as the uterine lining sheds.
*   **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** This phase overlaps with menstruation and continues until ovulation. Energy levels typically rise, and you might notice clearer skin and a better mood.
*   **Ovulation (Around Day 14):** The ovary releases an egg. You might experience a slight twinge of pain (mittelschmerz) and notice clear, stretchy cervical mucus.
*   **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy. If not pregnant, PMS symptoms like bloating, mood swings, and tender breasts can occur before the next period starts.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar
A simple calendar is the most basic tool. You simply mark the days you bleed and count the days between to estimate your phases.

### 2. BBT (Basal Body Temperature)
Tracking your waking temperature every morning can confirm ovulation, as your temperature slightly rises after an egg is released.

### 3. Cervical Mucus
Observing changes in cervical fluid throughout the month helps identify the fertile window and ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
At-home test kits can measure hormones like LH to pinpoint impending ovulation more accurately. You can log all these signs in [LunaCycle](/).

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14. | Ovulation day varies significantly between individuals and cycles. |
| The luteal phase varies a lot. | The luteal phase is usually a stable 12-14 days; the follicular phase varies more. |
| You can't get pregnant on your period. | While unlikely, it's possible for those with short cycles. |

### Core Principles
*   The cycle has four distinct phases.
*   Each phase has specific hormonal profiles.
*   Tracking helps predict symptoms and fertility.
*   Understanding [hormones](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained) is key to phase tracking.
*   Everyone's cycle length and phases can differ.
