## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters
Knowing your cycle phases is a powerful tool for health tracking, optimizing your fertility window, and adjusting your lifestyle. It allows you to anticipate physical and emotional changes, giving you the grace to rest when needed and push forward when your energy peaks.

## How to Identify Each Phase
- **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** Shedding of the uterine lining. Symptoms: bleeding, cramps, lower energy.
- **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** Your body prepares an egg. Estrogen rises, increasing energy and mood.
- **Ovulation (Day 14 approx):** The egg is released. Peak fertility, highest energy, and sometimes slight pelvic pain (mittelschmerz).
- **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** Preparation for possible pregnancy. Progesterone dominates. Symptoms: potential PMS, bloating, mood changes.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar
Tracking your period dates on a calendar (or in an app like LunaCycle) is the foundation of phase tracking.

### 2. BBT (Basal Body Temperature)
Tracking your waking temperature can confirm ovulation has occurred, as temperature slightly rises after the egg is released.

### 3. Cervical Mucus
Observing changes in vaginal discharge helps pinpoint the fertile window; mucus becomes clear and stretchy around ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) measure hormones in urine to predict ovulation before it happens.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases
| Myth | Fact |
|---|---|
| Ovulation is always on day 14 | Ovulation day varies greatly between women and even cycle to cycle. |
| The luteal phase varies in length | The luteal phase is typically the most consistent phase, lasting 12-14 days. |
| You can only get pregnant on the day of ovulation | Sperm can live in the reproductive tract for up to 5 days, creating a wider 'fertile window'. |
