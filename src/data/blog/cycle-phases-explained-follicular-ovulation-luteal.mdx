## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Your menstrual cycle is not just about your period. It is a continuous process divided into four distinct phases, each driven by different hormones. Understanding these phases—Menstruation, Follicular, Ovulation, and Luteal—allows you to optimize your lifestyle, diet, and exercise routines to match your body's natural rhythm.

## How to Identify Each Phase

- **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** Marked by bleeding as the uterine lining sheds. Energy levels are typically lowest.
- **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** Overlaps with menstruation. Estrogen rises, boosting energy and mood as follicles in the ovaries mature.
- **Ovulation (Day 14):** A mature egg is released. You may notice clear, stretchy cervical mucus and a peak in energy.
- **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** Progesterone rises. You might experience PMS symptoms, lower energy, and cravings as the body prepares for a potential pregnancy.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar Method
Tracking the start and end dates of your period to estimate the timing of different phases. [LunaCycle](/) makes this effortless.

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT)
Measuring your resting temperature daily to detect the slight spike that occurs after ovulation.

### 3. Cervical Mucus Monitoring
Observing changes in vaginal discharge to identify your fertile window and ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Using ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) to detect luteinizing hormone (LH) surges before ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
|------|------|
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14 | Ovulation day varies widely among women and cycles. |
| The luteal phase length varies a lot | The luteal phase is usually consistently 12-14 days. |
| You only have hormones during PMS | Hormones fluctuate continuously throughout all phases. |
