The menstrual cycle is divided into four phases, each with distinct hormonal changes and physical effects. Knowing them helps you understand your body's rhythm.

## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

For health tracking, fertility, and lifestyle optimization, knowing which phase you are in can be empowering. It explains why you might feel energetic one week and fatigued the next.

## How to Identify Each Phase

- **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** Shedding of the uterine lining. Symptoms include bleeding and cramps.
- **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** Estrogen rises as follicles mature. You may feel a boost in energy and mood.
- **Ovulation (Day 14):** An egg is released. Look for clear, stretchy cervical mucus and a slight temperature dip followed by a spike.
- **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** Progesterone dominates to prepare for potential pregnancy. Energy might drop, and PMS can occur.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar
Simple tracking of dates to estimate phases based on averages.

### 2. BBT (Basal Body Temperature)
Taking your temperature every morning to spot the post-ovulation shift.

### 3. Cervical mucus
Observing changes in vaginal discharge to identify the fertile window.

### 4. Hormonal tests
Using OPKs (Ovulation Predictor Kits) to detect the LH surge before ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
|---|---|
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14 | Ovulation day varies significantly between individuals and cycles. |
| The luteal phase is highly variable | The luteal phase is usually a stable 12-14 days. |
