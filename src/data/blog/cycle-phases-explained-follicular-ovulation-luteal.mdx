## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Knowing your phases helps with health tracking, fertility, and lifestyle optimization. It explains why you might feel energetic on day 10 but tired and crampy on day 26.

## How to Identify Each Phase

Each phase has its own signs:
- **Menstruation:** Bleeding, potential cramps, lower energy.
- **Follicular:** Rising estrogen, increasing energy, clearer skin.
- **Ovulation:** Peak energy, slippery cervical mucus, sometimes slight pelvic pain.
- **Luteal:** Rising progesterone, potential PMS symptoms, slight rise in basal body temperature.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar Tracking
The foundation of any method, noting the days of your period.

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT)
Tracking your resting temperature every morning to spot the spike that occurs after ovulation.

### 3. Cervical Mucus Observation
Noting changes in vaginal discharge, which becomes clear and stretchy around ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Using at-home ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) to detect the LH surge before ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
|------|------|
| Ovulation is always on day 14 | Ovulation day varies greatly between individuals and cycles. |
| The cycle is only about the period | The period is just one phase; the entire cycle is a continuous process. |
| PMS is all in your head | PMS is a real physical response to hormonal changes in the luteal phase. |
