## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Understanding your cycle phases goes beyond predicting your period. It provides insights into health tracking, fertility planning, and lifestyle optimization. Knowing what phase you are in can explain fluctuations in energy, mood, and physical symptoms.

## How to Identify Each Phase

- **Menstrual Phase (Days 1-5):** Marked by shedding the uterine lining (bleeding). Estrogen and progesterone are low.
- **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** Overlaps with menstruation. Estrogen rises, energy increases, and an egg matures.
- **Ovulation (Day 14):** A surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) triggers the release of the egg. This is peak fertility.
- **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** Progesterone rises to prepare for pregnancy. If no pregnancy occurs, hormone levels drop, leading to PMS and then menstruation.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar Tracking
Logging start and end dates of your period to estimate phases based on averages. [LunaCycle](/) makes this easy.

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT)
Tracking your resting temperature, which rises slightly after ovulation.

### 3. Cervical Mucus Monitoring
Observing changes in vaginal discharge, which becomes clear and stretchy around ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Using ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) to detect the LH surge before ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
|---|---|
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14. | Ovulation timing varies greatly between women and cycles. |
| The luteal phase varies in length. | The luteal phase is typically the most consistent phase, lasting 12-14 days. |
