## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Your cycle is a continuous loop of hormonal shifts that influence everything from your energy levels to your mood and physical health. By understanding the four phases—menstruation, follicular, ovulation, and luteal—you can anticipate these changes rather than being surprised by them. This knowledge is crucial for health tracking, managing fertility (whether trying to conceive or not), and optimizing your lifestyle to align with your body's natural strengths during each phase.

## How to Identify Each Phase

- **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** The start of your cycle, characterized by bleeding as the uterine lining sheds. Energy is often lowest here.
- **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** Overlaps with menstruation and continues until ovulation. Estrogen rises, typically bringing a boost in energy and mood.
- **Ovulation (Around Day 14):** A mature egg is released. You might notice clear, stretchy cervical mucus and a slight rise in basal body temperature.
- **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** Progesterone dominates. Energy may naturally wane, and you might experience PMS symptoms as your body prepares for the next cycle.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar Tracking
Logging the start and end dates of your period to estimate cycle length and predict future phases. Using [LunaCycle](/) is a great, private way to maintain this calendar.

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT)
Taking your temperature first thing in the morning. A slight sustained spike indicates ovulation has occurred.

### 3. Cervical Mucus Monitoring
Observing changes in vaginal discharge. Clear, slippery mucus indicates peak fertility during the ovulatory phase.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Using at-home ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) to detect the luteinizing hormone (LH) surge that precedes ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
|------|------|
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14. | Ovulation timing varies greatly depending on cycle length. |
| You can only get pregnant on the day of ovulation. | Sperm can live for up to 5 days, creating a wider fertile window. |
| PMS is just 'in your head'. | PMS is a real physical response to hormonal changes in the luteal phase. |
