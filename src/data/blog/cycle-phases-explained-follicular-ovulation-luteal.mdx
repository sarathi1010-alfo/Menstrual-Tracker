## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Understanding the distinct phases of your cycle is essential for health tracking, fertility management, and lifestyle optimization. Each phase brings different hormonal changes that affect your mood, energy levels, and physical symptoms. By recognizing these patterns, you can work with your body rather than against it.

## How to Identify Each Phase

Your cycle is split into four main parts:

1. **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** The shedding of the uterine lining. You might feel fatigued.
2. **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** Starts on day 1 overlapping with your period. Estrogen rises, boosting energy.
3. **Ovulation (Day 14):** An egg is released. You may notice clear, stretchy cervical mucus and a peak in libido.
4. **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** Progesterone rises. This is when PMS symptoms typically occur.

Lengths vary from person to person.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar Tracking
The simplest method involves logging your start and end dates using an app like [LunaCycle](/).

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT)
Taking your temperature every morning helps confirm when ovulation has occurred by detecting a slight spike.

### 3. Cervical Mucus Monitoring
Tracking changes in discharge can signal fertile days leading up to ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Ovulation Predictor Kits (OPKs) measure hormones in urine to anticipate ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
|---|---|
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14. | Ovulation varies greatly between individuals and cycles. |
| You are fertile every day of the month. | The fertile window is only about 6 days long, leading up to and including ovulation. |
| PMS is just 'in your head'. | PMS is caused by real, significant hormonal fluctuations during the luteal phase. |
