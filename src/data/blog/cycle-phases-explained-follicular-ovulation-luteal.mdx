## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Your menstrual cycle is not just about the days you bleed; it's a continuous, four-part process that influences your entire body. Knowing your cycle phases matters for health tracking, fertility, and lifestyle optimization. By understanding the hormonal shifts that occur in the follicular, ovulatory, luteal, and menstrual phases, you can better interpret your physical symptoms, mood changes, and energy levels.

## How to Identify Each Phase

The four phases overlap and transition seamlessly. Here's what to look for:

- **Menstruation (Days 1-7):** The shedding of the uterine lining. You'll experience bleeding, and potentially cramps and fatigue.
- **Follicular Phase (Days 1-14):** Overlaps with menstruation. As estrogen rises, energy and mood typically improve.
- **Ovulation (Around Day 14):** The release of an egg. Look for egg-white cervical mucus, a slight dip then rise in basal body temperature (BBT), and peak energy.
- **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** Progesterone rises, preparing the body for potential pregnancy. If unfertilized, PMS symptoms like bloating and mood changes may appear before the cycle restarts.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar Tracking
The foundational method. Log the start and end dates of your period to estimate cycle length and phase timing. [LunaCycle](/) automates this process securely.

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT)
Taking your temperature every morning before getting out of bed can confirm ovulation, marked by a sustained temperature shift.

### 3. Cervical Mucus Observation
Monitoring changes in discharge helps pinpoint the fertile window. It transitions from dry to sticky, creamy, and finally clear and stretchy (like raw egg whites) near ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Ovulation Predictor Kits (OPKs) measure Luteinizing Hormone (LH) surges in urine, signaling impending ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
|---|---|
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14. | Ovulation timing varies greatly between women and even cycle to cycle. |
| You can only get pregnant on the day of ovulation. | The fertile window spans about 6 days, including the 5 days before ovulation. |
| PMS is "all in your head." | PMS is a real physical and psychological response to hormonal shifts in the luteal phase. |
