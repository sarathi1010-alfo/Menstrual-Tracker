The menstrual cycle is divided into four phases, each with distinct hormonal changes and physical effects. Knowing them helps you understand your body's rhythm.

## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Understanding your cycle phases is essential for health tracking, fertility, and lifestyle optimization. It helps explain why you feel energetic one week and tired the next.

## How to Identify Each Phase

*   **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** The shedding of the uterine lining. Low hormone levels.
*   **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** Overlaps with menstruation. Estrogen rises to mature an egg.
*   **Ovulation (Day 14):** A surge in LH triggers the release of the egg.
*   **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** Progesterone rises to support potential pregnancy. If not pregnant, levels drop, triggering menstruation.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar
Tracking the days since your period started provides a basic estimate of your current phase.

### 2. BBT
Basal Body Temperature rises slightly after ovulation, confirming the shift to the luteal phase.

### 3. Cervical mucus
Changes in cervical fluid from dry to wet and stretchy indicate approaching ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal tests
Ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) measure LH surges to pinpoint ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
|---|---|
| Ovulation always happens on day 14. | Ovulation day varies widely among individuals and cycles. |
| The luteal phase length varies a lot. | The luteal phase is typically a consistent 12-14 days. |
