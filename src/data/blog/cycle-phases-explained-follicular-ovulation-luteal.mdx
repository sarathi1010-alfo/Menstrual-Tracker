The menstrual cycle is divided into four phases, each with distinct hormonal changes and physical effects. Knowing them helps you understand your body's rhythm.

## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Understanding the different phases of your cycle is essential for health tracking, fertility planning, and lifestyle optimization. Each phase brings different hormonal levels that can affect your mood, energy, appetite, and even how your skin looks. By knowing which phase you are in, you can adjust your diet, exercise, and social activities to better support your body's needs.

## How to Identify Each Phase

- **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** This is when bleeding occurs as the uterine lining sheds. You might experience cramps and lower energy.
- **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** Overlapping with menstruation, this phase focuses on maturing ovarian follicles. Estrogen rises, boosting energy and mood.
- **Ovulation (Day 14):** The release of a mature egg. You might notice changes in cervical mucus (stretchy and clear) and a slight increase in basal body temperature.
- **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** The body prepares for a possible pregnancy. Progesterone rises, which can lead to PMS symptoms if pregnancy doesn't occur.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar
A simple calendar method to track the start and end dates of your period.

### 2. BBT (Basal Body Temperature)
Tracking your resting temperature every morning to pinpoint ovulation.

### 3. Cervical mucus
Observing changes in vaginal discharge to identify your fertile window.

### 4. Hormonal tests
Using over-the-counter ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) to detect LH surges.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
|------|------|
| Ovulation always happens on day 14. | Ovulation can happen earlier or later, depending on cycle length. |
| The luteal phase length varies greatly. | The luteal phase is usually consistent, around 12-14 days. |
| You can only get pregnant on the day of ovulation. | Sperm can live for up to 5 days, creating a fertile window. |
