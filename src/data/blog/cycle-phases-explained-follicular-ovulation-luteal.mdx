## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Your menstrual cycle is not just about your period; it's a continuous, month-long cycle that affects your entire body. Knowing your cycle phases matters for health tracking, fertility, and lifestyle optimization. Different phases bring different hormonal profiles, which can influence your mood, energy levels, appetite, and cognitive function. By understanding these shifts, you can work with your body rather than against it.

## How to Identify Each Phase

- **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** The shedding of the uterine lining. You will notice bleeding, and may experience cramping or fatigue.
- **Follicular Phase (Days 1-14, overlaps with Menstruation):** The brain signals the ovaries to prepare an egg. Estrogen rises, often bringing increased energy and a clearer mind.
- **Ovulation (Around Day 14):** The release of a mature egg. You might notice a slight dip followed by a spike in basal body temperature, and cervical mucus becomes clear and stretchy (like egg whites).
- **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** The body prepares for potential pregnancy. Progesterone rises. If not pregnant, hormone levels drop, leading to PMS symptoms like bloating or mood changes before the cycle restarts.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar
Using a digital calendar or app like [LunaCycle](/tracker) is the simplest way to estimate phases based on your cycle length.

### 2. BBT (Basal Body Temperature)
Tracking your temperature immediately upon waking can confirm ovulation, as temperature rises slightly during the luteal phase.

### 3. Cervical mucus
Observing changes in vaginal discharge helps identify the fertile window leading up to ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal tests
Ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) test urine for a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), which triggers ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14. | Ovulation varies based on total cycle length and stress. |
| You can get pregnant any day of the cycle. | You are only fertile for a small window around ovulation. |
| PMS is "all in your head." | PMS is a real physiological response to shifting hormone levels. |
