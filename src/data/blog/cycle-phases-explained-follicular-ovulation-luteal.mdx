## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Understanding the four phases of your cycle is crucial for health tracking, fertility planning, and lifestyle optimization. It empowers you to work with your body's natural rhythm rather than against it.

## How to Identify Each Phase

*   **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** The shedding of the uterine lining. Symptoms include bleeding and cramping.
*   **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** The pituitary gland releases FSH to stimulate egg growth. Energy levels typically rise.
*   **Ovulation (Day 14):** A mature egg is released from the ovary. You are most fertile during this time.
*   **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy. If not pregnant, PMS symptoms may occur before the next period.

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar Method
Tracking the length of your past cycles to estimate the phases of your current cycle. Best used alongside an app like [LunaCycle](/).

### 2. Basal Body Temperature (BBT)
Taking your temperature every morning before getting out of bed. A slight rise indicates ovulation has occurred.

### 3. Cervical Mucus
Observing changes in cervical discharge. Clear, stretchy mucus often indicates the fertile window around ovulation.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Using ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) to detect the surge in luteinizing hormone (LH) that precedes ovulation.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ovulation always occurs on day 14. | Ovulation can vary significantly from person to person and cycle to cycle. |
| You can only get pregnant on the day of ovulation. | Sperm can live in the reproductive tract for up to 5 days, so the fertile window is longer. |
| All periods are 28 days long. | A healthy cycle can range from 21 to 35 days. |
