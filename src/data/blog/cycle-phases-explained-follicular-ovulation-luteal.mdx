## Why Knowing Your Cycle Phases Matters

Your menstrual cycle is not just about the days you bleed. It is a complex process divided into four distinct phases. Understanding what happens during each phase provides insight into your physical and emotional well-being, helping you align your lifestyle with your body's natural rhythms.

## How to Identify Each Phase

- **Menstruation (Days 1-5):** The shedding of the uterine lining. You may experience bleeding, cramping, and lower energy levels.
- **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** Begins on the first day of your period and lasts until ovulation. Rising estrogen levels boost energy and mood.
- **Ovulation (Day 14):** The release of a mature egg from the ovary. You might notice a slight increase in body temperature and changes in cervical mucus.
- **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** Follows ovulation. Progesterone rises, which can cause symptoms like bloating, breast tenderness, and mood changes (PMS).

## Top 4 Cycle Phase Tracking Tools

### 1. Calendar
Using a calendar or a digital tracker like [LunaCycle](/) is the most common method. It helps establish a baseline for your cycle length.

### 2. BBT (Basal Body Temperature)
Tracking your resting temperature can confirm when ovulation has occurred.

### 3. Cervical Mucus
Monitoring changes in discharge can help identify your most fertile days.

### 4. Hormonal Tests
Ovulation predictor kits (OPKs) measure hormones to predict when ovulation will happen.

## Common Misconceptions About Phases

| Myth | Fact |
| --- | --- |
| Ovulation always happens on Day 14 | Ovulation can vary significantly from woman to woman and cycle to cycle. |
| You can get pregnant at any time | The fertile window is limited to a few days leading up to and including ovulation. |
| A 28-day cycle is the only normal cycle | Normal cycles can range anywhere from 21 to 35 days. |
