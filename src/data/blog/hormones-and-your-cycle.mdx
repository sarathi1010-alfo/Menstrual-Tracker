## The Biological Choreography of Your Cycle

Your menstrual cycle is governed by a sophisticated feedback loop between your brain and your ovaries. This communication is carried out by hormones, which act as chemical messengers. Understanding these 'Big Four' hormones is key to decoding why you feel the way you do at different times of the month.

## The Key Players

### 1. Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH)
Produced by the pituitary gland, FSH is the 'starter pistol.' It signals the ovaries to begin maturing several follicles, each containing an egg.

### 2. Estrogen
As the follicles grow, they produce estrogen. This hormone is the 'builder'—it thickens the lining of your uterus and signals the brain that an egg is ready.

### 3. Luteinizing Hormone (LH)
When estrogen reaches its peak, the brain releases a surge of LH. This is the 'trigger' that causes the most mature follicle to burst and release its egg (ovulation).

### 4. Progesterone
After ovulation, the empty follicle (now called the corpus luteum) begins producing progesterone. This is the 'nurturer'—it prepares the body for a potential pregnancy by keeping the uterine lining secure.
