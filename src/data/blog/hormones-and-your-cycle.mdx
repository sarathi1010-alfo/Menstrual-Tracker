Hormones are the conductors of your body's orchestra. During a menstrual cycle, four main hormones rise and fall in a specific sequence to prepare your body for potential reproduction.

## The Big Four Hormones

### 1. Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH)
Produced by the pituitary gland, FSH tells your ovaries to start maturing follicles (which contain eggs) at the start of your cycle.

### 2. Estrogen
As follicles grow, they produce estrogen. This hormone builds up the lining of your uterus and signals the brain when it's time to ovulate.

### 3. Luteinizing Hormone (LH)
When estrogen hits a certain level, the brain sends out a surge of LH. This is the "go" signal for the ovary to release its most mature egg.

### 4. Progesterone
After ovulation, the empty follicle produces progesterone. Its job is to keep the uterine lining stable and "sticky" for an embryo. If no embryo arrives, progesterone falls, and the lining sheds.

## How Fluctuations Affect You
  - "**High Estrogen:** Often associated with high energy, confidence, and social desire."
  - "**High Progesterone:** Often associated with a desire to "nest," increased appetite, and potentially lower energy."
  - "**Hormone Withdrawal:** The drop before your period can cause irritability, headaches, or fatigue—commonly known as PMS."
