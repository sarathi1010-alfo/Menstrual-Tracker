**Endometriosis** is a chronic condition where tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows outside the uterus. Because this tissue responds to the same hormonal signals as your uterine lining, tracking your cycle is a vital part of managing the condition.

## How Endometriosis Affects the Cycle
While endometriosis is present all month, symptoms often peak during specific phases. Estrogen, which rises during the follicular phase and peaks at ovulation, can cause endometriosis lesions to grow and inflame, leading to increased pain.

## How Cycle Tracking Helps
1. **Identifying Patterns:** Do your migraines happen during ovulation? Does your pelvic pain start 3 days before your period? Tracking helps you answer these questions.
2. **Predictive Planning:** If you know your highest pain days are coming, you can adjust your schedule and ensure you have your management tools ready.
3. **Medical Advocacy:** Doctors often ask for a history of symptoms. A 6-month log of pain levels and cycle dates is much more powerful than a vague memory.

## What to Look For in Your Data
- **Pain Intensity:** Use a 1-10 scale for different types of pain (cramps, back pain, bowel pain).
- **Bleeding Heaviness:** Note if your periods are consistently heavy (menorrhagia).
- **Ovulation Pain:** Also known as *mittelschmerz*, this can be more intense for those with endo.
- **Symptom Triggers:** Note if certain foods or activities correlate with flare-ups.

## When to Consult a Provider
Consult a specialist if:
- Your period pain prevents you from doing daily activities.
- You experience pain during intercourse.
- You have chronic pelvic pain throughout the month.
- You notice a significant change in your "normal" symptom pattern.

## Quick Reference Table

| Symptom | Cycle Correlation | Why it Happens |
|---------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Severe Cramps** | Menstrual Phase | Inflamed lesions bleeding into the pelvic cavity. |
| **Ovulation Pain** | Mid-Cycle | Estrogen peak causing lesion inflammation. |
| **Bloating ("Endo Belly")** | Luteal Phase | Hormonal shifts and internal inflammation. |
| **Heavy Bleeding** | Menstrual Phase | Often associated with adenomyosis or hormonal imbalance. |
