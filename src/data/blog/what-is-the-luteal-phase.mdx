The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, occurring after ovulation and before the next period starts.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the egg is released, the empty follicle transforms into the **corpus luteum**. This temporary gland produces progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a possible pregnancy. If the egg isn't fertilized, the corpus luteum withers, progesterone drops, and your period begins.

## Real-World Example of the Luteal Phase

If you have a 28-day cycle and ovulate on Day 14, your luteal phase is the period from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, you might experience symptoms like bloating or mood shifts due to the high progesterone levels.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (usually 12-16 days) is necessary to allow a fertilized egg to implant in the uterus. A "short luteal phase" (less than 10 days) can make it difficult to maintain a pregnancy.

## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---------|------------------|--------------|
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Goal | Egg Maturation | Uterine Preparation |
| Timing | Before Ovulation | After Ovulation |
