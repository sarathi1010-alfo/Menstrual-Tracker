The luteal phase is the crucial second half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the ovary releases an egg, the empty follicle transforms into a temporary endocrine structure called the corpus luteum. This structure produces progesterone, a hormone that thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a potential fertilized egg. If pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example of the Luteal Phase in Symptoms

The hormonal fluctuations—specifically the rise and subsequent fall of progesterone—are responsible for Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS). By tracking your cycle in [LunaCycle](/tracker), you can anticipate the onset of your luteal phase and prepare for common symptoms like mood changes, bloating, and fatigue.

## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase

| Phase | Timing | Dominant Hormone | Primary Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Follicular** | Before Ovulation | Estrogen | Maturing the egg. |
| **Luteal** | After Ovulation | Progesterone | Preparing the uterus for pregnancy. |

Understanding the luteal phase is key to predicting your period accurately and managing premenstrual symptoms.
