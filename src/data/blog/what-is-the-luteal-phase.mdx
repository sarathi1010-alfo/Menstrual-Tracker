The luteal phase is the crucial second half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends with the onset of your next period (menstruation). This phase is entirely focused on preparing the body for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

Once the ovary releases an egg, the empty follicle transforms into a temporary endocrine structure called the corpus luteum. This structure begins producing high levels of progesterone, alongside some estrogen. Progesterone thickens the uterine lining (endometrium), making it receptive to a fertilized egg. If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels plummet, and the uterine lining begins to shed, starting a new cycle.

## Real-World Example of Luteal Phase Symptoms

The hormonal shifts during the luteal phase are responsible for Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS). You might experience bloating, mood swings, or breast tenderness in the days leading up to your period. ## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase (Table)

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation. | Pre-ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Body's Focus** | Preparing the uterus for implantation. | Maturing an egg for release. |
