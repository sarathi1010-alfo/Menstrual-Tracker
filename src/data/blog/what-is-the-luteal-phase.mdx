## Definition

The **luteal phase** is the final phase of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. During this phase, the body prepares the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the egg is released, the ruptured ovarian follicle transforms into a structure called the *corpus luteum*. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone, which thickens and stabilizes the uterine lining, making it a receptive environment for a fertilized egg to implant.

If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, causing progesterone and estrogen levels to drop. This hormonal drop triggers the shedding of the uterine lining—menstruation.

## Real-World Example of Luteal Phase Tracking

A consistent luteal phase is a sign of hormonal health. By using an app like [LunaCycle](/) to track cycle length and estimating ovulation, women can determine their luteal phase length. If it is consistently shorter than 10 days, it might indicate low progesterone levels.

## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Primary Goal** | Prepare uterus for implantation | Mature an egg for release |
| **Length** | Usually fixed (12-14 days) | Highly variable |
