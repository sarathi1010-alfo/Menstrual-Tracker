The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after [ovulation](/what-is-ovulation) and ends the day before your next menstrual period starts. This phase is characterized by the body's preparation for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

Once the ovary releases an egg, the empty follicle transforms into a temporary endocrine structure called the corpus luteum. The primary job of the corpus luteum is to secrete **progesterone**.

Progesterone is the dominant hormone of the luteal phase. It thickens the uterine lining (endometrium), making it receptive to a fertilized egg. It also causes a slight rise in basal body temperature.

If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down after about 10-14 days. This causes a steep drop in both progesterone and estrogen levels. This hormonal withdrawal triggers the shedding of the uterine lining, marking the start of a new [menstrual cycle](/what-is-a-menstrual-cycle).

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

Unlike the follicular phase, which can vary wildly in length, the luteal phase is relatively consistent for each individual (usually around 14 days). This consistency is why apps like [LunaCycle](/) use reverse calculation (subtracting 14 days from the next expected period) to estimate ovulation.

## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Length** | Usually fixed (11-17 days). | Highly variable. |
