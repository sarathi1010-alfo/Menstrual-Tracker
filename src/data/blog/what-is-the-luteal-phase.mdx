The luteal phase is the crucial second half of the menstrual cycle. It bridges the gap between ovulation (the release of the egg) and the beginning of the next menstruation. During this time, the body is heavily focused on preparing a hospitable environment in the uterus, just in case the released egg was fertilized.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

The defining feature of the luteal phase is the corpus luteum, a temporary endocrine structure:

1.  **Formation:** Immediately after the dominant follicle releases its egg during ovulation, the collapsed follicle transforms into the corpus luteum.
2.  **Progesterone Production:** The primary job of the corpus luteum is to secrete massive amounts of progesterone (along with some estrogen).
3.  **Uterine Preparation:** Progesterone causes the endometrium (the lining of the uterus) to thicken, mature, and become receptive to the implantation of a fertilized egg. It also suppresses further ovulation and raises the body's resting temperature.
4.  **Resolution:** If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum naturally degrades after about 12 to 14 days. This causes a steep drop in progesterone and estrogen levels, which ultimately triggers the shedding of the uterine lining (your period).

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

Unlike the follicular phase (which can vary wildly in length depending on stress, illness, or travel), the luteal phase is relatively static for each individual, usually lasting 12 to 14 days. When using tools like [LunaCycle](/), if your overall cycle length changes from month to month, it is almost always the first half of your cycle fluctuating. Knowing your consistent luteal phase length helps the app accurately back-calculate when ovulation likely occurred.

## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation to menstruation. | Menstruation to ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Primary Function** | Preparing the uterus for implantation. | Maturing an egg for release. |
| **Length** | Highly consistent (11-17 days). | Highly variable. |
