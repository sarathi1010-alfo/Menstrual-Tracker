## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

During the luteal phase, the empty ovarian follicle that released the egg transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a potential pregnancy. If a fertilized egg doesn't implant, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels fall, and the uterine lining sheds, starting a new cycle.

## Real-World Example of the Luteal Phase in Cycle Length

The luteal phase is typically the most consistent part of a woman's cycle, usually lasting between 10 to 16 days (averaging 14). Variations in overall cycle length are more often due to a varying follicular phase. [LunaCycle](/) settings allow you to adjust your assumed luteal phase length to refine your predictions.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| **Primary Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Function** | Prepare uterus for pregnancy. | Prepare egg for release. |
