The luteal phase is the latter half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends when your next period starts, unless a pregnancy occurs.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the ovary releases an egg, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone (and some estrogen). Progesterone thickens the uterine lining, making it a receptive environment for a fertilized egg to implant. If implantation does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the uterine lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

During the luteal phase, many women experience premenstrual syndrome (PMS) symptoms like breast tenderness, bloating, or mood changes due to the fluctuation in progesterone. Tracking your cycle with [LunaCycle](/) helps you anticipate these changes.
