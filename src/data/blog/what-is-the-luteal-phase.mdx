## What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the latter portion of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next menstrual period begins.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

When the ovary releases an egg during ovulation, the empty follicle that is left behind transforms into a temporary endocrine structure called the **corpus luteum**. The corpus luteum's primary job is to produce progesterone (and some estrogen).

Progesterone is crucial because it stabilizes the uterine lining (endometrium), making it thick and nutrient-rich in preparation for a fertilized egg to implant.

If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum degrades after about 10-14 days. This causes progesterone and estrogen levels to drop rapidly. This hormonal plunge is what triggers the shedding of the uterine lining—the start of your period.

## The Luteal Phase and Tracking

Because the length of the luteal phase is generally consistent for an individual (unlike the follicular phase, which can vary widely), knowing your luteal phase length is a powerful tool for prediction. Once you identify when you ovulate, you can predict your next period with high accuracy.
