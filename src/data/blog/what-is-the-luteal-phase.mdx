## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

The luteal phase makes up the entire second half of your menstrual cycle. It begins the moment an egg is released during ovulation and concludes on the last day before your next menstrual period begins.

This phase is defined by the creation of the *corpus luteum*. When the ovarian follicle ruptures to release the egg, the empty "shell" left behind transforms into this temporary endocrine gland. The corpus luteum's primary job is to produce massive amounts of the hormone **progesterone** (along with some estrogen).

Progesterone is the star of the luteal phase. It prevents the uterus from contracting and causes the endometrial lining to thicken and secrete nutrients, creating a perfect, stable environment for a fertilized egg to implant.

If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum begins to degrade after about 10-12 days. As it shrinks, progesterone and estrogen levels plummet. This sudden withdrawal of hormones triggers the shedding of the uterine lining—and a new cycle begins with menstruation.

## Symptoms of the Luteal Phase

Because of the dominance of progesterone, you may experience specific physical and emotional changes during this time:

*   **Higher Body Temperature:** Progesterone causes your resting basal body temperature (BBT) to rise slightly and stay elevated throughout the phase.
*   **Thicker Cervical Mucus:** Progesterone causes cervical fluid to dry up and become thick and sticky, forming a plug that prevents bacteria (and sperm) from entering the uterus.
*   **Lower Energy:** While estrogen provides a boost of energy, progesterone has a more sedating, calming effect, often leading to lower energy levels.
*   **Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS):** In the final days of the luteal phase, the steep drop in hormones can cause bloating, breast tenderness, mood swings, and cravings.

## Real-World Example: Luteal Phase Defect

Consider Rachel, who is tracking her cycle with [LunaCycle](/tracker) to get pregnant. While her overall cycle is 26 days, she notices through temperature tracking that she doesn't ovulate until Day 17. This means her luteal phase is only 9 days long (Days 18-26).

A luteal phase shorter than 10 days is often referred to as a Luteal Phase Defect. Even if an egg is fertilized, 9 days may not provide enough time for the embryo to travel down the fallopian tube and implant firmly before the progesterone drops and the lining begins to shed. Armed with this tracked data, Rachel can consult her doctor, who might prescribe progesterone supplements to support the luteal phase.

## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase

The menstrual cycle is a tale of two halves.

| Feature | Follicular Phase (First Half) | Luteal Phase (Second Half) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Day 1 of period until Ovulation. | After Ovulation until next period. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen. | Progesterone. |
| **Primary Goal** | Mature an egg and thicken the lining. | Maintain the lining for potential pregnancy. |
| **Length Variability** | Highly variable (can be 10 days or 30 days depending on stress/health). | Very consistent (almost always 12-14 days). |
