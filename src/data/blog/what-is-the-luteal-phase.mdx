The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after [ovulation](/what-is-ovulation) and ends the day before your next period starts.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the egg is released, the ruptured follicle on the ovary transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. The corpus luteum begins producing progesterone, a hormone that thickens the uterine lining to prepare it for a fertilized egg. If pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the uterine lining sheds, starting the [menstrual cycle](/what-is-a-menstrual-cycle) over again.

## Real-World Example of Luteal Phase Symptoms

During the luteal phase, especially in the week before your period, you might experience PMS (Premenstrual Syndrome). Using a tracker like [LunaCycle](/), you can log symptoms like bloating, tender breasts, or irritability. Over time, you'll see these patterns repeat in your luteal phase, allowing you to prepare with rest and self-care.

## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase

| Term | Definition | Primary Hormone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Luteal Phase** | Occurs after ovulation, prepares the uterus for pregnancy. | Progesterone |
| **Follicular Phase** | Occurs before ovulation, matures the egg. | Estrogen |
