## What is the Luteal Phase?

The **luteal phase** is the second half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends when your next period starts. Its primary purpose is to prepare the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the ovary releases an egg, the ruptured follicle transforms into a temporary endocrine structure called the *corpus luteum*. This structure produces large amounts of progesterone and some estrogen.

Progesterone thickens the uterine lining, making it receptive to a fertilized egg. It also causes a slight increase in basal body temperature (BBT). If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels plummet, and the uterine lining begins to shed, marking the start of a new cycle (menstruation).

## Real-World Example in Tracking

A user might notice that while their overall cycle length varies (e.g., between 28 and 32 days), the time from suspected ovulation to their period is almost always exactly 14 days. This consistency is a hallmark of a healthy luteal phase. Tracking with tools like [LunaCycle](/) helps establish this baseline.

## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation. | Pre-ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Duration Variability**| Very consistent (usually 12-14 days). | Highly variable; determines overall cycle length. |
