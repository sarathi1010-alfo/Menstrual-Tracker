## Your Period as a Health Indicator

Your period is a window into your internal health. While every body is different, there are established ranges for what is generally considered healthy. By monitoring your 'normal,' you can quickly identify when something is off.

## Defining the 'Normal' Range

### Cycle Length
The time from the first day of one period to the first day of the next should ideally be between 21 and 35 days. Consistency is key here; a cycle that varies by more than a few days each month might be considered irregular.

### Period Duration
Bleeding typically lasts between 3 and 7 days. If you consistently bleed for more than 8 days, it may be a sign of a condition like fibroids or polyps.

### Flow Intensity
A normal flow requires changing a pad or tampon every few hours. If you find yourself soaking through products in less than an hour, or if you need to wake up at night to change them, this is considered 'heavy' (menorrhagia).

## Color and Consistency

| Color | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Bright Red** | Fresh blood, typically seen during the heaviest days. |
| **Dark Red/Brown** | Older blood that has taken longer to leave the uterus. |
| **Pink** | Often seen during spotting or if blood is mixed with cervical fluid. |
| **Gray/Orange** | Can sometimes indicate an infection; consult a doctor. |

## When to Consult a Professional

You should reach out to a healthcare provider if you experience:
-   **Amenorrhea:** No period for three months or more.
-   **Menorrhagia:** Extremely heavy bleeding that interferes with life.
-   **Dysmenorrhea:** Severe, debilitating period pain.
-   **Intermenstrual Bleeding:** Bleeding between periods.
