# What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the very first day of your period (menstruation) and concludes when you ovulate. This phase is characterized by the maturation of ovarian follicles, one of which will eventually release an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

During this phase, the pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts the ovaries to develop several follicles. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. The rising estrogen levels signal the uterine lining to thicken and eventually trigger the LH surge that causes [ovulation](/what-is-ovulation).

## Real-World Example

If a cycle is 28 days long, the follicular phase lasts from Day 1 to Day 13. However, if a cycle is 35 days long and the [luteal phase](/what-is-luteal-phase) is 14 days, the follicular phase will last for 21 days. This phase's flexibility is what causes cycle length variation.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

It sets the stage for reproduction. A healthy follicular phase ensures that an egg properly matures and the uterine lining is adequately prepared for potential implantation.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timing | First half | Second half |
| Action | Follicle maturation | Uterine preparation |
| Length | Variable | Consistent (12-14 days) |
