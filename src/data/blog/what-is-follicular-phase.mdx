The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the very first day of menstrual bleeding and ends the moment the ovary releases an egg (ovulation). During this phase, the body is focused on preparing a new egg for potential fertilization.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

At the start of the cycle, the brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which tells the ovaries to begin maturing several tiny sacs called follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle takes over, and the rising estrogen thickens the uterine lining in preparation for a fertilized egg.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 30-day cycle and ovulates on Day 16, her follicular phase spans from Day 1 to Day 15. In the first few days, she experiences bleeding. By Day 8 or 9, as estrogen rises, she might notice higher energy levels and a more outgoing mood.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase is responsible for the overall length of your menstrual cycle. While the luteal phase (after ovulation) is almost always 12-14 days long, the follicular phase can vary widely due to stress, illness, or travel, causing a period to arrive "late" or "early."

## Follicular Phase vs Menstruation

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire process of preparing the egg. | The shedding of the uterine lining. |
| **Duration** | Typically 10-16 days. | Typically 2-7 days. |
| **Relationship** | Menstruation occurs *during* the early part of the follicular phase. |
