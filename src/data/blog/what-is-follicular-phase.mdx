# What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It marks the time from the first day of menstruation until ovulation occurs. Its primary purpose is to mature an egg in preparation for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

This phase begins when the hypothalamus signals the pituitary gland to release Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH). FSH stimulates the ovaries to develop several fluid-filled sacs called follicles, each containing an immature egg.

As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle emerges and continues to mature, while the others disintegrate. The rising estrogen levels from this dominant follicle cause the uterine lining to thicken in preparation for a potential pregnancy. The phase concludes when estrogen reaches a peak, triggering a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

Because the length of the follicular phase can vary greatly due to factors like stress, illness, or travel, it is the main reason why your cycle length might change from month to month. Tracking your cycle with [LunaCycle](/) helps you visualize these variations and understand that a delayed period is often simply a longer follicular phase, meaning you ovulated later than usual.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Starts** | First day of period. | Right after ovulation. |
| **Ends** | At ovulation. | Before the next period. |
| **Key Hormones** | FSH and Estrogen. | Progesterone. |
| **Length Variation** | Highly variable. | Generally consistent. |
