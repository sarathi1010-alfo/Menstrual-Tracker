## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first part of your menstrual cycle. It officially begins on the first day of your period (Day 1) and ends when you ovulate. This phase is all about preparation, as the body works to mature an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

During this phase, the pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg. Usually, only one follicle becomes dominant and continues to mature. As this dominant follicle grows, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase typically lasts about 13 to 14 days. During the later part of this phase, as estrogen rises, you may notice increased energy levels, improved mood, and changes in cervical mucus.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase is the most variable part of the menstrual cycle. If you have a cycle that is longer or shorter than usual, it is almost always due to a longer or shorter follicular phase. Tracking your cycle with [LunaCycle](/tracker) helps you see these variations.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Goal | Mature an egg | Prepare uterus for pregnancy |
| Variability | Highly variable | Consistent |
