## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

This phase actually overlaps with menstruation. While the uterus sheds its lining, the pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH). This tells the ovaries to start maturing several egg follicles. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Rising estrogen eventually thickens the uterine lining and triggers the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase roughly spans from Day 1 to Day 13. During the later days of this phase (Days 7-13), as estrogen rises, you might notice an increase in energy, a better mood, and clearer skin.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase dictates the length of your overall menstrual cycle. While the luteal phase is usually a static 12-14 days, the follicular phase can be shortened or lengthened by stress, diet, or illness. A healthy follicular phase ensures a mature, viable egg is released.

## Follicular Phase vs Menstruation

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Relationship** | Encompasses menstruation | A sub-event of the early follicular phase |
| **Duration** | Typically 10-16 days | Typically 3-7 days |
| **Key Action** | Growing an egg | Shedding the old lining |
