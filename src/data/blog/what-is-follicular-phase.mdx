# What is the Follicular Phase?

**The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends with ovulation. During this phase, rising levels of estrogen cause the uterine lining to thicken and prepare an egg for release.**

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The brain's pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles (fluid-filled sacs containing immature eggs). As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Usually, only one follicle becomes fully mature. The rising estrogen levels signal the uterus to rebuild its lining, preparing for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase lasts from Day 1 to Day 13 or 14. A woman may feel fatigued during the first few days (while menstruating) but typically experiences a surge in energy, clearer skin, and improved mood as estrogen rises throughout the rest of the phase.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase lays the groundwork for reproduction by maturing the egg and building the uterine lining. It is characterized by high energy and sociability due to rising estrogen, making it an optimal time for demanding tasks or social events.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Before ovulation (Days 1-13 approx) | After ovulation (Days 15-28 approx) |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Body Goal** | Develop a mature egg | Prepare for possible pregnancy |
