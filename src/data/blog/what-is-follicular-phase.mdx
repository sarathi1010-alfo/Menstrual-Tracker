The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends when ovulation occurs. During this phase, the body is preparing to release an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which signals the ovaries to develop several follicles (fluid-filled sacs containing immature eggs). One dominant follicle eventually emerges and begins producing increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase roughly spans Days 1 to 13. However, if she experiences stress or illness, this phase can easily stretch to 20 days or more, delaying ovulation and resulting in a longer overall cycle length.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase is responsible for maturing a healthy egg for potential fertilization. Because its length can fluctuate significantly, it is the primary reason why cycle lengths vary from month to month for many women.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Day 1 until Ovulation. | Ovulation until the next period. |
| **Length Variability** | Highly variable. | Very consistent (usually 12-14 days). |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen. | Progesterone. |
