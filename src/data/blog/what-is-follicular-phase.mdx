The follicular phase begins on the first day of your period and ends with ovulation. During this time, estrogen rises, and ovarian follicles mature.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to develop several follicles. One of these follicles will become dominant and eventually release a mature egg. As the follicles grow, they produce estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

For many, this phase lasts from Day 1 to Day 13 of a 28-day cycle. You might notice feeling more energized and upbeat as estrogen levels rise in the second week of your cycle.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

It sets the stage for ovulation and prepares the reproductive system for a potential pregnancy. The length of this phase is the primary reason why menstrual cycle lengths vary.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|------------------|--------------|
| Can vary significantly in length. | Usually a consistent 12-14 days. |
| Estrogen dominant. | Progesterone dominant. |
