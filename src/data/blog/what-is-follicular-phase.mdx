The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of your period (menstruation) and ends when you ovulate.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

During this time, the pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts the ovaries to develop several follicles. Usually, only one dominant follicle will mature fully to release an egg. As the follicle grows, it produces estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

In a cycle that lasts 30 days with ovulation on Day 16, the follicular phase spans Days 1 to 15. As estrogen rises during the second week, you may notice increased energy and an improved mood.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase is responsible for preparing a healthy egg for ovulation and building a hospitable environment in the uterus. Its length can vary significantly due to stress or illness, which is why cycle lengths fluctuate.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | Beginning to middle of cycle | Middle to end of cycle |
| Main Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Variability | Highly variable | Relatively constant |
