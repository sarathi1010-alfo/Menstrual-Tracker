Definition: The follicular phase is the first stage of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends with ovulation. During this phase, the body works to mature an ovarian follicle containing an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles. Usually, only one follicle becomes dominant and matures. As this follicle grows, it secretes increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining in preparation for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase spans days 1 through 13. While you are bleeding during the first few days, your body is simultaneously starting the process of preparing a new egg. As estrogen rises toward the end of this phase, you may notice increased energy and improved mood.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase dictates the overall length of your menstrual cycle. Because its duration can vary significantly due to stress or illness, tracking it using tools like [LunaCycle](/) helps explain why your cycle might be early or late in a given month.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|------------------|--------------|
| Precedes ovulation | Follows ovulation |
| Variable length | Consistent length |
| Matures an egg | Prepares uterus for implantation |
