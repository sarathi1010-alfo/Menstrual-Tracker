## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the very first day of menstrual bleeding and concludes with ovulation. This phase is characterized by the maturation of ovarian follicles to prepare an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to produce several follicles. Usually, only one dominant follicle matures fully. As it grows, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining and often increases energy and mood.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle and ovulate on Day 14, your follicular phase spans from Day 1 to Day 13 (overlapping with the days you are bleeding). If your cycle is 35 days and you ovulate on Day 21, your follicular phase is 20 days long.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The length of the follicular phase dictates the overall length of your menstrual cycle. It is the phase most susceptible to stress, diet, and environmental changes, which is why cycle lengths can vary from month to month.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Before ovulation. | After ovulation. |
| **Length** | Highly variable. | Generally fixed (12-14 days). |
| **Hormone** | Estrogen. | Progesterone. |
