The follicular phase marks the beginning of the menstrual cycle. It starts on the very first day of your period and continues until ovulation occurs. This phase is all about preparation and growth.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

During this phase, the pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts several follicles in the ovaries to grow. Usually, only one of these follicles becomes dominant and matures completely, producing increasing amounts of estrogen as it grows.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, the follicular phase roughly covers Days 1 through 13. While you are menstruating during the first few days, your body is already beginning the process of preparing a new egg for the current cycle.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase sets the stage for the rest of the cycle. The rising estrogen levels not only thicken the uterine lining but can also boost energy, mood, and cognitive function for many women.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Action** | Maturing an egg | Preparing for implantation |
| **Duration** | Variable (often 10-16 days) | Relatively stable (11-17 days) |
