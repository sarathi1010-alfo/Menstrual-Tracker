## Definition
The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the very first day of menstrual bleeding and concludes with ovulation.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?
The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to develop several follicles. One follicle becomes dominant and matures an egg. As the follicle grows, it produces estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example
If you have a 32-day cycle and ovulate on Day 18, your follicular phase spans from Day 1 to Day 17. During this time, you might feel a steady increase in energy as estrogen levels climb.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?
It sets the stage for ovulation. Because its length can vary greatly due to stress or illness, it is the primary reason why your period might arrive earlier or later than expected.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---------|------------------|--------------|
| Timing | Before ovulation | After ovulation |
| Variability | Highly variable in length | Very consistent in length |
