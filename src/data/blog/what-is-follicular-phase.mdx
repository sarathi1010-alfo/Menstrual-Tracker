## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle, starting on the first day of your period and ending with ovulation. During this phase, the body develops follicles in the ovaries, one of which will eventually release an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting several follicles to grow. As they grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures, while the others stop growing. The rising estrogen thickens the uterine lining in preparation for pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

If your cycle is 28 days long and you ovulate on day 14, your follicular phase lasts from day 1 to day 14. The length of this phase can vary greatly among individuals and is what usually causes variations in total cycle length.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

It is responsible for setting the stage for ovulation and a potential pregnancy. The rising estrogen during this phase often brings increased energy and improved mood.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | First half of cycle. | Second half of cycle. |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Estrogen. | Progesterone. |
| **Focus** | Egg development. | Uterine preparation. |
