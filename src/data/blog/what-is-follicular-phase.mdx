The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends when an egg is released (ovulation).

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?
During this phase, the pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts the ovaries to produce multiple follicles. Usually, only one follicle matures into a fully developed egg. As the follicle grows, it produces estrogen, which signals the uterine lining to thicken.

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase usually lasts about 13 to 14 days. As estrogen levels rise toward the end of this phase, you might notice an increase in energy, a better mood, and clearer skin.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?
The length of the follicular phase dictates the total length of your menstrual cycle. Stress, illness, or travel can delay ovulation, thereby lengthening the follicular phase and causing a "late" period. It is the body's time to build up resources for potential reproduction.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---------|------------------|--------------|
| **Focus** | Maturing an egg | Preparing the uterus |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Duration Variability** | Highly variable | Very consistent |
