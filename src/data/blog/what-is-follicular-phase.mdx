The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It starts on the first day of menstruation and ends when ovulation occurs. Its main purpose is to mature an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?
The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting several follicles in the ovaries to grow. Usually, only one dominant follicle matures fully. As it grows, it produces estrogen, which rebuilds the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase usually runs from Day 1 to Day 13 or 14 (overlapping with the bleeding days).

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?
This phase dictates the length of your cycle. A longer cycle usually means a longer follicular phase, as the luteal phase remains relatively stable.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | Pre-ovulation | Post-ovulation |
| Purpose | Mature an egg | Prepare uterus for pregnancy |
