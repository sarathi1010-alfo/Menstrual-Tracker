## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

Starting on the first day of menstrual bleeding, the brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH). This tells the ovaries to grow several follicles, each containing an immature egg. As the follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures fully, leading up to [ovulation](/what-is-ovulation).

## Real-World Example

If a cycle is 28 days long and ovulation is on Day 14, the follicular phase spans Days 1 through 13. While the first few days involve menstrual bleeding and potentially low energy, the rising estrogen later in the phase often brings a surge in energy, improved mood, and clear skin.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase is foundational for a healthy cycle. The rising estrogen builds the uterine lining and creates fertile cervical mucus. Because the length of this phase can vary greatly from cycle to cycle (due to stress or illness), it is usually the reason why a period arrives "early" or "late." Tracking it with [LunaCycle](/) helps you understand these variations.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---------|------------------|--------------|
| **Timing** | First half of cycle | Second half of cycle |
| **Length** | Highly variable | Relatively consistent (11-17 days) |
| **Energy Level**| Often increasing | Often decreasing (PMS) |
