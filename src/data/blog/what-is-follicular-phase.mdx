## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first major phase of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation (day 1 of your period) and concludes when you ovulate. Its primary function is to prepare a mature egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

This phase is initiated by the brain releasing Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH). FSH stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles, each containing an immature egg. Usually, only one follicle becomes dominant and matures fully. As this follicle grows, it secretes increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining in preparation for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase typically lasts from day 1 to day 13 or 14. During the early part (your period), you might feel sluggish. However, as the period ends and estrogen begins to surge in the second week, many people report feeling more energetic, focused, and sociable.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The length of the follicular phase determines the overall length of your menstrual cycle. While the luteal phase (after ovulation) is fairly consistent in length (about 14 days), the follicular phase can vary significantly from person to person and cycle to cycle, which is why ovulation day can be unpredictable.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | Starts day 1, ends at ovulation. | Starts after ovulation, ends before period. |
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Physiological Focus | Maturing an egg. | Preparing uterus for implantation. |
