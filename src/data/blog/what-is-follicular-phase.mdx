The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle, starting on the first day of your period and ending with ovulation. Its primary focus is preparing an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to produce several follicles, each containing an immature egg. Usually, only one follicle becomes dominant and matures. As this follicle grows, it produces estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining. This process is detailed in our [cycle phases explained](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal) guide.

## Real-World Example

Starting on Day 1 of bleeding, FSH stimulates follicle growth. By Day 8, bleeding has usually stopped, and rising estrogen levels might lead to increased energy and clearer skin. By Day 13, estrogen peaks, leading to the LH surge that triggers ovulation around Day 14.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase dictates the length of your entire cycle. If you experience a longer or shorter cycle than usual, it is almost always because the follicular phase varied in length. Tracking the start of your period in [LunaCycle](/) marks the beginning of this vital phase.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | Start of period to ovulation. | Ovulation to start of next period. |
| Variability | Highly variable in length. | Generally consistent (12-14 days). |
| Physical Effect | Ripening of the egg. | Thickening of the uterine lining. |

### Core Principles
*   It begins on Day 1 of your cycle.
*   It overlaps with menstruation.
*   Estrogen is the dominant hormone later in the phase.
*   Its length determines your total cycle length.
