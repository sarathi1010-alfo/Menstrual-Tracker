## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The follicular phase begins on Day 1 of your menstrual cycle (the first day of bleeding). At this time, the pituitary gland in the brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH).

FSH travels to the ovaries and stimulates the growth of several follicles (fluid-filled sacs containing immature eggs). As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one follicle becomes dominant, and the others are reabsorbed by the body.

The rising levels of estrogen from the dominant follicle serve two main purposes: they signal the uterine lining to start thickening again (after your period ends), and they eventually trigger the LH surge that causes ovulation, marking the end of the follicular phase.

## Real-World Example of Follicular Energy

Many people notice a distinct shift in their energy and mood during the follicular phase, especially after their period ends. As estrogen rises, you might feel more outgoing, energetic, and focused. This is often considered a great time to tackle complex projects or engage in more intense workouts. ## Follicular Phase vs. Luteal Phase

| Phase | Timing | Key Event | Dominant Hormone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Follicular Phase**| Start of period until ovulation. | Follicle development | Estrogen |
| **Luteal Phase** | After ovulation until next period. | Corpus luteum forms | Progesterone |
