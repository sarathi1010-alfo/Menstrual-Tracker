## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?
The phase begins on day one of your period. The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles, each containing an immature egg. As these follicles grow, they release estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures fully.

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase lasts from Day 1 to roughly Day 13. While you are bleeding during the first few days of this phase, your body is already actively preparing the next egg for ovulation.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?
This phase sets the stage for the entire cycle. The rising estrogen levels often lead to increased energy and improved mood. Variations in the length of a woman's menstrual cycle are almost always due to variations in the length of the follicular phase.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | ~28 days | Highly variable (10-22 days) |
| Purpose | Complete cycle process | Mature an egg for release |
| Key Hormone | Fluctuating Hormones | Estrogen rising |
