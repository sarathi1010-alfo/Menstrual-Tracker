## Definition
The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends when ovulation occurs. During this time, the body prepares to release a mature egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?
The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting several follicles in the ovaries to grow. Usually, only one follicle becomes dominant and matures. As it grows, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example
If you have a 32-day cycle and a standard 14-day luteal phase, your ovulation occurs on day 18. Your follicular phase, therefore, lasts from day 1 to day 18.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?
The follicular phase sets the stage for ovulation and determines the overall length of your menstrual cycle. It's a time when you might feel your best physically and mentally due to rising estrogen.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase
| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Before ovulation | After ovulation |
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Duration | Variable | Consistent |
