The follicular phase is the first phase of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and concludes with ovulation. Its primary purpose is to mature an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles (fluid-filled sacs containing immature eggs). Usually, only one dominant follicle matures fully. As it grows, this follicle produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining in preparation for a fertilized egg.

## Real-World Example

In a typical cycle, this phase might last 14 days. During the first few days (menstruation), energy might be low. But as the period ends and estrogen rises from days 6-14, a woman often experiences increased energy, a better mood, and clearer skin.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase sets the stage for the rest of the cycle. A healthy follicular phase ensures a mature, viable egg is released and that the uterine lining is adequately prepared. The length of this phase dictates the overall length of the menstrual cycle.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | Start of period to ovulation. | Ovulation to start of period. |
| Length | Highly variable. | Consistent (12-14 days). |
| Key Event | Maturing the egg. | Preparing for implantation. |
