The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends when ovulation occurs. During this phase, the body is preparing to release an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg. Usually, only one follicle matures fully. As it grows, it releases increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase roughly covers Day 1 through Day 13. You might notice your energy levels and mood improving as the phase progresses and estrogen rises.

This phase is responsible for maturing the egg and rebuilding the uterine lining after menstruation. The length of the follicular phase dictates the overall length of your menstrual cycle. Track it easily with [LunaCycle](/).

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | First half of cycle | Second half of cycle |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Variability** | Highly variable in length | Usually a consistent 12-14 days |
