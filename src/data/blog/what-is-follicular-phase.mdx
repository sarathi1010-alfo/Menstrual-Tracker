## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

This phase begins on the first day of menstruation. The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts the ovaries to produce multiple follicles. Usually, only one of these follicles matures into an egg. As the follicle grows, it produces estrogen.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase lasts roughly 14 days. During the first few days, you will experience menstrual bleeding. After the bleeding stops, you may notice increasing energy levels as estrogen rises leading up to ovulation.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase is crucial for developing a healthy egg. The length of this phase can vary significantly from woman to woman and cycle to cycle, which is why cycle lengths differ.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Pre-ovulation | Post-ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Variability** | Highly variable | Usually constant (12-14 days) |
