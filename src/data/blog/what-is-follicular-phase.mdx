The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle, starting on day one of menstruation and ending with ovulation. It is characterized by the maturation of ovarian follicles and rising estrogen levels.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?
The pituitary gland releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting several follicles in the ovary to grow. Usually, one becomes dominant. As it grows, it produces estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase spans Days 1 through 13. As estrogen rises after your period ends, you'll likely notice an increase in physical energy and mental clarity.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?
This phase dictates the length of your cycle and ensures that a healthy egg is prepared for ovulation. It is the most variable phase—stress or illness can easily lengthen it.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Goal | Mature an egg | Prepare for pregnancy |
