The follicular phase is the longest phase of the menstrual cycle, starting on the first day of menstruation and ending with ovulation. Its primary purpose is to mature an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts the ovaries to produce several follicles. Usually, only one dominant follicle matures. As it grows, it produces estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining in preparation for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

Starting from Day 1 of your cycle (the first day of bleeding), you are in the follicular phase. As bleeding stops around Day 5, estrogen levels begin to rise significantly. By Day 10, you might notice increased energy, clearer skin, and a better mood leading up to ovulation on Day 14.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase dictates when ovulation will occur. Unlike the luteal phase, which is generally stable in length, the follicular phase can vary greatly from cycle to cycle, which is why periods are sometimes 'late' or 'early.'

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Goal** | Mature an egg | Prepare uterus for implantation |
| **Length** | Highly variable (often 10-16 days) | Stable (usually 12-14 days) |
| **Energy Levels** | Generally increasing/high | Decreasing (premenstrual fatigue) |
