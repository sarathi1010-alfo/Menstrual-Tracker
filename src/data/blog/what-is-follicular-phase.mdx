## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstrual bleeding and concludes with ovulation.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg. As the follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one follicle becomes dominant, and the others are reabsorbed by the body. The rising estrogen thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase roughly covers Days 1 through 13. During the later part of this phase, as estrogen rises, you may experience increased energy and notice a change in cervical mucus, becoming wetter and more abundant.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase is critical for the development of a healthy egg. The length of the follicular phase dictates the overall length of your menstrual cycle; variations in cycle length are usually due to a longer or shorter follicular phase.

## Follicular Phase vs Ovulation

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Preparing the egg | Releasing the egg |
| **Duration** | Variable (often 10-16 days) | 12-24 hours |
| **Hormone Focus**| FSH & Estrogen | LH Surge |
