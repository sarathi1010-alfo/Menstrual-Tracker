## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It officially begins on Day 1 of your period and ends when you ovulate. During this time, the brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting several follicles in the ovaries to grow. Usually, only one becomes the dominant follicle and matures an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

As the follicles grow, they produce estrogen. This rising estrogen thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a potential pregnancy and eventually triggers the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 28-day cycle, her follicular phase lasts from Day 1 to Day 13. By Day 10, she might notice increasing energy levels as estrogen rises.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

It sets the stage for a healthy ovulation. Irregularities in this phase are common causes of irregular overall cycles.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | Variable (usually 10-16 days) | Consistent (~14 days) |
| Hormone | Rising Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Goal | Mature an egg | Prepare uterus |
