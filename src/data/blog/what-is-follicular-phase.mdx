The follicular phase is the first part of your menstrual cycle, beginning on the first day of menstrual bleeding and concluding when ovulation occurs.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts the ovaries to produce several follicles, each containing an immature egg. Usually, only one of these follicles becomes dominant and fully matures. As this dominant follicle grows, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase usually covers days 1 through 14. During the second week (days 7-14), as estrogen peaks, you might notice clearer skin and higher energy levels.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase sets the stage for reproduction. Its length determines the overall length of your cycle and the timing of ovulation.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | First half of cycle | Second half of cycle |
| **Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Variability** | Highly variable in length | Very consistent length (12-14 days) |
