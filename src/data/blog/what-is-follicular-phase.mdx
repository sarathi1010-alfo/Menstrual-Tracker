The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle, starting on the first day of your period and ending with ovulation. Its primary focus is maturing an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

Beginning on Day 1 of your period, your brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH). This tells your ovaries to prepare several follicles, each containing an immature egg. Usually, only one follicle becomes fully mature. As it grows, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining and eventually triggers the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example

In a typical cycle, this phase might last from Day 1 to Day 14. During the early days (menstruation), energy might be low. However, as the period ends and estrogen rises (Days 6-13), a woman will often feel a surge in energy, mental clarity, and sociability, making it a great time for demanding tasks.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase sets the stage for the entire cycle. The rising estrogen not only prepares the body for reproduction but also profoundly affects brain chemistry, bone density, and cardiovascular health. Because its length can vary, it is the primary reason why cycle lengths change from month to month.

## Follicular Phase vs Menstruation

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Relationship** | Encompasses menstruation and the days after | A specific event within the follicular phase |
| **Activity** | Maturing an egg | Shedding the uterine lining |
| **Hormonal Trend** | Estrogen is rising | Hormones are at their lowest |
