The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends when ovulation occurs. Its primary function is to mature an ovarian follicle to release an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts several follicles in the ovaries to grow. As they grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures fully, while the others dissolve.

## Real-World Example

In a standard 28-day cycle, the follicular phase spans from Day 1 to Day 13. However, if you have a longer cycle, say 35 days, your follicular phase might last until Day 20 before ovulation occurs.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The length of the follicular phase determines the overall length of your menstrual cycle. The rising estrogen during this phase also rebuilds the uterine lining after menstruation and typically brings a boost in energy and mood.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Length** | Variable (often 10-21 days) | Consistent (usually 12-14 days) |
| **Key Action** | Egg maturation | Uterine lining maintenance |
| **Energy Levels** | Generally high and rising | Tends to decrease toward the end |
