## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

Triggered by Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH) from the brain, the ovaries begin to mature several follicles, each containing an egg. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle emerges, and the rising estrogen triggers the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase usually lasts from Day 1 to Day 13. As bleeding stops around Day 5, rising estrogen levels often lead to increased energy, better focus, and a brighter mood leading up to Day 14.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase is highly variable. If you are stressed or sick, ovulation can be delayed, meaning the follicular phase is extended. This is the primary reason why periods are sometimes "late."

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | Mature an egg | Prepare uterus for pregnancy |
| **Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Energy Level** | Generally rising/high | Generally stable/decreasing |
