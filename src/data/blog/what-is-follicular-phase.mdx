The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle, starting on the first day of your period and ending with ovulation. During this phase, follicles mature in the ovaries to prepare an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), which stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles. As these grow, they produce estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase typically lasts from Day 1 (start of bleeding) to Day 13 (just before ovulation).

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase is responsible for preparing the body for potential ovulation and pregnancy. The length of this phase determines the overall length of your menstrual cycle.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | Variable (can be short or long) | Fixed (usually 12-14 days) |
| Primary focus | Maturing an egg | Preparing for implantation |
