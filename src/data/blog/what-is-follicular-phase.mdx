## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts the ovaries to develop several follicles, each containing an immature egg. As the follicles grow, they produce estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures fully, leading to ovulation.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 30-day cycle and ovulate on Day 16, your follicular phase spans from Day 1 to Day 16. The first few days of this phase overlap with menstruation (bleeding).

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase is responsible for creating a healthy, mature egg. Because stress, illness, or travel can delay ovulation, the length of the follicular phase is usually what causes cycle lengths to vary from month to month.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Length Variability** | Highly variable. | Generally constant (12-16 days). |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen. | Progesterone. |
| **Energy Levels** | Often rising or high. | Often stabilizing or declining. |
