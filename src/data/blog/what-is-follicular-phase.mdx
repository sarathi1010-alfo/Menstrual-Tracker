## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The follicular phase begins on the first day of menstruation. The brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting several follicles in the ovaries to grow. These developing follicles produce rising levels of estrogen, which causes the uterine lining to thicken and eventually triggers the LH surge that leads to ovulation.

## Real-World Example

In the days following the end of menstruation, a person often experiences increased energy, a more stable mood, and a gradual increase in cervical mucus as rising estrogen levels prepare the body for the fertile window.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

It sets the stage for the entire reproductive cycle. The length and health of the follicular phase dictate when ovulation will occur and ensure the uterine lining is adequately prepared for a potential pregnancy.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Variable Length | Often highly variable | Usually consistent |
