## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstrual bleeding and ends when you ovulate. Its primary purpose is to develop a mature egg ready for fertilization.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts the ovaries to produce several follicles. Usually, only one follicle becomes dominant and matures into an egg. As the follicles grow, they produce estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 32-day cycle and ovulate on Day 18, your follicular phase lasts for the first 18 days. During the later part of this phase, you might notice an increase in energy and clear, slippery cervical mucus.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase sets the stage for the rest of the cycle. Its length can vary significantly depending on stress, illness, or travel, which is why cycle lengths often fluctuate. Understanding this phase explains why ovulation dates aren't always consistent.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Starts on Day 1, ends at ovulation | Starts after ovulation, ends at next Day 1 |
| Length | Highly variable (10-20+ days) | Usually fixed (12-16 days) |
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
