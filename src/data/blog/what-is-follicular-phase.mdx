## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It starts on the very first day of your period (Day 1) and concludes with ovulation. During this time, the body is preparing to release a mature egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The process is initiated by the brain:
1. The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH).
2. FSH stimulates several follicles (sacs containing immature eggs) in the ovaries to grow.
3. As follicles grow, they produce **estrogen**, which thickens the uterine lining.
4. Eventually, one dominant follicle emerges, and the rising estrogen triggers the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase lasts from Day 1 to Day 13.
- **Days 1-5:** You are menstruating, but FSH is already at work.
- **Days 6-13:** Bleeding has stopped, estrogen is rising, and you may notice increased energy and the appearance of clearer, stretchier cervical mucus as you approach ovulation.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase is the most variable part of the menstrual cycle. When a period is "late," it is almost always because ovulation was delayed, meaning the follicular phase was longer than usual. Stress, illness, and diet can all impact the length of this phase.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Starts on Day 1, ends at ovulation. | Starts after ovulation, ends before Day 1. |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Estrogen. | Progesterone. |
| **Length** | Highly variable. | Consistent (usually 12-14 days). |
