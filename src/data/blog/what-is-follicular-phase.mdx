## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It officially begins on the very first day of your period (menstruation) and ends with ovulation. During this time, the body is preparing to release an egg for potential fertilization.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

At the start of this phase, the pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH). This hormone stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles (fluid-filled sacs that contain immature eggs). As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle emerges, and the rising estrogen levels thicken the uterine lining in preparation for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

In a typical cycle, the follicular phase spans Days 1 through 13. While the first few days (menstruation) might involve fatigue, the latter half (Days 6-13) often brings a noticeable boost in energy, better mood, and clearer skin thanks to peaking estrogen levels.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase lays the groundwork for the rest of the cycle. A healthy follicular phase ensures that a mature egg is developed and that the uterine lining is adequately prepared. The length of your overall cycle is primarily determined by the length of this phase.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | *Before* ovulation | *After* ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Body Temperature** | Lower (baseline) | Higher (elevated) |
