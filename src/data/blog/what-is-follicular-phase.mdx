The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It officially begins on the first day of menstruation (when bleeding starts) and ends with ovulation.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

During this phase, the pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts the ovaries to produce several follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg. Usually, only one follicle becomes dominant and matures. As these follicles grow, they produce increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase spans Days 1 to 13. You will experience menstruation during the first few days. As the phase progresses and estrogen rises, you will likely feel an increase in physical energy and mental clarity.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The length of the follicular phase determines the length of your entire cycle. Stress, illness, or travel can delay ovulation by extending the follicular phase. It is also the phase where the body builds the necessary foundation for a potential pregnancy.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Concept | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Follicular Phase** | Pre-ovulation, estrogen-dominant, highly variable in length. |
| **Luteal Phase** | Post-ovulation, progesterone-dominant, consistent length. |
