The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It starts on the first day of your period and ends when you ovulate. Its primary function is to mature an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts several follicles in the ovaries to grow. Usually, only one becomes the dominant follicle. As it grows, it produces estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle and ovulate on Day 14, your follicular phase is Days 1-14. If you have a 35-day cycle and ovulate on Day 21, your follicular phase is 21 days long. This phase dictates the overall length of your cycle.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

Rising estrogen during this phase often brings increased energy and a better mood. Tracking its length via [LunaCycle](/) helps pinpoint ovulation.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Variability | Highly variable in length | Very consistent (12-14 days) |
| Function | Matures the egg | Prepares the uterus |
