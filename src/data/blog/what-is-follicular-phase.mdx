## What is the Follicular Phase?

**Definition:** The follicular phase is the initial phase of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and concludes with ovulation, marked by the growth of ovarian follicles.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?
The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to produce 5-20 follicles. One dominant follicle matures and releases increasing amounts of estrogen, which signals the uterus to rebuild its lining.

## Real-World Example
If your cycle is 28 days and you bleed for 5 days, the follicular phase covers days 1 through 13. By day 10, you might notice increasing energy and clearer skin due to rising estrogen.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?
This phase sets the stage for a healthy ovulation. Because its length can fluctuate due to stress or illness, it is usually the reason your overall cycle length varies.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase
| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Consistency | Highly variable | Usually fixed (12-14 days) |
| Dominant Event | Egg maturation | Uterine lining maintenance |
