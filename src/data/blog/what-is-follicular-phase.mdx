The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends when ovulation occurs. During this phase, the body works to develop a mature egg ready for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?
The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which stimulates several follicles in the ovaries to grow. Usually, only one follicle becomes dominant and matures. As it grows, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen.

## Real-World Example
In a typical cycle, the follicular phase lasts about 13-14 days. During the early part (your period), energy might be low. As estrogen rises toward the end of the phase, you often feel an increase in energy, sociability, and a brighter mood.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?
This phase is crucial because it sets the stage for ovulation and potential conception. The rising estrogen also helps rebuild the uterine lining shed during the previous period.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | First half of cycle (Starts Day 1) | Second half (After ovulation) |
| Goal | Develop a mature egg | Prepare for a fertilized egg |
| Hormone | Rising Estrogen | High Progesterone |
