The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation (Day 1 of your cycle) and ends with ovulation. Its primary purpose is to mature an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

During this phase, the brain's pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH). This hormone stimulates the ovaries to produce several tiny sacs called follicles, each containing an immature egg. Usually, only one of these follicles will become fully mature. As the dominant follicle grows, it produces estrogen, which causes the uterine lining to thicken.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase lasts roughly from Day 1 to Day 13. While you are bleeding (menstruating) for the first few days, your body is simultaneously starting the process of preparing a new egg. As estrogen rises toward the end of this phase, you often feel more energetic and upbeat.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase is the foundation of the reproductive cycle. It ensures the development of a healthy egg and prepares the uterine environment. It is also the phase most susceptible to stress, diet, or travel, which is why cycle lengths can vary from month to month.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Day 1 until ovulation. | Ovulation until the next period. |
| **Primary Action** | Maturing an egg. | Preparing uterus for pregnancy. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen (rising). | Progesterone (dominant). |
