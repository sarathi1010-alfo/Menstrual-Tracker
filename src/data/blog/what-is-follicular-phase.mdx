**Definition:** The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle, beginning on the first day of menstruation and ending with ovulation. During this time, the body prepares to release an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which tells the ovaries to develop several follicles. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures fully, leading to ovulation.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, this phase lasts from Day 1 to Day 13 or 14. Because this phase's length can vary greatly depending on stress, illness, or travel, it is the primary reason why a woman's overall cycle length might change from month to month.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

A healthy follicular phase ensures the development of a mature, viable egg and the production of enough estrogen to build a healthy uterine lining.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Menstruation to ovulation. | Ovulation to menstruation. |
| **Length Variability** | Highly variable. | Usually consistent (12-14 days). |
| **Key Event** | Egg maturation. | Uterine lining maintenance. |
