**Definition:** The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends with ovulation.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), stimulating the ovaries to produce several follicles. One dominant follicle matures, producing rising levels of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

If a cycle is 28 days long, the follicular phase (including menstruation) lasts roughly from Day 1 to Day 13. If a cycle is 35 days long, the follicular phase is longer, perhaps lasting until Day 20.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

It dictates the length of your cycle. It is responsible for maturing the egg that will be released during ovulation, making it a foundation for fertility.

## Follicular Phase vs Ovulation

| Follicular Phase | Ovulation |
|---|---|
| Preparation phase | The main event |
| Variable length (days to weeks) | Very short (12-24 hours) |
| Rising estrogen | LH surge triggers it |
