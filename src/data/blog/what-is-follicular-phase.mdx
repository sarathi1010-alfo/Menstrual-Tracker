**The follicular phase is the initial phase of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and culminates with ovulation, focused primarily on preparing a mature egg for release.**

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

Triggered by the pituitary gland releasing Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), the ovaries begin to develop several follicles. Usually, only one follicle becomes fully mature. As this follicle grows, it secretes increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase roughly spans days 1 to 13. While a woman is menstruating during the first few days, the follicular processes are already underway internally. Many women report feeling more energetic and positive as estrogen levels rise later in this phase.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The successful maturation of an egg during this phase is the prerequisite for ovulation. The variability in the length of a woman's overall menstrual cycle is almost entirely due to variations in the length of the follicular phase.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Start of period to ovulation | Ovulation to next period |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Focus** | Egg maturation | Uterine preparation |
