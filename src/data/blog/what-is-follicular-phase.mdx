The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the very first day of menstrual bleeding and ends when you ovulate. During this time, the body is preparing an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to produce several fluid-filled sacs called follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg. Usually, only one dominant follicle matures fully. As it grows, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

If your period starts on the 1st of the month, that is Day 1 of your follicular phase. For the first few days, you bleed. But by Day 7 or 8, as estrogen rises, you might notice a surge in physical energy, a brighter mood, and clearer skin, continuing until ovulation around the middle of the month.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase dictates the overall length of your cycle. While the luteal phase (post-ovulation) is usually a rigid 14 days, the follicular phase can vary greatly due to stress, travel, or illness, which is why periods are sometimes 'late'.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Biological Goal | Mature an egg | Prepare uterus for egg implantation |
| Energy Levels | Typically high and rising | Tends to be lower, especially near the end |
