## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?
The follicular phase begins on the very first day of menstrual bleeding. During this time, the pituitary gland in the brain releases follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). This hormone signals the ovaries to start developing several fluid-filled sacs called follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg. As these follicles grow, they secrete increasing amounts of estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle emerges and continues to mature, while the others stop growing and are reabsorbed by the body. The rising estrogen levels also cause the lining of the uterus to thicken in preparation for a potential pregnancy. The phase concludes when a surge in LH triggers the dominant follicle to release its egg.

## Real-World Example
In an average 28-day cycle, the follicular phase usually spans from Day 1 to Day 13. However, this phase is highly variable. If you are sick, traveling, or experiencing high stress, your body may delay ovulation, which extends the length of the follicular phase. This variability is the main reason why cycles can be unpredictable in length.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?
This phase is the foundational stage of the cycle. Without a healthy follicular phase, ovulation cannot occur. The rising levels of estrogen during this time also have widespread effects on the body, often leading to increased energy levels, improved mood, and clearer skin compared to other parts of the cycle.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase
| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Goal | Mature an egg | Prepare uterus for pregnancy |
| Length | Highly variable | Very consistent |
