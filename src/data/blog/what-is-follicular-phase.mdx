The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends with ovulation. During this time, the body prepares an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?
The brain releases follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to develop several follicles. Usually, only one follicle becomes fully mature. As it grows, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which causes the uterine lining to thicken.

## Real-World Example
If you have a 32-day cycle and a standard 14-day luteal phase, your follicular phase lasts for 18 days (days 1 through 18), ending with ovulation on day 18.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?
This phase sets the stage for the rest of the cycle. Its length determines when ovulation will occur and thus when the next period will start. High estrogen during this phase often brings feelings of increased energy and elevated mood. You can track these patterns in [LunaCycle](/).

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase
| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---------|------------------|--------------|
| Timing | Before ovulation | After ovulation |
| Main Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
