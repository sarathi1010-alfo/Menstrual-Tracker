**Definition:** The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends when ovulation occurs. During this phase, the body works to mature an ovarian follicle (which contains an egg).

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to develop several follicles. Usually, only one becomes the 'dominant' follicle. As this follicle grows, it produces estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining in preparation for pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

Starting on Day 1 of your cycle (when bleeding begins), the follicular phase can last anywhere from 10 to 22 days, though 14 days is average. As estrogen rises toward the end of this phase, you might notice increased energy and clearer cervical mucus. You can monitor these signs in [LunaCycle](/).

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase dictates the overall length of your cycle. While the luteal phase (post-ovulation) is usually a fixed length, stress, illness, or travel can easily delay ovulation by extending the follicular phase.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | First half (starts day 1) | Second half (post-ovulation) |
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Variability | Highly variable | Usually fixed (12-14 days) |
