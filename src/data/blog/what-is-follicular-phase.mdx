The follicular phase is the first half of the [menstrual cycle](/what-is-menstrual-cycle), starting on the first day of your period and ending with [ovulation](/what-is-ovulation). It is characterized by rising estrogen levels.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

During this phase, the pituitary gland releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to mature several egg follicles. As these follicles grow, they secrete increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining in preparation for a potential pregnancy. Eventually, one dominant follicle emerges to release an egg.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 32-day cycle, her follicular phase might last for 18 days (Days 1-18), culminating in ovulation around Day 18. During this time, as her period ends and estrogen rises, she may notice increased energy, improved mood, and clearer skin.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase is the variable portion of the menstrual cycle. Stress, illness, or changes in routine can delay ovulation, thereby lengthening the follicular phase and the entire cycle. Understanding this variability is key to predicting periods accurately.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | Starts day 1 of period, ends at ovulation. | Starts after ovulation, ends at menstruation. |
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen. | Progesterone. |
| Length | Highly variable (causes irregular cycles). | Very consistent (usually 12-14 days). |
