The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle, starting on the first day of your period and ending with ovulation. During this time, the body is preparing a new egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting several follicles in the ovaries to grow. Usually, only one dominant follicle matures fully. As it grows, it produces estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining and often boosts energy and mood.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, this phase overlaps with menstruation (Days 1-5) and continues until ovulation around Day 14. If a woman experiences stress or illness, her body may delay ovulation, lengthening the follicular phase and resulting in a longer overall cycle (e.g., a 35-day cycle).

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

It is the foundation of the cycle. Proper follicular development is necessary for ovulation and sufficient estrogen production, which impacts bone health, heart health, and mood.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| **Length** | Highly variable | Consistently 12-14 days |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Physical Effect** | Often higher energy | Often lower energy / PMS |
