The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle, starting on the first day of your period and ending with ovulation. It focuses on maturing an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?
This phase begins simultaneously with menstruation. The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts the ovaries to produce several follicles. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures fully.

## Real-World Example
In a standard 28-day cycle, the follicular phase spans from Day 1 to Day 13 or 14. However, unlike the luteal phase, the follicular phase can vary greatly in length depending on stress, illness, or travel.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?
This phase sets the stage for the rest of the cycle. The rising estrogen levels often lead to increased energy, better mood, and higher libido. Monitoring the length of this phase can explain why a period is late (a delayed ovulation means a delayed period).

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Starts Day 1 | After ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Length** | Highly variable | Fixed (12-14 days) |
