Definition: The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstrual bleeding and concludes with ovulation, focusing on the maturation of an ovarian follicle.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which tells the ovaries to prepare several follicles (sacs containing immature eggs). As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle emerges, and the rising estrogen thickens the uterine lining and eventually triggers the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example

In a typical cycle, this phase lasts about 13-14 days. During the first few days (menstruation), energy may be low. By Day 8 or 9, as estrogen climbs, you might notice an increase in energy, sociability, and clearer skin.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase sets the stage for the entire cycle. The healthy development of the follicle and the corresponding rise in estrogen are essential for successful ovulation and a healthy luteal phase to follow.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| Goal | Mature an egg | Prepare uterus for pregnancy |
| Length Variability | High | Very low |
