## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It officially begins on the first day of your period (menstruation is actually part of this phase) and ends when you ovulate. Its primary purpose is to mature an ovarian follicle so it can release an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which tells the ovaries to start maturing several follicles. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle emerges. The rising estrogen thickens the uterine lining and eventually triggers the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle, your follicular phase lasts roughly 14 days. However, if you are highly stressed and ovulation is delayed until Day 20, your follicular phase for that month was 20 days long, while your luteal phase remains a standard 14 days, resulting in a 34-day cycle.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The length of the follicular phase dictates the total length of your cycle for any given month. Because rising estrogen levels generally make people feel more energetic, focused, and sociable, understanding when you are in this phase can help you plan demanding tasks or intense workouts.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Maturing an egg | Preparing for a fertilized egg |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Vibe** | High energy, outgoing | Lower energy, reflective |
