The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends with ovulation. During this phase, the body is preparing to release an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), which stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures fully, while the others dissolve.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase lasts from Day 1 to Day 13. A woman might notice her energy levels rising and her cervical mucus becoming more abundant and clear as ovulation approaches, driven by rising estrogen levels.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The length of the follicular phase determines the length of your entire menstrual cycle. While the luteal phase is typically a consistent 12-14 days, the follicular phase can vary greatly due to stress, illness, or diet, causing early or delayed periods.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Maturing an egg. | Preparing the uterus for pregnancy. |
| **Timing** | First half (starts with period). | Second half (after ovulation). |
| **Variability** | Highly variable in length. | Generally consistent. |
