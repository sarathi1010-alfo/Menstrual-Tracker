## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The phase begins on the first day of menstrual bleeding. The pituitary gland in the brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which signals the ovaries to start maturing several fluid-filled sacs called follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg. As these follicles grow, they secrete estrogen. This rising estrogen causes the uterine lining to thicken and eventually triggers the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 32-day cycle, your follicular phase might be roughly 18 days long (Days 1-18). During the latter half of this phase (after menstruation ends), you will likely experience rising energy levels, clearer skin, and an increased sex drive thanks to peaking estrogen.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The health of the follicular phase dictates the quality of the egg released and the thickness of the uterine lining, both crucial for fertility. It is also the phase most susceptible to external factors like stress, diet, and illness, which is why your period might be late during stressful months.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | First half of the cycle. | Second half of the cycle. |
| **Length** | Highly variable. | Generally fixed (12-14 days). |
| **Energy Levels** | Typically rising and high. | Typically decreasing and low. |
