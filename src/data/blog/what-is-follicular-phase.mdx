## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to develop several follicles. Usually, only one follicle fully matures. As this follicle grows, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining and often boosts energy and mood.

## Real-World Example

A woman is stressed and her ovulation is delayed. Instead of her usual 14-day follicular phase, her body takes 20 days to mature a follicle and release an egg. Her period, therefore, arrives much later than expected, showing how the follicular phase dictates overall cycle length.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase is crucial for preparing a healthy egg for ovulation. Because the length of this phase is highly variable (affected by stress, travel, illness, or diet), it is the primary reason why menstrual cycle lengths change from month to month.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Preparing the egg | Preparing the uterus |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Variability** | Highly variable | Very consistent |
