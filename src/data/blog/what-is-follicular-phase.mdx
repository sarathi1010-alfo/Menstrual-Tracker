## Definition

The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstrual bleeding and concludes at the moment of ovulation. Its primary purpose is to mature an egg for fertilization.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting several follicles in the ovaries to grow. Usually, only one follicle becomes fully mature. As it grows, it produces estrogen, which causes the uterine lining to thicken. For a complete look at all phases, see [Cycle Phases Explained](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal).

## Real-World Example

If your period starts on January 1st and you ovulate on January 14th, your follicular phase ran from the 1st through the 14th. During the latter half of this phase, as your period ends and estrogen rises, you may feel an increase in energy and focus. Track these changes in [LunaCycle](/).

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The length of the follicular phase dictates the length of your entire cycle. Stress, travel, or illness during this time can delay ovulation, thereby delaying your next period.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | Highly variable. | Consistently 12-14 days. |
| **Hormone** | Driven by Estrogen and FSH. | Driven by Progesterone. |
| **Goal** | Mature the egg. | Sustain the uterine lining. |
