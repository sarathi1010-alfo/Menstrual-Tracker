The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle, starting on the first day of menstruation and ending with ovulation. During this phase, follicles in the ovary mature in preparation for releasing an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which stimulates the ovaries to grow several follicles. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. The rising estrogen thickens the uterine lining and eventually triggers the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example

If your period starts on January 1st and you ovulate on January 14th, your follicular phase lasted 14 days. This phase is highly variable; stress or illness can delay ovulation, making the follicular phase longer than usual.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

Because this phase varies so much in length, it determines the overall length of your entire menstrual cycle. The rising estrogen during this time often leads to increased energy, better mood, and clearer skin.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Before ovulation. | After ovulation. |
| **Length** | Highly variable. | Usually a consistent 11-14 days. |
