# What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It starts on the first day of menstrual bleeding and concludes with ovulation. During this phase, the body works to mature an ovarian follicle to eventually release an egg.

<MedicalDisclaimer />

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to develop several follicles. Usually, only one follicle becomes dominant and matures fully. As this follicle grows, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining in preparation for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

If your total cycle is 32 days, and you ovulate on Day 18, your follicular phase lasted 18 days. During the first 5 days, you might be menstruating. From days 6 to 18, as estrogen rises, you may feel an upward shift in energy, mood, and sociability, culminating around ovulation.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase is responsible for rebuilding the uterine environment after menstruation. Its length is the main variable that determines whether your overall cycle is short or long; unlike the luteal phase which is relatively fixed, the follicular phase can be delayed by stress, illness, or travel.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Action** | Maturing an egg | Preparing uterus for implantation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Length Variability** | Highly variable | Very consistent (usually 12-14 days) |
