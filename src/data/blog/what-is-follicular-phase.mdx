The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends when [ovulation](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal) occurs. It is characterized by rising levels of estrogen.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The brain releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH), signaling the ovaries to develop several follicles. Usually, only one follicle becomes dominant and matures an egg. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining in preparation for potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 32-day cycle and ovulates on Day 18, her follicular phase lasts from Day 1 to Day 18. During this time, she will likely feel her energy levels steadily increase as her period ends and estrogen rises.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase is responsible for maturing a healthy egg. The length of the follicular phase is the primary reason cycle lengths vary between women (and month to month). Track your unique patterns using [LunaCycle](/).

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Length Variability**| Highly variable (can be 10 to 20+ days). | Very consistent (usually 12-14 days). |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Estrogen. | Progesterone. |
| **General Feeling** | Often increasing energy, improved mood. | Often decreasing energy, potential PMS. |
