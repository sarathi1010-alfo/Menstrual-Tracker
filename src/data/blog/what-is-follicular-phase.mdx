The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It starts on the very first day of menstrual bleeding and concludes with ovulation. The primary goal of this phase is to prepare a mature egg to be released.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The phase is initiated by the pituitary gland, which releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH). FSH stimulates the ovaries to produce several tiny sacs called follicles, each containing an immature egg. As these follicles grow, they secrete estrogen. The rising estrogen thickens the uterine lining. Eventually, one follicle becomes dominant, and the others are reabsorbed. The phase ends when a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) triggers the dominant follicle to release its egg (ovulation).

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase typically spans Days 1 through 13. However, if a woman experiences significant stress or illness, this phase can be delayed, making her cycle longer (e.g., a 35-day cycle might have a 21-day follicular phase and a standard 14-day luteal phase).

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase is the variable part of your cycle. When your period is "late," it almost always means that your follicular phase was longer than usual, pushing ovulation (and subsequently, your period) back.

## Follicular Phase vs Menstruation

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | Spans until ovulation | Only the days you bleed |
| **Overlap** | Menstruation is actually the beginning part of the follicular phase | - |
