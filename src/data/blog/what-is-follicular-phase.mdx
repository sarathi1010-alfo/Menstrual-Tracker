# What is the Follicular Phase?

**Definition:** The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle, starting on the first day of menstruation and ending with ovulation. During this time, follicles in the ovary mature.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), stimulating the ovaries to produce follicles. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures fully.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, this phase overlaps with menstruation and continues until roughly Day 14. A woman often feels an increase in energy and mood as estrogen levels rise.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

It is responsible for preparing a healthy egg for ovulation and ensuring the uterine environment is rebuilding after menstruation. Variations in the length of the menstrual cycle usually stem from variations in the length of the follicular phase.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Before ovulation | After ovulation |
| **Length** | Highly variable | Relatively consistent |
