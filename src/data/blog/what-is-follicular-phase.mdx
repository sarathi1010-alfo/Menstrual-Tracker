The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle, beginning on the first day of menstruation and ending with ovulation. During this time, the body prepares to release a mature egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which stimulates several follicles in the ovaries to grow. Usually, only one follicle becomes dominant and matures. As it grows, it produces estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining in preparation for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

In a typical cycle, the follicular phase starts on Day 1 (first day of bleeding) and lasts until roughly Day 13 or 14. As estrogen rises during the second week, many women notice increased energy and clearer skin.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The length and health of the follicular phase dictate when ovulation will occur. Because its length can vary (unlike the more stable luteal phase), tracking it is key to predicting your period and fertile window.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | First half of the cycle. | Second half of the cycle. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Variability** | Highly variable in length. | Generally consistent (12-14 days). |
