# What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It starts on the first day of menstruation and ends when ovulation begins.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), prompting the ovaries to produce 5 to 20 follicles. One dominant follicle matures and produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase spans from Day 1 to roughly Day 13. While a woman is menstruating during the first few days, her body is already preparing the next egg for ovulation.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The length of the follicular phase dictates the total length of your cycle. It's also the phase where the body builds the environment necessary for a potential pregnancy.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Before ovulation | After ovulation |
| Energy Levels | Typically rising/high | Typically decreasing/inward |
| Variation | Highly variable length | Consistently 12-14 days |
