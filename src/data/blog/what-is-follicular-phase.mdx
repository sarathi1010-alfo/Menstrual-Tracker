The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of your period (Day 1) and lasts until ovulation occurs. During this time, the brain and ovaries work together to mature an egg for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which tells the ovaries to prepare several follicles. Each follicle contains an egg. As the follicles grow, they release estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle emerges and the others stop growing.

## Real-World Example of the Follicular Phase

If you have a period that lasts 5 days, and you ovulate on Day 14, your follicular phase is 14 days long. You might notice that your skin looks clearer and you have more physical energy toward the end of these two weeks as your estrogen levels peak.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The length of the follicular phase determines the length of your overall cycle. While the luteal phase is usually consistent, the follicular phase can vary due to stress, illness, or nutrition, which is why your period might arrive "late" some months.

| Aspect | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|--------|------------------|--------------|
| Energy Level | Usually increasing | Usually decreasing |
| Cervical Mucus | Dry to creamy to stretchy | Thick and sticky |
| Purpose | Egg maturation | Uterine preparation |
