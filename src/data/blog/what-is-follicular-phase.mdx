## Definition

The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It officially begins on the first day of menstrual bleeding (Day 1) and ends when ovulation occurs. During this phase, the body is focused on maturing ovarian follicles (tiny sacs containing immature eggs) and preparing the uterine lining for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The phase begins with the brain's pituitary gland releasing Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH). This hormone signals the ovaries to develop several follicles. As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one follicle becomes dominant and matures fully, while the others regress. The rising estrogen levels also cause the uterine lining (endometrium) to thicken. The phase concludes when estrogen reaches a peak, triggering the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

The length of the follicular phase is the primary variable that determines your overall cycle length. If you have a 28-day cycle, your follicular phase is likely around 14 days. If you have a 35-day cycle, your follicular phase might be 21 days. Logging your dates in [LunaCycle's Tracker](/tracker) helps you see this variability. Many women also report feeling more energized, focused, and sociable during the mid-to-late follicular phase as estrogen levels rise.

## Follicular Phase vs. Menstrual Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Menstrual Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Overlap** | The menstrual phase occurs *within* the early part of the follicular phase. | The days of actual bleeding. |
| **Primary Goal** | Maturing an egg. | Shedding the old uterine lining. |
| **Hormone Trend** | Estrogen is rising. | Estrogen and Progesterone are low. |
