The follicular phase is the initial phase of the menstrual cycle. It starts on the first day of your period and continues until ovulation occurs. Its main purpose is to develop a mature egg ready for release.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles, each containing an immature egg. Usually, only one follicle becomes fully mature. As this dominant follicle grows, it produces estrogen, which signals the uterine lining to thicken.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the follicular phase lasts from Day 1 to about Day 14. During this time, as estrogen levels rise, a person might notice an increase in energy levels, clearer skin, and improved mood.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

This phase sets the stage for the rest of the cycle. Proper follicle development and the resulting rise in estrogen are necessary to trigger ovulation and build a healthy uterine lining for potential implantation.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Start of cycle to ovulation | Ovulation to start of cycle |
| Primary Action | Maturing an egg | Preparing for implantation |
| Dominant Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
