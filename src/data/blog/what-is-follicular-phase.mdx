## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The follicular phase begins with menstruation. Concurrently, the pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts several follicles in the ovaries to grow. As they grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures fully and prepares to release an egg, bringing this phase to an end.

## Real-World Example

If your period starts on Day 1, you are in the follicular phase. By Day 8, bleeding has stopped, and rising estrogen makes you feel more energetic and focused. This phase continues until ovulation, typically around Day 14.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase sets the stage for the rest of the cycle. The length of this phase determines the overall length of your menstrual cycle. Stress, illness, or travel can delay ovulation, thereby lengthening the follicular phase and delaying your next period.

## Follicular Phase vs Menstruation

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Menstruation |
|---|---|---|
| **Relationship** | Encompasses menstruation | A sub-phase of the follicular phase |
| **Physical Sign** | Maturing egg | Bleeding |
| **Hormone Trend** | Rising Estrogen | Low hormones initially |
| **Energy Level** | Increasing | Generally lower |
