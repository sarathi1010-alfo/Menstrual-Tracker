## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The cycle begins with low hormone levels, prompting the brain to release Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH). This hormone stimulates the ovaries to produce 5 to 20 small sacs called follicles, each housing an immature egg. Usually, only one dominant follicle matures fully. As the follicles grow, they release estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining and boosts your energy.

## Real-World Example

Starting on Day 1 (the first day of bleeding), you might feel sluggish initially. But by Day 6 or 7, as your period ends and estrogen begins to climb steadily, you'll likely notice increased physical energy, better focus, and a more outgoing mood, leading up to ovulation around Day 14.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase sets the stage for the rest of the cycle. Its length is highly susceptible to stress, travel, or illness, which is why your period might be "late" some months—it just means your follicular phase was longer than usual before ovulation happened.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | First half of cycle | Second half of cycle |
| **Goal** | Mature an egg | Prepare uterus for pregnancy |
| **Energy Levels** | Generally rising and high | Generally stabilizing then dropping |
