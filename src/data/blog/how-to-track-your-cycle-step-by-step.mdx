Cycle tracking is simple when you know what to log: start date, flow intensity, symptoms, and any other signs. [LunaCycle](/) makes it even easier. By establishing a tracking routine, you take control of your health data.

## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Your memory isn't perfect, but a daily log is. Consistent tracking allows you to move away from guessing when your period might arrive and provides concrete data to share with your doctor if issues arise. To understand why this is broadly important, read our [Ultimate Cycle Tracking Guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide).

## How to Log Your Cycle with LunaCycle

[LunaCycle](/) is designed to be frictionless and totally private.
1.  **Open the App:** No login required.
2.  **Select the Date:** Tap the current day on the calendar.
3.  **Log Flow:** Indicate if you are bleeding (light, medium, heavy). The first day of heavy bleeding is Day 1. (See [Understanding Your Period](/blog/understanding-your-period-normal-vs-not-normal) for what flow is normal).
4.  **Add Symptoms:** Note your mood, energy, or pain levels.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. Calendar
Simply circling the days you bleed on a wall calendar. It's basic but effective for predicting start dates if you have a highly regular cycle.

### 2. Symptom Journal
A physical notebook where you write down daily observations. Great for deep-diving into how you feel, but hard to analyze for long-term trends.

### 3. Tech App
Using a digital tracker like [LunaCycle](/) combines the ease of a calendar with the depth of a journal, plus algorithmic predictions.

## Common Tracking Errors

| Error | Why it's a problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Forgetting to log | Breaks the algorithm's predictions | Set a daily alarm or tie it to a habit (like brushing teeth) |
| Counting spotting as Day 1 | Skews your cycle length calculation | Only log full flow as Day 1 |
| Only tracking bleeding | Misses the holistic health picture | Try logging at least one symptom (like mood) daily |
