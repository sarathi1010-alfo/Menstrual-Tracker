## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Consistent tracking provides accurate predictions and valuable health insights. It transforms vague feelings about your body into actionable data. If you are new to this, read [Menstrual Cycle 101](/blog/menstrual-cycle-101-beginners-guide) first.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. The Calendar Method
Simply marking the days you bleed to estimate cycle length. Best for those with highly regular periods.

### 2. The Symptom Journal
Logging physical and emotional changes daily to identify phase transitions. Excellent for understanding [hormonal shifts](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained).

### 3. Tech App Tracking
Using an app that combines calendar and symptom data to run predictive algorithms.

## Common Tracking Errors

| Error | Why it's a mistake | The Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Forgetting to log the end date | Skews the length of your period data | Log the first clear, dry day as the end. |
| Counting spotting as Day 1 | Day 1 must be full flow; spotting is part of the previous cycle. | Wait for red, steady flow to mark Day 1. |
| Giving up after one irregular cycle | Irregularities happen due to stress, illness, etc. | Keep tracking; long-term data is what matters. |

Once you've mastered tracking, you can learn to distinguish between what's [normal and what's not](/blog/understanding-your-period-normal-vs-not-normal) regarding your period.
