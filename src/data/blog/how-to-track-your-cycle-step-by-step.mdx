## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Tracking your menstrual cycle offers profound insights into your overall health. It's not just for those trying to conceive or avoid pregnancy; it's a powerful tool for body literacy. Consistent tracking allows you to anticipate your period, manage [PMS symptoms](/conditions/pms-deep-dive), and quickly identify if something is outside your normal pattern, which can be crucial for discussing health concerns with a doctor.

## How to Log Your Cycle with LunaCycle

Getting started with [LunaCycle](/) is straightforward, especially since it doesn't require an account and keeps all your data safely on your device.

**Step 1: Record Day 1**
The most crucial piece of data is the start date. Log the first day you experience full menstrual bleeding (spotting doesn't count as Day 1).

**Step 2: Note Your Flow**
Each day of your period, quickly note if your flow is light, medium, heavy, or just spotting.

**Step 3: Add Symptoms**
Use the daily log to note mood changes, energy levels, or physical symptoms like cramps or headaches.

**Step 4: Review Your Patterns**
After a few cycles, review your history chart to see your estimated [fertile window](/what-is-fertile-window) and upcoming cycle predictions.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. Calendar Tracking
The simplest method. You simply mark the days you bleed on a physical or digital calendar to calculate your cycle length.

### 2. Symptom Journaling
A more detailed approach where you record physical and emotional changes daily to understand how [hormones](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained) affect you throughout the month.

### 3. Tech App Tracking
Using a dedicated tool like LunaCycle combines calendar and symptom tracking, using algorithms to automatically predict future [cycle phases](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal).

## Common Tracking Errors

| Error | How to Fix It |
|-------|---------------|
| Forgetting to log the end of the period | Set a simple daily reminder or log retrospectively if you remember. |
| Counting spotting as Day 1 | Wait for full, active bleeding to mark the start of your cycle. |
| Changing methods frequently | Stick to one method or app for at least 3-6 months to establish a solid baseline. |
| Ignoring data | Regularly review your tracking history to spot trends or irregularities. |
