## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

For accurate predictions and health insights, consistency is everything. Your cycle is unique to you, and algorithms (whether in an app or just your own mental math) rely on historical data to anticipate the future.

Consistent tracking not only helps you know when to expect your period, but it also establishes a baseline of what is "normal" for *your* body. When something deviates from this baseline, you will be the first to know.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. The Calendar Method
This is the foundational tracking technique. You simply mark the first day of bleeding on a calendar. Over time, you count the days between the start of one period and the start of the next to find your average cycle length.

### 2. The Symptom Journal
This technique involves daily check-ins. You write down physical and emotional changes every day, regardless of whether you are bleeding. This is excellent for identifying patterns related to PMS or ovulation.

### 3. The Tech App Approach
Modern apps combine the calendar method with symptom tracking, automating the math and pattern recognition. The key is choosing an app that aligns with your privacy values.

## Common Tracking Errors

| Mistake | Consequence |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Forgetting to log Day 1** | Throws off calculations for the entire cycle. |
| **Counting spotting as Day 1** | Artificially lengthens your cycle data. Day 1 is full flow. |
| **Ignoring the luteal phase** | Missing out on crucial PMS symptom data. |
| **Relying solely on app predictions** | Apps use averages; your body is not a machine. Always listen to your physical signs. |
