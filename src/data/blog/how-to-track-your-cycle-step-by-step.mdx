Cycle tracking is simple when you know what to log: start date, flow intensity, symptoms, and any other signs. LunaCycle makes it even easier.

## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Consistency is the cornerstone of effective cycle tracking. Without regular input, any tool or app will struggle to provide accurate predictions or meaningful health insights. Consistent tracking allows you to establish your baseline—what is 'normal' for you. Once this baseline is established, it becomes much easier to identify anomalies, predict your periods accurately, and communicate effectively with your healthcare provider about your reproductive health.

## How to Log Your Cycle with LunaCycle

Tracking your cycle should be intuitive. Here is how to use [LunaCycle](/):

1. **Open the App**: Navigate to the tracker interface.
2. **Select the Date**: On the calendar view, tap the date your period started.
3. **Log Flow**: Indicate whether the bleeding is light, medium, or heavy.
4. **Add Symptoms**: Note any physical or emotional symptoms you are experiencing.
5. **Review**: The app will automatically calculate your current cycle day and update future predictions.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. Calendar Tracking
The most basic method. You simply mark the first day of your period on a calendar and count the days until your next period starts. It's good for tracking basic regularity.

### 2. Symptom Journaling
In addition to dates, you write down daily symptoms, mood, and energy levels. This provides a detailed qualitative view of your health.

### 3. Tech Applications
Using an app like [LunaCycle](/) combines the benefits of a calendar and a journal, automating calculations and visualizing patterns over time, all while maintaining your data privacy.

## Common Tracking Errors

| Mistake | Impact |
|---|---|
| Forgetting to log the start date | Skews average cycle length calculations. |
| Logging spotting as Day 1 | Day 1 should be the first day of full flow. |
| Inconsistent symptom logging | Makes it hard to identify true patterns. |
| Relying solely on app predictions | Apps provide estimates; your body may vary. |
