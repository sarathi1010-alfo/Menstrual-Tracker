# How to Track Your Cycle: A Step-by-Step Guide for 2026

Cycle tracking is simple when you know what to log: start date, flow intensity, symptoms, and any other signs. LunaCycle makes it even easier.

<MedicalDisclaimer />

## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Inconsistent data leads to inaccurate predictions. Consistent tracking over several months builds a personalized dataset that is invaluable for accurate period predictions, recognizing your unique symptom patterns, and providing concrete data for healthcare discussions.

## How to Log Your Cycle with LunaCycle

Tracking shouldn't feel like a chore. Here is a step-by-step guide to building a consistent habit, particularly using a simple tool like [LunaCycle](/):

1. **Wait for Day 1:** The easiest way to start is on the first day of full bleeding.
2. **Open the App:** Navigate to the calendar interface.
3. **Tap the Date:** Select today's date to mark the start of your period.
4. **Log the Flow:** If the app allows, quickly note if the flow is light, medium, or heavy.
5. **Add Symptoms:** Take 10 seconds to select any notable symptoms (cramps, headache, mood).
6. **Repeat Daily:** Make it part of your daily routine—perhaps right after brushing your teeth at night.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. The Calendar Method
The traditional approach. Mark an 'X' on a physical calendar on the days you bleed. Best for those who want completely analog tracking, though it lacks predictive power.

### 2. The Symptom Journal
Keeping a daily diary of physical and emotional changes. Excellent for deep self-reflection and identifying PMDD or severe PMS, but requires significant daily effort.

### 3. The Tech App
Using a digital tracker combines the ease of a calendar with the power of algorithms to predict future cycles. Apps range from highly complex to simple and privacy-focused.

## Common Tracking Errors

| Error | Impact | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Forgetting to log the end date | Skews average cycle length data | Log flow intensity daily until it stops. |
| Ignoring spotting | Can mask luteal phase issues | Log spotting as a distinct symptom, separate from full flow. |
| Changing methods mid-cycle | Creates fragmented, useless data | Stick to one tracking method for at least 3-6 full cycles. |
