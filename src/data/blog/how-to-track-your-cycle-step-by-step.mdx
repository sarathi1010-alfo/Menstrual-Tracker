Cycle tracking is simple when you know what to log: start date, flow intensity, symptoms, and any other signs. LunaCycle makes it even easier.

## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

For accurate predictions and health insights, consistency is key. Sporadic logging leads to inaccurate period and ovulation predictions. Tracking consistently helps you understand [what is normal for you](/blog/understanding-your-period-normal-vs-not-normal) and serves as an important record of your reproductive health. See our [Ultimate Tracking Guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide) for more context.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. Calendar Method
The simplest form of tracking. You mark the start and end of your period on a calendar. It's useful for predicting future periods if your cycle is very regular. Understand your [cycle basics here](/blog/menstrual-cycle-101-beginners-guide).

### 2. Symptom Journaling
Writing down daily physical and emotional changes. This is excellent for identifying patterns in mood or physical ailments related to [hormonal fluctuations](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained).

### 3. Tech App
Using an app like LunaCycle automates predictions and provides easy visualization of your cycle history and patterns, without compromising privacy.

## Common Tracking Errors

| Error | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Forgetting to log the start date | Skews all future predictions |
| Logging spotting as Day 1 | Day 1 should be the first day of full flow |
| Ignoring secondary signs | Misses the opportunity to identify ovulation |
