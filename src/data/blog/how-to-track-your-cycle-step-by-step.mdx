## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Cycle tracking is most effective when done consistently. Regular logging ensures that the data used to calculate your averages and predict future cycles is accurate. Beyond predictions, consistent tracking serves as a continuous health record, making it easier to identify anomalies and provide concrete data to healthcare providers if issues arise.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. The Calendar Method
The simplest form of tracking. You mark the start and end of your period on a calendar. Over time, you calculate the average length between start dates. It's best for period prediction but lacks health context.

### 2. The Symptom Journal
A more detailed approach where you record daily physical and emotional changes. This is excellent for understanding how hormones affect you throughout the month, beyond just bleeding days.

### 3. The Tech App Approach
Combines calendar and symptom tracking using algorithms to provide automated insights. Apps like LunaCycle offer the convenience of digital tracking while maintaining the security of an offline journal by utilizing local storage.

## Common Tracking Errors

Avoid these common mistakes to keep your data clean:

| Mistake | Impact | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Counting spotting as Day 1 | Skews cycle length | Wait for full flow to log Day 1. |
| Inconsistent logging | Inaccurate predictions | Use daily reminders. |
| Ignoring lifestyle factors | Missing context | Note high stress, travel, or illness. |
| Switching tracking methods mid-cycle | Fragmented data | Stick to one primary tool. |
