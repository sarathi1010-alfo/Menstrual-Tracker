## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Cycle tracking is only as good as the data you put into it. Consistent tracking allows you to see the true patterns of your body, rather than relying on generalized averages. Over time, accurate data collection leads to better predictions for your next period, your fertile window, and your expected PMS symptoms.

Furthermore, having a reliable record of your menstrual history is invaluable when consulting with healthcare providers. It transforms vague descriptions ("my periods are sometimes heavy") into concrete data ("I've had 8 days of heavy flow for the last 3 cycles").

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. Calendar Method
This is the baseline technique. You track the dates of menstruation to determine the length of your cycle (from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next).

### 2. Symptom Journaling
A step up from the calendar method. This involves logging physical symptoms (cramps, breast tenderness, acne) and emotional states (mood, energy) every day of the month.

### 3. Tech App Integration
Using a privacy-first web app like LunaCycle combines the Calendar and Symptom methods, visualizing the data for you through charts and automated predictions.

## Common Tracking Errors

| Mistake | Consequence | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| **Forgetting to Log Daily** | Gaps in data lead to skewed predictions. | Set a daily alarm or track while drinking morning coffee. |
| **Counting Spotting as Day 1** | Miscalculates the start of your true cycle. | Wait until full, continuous bleeding starts to log 'Day 1'. |
| **Stopping Tracking Post-Period** | Misses crucial ovulatory and luteal data. | Continue logging mood and symptoms throughout the entire month. |
| **Using Intrusive Apps** | Risks your sensitive health data being sold. | Use local-storage apps like LunaCycle. |
