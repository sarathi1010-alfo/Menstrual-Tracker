## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Tracking your cycle is about gathering data on your own body. Without consistent data, it is impossible to identify patterns, predict future periods, or notice when something is off. By tracking regularly, you create a personal health record that can help you manage symptoms, plan for pregnancy (or avoid it), and have more informed conversations with your doctor.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. Calendar Tracking
The most basic method. You simply record the days you bleed on a calendar. Over several months, you calculate the average length of your cycle to predict future dates.

### 2. Symptom Journaling
Alongside the calendar, you write down daily symptoms like mood, energy, cervical fluid, and physical sensations. This helps map out the different phases of your cycle.

### 3. Tech Applications
Using an app like [LunaCycle](/tracker) combines calendar tracking and calculations in one simple interface, doing the math for you securely.

## Common Tracking Errors

| Error | Consequence | Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Counting spotting as Day 1** | Skews the entire cycle length calculation. | Wait for full, continuous bleeding to mark Day 1. |
| **Forgetting to log the end date** | Makes period length averages inaccurate. | Mark the calendar when bleeding completely stops. |
| **Inconsistent logging** | Prevents the app from generating accurate predictions. | Make it a habit to check the app during your period. |
| **Ignoring secondary symptoms** | Misses the larger picture of hormonal health. | Try to note major mood or physical shifts when they happen. |
