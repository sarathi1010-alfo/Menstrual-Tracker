## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Cycle tracking is only as useful as the data you put into it. Consistent tracking allows you to see the "big picture" of your reproductive health. It helps prediction algorithms establish your baseline, meaning your period and fertile window forecasts become increasingly accurate over time. Furthermore, a detailed log of your symptoms is one of the most valuable tools you can bring to a doctor's appointment if you ever experience abnormal pain or irregularities.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

While digital apps are convenient, understanding the underlying methods is empowering.

### 1. Calendar Tracking
The foundational method. You log the start date and duration. By counting the days between start dates, you find your average cycle length.

### 2. Symptom Journaling
This involves keeping a detailed daily record of your mood, energy, physical pain, and cravings to understand your body's response to hormonal shifts.

### 3. Tech-Assisted Tracking
Using a privacy-first app combines calendar tracking, symptom journaling, and prediction algorithms into one easy-to-use interface.

## Common Tracking Errors

Avoid these common mistakes to ensure your data is as useful as possible:

| Tracking Error | Consequence |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Counting spotting as Day 1** | Skews the cycle length and ovulation predictions. |
| **Forgetting to log the end date** | Makes it hard to track the average duration of bleeding. |
| **Only tracking when symptomatic** | You miss the baseline of what your "normal" days look like. |
| **Ignoring external factors** | Failing to note major stress or illness makes unexpected delays confusing. |

By committing to a few seconds of logging each day, you build a comprehensive map of your health.
