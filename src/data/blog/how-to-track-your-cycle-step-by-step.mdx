## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Cycle tracking is only as good as the data you provide. Consistent, accurate logging transforms a simple calendar into a powerful predictive tool. Whether you are trying to predict your next period, manage chronic symptoms, or identify your fertile window, consistency is the key to actionable health insights.

When you track reliably, you empower algorithms to recognize your unique patterns rather than relying on generic textbook averages.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. Calendar Method
The simplest approach. You only log the start and end dates of your menstrual bleeding to track overall cycle length and regularity.

### 2. Symptom Journaling
A more comprehensive method. Alongside bleeding, you track emotional and physical symptoms to anticipate PMS and energy shifts.

### 3. Tech-Assisted Tracking
Using privacy-first web apps like [LunaCycle](/tracker) to automate calculations, visualize data, and generate predictions without sacrificing data ownership.

## Common Tracking Errors

| Mistake | Impact | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Counting spotting as Day 1 | Skews cycle length calculations | Wait for full, steady flow to log Day 1. |
| Forgetting to log the end date | Inflates period duration | Make a habit of logging when bleeding completely stops. |
| Deleting past data | Ruins algorithmic predictions | Keep historical data; apps need history to predict the future. |
| Inconsistent symptom logging | Misses key health patterns | Try to log symptoms at the same time each day. |
