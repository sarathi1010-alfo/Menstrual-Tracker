## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Cycle tracking is only as useful as the data you put into it. Consistent tracking provides the historical data needed to generate accurate predictions for your next period and fertile window.

More importantly, it builds a personal health record. By tracking consistently, you create a baseline of what is "normal" for your body. When you know your baseline, you are immediately alerted when something changes—whether that's a sudden shift in cycle length, unusually heavy flow, or new symptoms—allowing you to seek medical advice promptly if necessary.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. The Calendar Method
The simplest form of tracking. You mark the start and end dates of your period to calculate cycle length and predict future dates based on averages.

### 2. The Symptom Journal
A more holistic approach where you log daily physical sensations, emotions, and energy levels to understand how your hormones impact your daily life.

### 3. The Tech-Assisted Approach
Using a dedicated tool like LunaCycle combines calendar math with easy symptom logging, automatically visualizing patterns over time without the need for manual calculations.

## Common Tracking Errors

| Mistake | Impact on Data | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Forgetting to log Day 1 immediately | Skews cycle length calculations | Log it as soon as bleeding starts. |
| Counting spotting as Day 1 | Incorrectly lengthens the period | Wait for full flow to mark Day 1. |
| Only tracking when trying to conceive | Misses broader health insights | Track continuously for overall health awareness. |
| Using apps that sell health data | Compromises personal privacy | Choose local-storage, no-account trackers. |
