## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Tracking your cycle is about more than just knowing when to carry tampons. Consistent tracking builds a personalized database of your bodily rhythms. This data allows for accurate period and ovulation predictions, helps you anticipate and manage symptoms like PMS or migraines, and provides invaluable information to share with your healthcare provider if you experience irregularities or are trying to conceive.

## How to Log Your Cycle with LunaCycle

Tracking is effortless with a privacy-focused tool like [LunaCycle](/). Here is how to get started:

1.  **Log Day 1:** When you experience your first day of full bleeding (not spotting), open LunaCycle and mark that date on the calendar as the start of your period.
2.  **Record Flow Intensity:** For the duration of your period, select whether your flow is light, medium, or heavy.
3.  **Add Daily Symptoms:** Make it a habit to open the app once a day. Note any symptoms you experience, such as cramps, headaches, mood changes, breast tenderness, or changes in cervical fluid.
4.  **Review Patterns:** After a few months, use LunaCycle's visualization tools to see how your cycle lengths and symptoms align over time.

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. The Calendar Method
The simplest form of tracking. You log the dates your period starts and stops. Over time, you can calculate your average cycle length. [LunaCycle](/) automates this process for you.

### 2. Symptom Journaling
Beyond dates, this involves recording physical and emotional symptoms daily. This helps correlate feelings (like extreme fatigue) with specific cycle days (like the late luteal phase).

### 3. Comprehensive Tech Apps
Using digital tools that synthesize calendar data, symptom logs, and sometimes even temperature or LH test results to provide holistic cycle insights.

## Common Tracking Errors

| Error | Consequence | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Counting spotting as Day 1 | Skews cycle length calculations. | Only count the first day of *full flow* as Day 1. |
| Forgetting to log symptoms | Misses the opportunity to identify patterns like predictable PMS. | Set a daily phone reminder to log data. |
| Giving up after one irregular cycle | Loses long-term data trends. | Keep logging! Irregularities are valuable data points. |
