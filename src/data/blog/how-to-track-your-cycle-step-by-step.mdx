## Why Consistent Tracking Matters

Cycle tracking is only as useful as the data you put into it. While noting the day your period starts is helpful, consistent and detailed tracking offers a wealth of health insights. It helps you accurately predict future periods, anticipate [hormonal changes](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained), manage PMS, and understand your unique [cycle phases](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal). Consistent tracking is the foundation of menstrual health literacy, empowering you with knowledge about what is [normal for your body](/blog/understanding-your-period-normal-vs-not-normal).

## Top 3 Tracking Techniques

### 1. The Calendar Method
The simplest approach. You just log the start and end dates of your period. This is great for basic prediction and tracking cycle length averages.

### 2. The Symptom Journal
In addition to dates, you actively record daily symptoms like headaches, acne, mood changes, and cravings. This helps you anticipate PMS and understand how your cycle affects your daily life.

### 3. The Tech-Assisted Approach
Using an app like LunaCycle combines the calendar and symptom journal, using algorithms to predict your next period and fertile window based on your historical data.

## Common Tracking Errors

Avoid these pitfalls to ensure your data is as accurate as possible.

| Error | Impact on Tracking | How to Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Forgetting to log Day 1 | Throws off cycle length calculations | Log the day you start bleeding immediately. |
| Counting light spotting as a period | Skews cycle phase estimations | Wait for continuous flow to mark Day 1. |
| Stopping tracking after a few months | Decreases prediction accuracy | Make tracking a quick, daily habit. |
| Ignoring irregular cycles | Misses potential health warning signs | If your cycle is consistently >35 days, see a doctor. |
