## How Does Ovulation Work?

During the first half of your cycle, Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH) causes several follicles in the ovary to mature. Usually, only one becomes fully dominant. A sudden surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) causes this dominant follicle to rupture, releasing the mature egg into the fallopian tube, where it awaits potential fertilization.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 30-day cycle, she will likely ovulate around Day 16 (since the luteal phase post-ovulation is almost always 14 days). Around Day 14 or 15, she might notice her cervical mucus becoming clear and stretchy, signaling impending ovulation.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the cornerstone of fertility. Without it, pregnancy cannot occur. Furthermore, regular ovulation is a sign of hormonal health, as the corpus luteum (the structure left behind after ovulation) produces progesterone, which is essential for mood stability and bone health.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Release of an egg from the ovary. | Shedding of the uterine lining. |
| **Timing** | Mid-cycle (approx. 14 days before next period). | Beginning of the cycle (Days 1-5). |
| **Physical Sign** | Clear, stretchy mucus, mild pelvic twinge. | Vaginal bleeding, cramping. |
