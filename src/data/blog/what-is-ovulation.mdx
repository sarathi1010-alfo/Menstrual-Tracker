## What is Ovulation?

Ovulation is the specific event during the menstrual cycle when a mature egg is released from one of the ovaries. It is the defining moment of the cycle's fertile window. Once released, the egg travels down the fallopian tube where it can potentially be fertilized by sperm.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

During the first half of your cycle (the follicular phase), hormones stimulate the growth of several follicles in the ovaries. Usually, only one follicle becomes fully mature. A surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) triggers this dominant follicle to rupture and release its egg—this is ovulation.

## Real-World Example

If you have a consistent 28-day cycle, ovulation will likely occur around Day 14. If you have a 32-day cycle, ovulation will likely occur around Day 18 (since the phase *after* ovulation is usually a consistent 14 days).

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is essential for reproduction. Without it, pregnancy cannot occur naturally. Furthermore, regular ovulation is a sign of hormonal balance and overall health, as it confirms your body is producing the necessary sequence of hormones.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Releasing an egg | Shedding the uterine lining |
| **Timing** | Middle of the cycle | Beginning of the cycle (Day 1) |
| **Key Hormone** | Luteinizing Hormone (LH) surge | Drop in estrogen and progesterone |
