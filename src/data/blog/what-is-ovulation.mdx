# What is Ovulation?

Ovulation is the process where a mature egg is released from the ovarian follicle into the fallopian tube. It usually occurs once per menstrual cycle and is the only time during the cycle when fertilization, and therefore pregnancy, can occur. The egg survives for 12 to 24 hours.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Triggered by a surge in luteinizing hormone (LH), the ovary releases an egg. The egg travels down the fallopian tube toward the uterus. If sperm is present, fertilization may happen. If not, the egg dissolves, leading to menstruation. Read more about the full [menstrual cycle phases](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal).

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 28-day cycle, she will likely ovulate around Day 14. In the days leading up to Day 14, she might notice an increase in clear, stretchy cervical mucus and a slight rise in basal body temperature shortly after ovulation occurs.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the cornerstone of fertility. Knowing when it happens helps those trying to conceive and those practicing fertility awareness to prevent pregnancy. It is also an indicator of overall hormonal health.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What happens | Egg is released | Uterine lining is shed |
| Timing | Mid-cycle | Beginning of cycle |
| Duration | 12-24 hours | 2-7 days |
