## How Does Ovulation Work?

Ovulation is the central event of the menstrual cycle. It is triggered by a sudden spike in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), which is produced by the pituitary gland. This LH surge causes a mature follicle in the ovary to rupture and release an egg into the fallopian tube.

Once released, the egg survives for only 12 to 24 hours. For pregnancy to occur, it must be fertilized by sperm within this short window. However, because sperm can survive in the reproductive tract for up to five days, the actual "fertile window" is longer than just the day of ovulation.

## Real-World Example in Fertility Tracking

If you are trying to conceive (or trying to avoid pregnancy using natural methods), pinpointing ovulation is crucial. Women often track signs of approaching ovulation, such as changes in cervical mucus (which becomes clear and slippery, like raw egg whites) or a slight dip followed by a sustained rise in Basal Body Temperature (BBT). Tracking these signs alongside your cycle dates in [LunaCycle](/) can help you estimate when ovulation is likely to occur.

## Ovulation vs. Fertile Window

| Term | Definition | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ovulation** | The actual release of the egg from the ovary. | 12-24 hours |
| **Fertile Window** | The days during the menstrual cycle when pregnancy is possible. | About 6 days (5 days leading up to ovulation + ovulation day) |
