## What is Ovulation?

Ovulation is the specific phase in the menstrual cycle when a mature egg is released from the ovarian follicle into the fallopian tube. It represents the peak of fertility and is the only time during the cycle when conception can occur.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Ovulation is driven by a complex hormonal feedback loop:
1. **Preparation:** During the follicular phase, FSH stimulates follicles to mature.
2. **The Surge:** Rising estrogen levels trigger a sudden spike in Luteinizing Hormone (LH).
3. **The Release:** This LH surge causes the dominant follicle to rupture and release the mature egg.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 28-day cycle, ovulation usually occurs around Day 14. She might notice clear, stretchy cervical mucus (like egg whites) leading up to this day, and perhaps a slight twinge of pain on one side of her lower abdomen (mittelschmerz).

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is essential for reproduction; without it, pregnancy is impossible. Furthermore, regular ovulation is a sign of a healthy endocrine system. Even if you aren't trying to conceive, the hormones produced after ovulation (like progesterone) are important for bone, heart, and mood health.

## Ovulation vs Fertile Window

| Feature | Ovulation | Fertile Window |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The release of the egg. | The days when intercourse can lead to pregnancy. |
| **Duration** | 12-24 hours. | Up to 6 days (5 days before ovulation + day of). |
| **Focus** | The egg's lifespan. | The sperm's lifespan combined with the egg's. |
