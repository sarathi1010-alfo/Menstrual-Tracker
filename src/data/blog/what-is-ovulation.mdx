Ovulation is the process in which a mature egg is released from one of the ovaries. Triggered by a surge in luteinizing hormone (LH), the egg travels down the fallopian tube where it can be fertilized by sperm. It is the central event of the menstrual cycle.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

During the first half of your cycle, follicles in the ovary mature. Usually, only one follicle reaches full maturity. A sudden spike in LH causes this follicle to rupture and release its egg. If the egg is not fertilized within 12 to 24 hours, it dissolves, and the body prepares to shed the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 30-day cycle, ovulation likely occurs around Day 16 (since the luteal phase after ovulation is consistently about 14 days). In the days leading up to Day 16, you might notice clear, stretchy cervical mucus indicating high fertility.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the only time during the menstrual cycle when a woman can conceive. Even if you are not trying to get pregnant, regular ovulation is a sign of healthy hormone production, specifically estrogen and progesterone, which support bone, heart, and metabolic health.

## Ovulation vs Fertile Window

| Feature | Ovulation | Fertile Window |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The release of the egg. | The days pregnancy is possible. |
| **Duration** | 12-24 hours. | About 6 days. |
| **Timing** | Typically midway through the cycle. | The 5 days leading up to and the day of ovulation. |
