Definition: Ovulation is the specific event in the menstrual cycle when a mature egg is released from the ovarian follicle into the fallopian tube, making it available for fertilization.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Triggered by a sharp spike in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), the dominant ovarian follicle ruptures, releasing the egg. The egg then travels down the fallopian tube toward the uterus. If sperm is present, fertilization can occur. If not, the egg dissolves within 24 hours.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, ovulation usually happens around Day 14. However, if a cycle is 35 days long, ovulation is more likely to occur around Day 21, as the luteal phase (following ovulation) is almost always 14 days long.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the only time during the menstrual cycle when pregnancy can occur. Even if you are not trying to conceive, regular ovulation is a key indicator of estrogen and progesterone balance, supporting bone, heart, and brain health.

## Ovulation vs Fertile Window

| Feature | Ovulation | Fertile Window |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 12-24 hours | Up to 6 days |
| Biological Event | Release of the egg | Lifespan of sperm + lifespan of egg |
| Focus | The egg's availability | The time frame pregnancy is possible |
