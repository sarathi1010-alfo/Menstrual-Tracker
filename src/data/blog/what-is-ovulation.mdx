Ovulation is the phase of the menstrual cycle where a mature ovarian follicle ruptures and discharges an ovum (also known as an egg). It typically occurs around the middle of the menstrual cycle and marks the peak of the fertile window.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Triggered by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), the ovary releases a mature egg into the fallopian tube. It waits there for 12 to 24 hours. If sperm is present, fertilization can occur. If not, the egg dissolves, and the cycle moves into the luteal phase.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, if Day 1 is the start of your period, ovulation will generally occur around Day 14. However, because cycle lengths vary, a person with a 32-day cycle will likely ovulate around Day 18.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the defining event of fertility. Without it, pregnancy is impossible. Furthermore, consistent ovulation is a primary indicator of a healthy endocrine (hormone) system.

## Ovulation vs The Fertile Window

| Feature | Ovulation | Fertile Window |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | 12-24 hours | Up to 6 days |
| **What it is** | The release of the egg | The days when intercourse can lead to pregnancy |
| **Key Factor** | The egg's lifespan | Sperm's ability to survive in the body (up to 5 days) + the egg's lifespan |
