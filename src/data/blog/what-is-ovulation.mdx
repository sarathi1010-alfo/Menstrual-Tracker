## What is Ovulation?

Ovulation is the phase of the menstrual cycle when an ovary releases a mature egg into the fallopian tube, making it available for fertilization. It typically occurs once a month, halfway through the menstrual cycle, and is the defining event that makes pregnancy possible.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

As the follicular phase progresses, rising estrogen levels cause the pituitary gland to release a surge of Luteinizing Hormone (LH). This LH surge triggers the dominant follicle in the ovary to rupture and release its egg. The egg then travels down the fallopian tube toward the uterus.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle, ovulation usually happens around Day 14. However, if your cycle is 32 days long, ovulation might occur closer to Day 18. This is why tracking your specific cycle, rather than relying on averages, is crucial.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is essential for reproduction. Without it, pregnancy cannot occur naturally. Furthermore, regular ovulation is a strong indicator of healthy endocrine (hormonal) function. Anovulation (lack of ovulation) can be a sign of conditions like PCOS or extreme stress.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Release of an egg | Shedding of the uterine lining |
| **Timing** | Middle of the cycle | Beginning of the cycle (Day 1) |
| **Hormone** | Driven by LH surge | Triggered by a drop in Progesterone |
