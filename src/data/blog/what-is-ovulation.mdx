Ovulation is the process in a female's menstrual cycle when a mature ovarian follicle ruptures and discharges an ovum (also known as an egg). This event typically occurs midway through the menstrual cycle and is essential for reproduction.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Ovulation is triggered by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH). Once the egg is released from the ovary, it travels down the fallopian tube, where it may meet sperm and become fertilized. If fertilization does not occur within 12-24 hours, the egg disintegrates.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, estrogen levels rise during the first two weeks, causing the uterine lining to thicken. Around Day 13, an LH surge happens, prompting the ovary to release an egg on Day 14. This is your day of ovulation.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the definitive sign of fertility in a given cycle. Without it, pregnancy cannot naturally occur. Furthermore, regular ovulation is a sign of balanced hormones and good overall health. You can monitor signs of ovulation using [LunaCycle](/).

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Action | Releasing an egg | Shedding the uterine lining |
| Timing | Mid-cycle | Start of the cycle |
| Hormonal Driver | LH Surge | Drop in estrogen and progesterone |
