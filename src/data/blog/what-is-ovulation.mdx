Ovulation is the release of a mature egg from the ovary, making fertilization possible. It typically occurs around the midpoint of the menstrual cycle and lasts 12 to 24 hours.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Following the [follicular phase](/what-is-follicular-phase), rising estrogen levels trigger a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH). This LH surge causes the dominant follicle in the ovary to rupture, releasing the egg into the fallopian tube where it awaits fertilization.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a regular 28-day [menstrual cycle](/what-is-menstrual-cycle), she will likely ovulate around Day 14. In the days leading up to Day 14, she might notice an increase in clear, slippery cervical mucus (often compared to raw egg whites), which helps sperm travel to the egg.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the defining event for fertility. Without it, pregnancy cannot occur naturally. Furthermore, regular ovulation is a sign of hormonal balance, as it triggers the production of progesterone during the subsequent [luteal phase](/what-is-luteal-phase).

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| What happens? | An egg is released from the ovary. | The uterine lining sheds. |
| Timing | Roughly midway through the cycle. | The beginning of a new cycle (Days 1-5). |
| Primary Hormones | Peaking Estrogen, LH surge. | Low Estrogen and Progesterone. |
