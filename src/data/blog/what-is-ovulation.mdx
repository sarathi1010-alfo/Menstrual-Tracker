Ovulation is the phase of the menstrual cycle when a mature egg is released from the ovary, making pregnancy possible. It is the defining event of the cycle and the center of the fertile window.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

During the [follicular phase](/what-is-follicular-phase), rising estrogen levels thicken the uterine lining. Once estrogen reaches a peak threshold, it triggers a sudden surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH). This LH surge is the catalyst that causes the most mature follicle in the ovary to rupture, releasing the egg into the fallopian tube, where it waits to be fertilized by sperm.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 30-day cycle, you likely do not ovulate on Day 14. Because the [luteal phase](/what-is-luteal-phase) (the phase after ovulation) is usually a consistent 14 days, you would likely ovulate around Day 16 (30 - 14 = 16). The egg released on Day 16 will only survive until Day 17. If unfertilized, it dissolves.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is essential for reproduction; without it, pregnancy cannot occur. Furthermore, regular ovulation is a sign of healthy [hormonal balance](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained). Tracking ovulation using an app like [LunaCycle](/) is critical for both trying to conceive and avoiding pregnancy using natural family planning methods.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation (Period) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The release of an egg from the ovary. | The shedding of the unneeded uterine lining. |
| **Timing** | Occurs roughly mid-cycle (14 days *before* the next period). | Marks Day 1 of the cycle. |
| **Primary Hormone** | Driven by a spike in Luteinizing Hormone (LH). | Triggered by a drop in Estrogen and Progesterone. |
| **Physical Sign** | Often silent, or marked by clear, stretchy cervical mucus. | Visible bleeding for 2-7 days. |
