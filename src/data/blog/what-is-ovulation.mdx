Ovulation is the pivotal event of the menstrual cycle. It is the physiological process during which a mature ovarian follicle ruptures and discharges a single mature egg (ovum). Once released, the egg travels down the fallopian tube toward the uterus, where it becomes available for fertilization by sperm.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

The process is meticulously controlled by a hormonal cascade:

1.  **Follicle Maturation:** During the first half of the cycle, Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH) prompts several follicles in the ovaries to develop.
2.  **Estrogen Rise:** As the follicles grow, they produce estrogen. The dominant follicle emerges and continues to mature.
3.  **The LH Surge:** High estrogen levels eventually trigger a sudden, massive spike in Luteinizing Hormone (LH).
4.  **Release:** This LH surge acts as the catalyst, causing the dominant follicle to burst and release the egg approximately 24 to 36 hours later.

After ovulation, the egg is viable for a remarkably short window—only 12 to 24 hours. However, because sperm can survive in the female reproductive tract for up to five days under optimal conditions, the "fertile window" is longer than the lifespan of the egg itself.

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

Tracking ovulation is critical for anyone trying to conceive (or trying to avoid it). By using [LunaCycle](/), you can input your period dates to get an algorithmic estimation of your ovulation day. Many users supplement this by tracking secondary symptoms, such as an increase in clear, stretchy cervical mucus (often compared to raw egg whites), which indicates that ovulation is imminent.

## Ovulation vs. The Fertile Window

| Feature | Ovulation | Fertile Window |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The exact moment/day the egg is released. | The sequence of days leading up to and including ovulation. |
| **Duration** | 12 to 24 hours (lifespan of the egg). | Roughly 6 days (5 days before + ovulation day). |
| **Hormonal Driver** | LH Surge. | Rising estrogen levels (preparing cervical mucus). |
