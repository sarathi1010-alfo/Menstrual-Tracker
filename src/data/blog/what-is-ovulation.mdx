Ovulation is the specific event in the menstrual cycle when a mature egg is released from one of the ovaries. Once released, the egg travels down the fallopian tube, where it can be fertilized by sperm for a window of about 12 to 24 hours.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

A surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) triggers the most dominant follicle in the ovary to rupture and release its egg. This surge usually happens 24 to 36 hours before the egg is actually released. After release, the empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum and starts producing progesterone.

## Real-World Example of Ovulation

In a standard 28-day cycle, ovulation typically occurs around Day 14. You might notice physical signs like clearer, stretchy cervical mucus (similar to raw egg whites) or a slight twinge of pain in your lower abdomen (known as Mittelschmerz).

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Without ovulation, a natural pregnancy cannot occur. Beyond fertility, ovulation is the only way the body produces significant amounts of progesterone, which is essential for bone, breast, and brain health.
