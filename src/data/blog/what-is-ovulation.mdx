## What is Ovulation?

Ovulation is the process in which a mature egg is released from one of the ovaries. It is a critical event in the menstrual cycle, marking the time when a woman is most fertile and conception is possible.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Triggered by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), the ovary releases an egg into the fallopian tube. The egg then travels toward the uterus. If sperm is present, fertilization can occur. The egg only survives for 12-24 hours after release.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 30-day cycle, ovulation might occur around day 16 (since the luteal phase is usually 14 days long). You might notice clear, stretchy cervical mucus and a slight twinge in your lower abdomen a day or two before.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Without ovulation, natural conception is impossible. Furthermore, ovulation triggers the production of progesterone, a hormone crucial for maintaining the uterine lining and supporting early pregnancy, as well as balancing the effects of estrogen in the body.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Middle of the cycle | Beginning of the cycle |
| Primary Event | Releasing an egg | Shedding the uterine lining |
| Fertility | Peak fertility | Lowest fertility |
