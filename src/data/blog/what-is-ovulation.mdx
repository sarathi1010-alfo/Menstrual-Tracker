Ovulation is the process in the menstrual cycle where a mature egg is released from the ovarian follicle. This occurs when luteinizing hormone (LH) surges, typically around the midpoint of a woman's cycle.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

During the [follicular phase](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal), follicles grow in the ovaries. Only one typically matures fully. A surge in LH triggers this dominant follicle to rupture, releasing the egg into the fallopian tube where it awaits fertilization.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a regular 30-day cycle, ovulation will likely occur around day 16 (14 days before her next period is due). She might notice clear, stretchy cervical mucus around days 14-16.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the single most critical event for fertility. Without it, pregnancy cannot occur. Tracking it with [LunaCycle](/) helps identify the fertile window.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Release of an egg. | Shedding of the uterine lining. |
| **Timing** | Mid-cycle. | Start of the cycle. |
| **Sensation** | Sometimes mild pelvic twinges (mittelschmerz). | Often accompanied by cramping and bleeding. |
