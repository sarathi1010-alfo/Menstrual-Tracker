## What is Ovulation?

Ovulation is the release of a mature egg from one of the ovaries. It is a critical event in the menstrual cycle, marking the time when a woman is most fertile and can conceive. Once released, the egg travels down the fallopian tube, where it can be fertilized by sperm.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

During the follicular phase, rising estrogen levels cause the uterine lining to thicken. This high level of estrogen eventually triggers a rapid surge in luteinizing hormone (LH). The LH surge causes the dominant follicle to rupture and release the egg—this is ovulation.

## Real-World Example

If you have a standard 28-day cycle, you will likely ovulate around Day 14. You might notice signs like clear, stretchy cervical mucus (similar to raw egg whites) and a slight twinge of pain on one side of your lower abdomen (mittelschmerz).

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the prerequisite for natural conception. Understanding when you ovulate helps you identify your fertile window, whether you are trying to conceive or avoid pregnancy. Tracking your cycles with [LunaCycle](/tracker) can help you estimate your ovulation day.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Event | Release of an egg | Shedding of the uterine lining |
| Timing | Mid-cycle | Beginning of the cycle |
| Fertility | High | Low |
