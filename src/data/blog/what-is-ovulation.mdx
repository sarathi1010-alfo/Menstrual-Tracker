Ovulation is the release of a mature egg from the ovary. It typically occurs once a month and is the crucial event required for conception to take place.

## How Does Ovulation Work?
As the follicular phase progresses, rising estrogen levels trigger a sudden surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH). This LH surge causes the dominant follicle in the ovary to rupture and release a mature egg. The egg then travels down the fallopian tube, where it survives for 12-24 hours.

## Real-World Example
In a typical 28-day cycle, ovulation usually happens around Day 14. If you have a 32-day cycle, ovulation likely occurs around Day 18 (since the luteal phase post-ovulation is generally a consistent 14 days).

## Why is Ovulation Important?
Ovulation is the defining event of fertility. Without it, pregnancy cannot occur. Furthermore, healthy ovulation triggers the production of progesterone during the luteal phase, which is essential for overall hormonal balance, bone health, and heart health.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Releasing an egg | Shedding uterine lining |
| **Timing** | Mid-cycle (approx. Day 14) | Start of cycle (Days 1-5) |
| **Physical Sign** | Cervical mucus changes, slight temp rise | Bleeding, cramping |
