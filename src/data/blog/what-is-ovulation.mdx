Ovulation is a central event in the menstrual cycle, characterized by the release of a mature egg (ovum) from one of the ovaries. This process represents the peak of fertility and is essential for conception.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

During the follicular phase, a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) is triggered by rising estrogen levels. This LH surge causes the dominant follicle in the ovary to rupture and release the mature egg into the fallopian tube. The egg then travels down the tube towards the uterus, available for fertilization by sperm for a brief window of 12 to 24 hours.

## Real-World Example of Identifying Ovulation

Many individuals track physical signs to identify ovulation. For example, you might notice that your cervical mucus becomes clear, stretchy, and resembles raw egg whites just before you ovulate. ## Ovulation vs. Fertile Window (Table)

| Feature | Ovulation | Fertile Window |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The release of the egg. | The days leading up to and including ovulation. |
| **Duration** | 12–24 hours (lifespan of the egg). | Around 6 days (lifespan of sperm + lifespan of egg). |
| **Significance** | The moment fertilization becomes possible. | The broader timeframe when intercourse can lead to pregnancy. |
