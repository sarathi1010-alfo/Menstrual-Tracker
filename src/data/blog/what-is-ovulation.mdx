## How Does Ovulation Work?
Ovulation is the main event of the menstrual cycle, essential for reproduction. During the first half of the cycle, a hormone called FSH stimulates follicles in the ovary to grow. As they grow, they produce estrogen. When estrogen levels reach a certain threshold, it triggers a massive surge in luteinizing hormone (LH). This LH surge is the signal that causes the most mature follicle to rupture and release its egg into the fallopian tube. This entire process is incredibly precise and is the foundation of human fertility. Once released, the egg survives for only about 12 to 24 hours.

## Real-World Example
If you have a very regular 28-day cycle, ovulation will most likely occur around Day 14. However, if your cycle is 32 days long, ovulation is more likely to happen around Day 18, because the luteal phase (the time from ovulation to your next period) is typically a consistent 14 days. This is why tracking your specific cycle length is so important rather than relying on generic averages.

## Why is Ovulation Important?
Ovulation is the only time during the menstrual cycle when a woman can get pregnant. Whether you are trying to conceive or trying to avoid pregnancy, knowing if and when you ovulate is critical information. Furthermore, regular ovulation is a sign of good hormonal health, as the process requires a delicate balance of multiple endocrine signals.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation
| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
|---|---|---|
| Action | Release of egg | Shedding of uterine lining |
| Timing | Mid-cycle | Beginning of cycle (Days 1-5) |
| Symptoms | Mild pelvic twinge, clear stretchy mucus | Cramping, bleeding |
