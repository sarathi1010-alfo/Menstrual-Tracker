**Definition:** Ovulation is the process in a female's menstrual cycle where a mature ovarian follicle ruptures and discharges an ovum (also known as an egg). This is the only time during the cycle when conception can occur.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Triggered by a spike in estrogen followed by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), the ovary releases an egg into the fallopian tube. Once released, the egg survives for about 12 to 24 hours.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a textbook 28-day cycle, ovulation typically occurs around Day 14. She might notice clear, stretchy cervical mucus (like egg whites) and a slight increase in her basal body temperature around this time.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is essential for fertility. Without it, pregnancy cannot occur. Tracking it is vital for both trying to conceive and natural family planning.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
|---|---|---|
| **Action** | Releasing an egg. | Shedding the uterine lining. |
| **Hormone** | Driven by LH surge. | Driven by drops in estrogen/progesterone. |
| **Symptom** | Fertile mucus, mild cramping. | Bleeding, typical period cramps. |
