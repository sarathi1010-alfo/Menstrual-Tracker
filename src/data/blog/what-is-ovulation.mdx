Ovulation is the process where a mature ovarian follicle ruptures and discharges an ovum (egg) into the fallopian tube to be fertilized. It is the defining event of the menstrual cycle and the prerequisite for pregnancy.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

During the follicular phase, estrogen levels rise. When estrogen reaches a threshold, it triggers a rapid surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH). This LH surge causes the dominant follicle to burst, releasing the egg. The egg travels down the fallopian tube, where it waits to be fertilized by sperm.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle, ovulation likely occurs around Day 14. You might notice stretchy, egg-white cervical mucus around Day 12-14, signaling that ovulation is approaching. If you track your BBT, you will see a slight temperature shift the day *after* ovulation.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Without ovulation, natural pregnancy is impossible. Furthermore, regular ovulation is a sign of good hormonal health. Anovulatory cycles (cycles without ovulation) can lead to irregular periods and indicate conditions like PCOS.

## Ovulation vs Fertile Window

| Concept | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Ovulation** | The 12-24 hour period when the egg is released and alive. |
| **Fertile Window** | The ~6-day window leading up to and including ovulation when intercourse can result in pregnancy (sperm can live up to 5 days). |
