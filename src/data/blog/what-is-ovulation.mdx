Ovulation is the phase of the menstrual cycle where a mature egg is released from one of the ovaries. It is a critical event for reproduction; pregnancy can only occur if sperm fertilizes the egg during this brief window.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

During the follicular phase, a spike in estrogen triggers a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH). This LH surge causes the dominant follicle in the ovary to rupture and release the mature egg into the fallopian tube.

Once released, the egg travels down the fallopian tube toward the uterus. It survives for approximately 12 to 24 hours. If it is not fertilized by sperm within this timeframe, the egg dissolves, and hormone levels eventually drop, leading to menstruation.

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

In a standard 28-day cycle, ovulation usually occurs around Day 14. However, if your cycle is 32 days long, ovulation is more likely to occur around Day 18 (32 - 14 = 18). Tracking apps like [LunaCycle](/) use this standard calendar method calculation—subtracting 14 days from your next predicted period—to estimate your ovulation day.

## Ovulation vs. Fertile Window

| Term | Definition | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ovulation** | The specific release of the egg. | 12 - 24 hours |
| **Fertile Window** | The days leading up to and including ovulation when pregnancy is possible. | ~6 days |
