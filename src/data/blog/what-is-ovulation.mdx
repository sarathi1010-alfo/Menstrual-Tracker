Ovulation is the process where a mature egg is released from the ovarian follicle into the fallopian tube, making it available for fertilization by sperm. It is the defining event of the menstrual cycle regarding fertility.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Triggered by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), the ovary releases a mature egg. This egg travels down the fallopian tube toward the uterus. The egg only survives for 12 to 24 hours after release. If sperm is present, fertilization can occur. If not, the egg dissolves, and the body prepares for menstruation.

## Real-World Example

In a standard 28-day cycle, ovulation usually occurs around Day 14. A person might notice symptoms like a slight twinge of pelvic pain (mittelschmerz), an increase in clear, stretchy cervical mucus (resembling raw egg whites), and a slight increase in basal body temperature the following morning.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is essential for natural conception. Understanding when you ovulate helps define your fertile window. Furthermore, regular ovulation is a sign of a healthy, functioning endocrine (hormonal) system.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
|---|---|---|
| Action | Release of an egg | Shedding of uterine lining |
| Timing | Mid-cycle (approx.) | Start of cycle (Day 1) |
| Fertility | High fertility | Low fertility (usually) |
