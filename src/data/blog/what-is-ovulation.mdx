## Definition

Ovulation is the phase of the menstrual cycle where a mature egg is released from the ovarian follicle into the fallopian tube. It usually occurs once a month, approximately two weeks before the next period starts, and represents the peak of fertility.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Triggered by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), the ovary releases an egg. The egg then travels down the fallopian tube where it can be fertilized by sperm. If fertilization doesn't occur within 12 to 24 hours, the egg dissolves. To understand where this fits in the bigger picture, see [Cycle Phases Explained](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal).

## Real-World Example

If you have a 30-day cycle, ovulation likely happens around day 16 (30 minus 14 days). You might notice clear, egg-white-like discharge on days 14, 15, and 16. Tracking these signs in [LunaCycle](/) helps confirm when it happens for you.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Without ovulation, natural pregnancy is impossible. It is the defining event of the fertile window and a key indicator of functioning reproductive health.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Egg is released. | Uterine lining is shed. |
| **Timing** | Mid-cycle (approx. 14 days before period). | Start of the cycle (Days 1-5). |
| **Significance** | Peak fertility. | Start of a new cycle. |
