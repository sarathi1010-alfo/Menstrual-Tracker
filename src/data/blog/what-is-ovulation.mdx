# What is Ovulation?

Ovulation is a specific event within the menstrual cycle where a mature egg (ovum) is released from the ovarian follicle. After it is released, the egg travels down the fallopian tube, where it may be fertilized by a sperm.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Ovulation is triggered by a spike in luteinizing hormone (LH), which causes the most mature follicle in the ovary to rupture and release its egg. This usually happens about 24 to 36 hours after the LH surge. Once released, the egg is viable and can be fertilized for about 12 to 24 hours. If it is not fertilized, it disintegrates and is shed along with the uterine lining during menstruation.

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

Tracking ovulation is crucial for anyone trying to conceive or trying to avoid pregnancy. By logging data like basal body temperature or using ovulation predictor kits (OPKs), you can identify when ovulation is likely to occur. ## Ovulation vs Fertile Window

| Feature | Ovulation | Fertile Window |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The specific release of the egg. | The days you are most likely to get pregnant. |
| **Duration** | 12-24 hours. | Typically 6 days (5 days before ovulation + day of ovulation). |
| **Why it matters** | The precise moment an egg is available. | The broader timeframe when intercourse can lead to conception. |
