## What is Ovulation?

Ovulation is the phase in the menstrual cycle when a mature egg is released from the ovarian follicle into the fallopian tube, making it available for fertilization by sperm. It is the defining event of fertility.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Triggered by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), the ovary releases an egg. The egg then travels down the fallopian tube towards the uterus. The egg only survives for 12 to 24 hours. If sperm is present in the reproductive tract during this time, fertilization can occur. If not, the egg dissolves, and the body eventually sheds the uterine lining during menstruation.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle, ovulation might occur around day 14. However, you might notice signs of approaching ovulation, such as slippery, egg-white cervical mucus, starting around day 11 or 12.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the cornerstone of fertility. Without it, natural conception is not possible. Even if you are not trying to conceive, knowing if and when you ovulate is a key indicator of hormonal balance and overall reproductive health.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Action | Release of an egg. | Shedding of the uterine lining. |
| Hormonal Driver | LH surge. | Drop in estrogen and progesterone. |
| Fertility | Peak fertility. | Lowest fertility. |
