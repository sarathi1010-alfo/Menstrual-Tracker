# What is Ovulation?

Ovulation is the process in a menstrual cycle where a mature ovarian follicle ruptures and discharges an ovum (also known as an egg). It represents the peak of fertility, as this is the only time the egg is available to be fertilized by sperm.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Triggered by a spike in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), the ovary releases a mature egg into the fallopian tube. The egg then travels toward the uterus. If sperm is present, fertilization can occur. If not, the egg dissolves within 12 to 24 hours.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 30-day cycle, her luteal phase is typically 14 days. This means she will likely ovulate around Day 16 of her cycle. During days 14-16, she might notice an increase in stretchy, clear cervical mucus (like raw egg whites), indicating her body is preparing for ovulation.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the absolute prerequisite for natural pregnancy. Even if you are not trying to conceive, consistent ovulation is a primary indicator of healthy hormonal function and endocrine health.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Releasing an egg | Shedding the uterine lining |
| **Timing** | Mid-cycle | Beginning of the cycle (Day 1) |
| **Hormone Driver** | LH surge | Drop in estrogen and progesterone |
