Ovulation is the phase of the menstrual cycle when a mature egg is released from the ovarian follicle into the fallopian tube, making fertilization possible.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

Triggered by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH), the ovary releases a mature egg. This egg travels down the fallopian tube toward the uterus. If sperm is present, fertilization can occur. If not, the egg dissolves, and the uterine lining will eventually shed. To see how this fits into the bigger picture, read our [cycle phases explained](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal) guide.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 28-day cycle, her estrogen levels will peak around day 12 or 13, triggering an LH surge. About 24-36 hours later (around day 14), ovulation occurs. She might notice clear, stretchy cervical mucus resembling raw egg whites during this time.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is essential for conception. Identifying when ovulation occurs helps individuals either plan for or avoid pregnancy. It is also a sign of healthy endocrine function. Tracking basal body temperature in the [LunaCycle](/) app can help confirm that ovulation has happened.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Action | Release of an egg. | Shedding of the uterine lining. |
| Timing | Mid-cycle. | Beginning of the cycle. |
| Primary Hormone | LH surge. | Drop in progesterone and estrogen. |

### Core Principles
*   Ovulation is a singular event, not a prolonged phase.
*   It is required for natural conception.
*   Physical signs can indicate its approach.
*   Tracking helps pinpoint its occurrence.
