## Definition

Ovulation is a biological process in the menstrual cycle where a mature ovarian follicle ruptures and discharges an ovum (also known as an egg). This event makes the egg available for fertilization by sperm. It typically occurs once every menstrual cycle, usually around 14 days before the next period begins.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

As the follicular phase progresses, rising levels of estrogen signal the pituitary gland to release a surge of Luteinizing Hormone (LH). This "LH surge" is the trigger that causes the dominant follicle in the ovary to break open and release its mature egg. The egg then travels down the fallopian tube toward the uterus.

## Real-World Example

If you have a perfectly regular 28-day cycle, ovulation will likely occur right in the middle, on day 14. You might notice your cervical mucus becoming clear, slippery, and stretchy (like raw egg whites) a day or two before this happens, indicating you are highly fertile.

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Without ovulation, natural pregnancy is impossible. It is the absolute core requirement for conception. Even if you are not trying to conceive, healthy ovulation is crucial because it triggers the production of progesterone during the luteal phase, a hormone vital for mood regulation, bone health, and maintaining a healthy uterine lining.

## Ovulation vs Fertile Window

| Feature | Ovulation | Fertile Window |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **What is it?** | The exact moment/day the egg is released. | The 5-6 day period when conception is possible. |
| **Lifespan** | The egg lives for 12-24 hours. | Sperm can live up to 5 days, waiting for the egg. |
| **Timing** | ~14 days before next period. | Includes ovulation day and the 5 days prior. |
