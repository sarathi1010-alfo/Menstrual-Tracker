Ovulation is the phase of the menstrual cycle when an ovary releases a mature egg. This process is triggered by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) and is the most fertile time of the month.

## How Does Ovulation Work?

During the first half of the cycle, estrogen levels rise, causing the uterine lining to thicken. This estrogen surge also prompts a sudden increase in LH, known as the "LH surge." This surge causes the most mature follicle in the ovary to rupture and release its egg into the fallopian tube, where it waits to be fertilized by sperm.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 30-day cycle, ovulation usually doesn't occur on Day 14. Because the luteal phase (post-ovulation) is almost always 14 days long, ovulation in a 30-day cycle likely happens around Day 16 (30 - 14 = 16).

## Why is Ovulation Important?

Ovulation is the central event of the menstrual cycle. Without ovulation, pregnancy is impossible. Furthermore, the act of ovulating triggers the production of progesterone, a hormone crucial for maintaining early pregnancy and balancing the effects of estrogen in the body.

## Ovulation vs Menstruation

| Feature | Ovulation | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Action** | Release of an egg | Shedding of the uterine lining |
| **Hormone peak** | Luteinizing Hormone (LH) | Hormones are at their lowest |
