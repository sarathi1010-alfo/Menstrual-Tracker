For too long, fitness advice has been based on a 24-hour hormonal model. But if you have a menstrual cycle, your body operates on a different timeline. Syncing your workouts with your phases can help you see better results while avoiding burnout.

## Why Exercise Syncing Matters for Performance

Training according to your cycle isn't about being "weak" during certain times; it's about being smart. By pushing hard when your hormones support it and recovering when they don't, you reduce the risk of injury and support your long-term metabolic health.

## How to Sync Your Workouts (Step-by-Step)

1.  **Check Your Phase:** Open LunaCycle to see if you're in the first or second half of your cycle.
2.  **Plan Your Intensity:** Reserve HIIT and heavy lifting for your follicular and ovulatory phases.
3.  **Prioritize Recovery:** As you enter your luteal phase, increase your focus on stretching, mobility, and sleep.
4.  **Monitor Your Heart Rate:** You might notice your resting heart rate increases slightly in the luteal phase—this is a sign to dial back intensity.
5.  **Log Your "Feel":** Use the notes in LunaCycle to record which workouts felt great and which were a struggle.

## Top 4 Fitness Strategies by Phase

### 1. Menstrual Phase (The "Inner Winter")
**Best Workouts:** Yoga, walking, light stretching.
**Goal:** Movement for circulation and mood rather than performance.

### 2. Follicular Phase (The "Inner Spring")
**Best Workouts:** Cardio, light jogging, new fitness classes.
**Goal:** Capitalize on rising energy and increased pain tolerance.

### 3. Ovulatory Phase (The "Inner Summer")
**Best Workouts:** HIIT, heavy strength training, competitive sports.
**Goal:** Peak performance and social fitness activities.

### 4. Luteal Phase (The "Inner Autumn")
**Best Workouts:** Pilates, strength training (lower reps), long walks.
**Goal:** Functional movement and stress reduction as energy naturally tapers.

## Common Fitness Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why it's a Problem | Better Alternative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pushing through severe fatigue | Can lead to overtraining syndrome and hormonal disruption. | Take a "deload" week during your period or late luteal phase. |
| Comparing day-to-day performance | Your Day 14 self is hormonally different from your Day 26 self. | Compare your performance to the *same phase* of previous cycles. |
| Not eating enough for your training | Low energy availability can stop your cycle entirely. | Ensure you're fueling appropriately for your activity level. |
