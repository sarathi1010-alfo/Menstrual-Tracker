# What is Basal Body Temperature (BBT)?

Basal Body Temperature (BBT) is your body's temperature when it is at complete rest. For those tracking their menstrual cycle, it is a key biological marker used to confirm that ovulation has taken place.

## How Does BBT Work?
Before ovulation, your BBT stays in a lower range. After an egg is released, the corpus luteum (the follicle that held the egg) begins producing the hormone **progesterone**. Progesterone has a thermogenic effect, causing your body temperature to rise slightly—usually by about 0.5°F to 1°F (0.3°C to 0.6°C).

## Real-World Example
A user measures their temperature every morning at 7:00 AM before getting out of bed. For 14 days, the temperature is around 97.4°F. On day 15, it jumps to 97.9°F and stays elevated for the rest of the cycle. This "thermal shift" confirms that ovulation likely occurred on day 14.

## Why is BBT Important?
- **Confirming Ovulation:** Unlike many other signs that tell you ovulation is *coming*, BBT confirms that it has *happened*.
- **Identifying Luteal Phase Defects:** If the temperature stay elevated for less than 10 days, it may indicate a short luteal phase.
- **Predicting Your Period:** BBT usually drops just before or on the day your period begins.

| Feature | BBT Thermometer | Standard Thermometer |
|---------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Precision | To the 0.01 degree | To the 0.1 degree |

*Disclaimer: BBT is a retrospective sign and should not be used as the sole method for preventing pregnancy.*
