**Basal Body Temperature (BBT)** is your body's temperature when it is at complete rest. For most people, this is the temperature immediately upon waking up in the morning.

## How Does BBT Work?
In a typical menstrual cycle, your BBT stays in a lower range during the follicular phase. After ovulation occurs, the hormone progesterone causes your temperature to rise slightly (usually by about 0.5 to 1 degree Fahrenheit). This temperature shift confirms that ovulation has taken place.

## Real-World Example
If your pre-ovulation BBT is consistently 97.2°F and then jumps to 97.8°F and stays elevated for three days, you can be fairly certain you ovulated the day before the rise.

## Why is BBT Important?
BBT is a key indicator for:
1. **Confirming Ovulation:** It is one of the few at-home methods to prove ovulation actually happened.
2. **Identifying the Luteal Phase:** Helps determine the length of the second half of your cycle.
3. **Natural Family Planning:** Used to identify fertile and infertile days.

## BBT vs. Standard Temperature

| Feature | Basal Body Temperature (BBT) | Standard Body Temperature |
|---------|------------------------------|---------------------------|
| **When to Measure** | Immediately upon waking | Anytime |
| **Precision** | Needs 0.01 degree accuracy | 0.1 degree is sufficient |
| **Purpose** | Tracking hormonal shifts | Checking for fever/illness |
