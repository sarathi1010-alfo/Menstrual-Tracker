The follicular phase is the first part of your menstrual cycle. It begins on the very first day of your period (menstruation) and continues until ovulation occurs.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

During this phase, the pituitary gland in the brain releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH). This hormone stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles, each containing an immature egg. Usually, only one of these follicles will fully mature. As the follicles grow, they produce estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining in preparation for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example of the Follicular Phase in Daily Life

Many people notice a shift in their energy and mood during this phase. As estrogen levels rise after the period ends, you might feel more outgoing, energetic, and focused. If you log your daily energy in [LunaCycle](/tracker), you'll often see a pattern of higher energy levels during these days compared to the late luteal phase.

## Follicular Phase vs. Luteal Phase

| Phase | Length | Key Hormone | Energy Levels (Typical) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Follicular Phase** | Variable (often 10-16 days) | Estrogen (rising) | Often higher and more stable. |
| **Luteal Phase** | Relatively fixed (11-17 days) | Progesterone | Can be lower, especially late in phase. |

The variability of the follicular phase is why tracking your cycle length over time is essential for accurate predictions.
