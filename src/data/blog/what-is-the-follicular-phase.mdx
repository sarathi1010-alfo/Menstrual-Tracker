## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The follicular phase begins on day one of your cycle (the first day of bleeding). During this time, the pituitary gland releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which signals the ovaries to produce several follicles, each containing an immature egg. One follicle will become dominant and release a mature egg during ovulation. This phase is characterized by rising estrogen levels.

## Real-World Example of the Follicular Phase in Cycle Variability

The length of the follicular phase can vary significantly from woman to woman, and even from cycle to cycle for the same woman. This variability is often the cause of early or late periods. Stress, illness, or travel can delay ovulation, thus lengthening the follicular phase.

## Follicular Phase vs Menstrual Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Menstrual Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Day 1 to Ovulation. | Day 1 to end of bleeding. |
| **Relationship** | The menstrual phase is *part* of the follicular phase. | The start of both phases is simultaneous. |
| **Primary Action** | Maturing an egg. | Shedding the uterine lining. |
