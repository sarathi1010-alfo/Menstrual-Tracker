## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of your menstrual cycle. It officially begins on Day 1 of your cycle (the first day of full menstrual bleeding) and concludes when you ovulate.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

At the start of the cycle, the pituitary gland in the brain releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH). As the name suggests, this hormone stimulates the growth of several follicles in the ovaries. Each follicle contains an immature egg.

As these follicles grow, they produce estrogen. Usually, only one follicle becomes fully mature (the dominant follicle), while the others break down. The rising estrogen levels from this dominant follicle do two things:
1. They signal the uterus to start thickening its lining.
2. They eventually trigger the surge of Luteinizing Hormone (LH) that causes the follicle to burst and release the egg (ovulation).

## Cycle Variability

The follicular phase is the variable part of the menstrual cycle. While the luteal phase (after ovulation) is usually a consistent 12-14 days, the follicular phase can vary greatly due to stress, illness, travel, or hormonal imbalances. This variability is why period prediction apps like [LunaCycle](/tracker) use averages over multiple cycles to give you the most accurate forecast possible.
