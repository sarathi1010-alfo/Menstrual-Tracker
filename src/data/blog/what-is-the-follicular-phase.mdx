The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation (your period) and ends when you [ovulate](/what-is-ovulation). During this time, your body is working to mature an egg for potential fertilization.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The phase gets its name from follicles—small sacs in the ovaries that contain immature eggs.

1. The brain's pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH).
2. FSH stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles.
3. As these follicles grow, they produce **estrogen**.
4. Typically, only one follicle becomes the "dominant" follicle, maturing fully while the others break down.
5. The rising estrogen levels cause the uterine lining to thicken and eventually trigger the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

The follicular phase is the main reason cycle lengths vary. Stress, illness, or travel can delay the development of the dominant follicle, pushing ovulation later. When you log irregular cycle lengths in [LunaCycle](/), you are usually observing a lengthened or shortened follicular phase, while your [luteal phase](/what-is-the-luteal-phase) remains relatively constant.

## Follicular Phase vs. Menstrual Phase

| Term | Definition | Relationship |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Follicular Phase** | The entire process of maturing an egg (Day 1 to Ovulation). | Encompasses the Menstrual Phase. |
| **Menstrual Phase** | The specific days of bleeding. | Occurs at the start of the Follicular Phase. |
