## What is the Follicular Phase?

The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It officially starts on the first day of your period (menstruation) and continues until ovulation occurs.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

During this phase, the pituitary gland releases follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), which signals the ovaries to develop several follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg. As the follicles grow, they produce estrogen, which causes the uterine lining to thicken. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures fully and is released during ovulation.

## Real-World Example of Follicular Phase Variability

The length of the follicular phase can vary significantly from cycle to cycle, which is why cycle lengths fluctuate. Stress, illness, or travel can delay ovulation, thereby lengthening the follicular phase. ## Follicular Phase vs. Menstrual Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Menstrual Phase |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | The period of follicle development leading up to ovulation. | The shedding of the uterine lining (your period). |
| **Overlap** | The menstrual phase occurs *within* the early part of the follicular phase. | It is the visible beginning of the cycle. |
| **Hormone Activity** | Estrogen levels are rising. | Estrogen and progesterone are at their lowest. |
