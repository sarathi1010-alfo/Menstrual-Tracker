The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of your period and ends when you ovulate.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

This phase is named after the follicles in your ovaries. At the start of your cycle, the pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which prompts several follicles to begin maturing. As they grow, they produce estrogen. Eventually, one dominant follicle emerges and prepares to release an egg (ovulation). The rising estrogen also causes the lining of the uterus to thicken in preparation for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example of the Follicular Phase

Many people notice a shift in their energy and mood as the follicular phase progresses. While the first few days (during menstruation) might be low energy, the rising estrogen in the second week often brings a boost in stamina. ## Follicular Phase vs. Luteal Phase Length

| Phase | Length Variability |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Follicular Phase** | Highly variable; this phase is usually what dictates a long or short cycle. |
| **Luteal Phase** | Very consistent; almost always lasts 12-16 days. |
