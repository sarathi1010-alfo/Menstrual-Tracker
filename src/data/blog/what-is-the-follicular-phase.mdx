The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the very first day of menstrual bleeding and concludes with ovulation.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

During this phase, the pituitary gland releases follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). This hormone stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles, each containing an immature egg. As the follicles grow, they produce estrogen. The rising estrogen levels cause the lining of the uterus to thicken, preparing it to nourish a potential fertilized egg. Eventually, one follicle becomes dominant, leading to a surge in hormones that triggers ovulation.

## Real-World Example

If your period is late but you are not pregnant, it usually means your follicular phase was longer than usual that month, delaying ovulation. Stress or illness can easily cause this delay.

## Follicular Phase vs. Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Day 1 to Ovulation | Ovulation to next period |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Length** | Highly variable | Very consistent (12-14 days) |
