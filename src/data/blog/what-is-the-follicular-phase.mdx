The follicular phase is the foundational first half of the menstrual cycle. It is a period of growth and preparation, culminating in the main event of the cycle: ovulation. This phase overlaps with menstruation, meaning that as the body is shedding the unneeded uterine lining from the previous cycle, it is already working on maturing a new egg for the current one.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The phase is characterized by a precise hormonal feedback loop:

1.  **FSH Release:** The brain (specifically the pituitary gland) releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH). As the name implies, this hormone stimulates several follicles (tiny sacs in the ovaries, each containing an immature egg) to begin growing.
2.  **Estrogen Production:** As these follicles grow, they begin to produce estrogen.
3.  **Uterine Rebuilding:** While you are menstruating during the early days of this phase, the rising estrogen levels eventually halt the bleeding and instruct the uterus to begin rebuilding a fresh, healthy lining (endometrium).
4.  **Dominance:** One follicle typically outpaces the rest, becoming the "dominant" follicle. The high levels of estrogen produced by this mature follicle eventually trigger the LH surge, marking the end of the follicular phase and the onset of ovulation.

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

Because the luteal phase (post-ovulation) is biologically fixed at around 12 to 14 days, the follicular phase dictates the total length of your cycle. If you log a 35-day cycle in [LunaCycle](/), it is because your body took approximately 21 days to mature an egg and ovulate (a long follicular phase), followed by the standard 14-day luteal phase. Tracking these variations helps you understand how stress or lifestyle factors might be delaying your ovulation.

## Follicular Phase vs. Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Menstruation to ovulation. | Post-ovulation to next menstruation. |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Estrogen (and FSH). | Progesterone. |
| **Primary Goal** | Maturing an egg. | Maintaining the uterine lining. |
| **Variability** | Highly variable; easily affected by stress. | Generally static and consistent. |
