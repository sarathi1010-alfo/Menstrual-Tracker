The follicular phase is the first part of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the very first day of menstrual bleeding and concludes at the moment of ovulation. This phase is characterized by growth, renewal, and preparation for a new potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

At the start of the cycle, hormone levels are low, triggering the shedding of the uterine lining (your period). Concurrently, the pituitary gland releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH). This hormone stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles, each containing an immature egg. Usually, only one dominant follicle matures fully. As this follicle grows, it produces increasing amounts of estrogen, which signals the uterine lining to start thickening again.

## Real-World Example of Follicular Phase Energy

As estrogen levels climb steadily during the follicular phase, many individuals report feeling a surge in energy, improved mood, and increased sociability. If you track your cycle with [LunaCycle](/), you might use the knowledge that you are in your mid-follicular phase to tackle challenging projects or intense workouts, capitalizing on this natural energy boost.

## Follicular Phase vs. Luteal Phase (Table)

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Length Variability** | Highly variable, dictates overall cycle length. | Generally fixed (12-14 days). |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen. | Progesterone. |
| **Primary Goal** | Maturing an egg. | Preparing the uterus for a fertilized egg. |
