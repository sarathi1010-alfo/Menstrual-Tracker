## Definition

The **follicular phase** is the first phase of the menstrual cycle. It begins on the first day of menstruation and ends when ovulation occurs. During this time, the body is working to mature an egg to be released during ovulation.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

At the start of the cycle, the pituitary gland releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH). This hormone stimulates the ovaries to produce several follicles, each containing an immature egg.

Typically, only one of these follicles becomes the "dominant" follicle and fully matures. As this follicle grows, it produces estrogen, which signals the uterus to begin thickening its lining again after menstruation has finished.

## Real-World Example of Follicular Phase Variability

The length of the follicular phase is the main reason why menstrual cycles vary in length. While the luteal phase remains relatively constant, stress, travel, or conditions like PCOS can delay ovulation by extending the follicular phase. ## Follicular Phase vs. Luteal Phase

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Days 1 to Ovulation | Ovulation to next Period |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Estrogen | Progesterone |
| **Length** | Variable (10-22 days) | Consistent (12-14 days) |
| **Primary Event** | Egg maturation | Uterine lining maintenance |
