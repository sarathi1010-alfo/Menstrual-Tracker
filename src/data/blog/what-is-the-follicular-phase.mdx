## What is the Follicular Phase?

The **follicular phase** is the first half of the menstrual cycle. It starts on the first day of menstrual bleeding and concludes with ovulation. During this time, the body is preparing to release a new egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

The brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which signals the ovaries to start maturing several fluid-filled sacs called follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg.

As these follicles grow, they secrete increasing amounts of estrogen. This rising estrogen causes the uterine lining to thicken and changes the cervical mucus to become more sperm-friendly. Eventually, one dominant follicle matures fully, while the others dissolve. The peak in estrogen then triggers the LH surge that causes ovulation.

## Real-World Example in Tracking

If a user using [LunaCycle](/) has a cycle that arrives five days "late" due to stress, it is almost entirely because their follicular phase was extended. Stress can delay the hormonal signals needed to mature the follicle, pushing ovulation—and consequently, the next period—further out.

## Follicular Phase vs. Menstruation

| Feature | Follicular Phase | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Relationship** | The overarching phase leading to ovulation. | A specific event (bleeding) at the *start* of the follicular phase. |
| **Duration** | Varies (e.g., 10-21 days). | Typically 3-7 days. |
| **Hormonal State**| Estrogen is rising. | Estrogen and Progesterone are at their lowest. |
