The follicular phase is the first half of the menstrual cycle, during which follicles in the ovary mature and prepare to release an egg.

## How Does the Follicular Phase Work?

It begins on Day 1 of your period. The brain releases Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), which tells the ovaries to start developing several follicles. Each follicle contains an immature egg. As the follicles grow, they release estrogen, which thickens the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example of the Follicular Phase

In the first week (during your period), your estrogen is low. In the second week, as you approach ovulation, estrogen rises sharply. You might notice more energy, glowing skin, and a more positive mood during this time.

## Why is the Follicular Phase Important?

The follicular phase determines the total length of your cycle. While the luteal phase is usually constant, the follicular phase can be shortened or lengthened by stress, illness, or nutrition. Healthy follicle development is the foundation of a healthy ovulation.

## Follicular Phase vs. Ovulation

| Concept | Duration | Event |
|---------|----------|-------|
| Follicular Phase | 10-22 Days | Egg maturation and building lining. |
| Ovulation | 24 Hours | The actual release of the egg. |
