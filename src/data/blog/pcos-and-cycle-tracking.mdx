Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a hormonal condition that affects how the ovaries work. One of the most common symptoms is irregular periods, which can make traditional tracking feel frustrating. However, tracking is actually *more* important when you have PCOS.

## Why Track if Your Cycle is Irregular?

When your cycle doesn't follow a 28-day pattern, data becomes your best friend.
  - "**Identify Patterns:** You might notice that even if your period is late, your symptoms (like skin changes or mood) follow a consistent lead-up."
  - "**Identify Anovulation:** Tracking can help you see if you are actually ovulating or having "withdrawal bleeding.""
  - "**Better Doctor Visits:** Instead of saying "my period is irregular," you can show a chart of exactly how many days are between your cycles."

## What to Look for in Your Data

1. **Cycle Length:** Are your cycles consistently longer than 35 days?
2. **Symptom Clusters:** Track things like "hormonal acne," "excess hair growth (hirsutism)," and "energy crashes."
3. **Bleeding Patterns:** Is your flow very light or very heavy and prolonged?

## When to See a Doctor

If you suspect you have PCOS or are struggling to manage it, consult a professional if:
- You have fewer than 8 periods a year.
- You have sudden, excessive hair growth on your face or body.
- You have been trying to conceive for 12 months (or 6 months if over 35) without success.
