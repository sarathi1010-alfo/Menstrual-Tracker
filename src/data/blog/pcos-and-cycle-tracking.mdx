**Definition:** PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome) is a common hormonal disorder affecting roughly 10% of women of reproductive age. It is often characterized by irregular menstrual cycles, elevated androgen (male hormone) levels, and sometimes small cysts on the ovaries.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help

If you have or suspect you have PCOS, cycle tracking is one of your most powerful tools. It moves you from guessing about your symptoms to having hard data.

By logging your cycle length and associated symptoms (like acne breakouts, unusual hair growth, or weight fluctuations), you and your healthcare provider can identify patterns and evaluate if treatments are working.

## What to Look For in Your Cycle Data

When tracking with PCOS, pay special attention to:
- **Prolonged Cycles:** Cycles consistently lasting longer than 35 days.
- **Missed Periods:** Going months without a period.
- **Spotting:** Irregular bleeding between expected periods.
- **Ovulation Signs:** Difficulty identifying ovulation via temperature or mucus changes.

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

Cycle data is most useful when shared with a professional. You should consult a doctor if:
- Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
- You have severe symptoms like sudden weight gain, severe acne, or excessive facial hair.
- You are trying to conceive and having difficulty.
