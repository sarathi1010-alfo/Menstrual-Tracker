Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal condition that affects how the ovaries work. It is estimated to affect up to 10% of women of childbearing age, yet many remain undiagnosed. One of the primary symptoms of PCOS is irregular or missing periods, which makes cycle tracking both a challenge and a necessity.

## What PCOS is and how it affects the menstrual cycle

In a typical menstrual cycle, the brain sends signals to the ovaries to mature a follicle and release an egg. In women with PCOS, the body may produce higher levels of androgens (often called "male" hormones, though all women have them). These high levels of androgens can interfere with the development and release of eggs (ovulation).

Instead of being released, follicles may stay in the ovaries as small, fluid-filled sacs (often called "cysts," though they are actually immature follicles). Because ovulation doesn't occur regularly, the uterine lining doesn't receive the progesterone it needs to shed, resulting in irregular cycles, very heavy periods, or amenorrhea (no periods at all).

## How cycle tracking can help manage PCOS

Tracking gives you a data-driven view of your PCOS. It is not just about knowing when you'll bleed; it's about monitoring the underlying health of your endocrine system.

1. **Cycle Length Variability**: Exactly how long you go between periods. Tracking this over 6-12 months can provide your doctor with the data needed for a diagnosis.
2. **Ovulation Signs**: Or the lack thereof. For those with PCOS, ovulation may happen much later in the cycle than Day 14, or not at all (anovulation). Tracking cervical mucus and BBT can help you identify if you are actually ovulating.
3. **Symptom Patterns**: PCOS comes with various symptoms like acne, hirsutism (excess hair growth), and hair thinning. Logging these in LunaCycle helps you see if your management strategies (like diet or medication) are working.
4. **Metabolic Health**: Many women with PCOS have insulin resistance. Tracking your energy levels and sugar cravings alongside your cycle can reveal how your blood sugar affects your hormonal balance.

## What to look for in your cycle data with PCOS

When reviewing your LunaCycle history, look for the following patterns:
- **Long Cycles**: Consistently going more than 35 days between periods.
- **High Variability**: One cycle is 30 days, the next is 50 days, the next is 25 days.
- **Heavy or Prolonged Bleeding**: Bleeding that lasts more than 7 days or requires changing products every hour.
- **Anovulatory Patterns**: A lack of "egg white" cervical mucus or a lack of a temperature shift on your BBT chart.

## When to consult a healthcare provider

PCOS is a complex condition that requires a professional diagnosis, usually based on the "Rotterdam Criteria" (having two of three: irregular periods, high androgens, or polycystic ovaries on an ultrasound).

You should consult a doctor if:
- You have fewer than 8 periods a year.
- Your cycles are consistently longer than 35 days.
- You have sudden, excessive hair growth on your face or body.
- You have been trying to conceive for over 6 months without success.
