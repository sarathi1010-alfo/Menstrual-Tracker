Period pain, medically termed **dysmenorrhea**, is characterized by throbbing or cramping pains in the lower abdomen. It is often accompanied by other symptoms like lower back pain, nausea, or headaches.

## How Does Period Pain Work?
During your period, your uterus contracts to help shed its lining. Hormone-like substances called **prostaglandins** trigger these contractions. Higher levels of prostaglandins are associated with more severe menstrual cramps.

## Real-World Example of Period Pain
Someone with **Primary Dysmenorrhea** might experience sharp cramps starting a day before their period that gradually fade after the first two days. A person with **Secondary Dysmenorrhea** might find their pain lasts longer and is caused by an underlying condition like endometriosis.

## Why is Period Pain Important?
While mild discomfort is common, severe period pain can be a sign of an underlying medical issue. Understanding and tracking the timing and intensity of your pain in LunaCycle is crucial for identifying what is "normal" for you and when to seek medical advice.

## Primary vs. Secondary Dysmenorrhea

| Feature | Primary Dysmenorrhea | Secondary Dysmenorrhea |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cause** | Natural prostaglandins. | Underlying medical condition. |
| **Timing** | Starts with or just before period. | Can occur outside of period. |
| **Onset** | Usually starts in teens. | Often starts later in life. |

**Core Principles:**
- Pain is a signal, not just an inconvenience.
- Tracking intensity helps differentiate between types.
- Heat and movement are common natural relievers.
- Persistent, debilitating pain requires professional evaluation.
