**The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.**

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and involves four main phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** Shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The ovary prepares an egg for release.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of the mature egg.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares the uterus for a potential fertilized egg. If no pregnancy occurs, the cycle restarts.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, a woman might have her period (menstruation) from days 1-5. The follicular phase runs concurrently and continues until ovulation around day 14. The luteal phase then spans days 15-28, ending just before the next period begins.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

The menstrual cycle is a vital sign of reproductive health. Regular cycles indicate that the body's hormonal systems are functioning correctly, which is important for fertility and overall well-being.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process | A single event within the cycle |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| **Purpose** | Preparation for pregnancy | Release of an egg |
