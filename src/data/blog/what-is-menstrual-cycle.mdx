## Definition

The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through in preparation for the possibility of pregnancy. Each month, one of the ovaries releases an egg — a process called ovulation. At the same time, hormonal changes prepare the uterus for pregnancy. If ovulation takes place and the egg isn't fertilized, the lining of the uterus sheds through the vagina. This is a menstrual period.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is controlled by a complex orchestra of hormones produced by the pituitary gland and the ovaries. It is divided into four main phases: the menstrual phase (bleeding), the follicular phase (egg development), ovulation (egg release), and the luteal phase (preparation for pregnancy or next cycle).

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

When you use [LunaCycle](/), you log the first day of your bleeding (Day 1 of your menstrual cycle). By tracking this over several months, you can identify your average cycle length, helping you predict when your next period will arrive or when you are likely to be in your fertile window.

## Menstrual Cycle vs. Period

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Period (Menstruation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | Typically 21-35 days | Typically 3-7 days |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process from one period to the next. | The specific days of uterine bleeding. |
| **Purpose** | Preparation for potential pregnancy. | Shedding the unneeded uterine lining. |
