Definition: The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is a continuous loop of four distinct phases, governed by hormones:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3. **Ovulation:** The release of the egg.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

Imagine a standard 28-day cycle: Days 1-5 involve menstrual bleeding. From Day 1 to 13, the follicular phase overlaps and continues as an egg matures. On Day 14, ovulation occurs. Days 15-28 make up the luteal phase, where the body waits to see if pregnancy occurred, ending with the start of a new period if it hasn't.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond fertility, the menstrual cycle is a vital sign. Regular cycles generally indicate that your hormones are balanced and your reproductive system is functioning correctly.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| Purpose | Complete reproductive preparation | Release of a single egg |
| Scope | Entire month | Single event |
