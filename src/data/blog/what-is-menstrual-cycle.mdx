The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four main phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** Shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of the egg.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy; if none occurs, the cycle starts over.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
*   Days 1-5: Menstruation occurs.
*   Days 1-13: The follicular phase overlaps with menstruation and continues until ovulation.
*   Day 14: Ovulation occurs.
*   Days 15-28: The luteal phase occurs, ending right before the next period starts.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a vital indicator of reproductive health and overall well-being. Regular cycles generally suggest that your hormones are balanced and your reproductive system is functioning correctly.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Definition | The entire 21-35 day process. | The specific event of releasing an egg. |
| Duration | Weeks (approx. 4 weeks). | Hours (12-24 hours). |
| Purpose | Preparation for potential pregnancy. | Providing the egg for fertilization. |
