The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and divided into four phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg, driven by estrogen.
3. **Ovulation:** An egg is released from the ovary.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy; if none occurs, hormone levels drop, leading back to menstruation.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
- **Days 1-5:** Menstruation occurs.
- **Days 6-13:** The follicular phase continues.
- **Day 14:** Ovulation happens.
- **Days 15-28:** The luteal phase takes place.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a vital sign of reproductive and overall health. Regular cycles indicate that your body is producing balanced hormones. Tracking your cycle with tools like [LunaCycle](/) helps you understand these rhythms.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Concept | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Menstrual Cycle** | The entire ~28-day process involving multiple phases and hormones. |
| **Ovulation** | A single event within the cycle (lasting 12-24 hours) when an egg is released. |
