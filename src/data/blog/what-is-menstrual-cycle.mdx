The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is orchestrated by complex interactions between the brain and ovaries, driven by hormones like estrogen and progesterone. It is broadly divided into four parts: Menstruation (bleeding), the [Follicular Phase](/what-is-follicular-phase) (egg maturation), [Ovulation](/what-is-ovulation) (egg release), and the [Luteal Phase](/what-is-luteal-phase) (preparation for implantation).

## Real-World Example

Consider a textbook 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** Menstruation occurs.
*   **Days 1-13:** The follicular phase builds up the uterine lining.
*   **Day 14:** Ovulation happens.
*   **Days 15-28:** The luteal phase occurs. If no pregnancy happens, hormone levels drop, triggering the next period.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a vital indicator of reproductive and overall health. Regular cycles suggest balanced hormones, while irregular cycles can indicate underlying health issues. Tracking it with tools like [LunaCycle](/) empowers you to understand your body better.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| What is it? | The entire monthly process (approx 28 days). | A single event within the cycle (approx 12-24 hours). |
| Purpose | Prepare the body for pregnancy and shed lining if none occurs. | Release a mature egg for potential fertilization. |
| Observation | Tracked from the first day of one period to the next. | Often identified via BBT shifts or cervical mucus changes. |
