The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and divided into four main [phases](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal):

1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (the period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for potential pregnancy; if none occurs, the cycle restarts.

## Real-World Example

In an average 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** Menstruation.
*   **Days 1-13:** Follicular phase (overlapping with menstruation).
*   **Day 14:** Ovulation.
*   **Days 15-28:** Luteal phase.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a vital indicator of reproductive health and overall well-being. Regular cycles suggest balanced hormones, while irregularities can signal health issues. Tracking with [LunaCycle](/) helps you understand your unique pattern.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process of hormonal changes. | A specific event *within* the cycle. |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days. | 12-24 hours. |
| **Purpose** | To prepare the body for pregnancy and shed the lining if not pregnant. | To release a mature egg for fertilization. |
