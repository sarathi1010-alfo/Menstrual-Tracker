## What is a Menstrual Cycle?

The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four phases:
1. Menstruation: shedding the uterine lining.
2. Follicular Phase: preparing an egg for release.
3. Ovulation: releasing the egg.
4. Luteal Phase: preparing the uterus for a potential fertilized egg.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, menstruation occurs on days 1-5. The follicular phase overlaps and continues until ovulation around day 14. The luteal phase then spans days 15-28, ending when the next period begins.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

The menstrual cycle is a primary indicator of reproductive health and fertility. Beyond that, regular cycles indicate that the body's hormonal systems are functioning correctly, which impacts bone health, heart health, and overall well-being.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | The entire monthly process (21-35 days) | A single event within the cycle (12-24 hours) |
| Purpose | Prepare the body for pregnancy | Release an egg for fertilization |
| Indicators | Bleeding, temperature shifts, mucus changes | LH surge, clear stretchy mucus, BBT shift |
