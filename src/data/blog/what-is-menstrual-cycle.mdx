The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is typically divided into four phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3. **Ovulation:** The release of the mature egg.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares the uterus for a potential fertilized egg.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
- **Days 1-5:** Menstruation occurs.
- **Days 1-13:** The follicular phase overlaps and continues, building up a new lining.
- **Day 14:** Ovulation happens.
- **Days 15-28:** The luteal phase maintains the lining until it breaks down, starting the next cycle.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a key indicator of reproductive and overall health. Regular cycles suggest that your hormones are balanced and your reproductive system is functioning correctly.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|-----------------|-----------|
| The entire monthly process (21-35 days). | A single event within the cycle (lasts 12-24 hours). |
| Prepares the body for pregnancy. | The release of the egg. |
