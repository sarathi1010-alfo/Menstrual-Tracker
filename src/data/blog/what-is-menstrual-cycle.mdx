The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?
The cycle is driven by hormones and divided into four phases. It starts with menstruation (bleeding), followed by the follicular phase where an egg matures. Ovulation occurs when the egg is released, and the luteal phase follows, preparing the uterus for implantation. If pregnancy doesn't happen, the cycle repeats.

## Real-World Example
In a standard 28-day cycle, menstruation might last days 1-5. The follicular phase runs concurrently and ends around day 13. Ovulation happens on day 14. The luteal phase spans days 15-28, ending just before the next period starts.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?
It is a vital sign of reproductive health and overall well-being. Regular cycles generally indicate balanced hormones and normal bodily function. Tracking it with [LunaCycle](/) helps you stay informed.
