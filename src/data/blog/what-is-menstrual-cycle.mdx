## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?
The menstrual cycle is governed by a complex interaction of hormones, primarily estrogen and progesterone. It consists of four main phases: Menstruation (bleeding), the Follicular phase (preparing an egg), Ovulation (releasing the egg), and the Luteal phase (preparing the uterus for implantation).

## Real-World Example
In a standard 28-day cycle, menstruation might last from day 1 to 5. The follicular phase spans days 1 to 13. Ovulation typically occurs around day 14. Finally, the luteal phase covers days 15 through 28, leading up to the next period if pregnancy does not occur.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?
Beyond its reproductive purpose, the menstrual cycle is a vital sign. Regular cycles indicate a healthy hormonal balance, while significant irregularities can be an early warning sign for various health conditions.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | ~28 days (21-35 days) | 12-24 hours |
| Purpose | Prepare the entire body for potential pregnancy | The specific event of releasing a mature egg |
| Frequency | Monthly | Once per cycle |
