## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The menstrual cycle is controlled by a complex interaction of hormones and is divided into four main phases: Menstruation, the Follicular phase, Ovulation, and the Luteal phase. These phases orchestrate the development and release of an egg, and the preparation of the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

Consider a typical 28-day cycle:
- **Days 1-5:** Menstruation (bleeding) occurs.
- **Days 1-13:** The Follicular phase overlaps with menstruation and continues, preparing an egg.
- **Day 14:** Ovulation happens as the egg is released.
- **Days 15-28:** The Luteal phase prepares the uterine lining. If no pregnancy occurs, the cycle starts again.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond reproductive capabilities, the menstrual cycle is often considered a "fifth vital sign." Regular cycles are an indicator that your hormonal systems are functioning correctly, while irregularities can be the first sign of underlying health issues.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| **Duration** | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| **Function** | Overall reproductive process | Specific event of releasing an egg |
| **Tracking** | Tracked over a month | Tracked to find the fertile window |
