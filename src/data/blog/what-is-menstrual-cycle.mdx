## What is a Menstrual Cycle?

The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of physical and hormonal changes a woman's body goes through in preparation for a possible pregnancy. It begins on the first day of your period and ends the day before your next period starts. The average cycle is 28 days long, though a range of 21 to 35 days is considered normal for adult women.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four distinct phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg, driven by rising estrogen.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares the uterus for potential implantation, dominated by progesterone.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
*   Days 1-5: Menstruation (bleeding occurs).
*   Days 1-13: Follicular phase (egg matures).
*   Day 14: Ovulation (egg is released).
*   Days 15-28: Luteal phase (lining thickens, then sheds if no pregnancy occurs).

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

The menstrual cycle is often considered a "vital sign." Its regularity and characteristics are strong indicators of your overall reproductive and hormonal health. Changes in your cycle can signal stress, nutritional deficiencies, or underlying conditions like PCOS or thyroid issues.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process (approx 28 days) | A single event within the cycle (release of the egg) |
| **Duration** | 21 - 35 days | 12 - 24 hours |
| **Primary Function** | Prepare the body for potential pregnancy | Release an egg for potential fertilization |
