## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is a complex loop governed by the brain and ovaries. It involves four main phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3. **Ovulation:** The release of the mature egg.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares the uterus for a potential fertilized egg. If no pregnancy occurs, hormone levels drop, triggering menstruation again.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** Menstruation (bleeding occurs).
*   **Days 1-13:** Follicular phase (overlapping with menstruation, estrogen rises).
*   **Day 14:** Ovulation (egg is released).
*   **Days 15-28:** Luteal phase (progesterone rises, then drops if not pregnant).

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond reproduction, the cycle is a vital sign. Regular, healthy cycles indicate that your hormones are balanced and your body is functioning optimally. Irregularities can be the first sign of underlying issues like PCOS or thyroid dysfunction.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire month-long process of reproductive preparation. | A single event within the cycle where an egg is released. |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days. | 12-24 hours. |
| **Hormonal Driver** | Fluctuations of FSH, LH, Estrogen, and Progesterone. | A sudden surge of Luteinizing Hormone (LH). |
