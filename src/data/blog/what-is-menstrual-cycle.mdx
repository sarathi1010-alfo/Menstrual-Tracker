The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four main phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of the mature egg.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

Imagine a classic 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** Menstruation (bleeding).
*   **Days 1-13:** The follicular phase is happening concurrently, maturing an egg.
*   **Day 14:** Ovulation occurs.
*   **Days 15-28:** The luteal phase. If the egg isn't fertilized, hormone levels drop, leading back to Day 1.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond reproductive health and fertility, a regular menstrual cycle is a sign that your hormonal systems are functioning correctly. Irregularities can often be the first sign of underlying health conditions like thyroid issues or PCOS.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days (the entire month) | 12-24 hours |
| **Function** | The complete process of preparing for pregnancy | The specific event of releasing an egg |
| **Hormones** | Governed by Estrogen, Progesterone, LH, and FSH | Triggered specifically by an LH surge |
