The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is governed by hormones and consists of four main phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg, driven by rising estrogen.
3. **Ovulation:** A mature egg is released from the ovary.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy, driven by progesterone. If no pregnancy occurs, hormone levels drop, and the cycle starts again.

## Real-World Example

Imagine a standard 28-day cycle:
- **Days 1-5:** Menstruation occurs.
- **Days 1-13:** The follicular phase is ongoing.
- **Day 14:** Ovulation happens.
- **Days 15-28:** The luteal phase prepares the uterus, ending with a hormone drop that triggers the next period.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

The menstrual cycle is a crucial indicator of reproductive health and overall well-being. It dictates fertility and significantly impacts energy levels, mood, and physical symptoms throughout the month. Tracking it with [LunaCycle](/) helps you stay informed.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Definition | The entire monthly process | A single event within the cycle |
| Duration | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| Purpose | Prepare the body for pregnancy | Release an egg for fertilization |
