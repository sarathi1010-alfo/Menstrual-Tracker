The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four main phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of the egg from the ovary.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy; if none occurs, the cycle starts again.

For a deeper dive into these stages, read our [detailed guide on cycle phases](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal).

## Real-World Example

Imagine a woman with a typical 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** She experiences menstruation (bleeding).
*   **Days 1-13:** The follicular phase is occurring simultaneously, preparing an egg.
*   **Day 14:** She likely ovulates, releasing the egg.
*   **Days 15-28:** She is in the luteal phase. If she doesn't get pregnant, her hormone levels drop around Day 28, triggering her next period on Day 1 of the new cycle.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond reproductive capabilities, the menstrual cycle acts as a vital sign for overall health. Regular cycles generally indicate balanced hormones and a healthy reproductive system. ## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process (approx 28 days). | A single event within the cycle (release of an egg). |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days continuously. | Lasts only 12-24 hours. |
| **Hormonal Driver** | Fluctuations of estrogen, progesterone, LH, FSH. | A sharp surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH). |
| **Visibility** | Marked by visible bleeding (menstruation). | Often has invisible or subtle signs (BBT rise, mucus changes). |
