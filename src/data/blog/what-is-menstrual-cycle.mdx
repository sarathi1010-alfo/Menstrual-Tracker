## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is divided into four main phases driven by rising and falling hormones:
1. **Menstruation**: Shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase**: The body prepares to release an egg.
3. **Ovulation**: The ovary releases a mature egg.
4. **Luteal Phase**: The body prepares for a potential pregnancy. If conception doesn't happen, hormone levels drop, triggering the next period.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
- **Days 1-5**: Menstruation occurs.
- **Days 1-13**: The Follicular phase (overlapping with menstruation).
- **Day 14**: Ovulation typically occurs around this midpoint.
- **Days 15-28**: The Luteal phase prepares the uterus before the cycle restarts.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond reproduction, the menstrual cycle is an indicator of your overall health. Regular cycles suggest that your reproductive hormones are balanced, while irregularities can be early signs of thyroid issues, stress, or conditions like PCOS.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days (entire month) | 12-24 hours |
| **Function** | Entire process of preparing for pregnancy | The specific event of releasing an egg |
| **Tracking** | Tracked from period to period | Tracked via BBT, OPKs, or cervical mucus |
