## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The menstrual cycle is a complex biological process controlled by hormones, primarily estrogen and progesterone, produced by the ovaries. These hormones act as chemical messengers, signaling different parts of the reproductive system to perform specific actions at specific times.

The cycle begins on the first day of menstrual bleeding (Day 1) and ends the day before the next period starts. A typical cycle lasts 28 days, but anything from 21 to 35 days is considered normal for adults.

The cycle is designed to prepare the body for a potential pregnancy. If a fertilized egg does not implant in the uterus, the lining sheds, resulting in a period, and the cycle begins anew.

## The Four Phases

The cycle is divided into four main phases:

1.  **Menstrual Phase:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The preparation of follicles in the ovary, one of which will mature into an egg. Estrogen rises.
3.  **Ovulation Phase:** The release of the mature egg from the ovary.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for potential implantation. Progesterone peaks. If no pregnancy occurs, hormone levels drop, leading to the next period.

## Real-World Example of Tracking in Daily Life

Understanding your menstrual cycle isn't just about periods; it's about overall wellness. For example, knowing you are entering your luteal phase might explain why you feel more tired or prone to cravings, allowing you to give yourself grace and adjust your routine accordingly. ## Menstrual Cycle vs. Period

| Term | Definition | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Menstrual Cycle** | The entire monthly process of hormonal changes and physical preparation for pregnancy. | 21-35 days (average 28) |
| **Period (Menstruation)** | The specific phase of the cycle where the uterine lining is shed as bleeding. | 3-7 days |
