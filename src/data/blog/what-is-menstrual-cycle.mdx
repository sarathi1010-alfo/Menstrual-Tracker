## What is a Menstrual Cycle?

The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal in healthy adults.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is broken down into four main phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg, driven by rising estrogen.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares the uterus for a potential fertilized egg; if none implants, hormone levels drop, triggering the next menstruation.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, menstruation might occur on Days 1-5. The follicular phase overlaps and continues until ovulation, which often happens around Day 14. The luteal phase then spans Days 15-28, ending just before the next period begins.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond reproduction, the menstrual cycle acts as a vital sign. Regular, healthy cycles indicate that your hormones are balanced. Irregularities can be the first sign of underlying health issues like thyroid dysfunction, stress, or PCOS.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days (the entire month) | 12-24 hours |
| **Function** | Overall preparation for pregnancy | The specific release of the egg |
| **Result if no pregnancy** | Menstruation (a period) | The egg dissolves |
