The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four main phases: menstruation (the shedding of the uterine lining), the follicular phase (preparation of an egg), ovulation (release of the egg), and the luteal phase (preparation of the uterus for a potential fertilized egg).

## Real-World Example

Consider a typical 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** Menstruation (bleeding) occurs.
*   **Days 1-13:** The follicular phase, overlapping with menstruation.
*   **Day 14:** Ovulation, when the egg is released.
*   **Days 15-28:** The luteal phase, leading up to the next period if pregnancy doesn't occur.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond fertility, the menstrual cycle is often considered a "fifth vital sign" because its regularity (or lack thereof) can provide significant insights into a woman's overall health and well-being.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process | A single event within the cycle |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
