## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The menstrual cycle is a complex biological process controlled by the endocrine system. It operates in a continuous loop comprising four phases: Menstruation, the Follicular phase, Ovulation, and the Luteal phase. Hormones like estrogen and progesterone act as messengers, signaling the ovaries to release an egg and the uterus to thicken its lining in preparation for a fertilized egg.

## Real-World Example

Consider a typical 28-day cycle:
- **Days 1-5:** Menstruation (bleeding occurs).
- **Days 1-13:** Follicular phase (overlapping with menstruation, an egg matures).
- **Day 14:** Ovulation (the egg is released).
- **Days 15-28:** Luteal phase (the uterine lining thickens, then breaks down if no pregnancy occurs).

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

The menstrual cycle is not just about reproduction. It is a vital sign of overall health. Regular cycles indicate that the hormonal system is functioning correctly, supporting bone density, heart health, and mental well-being. ## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process | A single event within the cycle |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| **Purpose** | Preparation for pregnancy | Release of an egg |
| **Hormone Driver** | Estrogen and Progesterone | Luteinizing Hormone (LH) surge |
