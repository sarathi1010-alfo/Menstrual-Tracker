## What is a Menstrual Cycle?

The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four main phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of a mature egg.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy; if none occurs, the cycle restarts.

Read a more detailed breakdown in our guide: [Cycle Phases Explained](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal).

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, menstruation might occur from days 1 to 5. The follicular phase overlaps this and continues until day 13. Ovulation happens around day 14. Finally, the luteal phase spans days 15 to 28, leading up to the next period.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

The menstrual cycle is crucial for reproductive health. It is not only the mechanism for fertility but also serves as a vital sign for your overall well-being. Regular cycles generally indicate balanced hormones, while irregularities can signal underlying health issues.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Definition | The entire monthly process (21-35 days). | The specific event of releasing an egg. |
| Duration | Weeks | 12-24 hours |
| Purpose | Preparation for potential pregnancy. | Providing the egg for fertilization. |
