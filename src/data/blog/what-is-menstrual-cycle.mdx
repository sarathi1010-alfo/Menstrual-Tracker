# What is a Menstrual Cycle?

The menstrual cycle is a series of natural changes in hormone production and the structures of the uterus and ovaries of the female reproductive system that make pregnancy possible. The cycle is required for the production of eggs, and for the preparation of the uterus for pregnancy.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is regulated by the complex interaction of hormones: luteinizing hormone (LH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), and the female sex hormones estrogen and progesterone.

The cycle is typically divided into four phases:
1.  **Menstrual Phase (Days 1-5):** The elimination of the thickened lining of the uterus (endometrium) from the body through the vagina.
2.  **Follicular Phase (Days 1-13):** The pituitary gland releases FSH, stimulating the ovaries to produce follicles. One follicle will mature into an egg.
3.  **Ovulation Phase (Day 14):** A surge in LH causes the ovary to release the mature egg.
4.  **Luteal Phase (Days 15-28):** After releasing the egg, the ruptured follicle develops into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone to prepare the uterine lining for a fertilized egg.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Period

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Period (Menstruation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process preparing for pregnancy. | The bleeding phase of the cycle. |
| **Duration** | Typically 21-35 days. | Typically 3-7 days. |
| **What happens** | Hormonal fluctuations, egg maturation, ovulation, and uterine lining changes. | Shedding of the uterine lining. |
