The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal. It is driven by the complex interaction of hormones from the brain and ovaries.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is a continuous loop broken down into four distinct [cycle phases](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal). It begins with menstruation (the period), where the lining of the uterus sheds. Following this, the follicular phase begins, where the body prepares an egg for release. Ovulation is the brief moment the egg is released. Finally, the luteal phase prepares the uterus for a potential fertilized egg. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the cycle begins again.

## Real-World Example

Imagine a typical 28-day cycle. Day 1 is the first day of bleeding. Menstruation might last from Day 1 to Day 5. The follicular phase runs concurrently, leading up to ovulation, which typically happens around Day 14. After Day 14, the luteal phase begins and lasts until Day 28. If no egg is fertilized, hormones drop, and a new Day 1 begins.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond its role in reproduction, the menstrual cycle acts as a vital sign for overall health. Regular, predictable cycles indicate that the endocrine system is functioning properly. Irregularities can be the first clue to conditions like PCOS, thyroid disorders, or high stress levels.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

It is common to confuse these terms, but they represent different things.

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **What it is** | The entire monthly process. | A single event within that process. |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days. | 12-24 hours. |
| **Purpose** | Prepare the body for pregnancy and shed lining if none occurs. | Release a mature egg for fertilization. |
| **Tracking** | Tracked by counting days from one period to the next using an app like [LunaCycle](/). | Tracked via BBT, LH strips, or cervical mucus. |
