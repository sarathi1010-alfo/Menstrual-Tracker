The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four main phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** Shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of the egg from the ovary.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential fertilized egg to implant.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, menstruation might occur on days 1-5. The follicular phase overlaps and continues until ovulation around day 14. The luteal phase then lasts from day 15 to 28, ending just before the next period begins.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a vital sign of reproductive and overall health. Regular cycles indicate that the hormonal system is functioning correctly, while irregularities can point to underlying health conditions.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | The entire month-long process of reproductive changes. | A single event where an egg is released. |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days. | 12-24 hours. |
