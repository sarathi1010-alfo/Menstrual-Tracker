The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by fluctuating hormones and is divided into four distinct phases. It begins with menstruation (the shedding of the uterine lining). This is followed by the follicular phase, where the body prepares an egg for release. Next comes ovulation, when the egg is released. Finally, the luteal phase prepares the uterus for a fertilized egg. For a deeper dive into these phases, read our article on [cycle phases explained](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal).

## Real-World Example

Consider a person with an average 28-day cycle. Day 1 through 5 is menstruation. The follicular phase overlaps and continues until around Day 14, when ovulation occurs. Days 15 through 28 make up the luteal phase, ending just before the next period begins.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a key indicator of overall reproductive health. Regular cycles suggest that the body is functioning normally and ovulating correctly. Tracking your cycle with a tool like [LunaCycle](/) helps you predict periods, manage symptoms, and understand your fertility window.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Definition | The complete monthly process of preparation for pregnancy. | The specific event of releasing an egg from the ovary. |
| Duration | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| Timing | Continuous | Occurs roughly mid-cycle |

### Core Principles
*   The cycle is a continuous monthly loop.
*   It consists of four main phases.
*   Hormones dictate the changes in each phase.
*   Tracking helps identify personal norms.
