The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four main phases. It begins with **Menstruation** (your period), where the uterine lining sheds. This overlaps with the **Follicular phase**, where an egg matures in the ovary. Around the middle of the cycle, **Ovulation** occurs, releasing the mature egg. Finally, the **Luteal phase** prepares the uterus for a potential fertilized egg. If pregnancy doesn't happen, the cycle starts again.

## Real-World Example

Imagine a typical 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** Menstruation (bleeding occurs).
*   **Days 1-13:** Follicular phase (egg matures).
*   **Day 14:** Ovulation (egg is released).
*   **Days 15-28:** Luteal phase (uterine lining thickens).

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond its role in reproduction, the menstrual cycle is considered a vital sign of overall health. Regular cycles generally indicate that your hormones are balanced and your reproductive system is functioning correctly.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process of preparation for pregnancy. | A single event within the cycle: the release of an egg. |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days on average. | Occurs on a single day (egg lives 12-24 hours). |
| **Purpose** | To mature an egg and prepare the uterus. | To make the egg available for fertilization. |
