## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The menstrual cycle is orchestrated by complex hormonal changes and is generally divided into four phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3. **Ovulation:** The release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy; if no pregnancy occurs, the cycle starts again.

## Real-World Example

Consider a typical 28-day cycle:
- **Days 1-5:** Menstruation (bleeding occurs).
- **Days 1-13:** Follicular phase (overlapping with menstruation, an egg matures).
- **Day 14:** Ovulation (the egg is released).
- **Days 15-28:** Luteal phase (the uterine lining thickens).

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Your menstrual cycle is a vital sign of your reproductive health. Regular cycles generally indicate that your body is ovulating and producing hormones as expected. Irregularities can sometimes be the first sign of underlying health conditions.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process | A single event within the cycle |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| **Purpose** | Prepare body for pregnancy | Release an egg for fertilization |
