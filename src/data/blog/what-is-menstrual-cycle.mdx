# What is a Menstrual Cycle?

**The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.**

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is controlled by a complex interaction of hormones, including estrogen and progesterone. It consists of four distinct phases:
1.  **Menstrual Phase:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** Preparation for an egg to be released.
3.  **Ovulation Phase:** The release of the mature egg.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
*   Days 1-5: Menstruation occurs.
*   Days 1-13: The follicular phase builds up the uterine lining.
*   Day 14: Ovulation happens.
*   Days 15-28: The luteal phase prepares the body for potential implantation.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

The menstrual cycle is a vital sign of reproductive health. Regular cycles indicate that the hormonal system is functioning correctly, while irregularities can signal underlying health issues or stress.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days (entire process) | 12-24 hours (specific event) |
| **Purpose** | Prepare body for potential pregnancy | Release an egg for fertilization |
| **Key Hormones** | Estrogen, Progesterone, LH, FSH | Luteinizing Hormone (LH) surge |
