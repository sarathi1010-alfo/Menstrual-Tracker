## Definition
The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?
The cycle is driven by hormones and divided into four phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3. **Ovulation:** The release of the mature egg.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example
In a typical 28-day cycle:
- Days 1-5: Menstruation occurs.
- Days 1-13: The follicular phase overlaps and continues, preparing an egg.
- Day 14: Ovulation happens.
- Days 15-28: The luteal phase readies the uterus; if no pregnancy occurs, the cycle restarts.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?
It is a crucial indicator of reproductive health and overall well-being. Tracking it helps you understand your body and predict your fertility window.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---------|-----------------|-----------|
| What is it? | The entire monthly process | A single event within the cycle |
| Duration | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| Purpose | Preparation for potential pregnancy | Release of an egg |
