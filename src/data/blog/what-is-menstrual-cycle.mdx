The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four main phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3. **Ovulation:** The release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy; if none occurs, the lining breaks down, starting the cycle again.

## Real-World Example

Imagine a classic 28-day cycle:
- **Days 1-5:** Menstruation occurs.
- **Days 6-13:** The follicular phase continues, building up estrogen.
- **Day 14:** Ovulation happens.
- **Days 15-28:** The luteal phase takes over, dominated by progesterone, leading up to the next period.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond fertility, the menstrual cycle is often considered a "fifth vital sign." Regular cycles generally indicate that your hormones are balanced and your reproductive system is functioning correctly. Irregularities can be the first clue to underlying health issues like thyroid problems or PCOS.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process (21-35 days) | A single event (release of an egg) |
| **Duration** | Weeks | 12-24 hours |
| **Purpose** | Preparation for potential pregnancy | Providing the egg for fertilization |
