The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It is driven by fluctuations in hormones. While it lasts about 28 days on average, anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered a normal length.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is divided into four main phases, each controlled by specific hormones:
1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The brain signals the ovaries to prepare an egg, while estrogen rises to thicken the uterine lining.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** Progesterone rises to maintain the uterine lining. If pregnancy does not occur, hormone levels drop, triggering menstruation.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** Menstruation occurs.
*   **Days 1-13:** The follicular phase overlaps with menstruation and continues until ovulation.
*   **Day 14:** Ovulation occurs (the egg is released).
*   **Days 15-28:** The luteal phase.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

The cycle is a vital sign of female reproductive health. Regular cycles generally indicate that the body is ovulating and producing a healthy balance of hormones. Tracking the cycle helps individuals predict periods, manage symptoms, and identify fertility windows.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process (21-35 days). | A single event within the cycle. |
| **Duration** | Weeks. | ~24 hours. |
| **Function** | Preparation for potential pregnancy. | Release of the egg. |
