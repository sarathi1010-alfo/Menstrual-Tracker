The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?
The cycle is driven by hormonal changes and is divided into four main phases: Menstruation, the Follicular phase, Ovulation, and the Luteal phase. Each phase plays a specific role in maturing an egg and preparing the uterus.

## Real-World Example
In a standard 28-day cycle, you might menstruate from Day 1 to Day 5. The follicular phase runs concurrently but lasts until about Day 13. Ovulation occurs around Day 14, followed by the luteal phase from Day 15 to Day 28.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?
Beyond fertility, the menstrual cycle is often considered a "fifth vital sign" because its regularity (or lack thereof) provides critical insight into overall health, hormone balance, and stress levels.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Definition | The entire monthly process | A single event (release of an egg) |
| Duration | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| Function | Preparation for pregnancy | Releasing the egg for fertilization |
