The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four main phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of the mature egg.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy. If none occurs, the cycle starts again.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** Menstruation (bleeding occurs).
*   **Days 1-13:** The Follicular phase (overlapping with menstruation).
*   **Day 14:** Ovulation (the egg is released).
*   **Days 15-28:** The Luteal phase.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond reproductive health, the menstrual cycle acts as a vital sign. Regular cycles indicate that your hormones are relatively balanced and your reproductive system is functioning correctly. Irregularities can point to underlying health issues like stress, thyroid problems, or PCOS.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process (approx 28 days). | A single event within the cycle (release of the egg). |
| **Duration** | 21 - 35 days. | 12 - 24 hours (lifespan of the egg). |
| **Function** | Prepares the body for pregnancy. | Provides the egg needed for fertilization. |
