# What is a Menstrual Cycle?

The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four main phases: the menstrual phase (bleeding), the follicular phase (preparing an egg), ovulation (releasing the egg), and the luteal phase (preparing the uterus for a fertilized egg). For a deeper dive, see our article on [cycle phases](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal).

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
- Days 1-5: Menstruation occurs.
- Days 1-13: The follicular phase prepares for ovulation.
- Day 14: Ovulation happens.
- Days 15-28: The luteal phase occurs before the next cycle begins.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a crucial indicator of reproductive health and fertility. Regular cycles generally suggest balanced hormones and normal ovulation, acting as a vital sign for a woman's overall well-being.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duration | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| Function | Prepares body for pregnancy | Releases the egg |
| Focus | Overall reproductive system | Ovaries |
