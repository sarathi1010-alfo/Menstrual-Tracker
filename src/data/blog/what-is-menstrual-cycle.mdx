The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and divided into four phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** Ovaries prepare to release an egg.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of the egg.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy; if none occurs, the cycle starts again.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** Menstruation (bleeding).
*   **Days 1-13:** Follicular phase (overlapping with menstruation).
*   **Day 14:** Ovulation occurs.
*   **Days 15-28:** Luteal phase.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a key indicator of reproductive health. A regular cycle suggests hormonal balance and normal ovulation, which is vital for fertility. Irregularities can be early warning signs of underlying health conditions.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process of preparation and shedding. | A single event within the cycle: the release of an egg. |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| **Significance** | Overall reproductive health indicator. | The window of peak fertility. |
