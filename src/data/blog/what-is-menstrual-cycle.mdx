## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The menstrual cycle is driven by complex hormonal changes. It consists of four main phases: Menstruation (shedding of the uterine lining), the Follicular phase (maturing an egg in the ovary), Ovulation (releasing the mature egg), and the Luteal phase (preparing the uterus for a potential fertilized egg). If pregnancy does not occur, hormone levels drop, triggering menstruation and starting the cycle anew.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, a person might experience menstruation from days 1 to 5. Their follicular phase continues until around day 14, when ovulation occurs. The luteal phase spans days 15 through 28, often accompanied by PMS symptoms in the final days, before a new cycle begins on day 1.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond its role in reproduction, the menstrual cycle is a crucial indicator of overall health. Regular, healthy cycles suggest balanced hormones and a functioning reproductive system, while persistent irregularities can signal underlying health conditions.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | ~28 days (varies) | ~1 day |
| Purpose | Complete reproductive preparation | Release of a single egg |
