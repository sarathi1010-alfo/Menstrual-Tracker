The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman&apos;s body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by fluctuating hormones and is divided into four main phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3. **Ovulation:** The release of the mature egg from the ovary.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy before the cycle starts again.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, menstruation might last from days 1 to 5. The follicular phase runs concurrently, leading up to ovulation around day 14. The luteal phase then spans days 15 through 28. If pregnancy does not occur, the cycle resets on day 1 of the next month.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a crucial indicator of reproductive health and overall well-being. Regular cycles suggest that the body&apos;s hormonal systems are functioning correctly, while irregularities can signal stress, nutritional deficits, or conditions like PCOS.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | The entire monthly process | A single event within the cycle |
| Duration | 21 - 35 days | 12 - 24 hours |
| Purpose | Prepares body for pregnancy | Releases an egg for fertilization |
