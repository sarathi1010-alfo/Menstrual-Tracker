**Definition:** The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and involves four distinct phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3. **Ovulation:** The release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy. If none occurs, the cycle starts over.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, menstruation might happen on days 1-5. The follicular phase overlaps this and continues until ovulation, which occurs around day 14. The luteal phase then spans days 15-28. Tracking these days is simple with a private tool like [LunaCycle](/).

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond reproductive health, the cycle acts as a vital sign. Regular cycles generally indicate healthy hormone levels, while irregularities can point to stress, nutritional deficits, or conditions like PCOS.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| Function | Prepares body for pregnancy | Releases the egg for fertilization |
| Scope | The entire monthly process | A single event within the cycle |
