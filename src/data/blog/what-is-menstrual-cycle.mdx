The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and divided into four phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** Hormones stimulate the ovaries to produce a mature egg.
3. **Ovulation:** The mature egg is released from the ovary.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for potential implantation; if it doesn't occur, the cycle restarts.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, menstruation might last days 1-5. The follicular phase runs concurrently and continues until ovulation around day 14. The luteal phase spans days 15-28, ending just before the next period begins.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

Beyond reproductive capabilities, the cycle is an indicator of overall health. Regular cycles suggest balanced hormones, while irregularities can signal stress, thyroid issues, or conditions like PCOS.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process (approx 28 days) | The specific event of releasing an egg |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| **Purpose** | Preparation for pregnancy and health regulation | Providing the egg for fertilization |
