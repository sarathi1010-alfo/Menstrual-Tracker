## Definition
The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It is regulated by hormones and lasts about 28 days on average, although anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal for adults.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?
The cycle is divided into four main phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2. **Follicular Phase:** The preparation of a new egg in the ovary.
3. **Ovulation:** The release of the mature egg.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The preparation of the uterus for a potential fertilized egg.

## Real-World Example
In a typical 28-day cycle, menstruation might last from day 1 to 5. The follicular phase overlaps and continues until ovulation around day 14. The luteal phase then runs from day 15 to 28, ending just before the next period begins.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?
The menstrual cycle is a crucial indicator of reproductive health and overall well-being. It determines your fertility and can provide early warnings for hormonal imbalances or other medical conditions.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation
| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| Function | Prepares body for pregnancy | Releases an egg |
| Frequency | Monthly | Once per cycle |
