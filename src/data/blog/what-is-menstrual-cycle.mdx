## Definition

The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of physiological changes that occur in the female reproductive system to prepare the body for a potential pregnancy. It is driven by fluctuations in hormones, primarily estrogen and progesterone.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is a continuous loop of hormonal feedback involving the brain (hypothalamus and pituitary gland) and the ovaries. It is divided into two main halves:

1. **The Follicular Phase:** Beginning on the first day of your period, this phase is characterized by rising estrogen levels which stimulate the growth of ovarian follicles and thicken the uterine lining.
2. **The Luteal Phase:** Occurring after ovulation, this phase is dominated by progesterone, which stabilizes the uterine lining. If a fertilized egg does not implant, hormone levels drop, leading to menstruation and the start of a new cycle.
