## What is a Menstrual Cycle?

The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It is driven by hormonal fluctuations and lasts about 28 days on average, though anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is divided into four main phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of the egg from the ovary.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** Menstruation (bleeding occurs).
*   **Days 1-13:** Follicular phase (overlapping with menstruation).
*   **Day 14:** Ovulation (the egg is released).
*   **Days 15-28:** Luteal phase (leading up to the next period).

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

The menstrual cycle is a crucial indicator of reproductive health and overall well-being. Regular cycles suggest that the body's hormonal systems are functioning correctly, while irregular cycles can be an early sign of health issues.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire month-long process. | A specific event within the cycle. |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days. | 12-24 hours. |
| **Purpose** | To prepare for potential pregnancy. | To release an egg for fertilization. |
