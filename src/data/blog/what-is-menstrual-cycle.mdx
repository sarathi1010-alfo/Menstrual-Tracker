The menstrual cycle is a monthly series of hormonal changes that prepare a person's body for a potential pregnancy. It is measured from the first day of one period (Day 1) to the first day of the next period.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by the rise and fall of hormones like estrogen and progesterone. It involves two main processes: the maturation and release of an egg (ovulation) and the preparation of the uterine lining (the endometrium). If the egg is not fertilized, the lining is shed during menstruation.

## Real-World Example of the Menstrual Cycle

If your period starts on January 1st and your next period starts on January 29th, your menstrual cycle was 28 days long. During those 28 days, your body went through four distinct phases, likely ovulating around January 14th or 15th.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is often called the "fifth vital sign" because its regularity and characteristics can provide deep insights into your overall hormonal, bone, and cardiovascular health.
