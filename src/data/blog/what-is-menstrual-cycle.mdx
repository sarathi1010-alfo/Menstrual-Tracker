## Definition

The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is broken down into four main phases: Menstruation (bleeding), the Follicular phase (egg preparation), Ovulation (egg release), and the Luteal phase (uterine lining stabilization). For a deeper dive into these stages, read our [Cycle Phases Explained](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal) guide.

## Real-World Example

In a standard 28-day cycle:
*   **Days 1-5:** Menstruation (your period).
*   **Days 1-13:** Follicular phase.
*   **Day 14:** Ovulation.
*   **Days 15-28:** Luteal phase.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is often referred to as a "fifth vital sign." Beyond reproductive health and fertility, a regular cycle indicates that your hormones are balanced and your body is functioning correctly.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **What is it?** | The entire month-long process. | A single event within the cycle. |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days. | 12-24 hours. |
| **Purpose** | Prepare body for pregnancy. | Release the egg for fertilization. |
