## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?
The menstrual cycle is a complex, carefully orchestrated process controlled by the endocrine system. It involves the rising and falling of several key hormones, including estrogen, progesterone, luteinizing hormone (LH), and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). These hormones work together to mature an egg in the ovaries, thicken the lining of the uterus to prepare for a fertilized egg, and then trigger the shedding of that lining if pregnancy does not occur. This shedding is what we experience as a period. The cycle is generally divided into four distinct phases: menstruation, the follicular phase, ovulation, and the luteal phase.

## Real-World Example
Consider a standard 28-day cycle. Day 1 is the first day of bright red bleeding (menstruation). This bleeding might last until Day 5. From Day 1 through roughly Day 13, the body is in the follicular phase, preparing a new egg. Around Day 14, ovulation occurs, releasing the egg. From Day 15 to Day 28, the body is in the luteal phase, maintaining the thickened uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, hormone levels drop, and a new cycle begins on Day 1 of the next month.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?
Beyond its role in reproduction, the menstrual cycle is a vital sign of overall health. A regular cycle typically indicates that your hormones are in balance and that your body is functioning correctly. Disruptions in the cycle can be early warning signs of stress, nutritional deficiencies, thyroid issues, or reproductive disorders like PCOS. Tracking your cycle helps you establish what is normal for your unique body.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation
| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | The entire monthly process | A single event within the cycle |
| Duration | 21-35 days | 12-24 hours |
| Purpose | Preparation for potential pregnancy | Release of the mature egg |
