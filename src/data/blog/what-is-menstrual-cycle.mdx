**Definition:** The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and involves four main phases:
1. **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining.
2. **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3. **Ovulation:** An egg is released from the ovary.
4. **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle:
- **Days 1-5:** Menstruation occurs.
- **Days 1-13:** The follicular phase overlaps and continues, building up the uterine lining.
- **Day 14:** Ovulation happens.
- **Days 15-28:** The luteal phase readies the body; if no pregnancy occurs, the cycle restarts.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a crucial indicator of overall reproductive health. Regular cycles suggest that the body's hormones are functioning normally.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
|---|---|
| The entire ~28-day process | A single event within the cycle |
| Involves multiple hormonal shifts | Specifically triggered by an LH surge |
| Prepares the body for pregnancy | The actual release of the egg |
