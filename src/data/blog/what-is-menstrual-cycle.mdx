## Definition

The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal. During this time, hormones fluctuate, causing an egg to develop and be released, while the uterus prepares for a potential embryo.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is broken down into four distinct phases:
1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** Estrogen rises, and an egg matures in the ovary.
3.  **Ovulation:** The mature egg is released into the fallopian tube.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** Progesterone rises to support a potential pregnancy; if no pregnancy occurs, hormone levels drop, triggering menstruation again.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, menstruation might last from days 1 to 5. The follicular phase overlaps, lasting from day 1 to 13. Ovulation typically occurs around day 14. Finally, the luteal phase lasts from day 15 to 28, bringing the cycle to a close before starting anew.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is the primary mechanism for human reproduction. Beyond fertility, the menstrual cycle acts as a "fifth vital sign." Regular cycles indicate that your hormonal systems are functioning correctly, while irregular cycles can be an early warning sign for conditions like thyroid disease or polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS).

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **What is it?** | A month-long process preparing the body for pregnancy. | A single event where an egg is released. |
| **Duration** | 21 - 35 days. | 12 - 24 hours. |
| **Primary Goal** | Complete reproductive cycle management. | Releasing an egg for fertilization. |
