## What is a Menstrual Cycle?

The menstrual cycle is the monthly series of changes a woman's body goes through to prepare for possible pregnancy. It lasts about 28 days on average, but anywhere from 21 to 35 days is considered normal.

## How Does the Menstrual Cycle Work?

The cycle is driven by hormones and is divided into four main phases:

1.  **Menstruation:** The shedding of the uterine lining (your period).
2.  **Follicular Phase:** The body prepares to release an egg.
3.  **Ovulation:** The release of a mature egg from the ovary.
4.  **Luteal Phase:** The body prepares for a potential pregnancy. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the cycle starts over.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, menstruation might last from days 1 to 5. The follicular phase runs concurrently, leading up to ovulation around day 14. Following ovulation, the luteal phase spans days 15 to 28. Remember, this is just an example, and individual cycles vary.

## Why is the Menstrual Cycle Important?

It is a crucial indicator of your reproductive and overall health. Regular cycles suggest that your hormones are functioning correctly and that you are ovulating, which is essential for fertility.

## Menstrual Cycle vs Ovulation

| Feature | Menstrual Cycle | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The entire monthly process. | A specific event within the cycle. |
| **Duration** | 21-35 days (average). | 12-24 hours. |
| **Purpose** | Preparation for pregnancy. | Release of an egg for fertilization. |
