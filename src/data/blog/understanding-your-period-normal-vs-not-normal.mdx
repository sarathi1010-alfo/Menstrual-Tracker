## Why Knowing Normal Matters

When it comes to menstruation, "normal" is a broad spectrum. What's normal for your best friend might not be normal for you. However, there are clinical guidelines that define healthy parameters. Knowing these parameters is crucial because your period acts as a barometer for your overall health. Recognizing when your cycle falls outside the normal range is the first step in seeking appropriate medical care and preventing long-term complications.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine if your period is healthy, you need to assess four key factors over several months using a tracker like [LunaCycle](/).

1.  **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next. The healthy range is 21 to 35 days.
2.  **Duration of Bleeding:** Note how many days you actively bleed. The healthy range is 2 to 7 days.
3.  **Flow Volume:** On average, a woman loses 2 to 3 tablespoons (30-45 ml) of blood per cycle. Needing to change a regular pad or tampon every 3-4 hours is typical.
4.  **Pain Level:** Mild cramping and discomfort in the lower abdomen or back are normal, caused by prostaglandins helping the uterus contract.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

If you experience any of the following, it's time to pay closer attention to your logs and consider speaking with a healthcare professional.

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding between expected periods (intermenstrual bleeding) is not typical and should be evaluated to rule out polyps, fibroids, or infections.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through your hygiene products every hour, or passing clots larger than a quarter, you may be experiencing menorrhagia, which can lead to anemia.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain
Cramps that cause vomiting, require prescription painkillers, or prevent you from going to work or school are not normal and may indicate conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
If your cycles are wildly unpredictable—sometimes 20 days, sometimes 50 days—it could indicate anovulation (not releasing an egg) or conditions like PCOS.

### 5. Absence of Periods
Missing your period for more than three months (when not pregnant or menopausal) is known as amenorrhea and requires medical investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **No period for 90+ days** | Take a pregnancy test; if negative, see a doctor. |
| **Bleeding after menopause** | Seek immediate medical evaluation. |
| **Sudden onset of severe pain** | Contact a healthcare provider promptly. |
| **Fever or unusual discharge** | See a doctor to rule out infection (e.g., PID or TSS). |
| **Periods lasting longer than 7 days** | Schedule an appointment to investigate the cause. |
