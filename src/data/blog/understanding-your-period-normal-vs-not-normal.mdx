## Why Knowing Normal Matters

What is "normal" for one person might be different for another. However, establishing your personal baseline and knowing the general medical parameters of a healthy menstrual cycle is crucial. Recognizing when your period deviates from these norms is often the first step in identifying underlying health issues such as hormonal imbalances, thyroid problems, or reproductive disorders.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine if your period is normal, you need to track four key metrics consistently:
1. **Cycle Length:** Ensure it falls roughly between 21 and 35 days.
2. **Duration of Bleeding:** Should typically last 2 to 7 days.
3. **Flow Intensity:** Note if you are soaking through products unusually fast.
4. **Pain Levels:** Monitor severity of cramps and if they respond to basic pain relief.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding outside of your normal menstruation window should always be checked by a healthcare provider.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia)
If you need to change your pad or tampon every hour, or if you are passing large clots, this is considered abnormally heavy.

### 3. Severe Pain (Dysmenorrhea)
Debilitating pain that causes you to miss work or school is not a normal part of menstruation.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
If your cycle length constantly fluctuates by more than 7-9 days each month, it's considered irregular.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row (when not pregnant or on continuous birth control) warrants medical investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding through products hourly | Seek immediate medical evaluation. |
| No period for >90 days | Schedule a routine checkup. |
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain | Go to urgent care or the emergency room. |
