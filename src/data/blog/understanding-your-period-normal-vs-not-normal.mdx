## Why Knowing Normal Matters

What is considered a 'normal' period can vary significantly from person to person. However, establishing your own baseline is crucial for early detection of potential health issues. By knowing what is typical for you in terms of length, flow, and symptoms, you can quickly identify when something is amiss.

## How to Assess Your Period

To assess your period, track the following metrics over several months using [LunaCycle](/):

*   **Cycle Length:** Count from the first day of one period to the first day of the next (normal is 21-35 days).
*   **Period Duration:** How many days you bleed (normal is 2-7 days).
*   **Flow Intensity:** Light, medium, or heavy. Note how often you change your pad/tampon.
*   **Pain Level:** Monitor the severity and duration of any cramping.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Spotting or bleeding when you are not on your period should always be investigated.

### 2. Heavy bleeding
Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several consecutive hours.

### 3. Severe pain
Cramping that is debilitating, causes nausea, or doesn't respond to typical pain relievers.

### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles that are consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or highly unpredictable.

### 5. Absence of period (Amenorrhea)
Missing your period for 3 or more months (if not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal).

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Bleeding through 1 pad/tampon per hour | Seek prompt medical evaluation. |
| Debilitating cramps | Consult your doctor to rule out endometriosis or fibroids. |
| Sudden change in regularity | Discuss with your healthcare provider. |
| Passing clots larger than a quarter | Mention at your next appointment. |
