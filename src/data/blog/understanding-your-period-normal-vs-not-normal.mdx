A regular period generally occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has a moderate flow. However, "normal" is highly individual. Understanding your unique baseline is crucial because significant deviations can signal underlying conditions.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Your menstrual cycle is often considered the fifth vital sign. When something is off with your overall health—be it stress, nutrition, or an underlying medical issue—your period is often the first thing to show it. Knowing what is normal *for you* allows for early detection and intervention. ## How to Assess Your Period

To determine your normal, you need data. Track the following over several cycles:
*   **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next.
*   **Period Duration:** How many days does the bleeding last?
*   **Flow Intensity:** How many pads/tampons do you use on your heaviest days?
*   **Pain Levels:** Are cramps manageable with over-the-counter medication, or do they disrupt your life?

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
While some women experience slight spotting during ovulation, noticeable bleeding between periods or after sex should be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia)
If you need to change your pad or tampon every hour for consecutive hours, or if you pass clots larger than a quarter, your flow is abnormally heavy.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain (Dysmenorrhea)
Mild cramps are common, but pain that causes you to miss work or school, or isn't relieved by standard painkillers, is not normal and could indicate conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
While occasional variation (e.g., due to stress) happens, cycles that consistently fall outside the 21-35 day range, or vary wildly in length month-to-month, require investigation.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
If you miss three or more periods in a row (and are not pregnant or menopausal), it's a significant red flag that needs medical attention.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **No period for 90 days** | Schedule an appointment to check for underlying causes like PCOS or thyroid issues. |
| **Soaking a pad/tampon every hour** | Seek medical advice promptly to manage blood loss and identify the cause. |
| **Severe pain unresponsive to meds** | Consult a doctor to evaluate for conditions like endometriosis or fibroids. |
| **Bleeding between periods** | Have this evaluated to rule out infections, polyps, or other concerns. |
