## Why Knowing Normal Matters
What is "normal" can vary slightly from person to person, but establishing your own baseline is crucial. Knowing what a typical period looks like for you allows you to quickly identify when something is off, enabling early intervention if there's a developing health issue.

## How to Assess Your Period
To understand your period, you need to track several factors consistently using a tool like [LunaCycle](/):
*   **Cycle Length:** The number of days from the start of one period to the start of the next.
*   **Duration:** How many days the bleeding lasts.
*   **Flow:** How heavy the bleeding is (e.g., how often you need to change products).
*   **Pain:** The severity of any cramping or discomfort.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch
### 1. Bleeding between periods
Unexplained spotting or bleeding when you don't expect your period.
### 2. Heavy bleeding
Requiring you to change pads or tampons every hour for consecutive hours.
### 3. Severe pain
Cramping that interferes with your daily life and isn't relieved by over-the-counter medication.
### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles that are consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or fluctuate wildly.
### 5. Absence of period
Missing a period when you are not pregnant, especially for three or more consecutive months.

## When to See a Doctor
| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Unusually heavy flow | Consult a doctor to rule out fibroids, polyps, or bleeding disorders. |
| Debilitating cramps | Seek medical advice to investigate potential causes like endometriosis. |
| Periods that suddenly stop | Take a pregnancy test; if negative, consult a doctor after missing 3 cycles. |
