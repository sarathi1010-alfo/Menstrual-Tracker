## Why Knowing Normal Matters

What is "normal" for a menstrual cycle can vary widely from person to person. However, there are established clinical baselines. Knowing what falls within these baselines matters because your period is a vital sign of your overall health.

When you understand the parameters of a healthy cycle, you are better equipped to recognize early warning signs of underlying issues, such as thyroid disorders, PCOS, or endometriosis. It empowers you to seek medical advice promptly rather than dismissing potentially serious symptoms.

## How to Assess Your Period

To assess your period, you need to track several factors consistently over a few months using a tool like [LunaCycle](/):

- **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of your period to Day 1 of your next period. (Normal: 21-35 days).
- **Period Duration:** How many days do you bleed? (Normal: 2-7 days).
- **Flow Volume:** Are you changing products reasonably, or soaking through quickly?
- **Pain Levels:** Are cramps manageable with over-the-counter medication, or are they debilitating?

Establishing your personal baseline is the first step in recognizing when something is "off."

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Spotting or bleeding outside of your normal period (intermenstrual bleeding) can be caused by hormonal imbalances, fibroids, or other medical issues and should always be checked.

### 2. Heavy bleeding
Menorrhagia, or abnormally heavy bleeding, is when you soak through a pad or tampon every hour for consecutive hours. It can lead to anemia.

### 3. Severe pain
Dysmenorrhea (severe cramps) that interferes with daily life, causes nausea, or doesn't respond to standard painkillers is a red flag for conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Irregular cycles
Consistently having cycles shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or cycles that vary wildly in length month-to-month.

### 5. Absence of period
Missing a period (amenorrhea) for three or more months when not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal requires investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Action Required |
| :--- | :--- |
| Soaking >1 pad/tampon per hour | Seek immediate medical evaluation. |
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain | Go to urgent care or emergency room. |
| Missing 3+ periods | Schedule an appointment with a gynecologist. |
| Bleeding after menopause | Contact a doctor immediately. |
