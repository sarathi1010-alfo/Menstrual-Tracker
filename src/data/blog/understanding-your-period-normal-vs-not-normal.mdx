## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what is 'normal' for a menstrual period provides a critical baseline for your health. It matters because the menstrual cycle is often considered a vital sign for women. Deviations from normal can be early indicators of thyroid issues, reproductive disorders like endometriosis, or hormonal imbalances. By knowing the standard parameters, you can identify anomalies quickly and seek timely medical advice.

## How to Assess Your Period

Assessing your period involves observing four key metrics over several months using a tool like [LunaCycle](/tracker):

1. **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next. Normal is 21-35 days.
2. **Duration:** How many days does the bleeding last? Normal is 2-7 days.
3. **Flow Intensity:** Are you soaking through a pad or tampon every hour? That is considered heavy. Moderate flow is normal.
4. **Pain Levels:** Note the severity of cramping. Mild to moderate discomfort is typical; agonizing pain is not.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding outside of your normal menstrual window (not related to ovulation spotting) should be evaluated.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you need to change your pad or tampon every hour for consecutive hours, or if you are passing clots larger than a quarter, consult a doctor.

### 3. Severe Pain (Dysmenorrhea)
Pain that causes nausea, vomiting, or forces you to miss work or school could be a sign of endometriosis or fibroids.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
While slight variations are normal, cycles that constantly shift by weeks or fall outside the 21-35 day range indicate hormonal disruption.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row (when not pregnant or menopausal) requires medical investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Action Required |
|---|---|
| Soaking a pad/tampon every hour | Seek immediate medical evaluation. |
| No period for 90 days | Schedule a doctor's appointment. |
| Severe pain unresponsive to meds | Consult a gynecologist to rule out endometriosis. |
| Sudden, unexplained irregularity | Discuss with a healthcare provider. |
