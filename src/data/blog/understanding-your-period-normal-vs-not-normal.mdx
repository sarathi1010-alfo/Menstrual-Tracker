## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Knowing what constitutes a 'normal' period is crucial for early detection of health issues. Your menstrual cycle is often described as a fifth vital sign; it reflects your overall health. By understanding the parameters of a healthy period, you can quickly identify when something is off and seek timely medical advice.

## How to Assess Your Period

To assess your period, track these key metrics:
- **Length**: How many days between Day 1 of one period and Day 1 of the next? (Normal: 21-35 days)
- **Duration**: How many days do you bleed? (Normal: 2-7 days)
- **Flow**: How many hygiene products do you use per day?
- **Pain level**: Rate your cramps on a scale of 1-10.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Spotting or bleeding when you aren't expecting your period should always be investigated by a doctor.

### 2. Heavy bleeding
If you need to change your pad or tampon every hour for several consecutive hours, or wake up at night to change protection, your flow may be too heavy (menorrhagia).

### 3. Severe pain
Debilitating cramps (dysmenorrhea) that prevent you from attending work or school are not normal and could indicate conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles that constantly vary in length by more than 7-9 days, or missing multiple periods in a row.

### 5. Absence of period
If your period stops entirely for more than three months (and you are not pregnant or menopausal), it's termed amenorrhea and requires evaluation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Missing period for >3 months | Schedule a doctor's visit |
| Soaking a pad/tampon every hour | Seek immediate medical attention |
| Severe pain not managed by OTC meds | Consult a gynecologist |
| Bleeding lasting longer than 7 days | Schedule a check-up |
