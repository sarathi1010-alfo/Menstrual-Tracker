A regular period generally occurs every 21 to 35 days, lasts between 2 and 7 days, and features a moderate blood flow. However, what is "normal" for one person might be different for another. The key is understanding *your* normal, so you can easily spot when things deviate.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Your menstrual cycle is often described as a fifth vital sign. When your body is functioning optimally, your cycle tends to be regular. When something is wrong—whether it is a hormonal imbalance, high stress, or a structural issue—your period is often the first thing to show signs of disruption. Knowing what is normal for you allows for the early detection of potential health issues.

For a foundational understanding of how the cycle works, read our [Menstrual Cycle 101](/blog/menstrual-cycle-101-beginners-guide).

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine your baseline normal, you need to track several factors consistently using a tool like [LunaCycle](/):

1.  **Cycle Length:** The number of days from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next. (Normal: 21-35 days)
2.  **Period Duration:** How many days you actually bleed. (Normal: 2-7 days)
3.  **Flow Volume:** How many pads or tampons you soak through. (Normal: Changing every 3-4 hours, not soaking through rapidly)
4.  **Pain Level:** The severity of your cramps. (Normal: Manageable with over-the-counter medication or rest)
5.  **Regularity:** How consistent your cycle length is from month to month. (Normal: Variation of 2-5 days is generally fine)

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

If you notice any of these signs, it is time to pay closer attention and likely consult a healthcare professional.

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding outside of your normal menstrual phase can be a sign of polyps, fibroids, infection, or hormonal issues. (Note: slight spotting during ovulation can sometimes be normal, but should still be tracked).

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for consecutive hours, or passing clots larger than a quarter, this is considered heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia) and requires medical evaluation.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain
While mild [cramps during your cycle](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal) are common, pain that causes you to miss work, school, or requires prescription medication is not normal and could indicate conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
If your cycles are consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or if the length varies wildly from month to month, it is worth investigating.

### 5. Sudden Absence of Period
If you miss three or more periods in a row (and are not pregnant or going through menopause), this is known as amenorrhea and requires a doctor's visit.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding through >1 pad/tampon per hour | Seek medical attention promptly. |
| Severe pain not relieved by standard NSAIDs | Schedule an appointment with a gynecologist. |
| Missing >3 periods in a row | Consult a healthcare provider for evaluation. |
| Bleeding after menopause has been established | Seek immediate medical evaluation. |

Always remember that tracking is a tool for awareness, not diagnosis. Share your [LunaCycle](/) data with your doctor to give them a clear picture of your symptoms. For more on general tracking, see our [ultimate cycle tracking guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide).
