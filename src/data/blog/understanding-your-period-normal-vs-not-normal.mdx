## Why Knowing "Normal" Matters

"Normal" is a spectrum, but knowing the medical baselines for a healthy menstrual cycle is crucial for early detection of reproductive health issues. What is normal for your friend might not be normal for you. Establishing your personal baseline by tracking with a tool like [LunaCycle](/) is the first step in recognizing when something is wrong.

## How to Assess Your Period

Evaluate your period based on these four pillars:
1. **Length**: The entire cycle should be 21-35 days.
2. **Duration**: Bleeding should last 2-7 days.
3. **Flow**: You should need to change a regular pad or tampon every 3-4 hours, not every hour.
4. **Pain**: Mild to moderate cramping is common; debilitating pain is not.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding outside of your normal menstrual window (intermenstrual bleeding) should be evaluated to rule out infections or polyps.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for consecutive hours, or passing clots larger than a quarter, this is considered heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia).

### 3. Severe Pain
Dysmenorrhea (severe cramps) that causes nausea, vomiting, or prevents you from functioning normally can be a sign of conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
Cycles that frequently fall outside the 21-35 day range, or vary in length by more than 7-9 days month-to-month.

### 5. Absence of Period
Missing three or more periods in a row (amenorrhea) when not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding lasting > 7 days | Schedule an appointment |
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain | Seek immediate medical care |
| Missed periods (> 90 days) | Schedule an appointment |
| Bleeding after menopause | Seek immediate medical care |
