## Why Knowing Normal Matters

What is "normal" varies greatly from person to person. However, there are clinical baselines. Knowing these baselines helps you determine when a change in your cycle is just a harmless fluctuation and when it's a signal to see a healthcare provider. Consistent tracking with [LunaCycle](/) is your best tool for establishing your personal baseline. For more on cycle basics, see [Menstrual Cycle 101](/blog/menstrual-cycle-101-beginners-guide).

## How to Assess Your Period

When evaluating your period, look at four key metrics:
1.  **Cycle Length:** The number of days from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next (21-35 days is typical).
2.  **Bleeding Duration:** How many days you bleed (2-7 days is typical).
3.  **Flow Volume:** How much you bleed (typically 2 to 3 tablespoons total).
4.  **Pain Levels:** Mild cramps are common; severe pain is a red flag.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding outside your normal window should always be checked by a doctor.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour or passing large clots, seek medical advice.

### 3. Severe Pain
Debilitating cramps (dysmenorrhea) can be a sign of conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
Cycles that fluctuate wildly in length or consistently fall outside the 21-35 day range.

### 5. Absence of Period
Missing three or more periods in a row (amenorrhea) when not pregnant.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding through a pad/tampon every hour | See a doctor promptly |
| Pain that disrupts daily life | Schedule an appointment |
| Bleeding after sex | Schedule an appointment |
| Period stops for >90 days | Consult your healthcare provider |
| Sudden, significant changes to your normal pattern | Discuss at your next checkup |
