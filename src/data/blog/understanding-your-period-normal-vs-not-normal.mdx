A regular period occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has moderate flow. Deviations can signal underlying conditions.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what constitutes a 'normal' period is the foundation of reproductive self-care. Because every body is different, what is normal for your friend might not be normal for you. However, there are established medical baselines. Knowing these baselines helps you distinguish between a harmless, temporary change (like a slightly late period due to stress) and a potential warning sign of an underlying health issue. ## How to Assess Your Period

To assess whether your period is healthy, you need to evaluate four main factors: length, flow, pain, and regularity.

1. **Length:** A normal cycle (from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next) is 21-35 days. The bleeding itself should last 2-7 days.
2. **Flow:** Normal blood loss is around 30-50ml (about 2-3 tablespoons) per cycle. If you need to change a soaked pad or tampon every hour for consecutive hours, your flow is considered heavy.
3. **Pain:** Mild cramping on the first day or two is common as the uterus contracts to shed its lining. However, pain that requires strong medication or causes you to miss work or school is not normal.
4. **Regularity:** While a variation of a few days from month to month is perfectly normal, your cycle length should generally be consistent.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Unexplained spotting or bleeding between your periods can be a sign of hormonal imbalance, polyps, or other conditions.

### 2. Heavy bleeding (Menorrhagia)
Soaking through feminine hygiene products every hour or passing large blood clots (larger than a quarter) requires medical evaluation.

### 3. Severe pain (Dysmenorrhea)
Intense, debilitating cramps could be a symptom of conditions like endometriosis or fibroids.

### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or cycles that vary wildly in length month-to-month, should be discussed with a doctor.

### 5. Absence of period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row (when not pregnant or on certain contraceptives) is a significant warning sign.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Sudden, severe changes to your cycle | Schedule an appointment soon. |
| Bleeding after menopause | See a doctor immediately. |
| Periods that suddenly stop | Take a pregnancy test; if negative and periods don't return, see a doctor. |
| Severe pain that OTC meds don't help | Consult a healthcare provider. |
