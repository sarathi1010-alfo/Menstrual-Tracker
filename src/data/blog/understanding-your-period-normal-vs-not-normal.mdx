## Why Knowing Normal Matters
Understanding what constitutes a "normal" period is crucial for your health. Your menstrual cycle is often considered the fifth vital sign. When you know your baseline, you can easily spot when something deviates from it. This early detection is key for diagnosing conditions like PCOS, endometriosis, or fibroids, and ensures you get the medical care you need promptly.

## How to Assess Your Period
To determine what's normal for you, track these key metrics for at least three months using [LunaCycle](/tracker):
- **Cycle Length:** From Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next (average is 21-35 days).
- **Period Duration:** How many days you bleed (average is 2-7 days).
- **Flow Intensity:** How often you need to change your menstrual products.
- **Pain Level:** Note the severity of any cramping or discomfort.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch
If you experience any of these, it's time to pay close attention:

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding outside of your normal period (intermenstrual bleeding) can be a sign of infection, hormonal imbalance, or other issues.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours in a row, or passing large clots (bigger than a quarter), this is considered menorrhagia and needs medical attention.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain
While mild cramps are normal, pain that causes you to miss work or school, or that doesn't improve with basic pain relief, is not normal and should be investigated.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
Cycles that fluctuate wildly in length, or are consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, warrant a checkup.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
If you miss three or more periods in a row (and aren't pregnant or menopausal), you need to consult a doctor to determine the underlying cause.

## When to See a Doctor
Don't hesitate to seek medical advice if you experience abnormalities:

| Symptom | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Soaking a pad/tampon every hour | See a doctor immediately |
| Bleeding after sex or between periods | Schedule a checkup |
| No period for 90 days | Consult a healthcare provider |
| Severe pelvic pain | Seek prompt medical evaluation |
