## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Your menstrual cycle is often called the "fifth vital sign." Knowing what a normal period looks like for you is crucial for early detection of potential health issues. While there is a broad range of what constitutes "normal," significant deviations from your established baseline warrant attention.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine what is normal for your body, you need consistent data. Start by tracking the following metrics using a tool like [LunaCycle](/):
-   **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of your period to Day 1 of the next.
-   **Period Duration:** How many days you bleed.
-   **Flow Intensity:** Light, medium, or heavy days.
-   **Pain Level:** Mild cramping vs. debilitating pain.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding outside of your normal period (intermenstrual bleeding) should be evaluated.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia)
If you need to change your pad or tampon every hour, or pass clots larger than a quarter, seek medical advice.

### 3. Severe Pain (Dysmenorrhea)
Cramps that disrupt your life, cause vomiting, or don't respond to over-the-counter medication are not normal.

### 4. Irregular Cycles
Cycles that fluctuate wildly in length, are consistently shorter than 21 days, or longer than 35 days.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row (if not pregnant or menopausal) requires investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| --- | --- |
| Bleeding between periods | Schedule an appointment with a gynecologist |
| Unusually heavy bleeding | Seek immediate medical evaluation if accompanied by dizziness |
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain | Go to the emergency room or urgent care |
| Missing periods for 3+ months | Consult a healthcare provider for hormonal testing |
