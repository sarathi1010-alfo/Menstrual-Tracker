## Why Knowing Normal Matters

When it comes to the [menstrual cycle](/blog/menstrual-cycle-101-beginners-guide), "normal" is a spectrum, not a single data point. However, knowing the boundaries of that spectrum is crucial. Your period is considered a vital sign. Significant deviations from standard parameters regarding length, flow, or pain levels can be the first indicator of underlying health issues like fibroids, endometriosis, or [PCOS](/conditions/pcos-and-cycle-tracking-guide). Recognizing these signs early allows for prompt medical consultation and better health outcomes.

## How to Assess Your Period

The best way to assess if your period is normal is by [tracking it consistently](/blog/how-to-track-your-cycle-step-by-step) using a tool like [LunaCycle](/). You should evaluate four main factors:

1.  **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of your period to Day 1 of your next period. The normal range is 21 to 35 days.
2.  **Period Duration:** How many days do you bleed? Normal is usually 2 to 7 days.
3.  **Flow Volume:** On average, a woman loses about 2 to 3 tablespoons (30-45 ml) of blood. If you are changing heavy-absorbency products hourly, your flow is heavy.
4.  **Pain Level:** Mild to moderate cramping in the first day or two is common as the uterus contracts. Pain that causes you to miss work or school is not normal.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

If you notice any of these signs, it's a good idea to seek medical advice.

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding at times other than your period (and outside of normal ovulatory spotting) should always be evaluated to rule out infections, polyps, or other concerns.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia)
Soaking through one or more pads or tampons every hour for several hours in a row, or passing clots larger than a quarter.

### 3. Severe Pain (Dysmenorrhea)
Cramps that are not relieved by over-the-counter pain medication and interfere with your daily life can be a symptom of endometriosis or fibroids.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
If your cycle length constantly fluctuates by more than 7-9 days (e.g., 25 days one month, 45 the next), it suggests an issue with ovulation.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row (when not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal) requires investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain | Go to urgent care or the ER immediately. |
| Bleeding through a pad every hour | Contact your gynecologist for a prompt appointment. |
| Cycles consistently >35 days | Schedule a non-urgent visit to discuss hormonal health. |
| Spotting after sex | Schedule an exam to check for cervical irritation or infection. |
