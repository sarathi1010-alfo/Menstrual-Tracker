# Understanding Your Period: What's Normal and What's Not in 2026

A regular period occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has moderate flow. Deviations can signal underlying conditions.

<MedicalDisclaimer />

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

What is considered a "normal" period covers a relatively broad spectrum, but establishing what is normal *for you* is incredibly important. Your menstrual cycle is a sensitive indicator of your overall health. By establishing your baseline, you can quickly identify deviations that might point to hormonal imbalances, structural issues, or other medical conditions, allowing for earlier intervention.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine your baseline, consistently track these four metrics:

1. **Cycle Length:** The total days from the start of one period to the start of the next (normal is 21-35 days).
2. **Period Length:** How many days you bleed (normal is 2-7 days).
3. **Flow Intensity:** How much blood you lose. A typical total volume is 2 to 3 tablespoons, though it often looks like more.
4. **Pain Levels:** Note the severity of cramping and whether it responds to basic pain relief.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or full bleeding outside of your expected period days (intermenstrual bleeding) warrants medical evaluation.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia)
Soaking through feminine hygiene products every hour or passing very large clots is a sign of menorrhagia and can lead to anemia.

### 3. Severe Pain (Dysmenorrhea)
Debilitating pain that causes you to miss work or school, or pain accompanied by nausea/vomiting, is not normal and may indicate endometriosis or fibroids.

### 4. Consistently Irregular Cycles
If your cycles jump unpredictably (e.g., 25 days, then 45 days, then 20 days), it's a sign of irregular ovulation.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row (when not pregnant or menopausal) is a significant red flag.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding through a pad/tampon every hour | See a doctor promptly |
| Sudden onset of severe pelvic pain | Seek immediate medical care |
| No period for 90 days (and negative pregnancy test) | Schedule an appointment with a gynecologist |
| Bleeding after menopause | Seek immediate medical evaluation |
