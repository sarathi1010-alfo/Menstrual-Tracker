## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Because menstrual health has historically been under-discussed, many women endure symptoms they assume are "normal" when they actually point to treatable conditions. Knowing what constitutes a healthy period empowers you to distinguish between typical menstrual discomfort and red flags that require medical attention.

Establishing your own personal baseline—what is normal *for you*—is just as important as knowing the clinical averages. If your normally light, painless 28-day cycle suddenly becomes a heavy, painful 40-day cycle, that change is a signal from your body that something needs investigating.

## How to Assess Your Period

When assessing your period, look at four key metrics using [LunaCycle](/):

1.  **Length:** How many days does the bleeding last? (Normal: 2-7 days).
2.  **Frequency:** How many days from Day 1 of this period to Day 1 of the next? (Normal: 21-35 days).
3.  **Flow:** How much blood are you losing? (Normal: 2-3 tablespoons total, changing products every 3-4 hours).
4.  **Pain Level:** Is the pain manageable with over-the-counter medication? (Normal: Mild to moderate cramping).

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding between active menstrual periods (intermenstrual bleeding) can be caused by hormonal fluctuations, but can also indicate polyps, fibroids, or infections.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia)
If you are soaking through pads or tampons every hour, or passing clots larger than a quarter, you are experiencing abnormally heavy bleeding which can lead to anemia.

### 3. Severe Pain (Dysmenorrhea)
Cramps that cause vomiting, prevent you from going to work or school, or do not respond to standard pain relievers are not normal and may indicate Endometriosis or Adenomyosis.

### 4. Irregular Cycles
Cycles that are consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or cycles that fluctuate wildly in length month-to-month, can be a sign of PCOS or thyroid disorders.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
If you have missed your period for three or more consecutive months and are not pregnant, breastfeeding, or in menopause, a medical evaluation is necessary.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | What it might mean | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Bleeding soaking >1 pad/hour** | Menorrhagia, fibroids, bleeding disorders. | See a doctor promptly. |
| **Pain preventing daily activities** | Endometriosis, adenomyosis, PID. | Schedule a gynecological exam. |
| **No period for 90+ days** | PCOS, thyroid issues, high stress/low weight. | Consult a healthcare provider. |
| **Bleeding after sex** | Cervical issues, infections. | Requires prompt medical evaluation. |
