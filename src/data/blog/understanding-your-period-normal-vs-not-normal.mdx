A regular period typically occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has a moderate flow. However, 'normal' varies from person to person. Understanding your baseline is crucial because significant deviations can signal underlying health conditions.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Your period is often referred to as your fifth vital sign. When your cycle is regular and manageable, it's a good indicator of overall health. When it's not, it's your body's way of telling you that something might be off, allowing for early detection of potential health issues.

## How to Assess Your Period

Use a tracker like [LunaCycle](/) to consistently monitor:
- **Length:** How many days the bleeding lasts.
- **Flow:** How many pads/tampons you soak, and if there are clots.
- **Pain Level:** Severity of cramps or backache.
- **Regularity:** The number of days between the start of one period and the start of the next.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding at times other than menstruation or ovulation should be checked.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia)
Soaking through one or more pads/tampons every hour for several consecutive hours.

### 3. Severe Pain (Dysmenorrhea)
Pain that prevents you from participating in normal daily activities.

### 4. Irregular Cycles
Cycles consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row (when not pregnant or menopausal).

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Clots larger than a quarter | Schedule a non-urgent appointment. |
| Bleeding lasting > 7 days | Consult a healthcare provider. |
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain | Seek immediate medical attention. |
