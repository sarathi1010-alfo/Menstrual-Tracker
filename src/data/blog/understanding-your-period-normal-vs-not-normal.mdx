## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what constitutes a "normal" period is crucial for your overall health. The menstrual cycle is often considered a vital sign; changes in its regularity, flow, or accompanying symptoms can be early indicators of underlying health issues.

By knowing the medical baselines for a normal period and, more importantly, establishing what is normal *for you*, you are better equipped to notice when something is off and seek timely medical advice.

## How to Assess Your Period

Assessing your period involves tracking several key factors over a few months using a tool like [LunaCycle](/).

*   **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of your period to Day 1 of your next period. A normal range is 21 to 35 days.
*   **Period Length:** How many days do you bleed? Normal is 2 to 7 days.
*   **Flow Intensity:** Pay attention to how often you need to change your menstrual products.
*   **Pain Level:** Monitor the severity of cramps or other discomforts.
*   **Regularity:** Does your cycle length stay relatively consistent, or does it vary wildly?

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding between your normal periods (intermenstrual bleeding) is a symptom that should always be evaluated by a healthcare provider.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia)
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for multiple hours in a row, or passing large clots, your bleeding is considered abnormally heavy.

### 3. Severe Pain (Dysmenorrhea)
While mild cramps are normal, pain that prevents you from going to work, school, or participating in daily activities is not. It could be a sign of conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Irregular Cycles
Consistently having cycles shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or cycles whose length varies by more than 7-9 days month-to-month, warrants investigation.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
If you miss three or more periods in a row and are not pregnant, or if you haven't started your period by age 15, consult a doctor.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Sudden changes in cycle regularity | Track for 2-3 months, then consult a doctor if it persists. |
| Severe pelvic pain during period | Schedule an appointment with a gynecologist promptly. |
| Soaking >1 pad/tampon per hour | Seek immediate medical attention. |
| Bleeding between periods | Schedule an evaluation with your doctor. |
