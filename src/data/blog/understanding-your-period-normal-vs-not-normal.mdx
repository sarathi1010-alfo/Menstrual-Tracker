## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding the parameters of a "normal" period is crucial for your reproductive health. What is normal for one person might differ slightly for another, but significant deviations from standard medical guidelines can be early warning signs of conditions like endometriosis, PCOS, fibroids, or thyroid issues.

## How to Assess Your Period

To understand your normal, you need data. Track the following using [LunaCycle](/):
*   **Cycle Length:** Count from day 1 of your period to day 1 of your next period (21-35 days is typical).
*   **Period Duration:** How many days you bleed (2-7 days is typical).
*   **Flow Intensity:** How often you need to change your pad, tampon, or cup.
*   **Pain Levels:** Note the severity of cramps or other discomfort.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods (Spotting)
While light spotting can happen during ovulation or when starting new contraception, unexpected bleeding between periods should be checked by a doctor.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours in a row, or passing large blood clots, this is known as menorrhagia and requires medical attention.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain
Mild cramping is common, but pain that prevents you from going to work, school, or participating in normal activities (dysmenorrhea) is not normal and could indicate conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
If your cycle length constantly fluctuates by more than a week or two, or if your cycles are consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, consult a healthcare provider.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
If you haven't had a period for three consecutive months (and you are not pregnant, breastfeeding, or in menopause), you should seek medical advice.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Action Recommended |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding lasting more than 7-8 days | Schedule a non-urgent doctor visit |
| Soaking a pad/tampon every hour | Seek immediate medical attention |
| Severe pain not relieved by medication | Consult your gynecologist |
| Missing periods for 3+ months | Schedule an evaluation |
| Bleeding after menopause | Seek immediate medical evaluation |
