## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Your menstrual cycle is an excellent indicator of your overall health. By understanding what is medically considered "normal," you can establish a baseline for your own body. What is normal for someone else might not be normal for you, but knowing the typical ranges helps you quickly identify when something is amiss. Early detection of deviations allows for timely intervention and better management of potential underlying health issues.

## How to Assess Your Period

Assessing your period involves evaluating several key factors:
- **Length:** The number of days between the start of one period and the start of the next (typically 21-35 days).
- **Duration:** The number of bleeding days (usually 2-7 days).
- **Flow:** The amount of blood loss (needing to change a pad or tampon every 3-4 hours is average).
- **Pain Level:** Mild cramping is common, but it shouldn't be debilitating.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding at times other than your expected period should always be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through sanitary products every hour or passing blood clots larger than a quarter, seek medical advice.

### 3. Severe Pain
Cramps that interfere with your daily life, cause vomiting, or do not respond to basic pain relief are not normal.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
While some variation is expected, cycles that are consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days require investigation.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing your period for three or more months consecutively (when not pregnant or breastfeeding) is a significant warning sign.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---------|--------------------|
| Heavy bleeding (soaking pad/hr) | See a doctor promptly. |
| Missing 3+ periods | Schedule an appointment. |
| Debilitating pain | Consult a healthcare provider. |
