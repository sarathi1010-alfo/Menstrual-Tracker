A regular period occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has moderate flow. Deviations can signal underlying conditions. By learning what is normal for you through [cycle tracking](/blog/how-to-track-your-cycle-step-by-step), you can take charge of your health.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Your menstrual cycle is often considered a "fifth vital sign." Knowing what a normal period looks like allows you to quickly identify when something is wrong. Early detection of abnormalities can lead to faster diagnosis and treatment of conditions like PCOS, endometriosis, or thyroid disorders.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine what is normal for *you*, use the [LunaCycle](/) app to track these key metrics for at least three months:
*   **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next.
*   **Period Duration:** How many days you bleed.
*   **Flow Intensity:** How often you need to change your pad/tampon/cup.
*   **Pain Levels:** Rate your cramps or other pain on a scale of 1-10.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding outside of your normal period can be a sign of hormonal imbalance, polyps, or infection.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding
If you soak through a pad or tampon every hour for several consecutive hours, or pass large clots, this is considered menorrhagia and needs medical evaluation.

### 3. Severe Pain
While mild cramps are common, debilitating pain (dysmenorrhea) is not normal and could indicate conditions like endometriosis or fibroids.

### 4. Irregular Cycles
Cycles consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or cycles that vary wildly in length month to month.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row (when not pregnant or using hormonal birth control) requires investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Soaking a pad/tampon every hour | Seek medical attention promptly. |
| Pain that prevents daily activities | Schedule an appointment with an OB/GYN. |
| No period for 3+ months | Consult a doctor for hormonal testing. |
| Post-menopausal bleeding | See a doctor immediately. |

### Core Principles
*   "Normal" varies slightly from person to person.
*   The key is identifying changes in *your* established baseline.
*   Do not ignore severe pain or heavy bleeding.
*   Tracking provides the data needed for a medical diagnosis.
*   Always consult a healthcare provider for significant changes.
