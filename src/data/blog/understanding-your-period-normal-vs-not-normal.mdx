## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Your menstrual cycle is a key indicator of your reproductive and overall health. Knowing what constitutes a "normal" period for you—and understanding the clinical definition of normal—is essential for early detection of health issues. When you know your baseline, you can quickly identify when something is amiss and seek medical advice promptly.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine if your period is normal, you need to track several factors consistently using a tool like [LunaCycle](/):

- **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of your period to the day before your next period begins (normal: 21-35 days).
- **Bleeding Duration:** How many days you bleed (normal: 2-7 days).
- **Flow Volume:** How many pads or tampons you use, and if they are fully soaked.
- **Pain Level:** Mild cramping is common; debilitating pain is not.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

If you experience any of the following, it's a sign that your cycle may not be normal:

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or heavier bleeding outside of your normal menstrual window requires investigation.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding
Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for consecutive hours, or passing large clots.

### 3. Severe Pain
Cramps that prevent you from attending school or work, known as dysmenorrhea.

### 4. Irregular Cycles
Cycles that consistently vary by more than 7-9 days, or occur more frequently than 21 days or less often than 35 days.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row (when not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal).

## When to See a Doctor

Use this quick reference to know when to seek medical help:

| Symptom | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Bleeding lasting longer than 7 days | See a doctor promptly. |
| Sudden, severe pain | Seek immediate medical care. |
| Fever or unusual discharge | See a doctor (possible infection). |
| Missing periods (if not pregnant) | Consult a doctor if absent for 3+ months. |
