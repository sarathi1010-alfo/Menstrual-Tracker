A regular period occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has moderate flow. Deviations can signal underlying conditions.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what constitutes a 'normal' period is the first step in advocating for your own health. While 'normal' varies from person to person, knowing the general medical guidelines helps you identify when something is amiss. Early detection of abnormalities can lead to timely diagnosis and treatment of underlying health issues, preventing complications and improving your quality of life.

## How to Assess Your Period

Assessing your period involves monitoring several key factors using a tool like [LunaCycle](/):
* **Length**: How many days does the bleeding last?
* **Cycle Duration**: How many days are there from the start of one period to the start of the next?
* **Flow**: How many pads or tampons do you soak through in a day?
* **Pain Level**: Are your cramps mild, moderate, or severe?
* **Regularity**: Is your cycle length consistent month to month?

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Unexplained spotting or bleeding when you are not expecting your period should be evaluated.

### 2. Heavy bleeding
Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several consecutive hours, or passing large blood clots, is considered abnormally heavy (menorrhagia).

### 3. Severe pain
While mild cramping is common, debilitating pain that interferes with your daily activities (dysmenorrhea) is not normal.

### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles that frequently fall outside the 21-35 day range, or wildly vary in length from month to month.

### 5. Absence of period
Missing three or more periods in a row (amenorrhea) when you are not pregnant or menopausal requires investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Unusually heavy bleeding | Seek medical advice promptly. |
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain | Go to urgent care or the emergency room. |
| Periods consistently >35 days apart | Schedule an appointment with your healthcare provider. |
| Bleeding after menopause | Contact your doctor immediately. |
