## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding the parameters of a "normal" period is essential for proactive health management. While every person's cycle is unique, medical professionals define normal limits to help identify potential issues. Knowing your baseline allows you to distinguish between a typical variation—such as a slightly late period due to stress—and a significant abnormality that warrants medical attention. Early detection of severe deviations can lead to quicker diagnosis and treatment of conditions like endometriosis or fibroids.

## How to Assess Your Period

To accurately assess your period, you need consistent data. Track the total length of your cycle (from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next), the number of days you bleed, and the intensity of your flow. Pay close attention to your pain levels; while mild cramping is common, debilitating pain is a red flag. ## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Unexplained spotting or bleeding should always be evaluated by a healthcare provider.

### 2. Heavy bleeding
Soaking through feminine hygiene products rapidly or passing abnormally large blood clots.

### 3. Severe pain
Cramps that interfere with your daily life, cause nausea, or do not respond to over-the-counter medication.

### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles that consistently fall outside the 21-35 day range or vary wildly in length from month to month.

### 5. Absence of period
Missing periods for several months (amenorrhea) when not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Action |
|---|---|
| Soaking a pad an hour | See a doctor immediately |
| Missing 3+ periods | Schedule a medical consultation |
| Severe, debilitating pain | Seek evaluation for endometriosis or other conditions |
