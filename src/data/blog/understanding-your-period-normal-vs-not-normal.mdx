A regular period occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has moderate flow. Deviations can signal underlying conditions.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what constitutes a "normal" period is essential because your menstrual cycle is a vital sign of your overall health. When you know the standard parameters for cycle length, bleeding duration, and flow intensity, you can quickly identify when something is wrong. Early detection of abnormalities can lead to faster diagnosis and treatment of conditions like endometriosis, fibroids, or thyroid disorders.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine what is normal for *you*, you need to track your cycle consistently using a tool like [LunaCycle](/).

1.  **Track Length:** Note the number of days between the start of one period and the start of the next.
2.  **Track Flow:** Pay attention to how many pads, tampons, or cups you use daily.
3.  **Track Pain:** Rate your cramps on a scale from 1 to 10.
4.  **Track Regularity:** Observe if your cycle length is relatively consistent from month to month.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Spotting or bleeding between your regular periods is unusual and should be evaluated by a healthcare provider.

### 2. Heavy bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for a few hours, or passing clots larger than a quarter, your bleeding is considered heavy.

### 3. Severe pain
Cramps that cause nausea, vomiting, or prevent you from going to work or school are not normal.

### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or cycles that vary wildly in length, warrant investigation.

### 5. Absence of period
If you miss a period (and are not pregnant) or go more than 90 days without bleeding, seek medical advice.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Severe pain disrupting daily life | Schedule an appointment with a gynecologist. |
| Soaking through protection hourly | Seek medical attention, especially if accompanied by dizziness. |
| Missing periods for 3+ months | Consult a doctor for hormonal evaluation. |
