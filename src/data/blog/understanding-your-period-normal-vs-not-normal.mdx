## Why Knowing Normal Matters

When it comes to menstrual health, the definition of "normal" is remarkably broad. However, for early detection of health issues, knowing the general parameters of a healthy period—and more importantly, establishing what is normal for *you*—is crucial.

Your period is often referred to as a "fifth vital sign." Just as blood pressure or heart rate provides clues about cardiovascular health, your menstrual cycle provides vital clues about your reproductive and hormonal health.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine if your period falls within normal ranges, you need to assess four key metrics. Using a tracker like [LunaCycle](/) makes this easy.

1.  **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of your period to Day 1 of your next period. A normal range is 21 to 35 days.
2.  **Period Length:** How many days do you bleed? Normal is 2 to 7 days.
3.  **Flow Volume:** Normal blood loss is roughly 2 to 3 tablespoons (30-45ml) per period, though it's hard to measure exactly.
4.  **Pain Level:** Mild cramping is normal; debilitating pain is not.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

If you experience any of the following, it is time to consult a healthcare provider:

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding between periods (intermenstrual bleeding) is generally not normal and should always be investigated to rule out infections, polyps, or other conditions.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia)
Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for consecutive hours, or passing blood clots larger than a quarter, requires medical attention. This can lead to anemia.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain (Dysmenorrhea)
If cramps prevent you from participating in your normal daily activities, cause nausea or vomiting, and aren't relieved by standard painkillers, it could be a sign of endometriosis or fibroids.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
If your cycle length varies wildly from month to month (e.g., 25 days one month, 45 days the next), or is consistently longer than 35 days or shorter than 21 days.

### 5. Complete Absence of Periods (Amenorrhea)
If you miss three or more periods in a row (and are not pregnant or menopausal), it requires medical evaluation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding after sex | Schedule an appointment promptly. |
| Periods that suddenly stop | Take a pregnancy test; if negative and persists, see a doctor. |
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain | Seek immediate medical attention. |
| Symptoms of toxic shock syndrome (fever, rash) | Go to the emergency room immediately. |
