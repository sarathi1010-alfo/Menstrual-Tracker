## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what constitutes a "normal" period is crucial for your overall health. Your menstrual cycle acts as a barometer for your well-being. When you know your own baseline and understand standard medical guidelines, you can spot early warning signs of reproductive health issues, endocrine disorders, and other underlying conditions before they become severe.

## How to Assess Your Period

To assess your period, track these four key metrics using [LunaCycle](/):
1. **Length:** How many days from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next? (Normal: 21-35 days)
2. **Duration:** How many days do you bleed? (Normal: 2-7 days)
3. **Flow:** How heavy is the bleeding? (Normal: needing to change a pad/tampon every 3-4 hours)
4. **Pain:** Is the pain manageable with over-the-counter medication? (Normal: mild to moderate cramping)

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Spotting or bleeding when you are not menstruating should always be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

### 2. Heavy bleeding (Menorrhagia)
Soaking through one or more pads/tampons every hour for several consecutive hours, or bleeding that lasts longer than 7 days.

### 3. Severe pain (Dysmenorrhea)
Cramps that are severe enough to cause nausea, vomiting, or prevent you from participating in normal daily activities.

### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles that are consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or cycles that vary wildly in length from month to month.

### 5. Absence of period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row if you are not pregnant, breastfeeding, or in menopause.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain | Seek immediate medical attention. |
| Bleeding through a pad an hour | Call your doctor promptly. |
| Cycles suddenly become irregular | Schedule an appointment to discuss potential causes. |
| No period for 90 days | Consult your healthcare provider. |
