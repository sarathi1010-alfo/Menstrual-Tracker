A regular period occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has moderate flow. Deviations can signal underlying conditions.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

What is "normal" for one woman might be abnormal for another. However, there are established medical baselines. Knowing these baselines, and more importantly, knowing *your* personal baseline by tracking with an app like [LunaCycle](/), allows you to quickly identify when something is wrong. Early detection of cycle irregularities can lead to earlier diagnosis of conditions like endometriosis, fibroids, or PCOS.

## How to Assess Your Period

When tracking, evaluate these four main factors:

- **Length of Cycle:** Count from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next (21-35 days).
- **Length of Bleeding:** How many days you actually bleed (2-7 days).
- **Flow Volume:** How many period products you soak through.
- **Pain Level:** Mild cramping vs. debilitating pain.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding outside of your normal menstruation (often called breakthrough bleeding) should be evaluated, as it can indicate polyps, fibroids, or infection.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding
If you need to change your pad or tampon every hour for several consecutive hours, or if you are passing clots larger than a quarter, your flow is considered heavy (menorrhagia).

### 3. Severe Pain
Dysmenorrhea (severe period pain) that interferes with your daily life is not just something you have to put up with. It is often a primary symptom of endometriosis.

### 4. Irregular Cycles
If your cycles are consistently shorter than 21 days, longer than 35 days, or vary wildly from month to month, it warrants investigation.

### 5. Absence of Period
Missing your period for 3 or more months (amenorrhea) when you are not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal is a clear sign of a hormonal or systemic issue.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding through a pad/tampon every hour | Seek medical attention immediately or schedule a prompt appointment. |
| Severe pain not relieved by NSAIDs | Schedule an appointment with a gynecologist. |
| No period for 90 days (and negative pregnancy test) | Schedule an appointment to check hormone levels. |
| Sudden change in cycle after years of regularity | Discuss with your doctor at your next visit or schedule a consultation. |
