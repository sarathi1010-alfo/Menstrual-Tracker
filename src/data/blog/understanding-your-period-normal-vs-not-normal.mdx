## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what constitutes a "normal" period is crucial for your health. While every body is different, medical guidelines define parameters for a healthy cycle. Deviations from these parameters are often the first, and sometimes only, sign of underlying health conditions such as Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS), endometriosis, uterine fibroids, or thyroid disorders. Early detection through awareness and tracking leads to faster diagnosis and better management.

## How to Assess Your Period

To assess whether your period is normal, you need to track four main factors using a tool like [LunaCycle](/):

1.  **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of your period to Day 1 of your next period. Normal is 21 to 35 days (up to 45 days for teens).
2.  **Bleeding Duration:** Normal is between 2 and 7 days of bleeding.
3.  **Flow Volume:** Normal blood loss is roughly 2 to 3 tablespoons (30-50 ml) per cycle.
4.  **Pain Level:** Mild cramping is common, but pain that causes vomiting, fainting, or requires missing work/school is abnormal.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding at times other than your period or expected ovulation window should always be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia)
If you need to change your pad or tampon every hour for several hours in a row, or if you pass blood clots larger than a quarter, your bleeding is abnormally heavy.

### 3. Severe Pain (Dysmenorrhea)
Debilitating cramps that don't respond to standard pain relievers or that interfere with your daily life can be a symptom of conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Irregular Cycles
Consistently unpredictable cycles, cycles shorter than 21 days, or cycles longer than 35 days (in adults) warrant investigation.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
If you have missed three or more periods in a row (and are not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal), consult a doctor.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Bleeding through a pad/tampon hourly | Seek immediate medical evaluation. |
| Missing >3 periods in a row | Schedule an appointment with your OB/GYN. |
| Severe, unmanageable pain | Consult your doctor to investigate causes like endometriosis. |
| New, unusual spotting between periods | Contact your healthcare provider for an exam. |
