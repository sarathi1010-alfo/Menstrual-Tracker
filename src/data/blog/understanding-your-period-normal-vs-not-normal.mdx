We often talk about the "average" 28-day cycle, but "normal" is a spectrum. Understanding what is normal *for you* is critical for proactive health management.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Your menstrual cycle is a vital sign. Significant deviations from your baseline can be the first indicator of underlying health issues, ranging from stress to thyroid disorders or endometriosis.

## How to Assess Your Period

To define your normal, track these four metrics using [LunaCycle](/):

1. **Length:** The number of days between Day 1 of one period and Day 1 of the next.
2. **Duration:** How many days the bleeding lasts.
3. **Flow Intensity:** How often you need to change your pad/tampon/cup.
4. **Pain Level:** Track cramping and other discomforts.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding when you aren't menstruating should be evaluated by a doctor.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding
If you soak through a pad or tampon every hour for consecutive hours, or pass large clots, seek medical advice.

### 3. Severe Pain
Debilitating pain is not just "bad cramps"—it could be a sign of endometriosis or fibroids.

### 4. Irregular Cycles
Cycles consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days (unless you are a teen or in perimenopause) are considered irregular.

### 5. Absence of Period
Missing your period for more than three months (when not pregnant or breastfeeding) requires investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Soaking >1 pad/tampon per hour | See a doctor promptly. |
| Pain that limits daily activities | Schedule an appointment to discuss endometriosis/fibroids. |
| Period stops for >90 days | Consult a healthcare provider. |
| Bleeding after menopause | Seek immediate medical evaluation. |
