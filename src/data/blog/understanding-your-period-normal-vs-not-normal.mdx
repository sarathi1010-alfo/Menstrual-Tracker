A regular period occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has moderate flow. Deviations can signal underlying conditions. Understanding what is "normal" for your body is one of the most vital aspects of [Menstrual Cycle 101](/blog/menstrual-cycle-101-beginners-guide). What is typical for one person might be unusual for another, which is why consistent tracking with tools like [LunaCycle](/) is essential for establishing your personal baseline.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Your menstrual cycle is often referred to as a "vital sign." Just like your heart rate or blood pressure, the regularity and characteristics of your period provide a window into your overall health. When [hormones](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained) are balanced and your reproductive system is functioning correctly, your period generally follows a predictable pattern.

Knowing what is normal *for you* allows you to detect subtle changes early. Early detection is crucial because menstrual irregularities are often the first sign of underlying health issues, such as thyroid disorders, [PCOS](/conditions/pcos-and-cycle-tracking-guide), or endometriosis. It empowers you to seek medical advice proactively rather than waiting for symptoms to become severe.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine if your period falls within healthy parameters, you need to assess four key metrics:

*   **Length (Cycle):** The number of days from the start of one period to the start of the next. A normal range is 21 to 35 days.
*   **Duration (Bleeding):** The number of days you experience menstrual bleeding. A normal range is 2 to 7 days.
*   **Flow (Volume):** The amount of blood lost. While hard to measure exactly, changing a pad or tampon every 3-4 hours is typical. Needing to change it every hour is considered heavy.
*   **Pain Level:** Mild cramping (dysmenorrhea) that can be managed with over-the-counter pain relievers or heat therapy is common. Pain that disrupts your life or causes vomiting is not.

By [tracking your cycle step-by-step](/blog/how-to-track-your-cycle-step-by-step), you can easily monitor these four metrics.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

While variations are normal, certain signs warrant closer attention.

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods (Spotting)

Any unexplained bleeding or spotting outside of your regular menstrual period should be noted. While it can sometimes be harmless (like ovulation spotting), it can also indicate cervical issues, polyps, or hormonal imbalances.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia)

If your flow requires changing protection every hour for several consecutive hours, or if you regularly pass clots larger than a quarter, this is considered heavy bleeding. It can lead to anemia and severe fatigue.

### 3. Severe Pain (Dysmenorrhea)

Cramps shouldn't leave you bedridden. If the pain is severe enough to cause you to miss work or school, or if it radiates to your lower back and thighs severely, it could be a sign of conditions like endometriosis or adenomyosis.

### 4. Irregular Cycles

If your cycle length varies wildly (e.g., 25 days one month, 45 days the next) or is consistently longer than 35 days or shorter than 21 days, this indicates an irregularity that should be investigated.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)

If you have missed three or more periods in a row (and you are not pregnant or menopausal), this is a significant warning sign. It often points to a disruption in the hormonal communication between the brain and the ovaries.

## When to See a Doctor

If you experience any of the warning signs, it is important to consult a healthcare professional.

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Soaking through a pad/tampon every hour** | Seek medical evaluation promptly, especially if accompanied by dizziness or shortness of breath. |
| **Pain that disrupts daily life** | Schedule an appointment to discuss pain management and rule out underlying conditions. |
| **No period for 3+ months (not pregnant)** | Consult a doctor for a hormonal evaluation and physical exam. |
| **Sudden irregularity after years of regular cycles** | Bring your tracking data to a doctor to investigate the sudden change. |
| **Bleeding after sex or between periods** | Schedule a pelvic exam to rule out infections or structural issues. |
