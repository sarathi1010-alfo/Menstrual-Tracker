## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Your menstrual cycle is a critical indicator of your reproductive and overall health. Knowing the parameters of a "normal" period allows you to quickly identify when something is wrong. By understanding the typical ranges for cycle length, bleeding duration, and flow intensity, you empower yourself to seek medical advice promptly if abnormalities arise, potentially catching issues like hormonal imbalances, fibroids, or endometriosis early on.

## How to Assess Your Period

Assessing your period requires tracking a few key metrics every month. Use a tool like [LunaCycle](/) to log:
- **Cycle Length:** The number of days from the first day of bleeding to the day before your next period starts (Normal: 21-35 days).
- **Duration of Flow:** How many days you bleed (Normal: 2-7 days).
- **Flow Intensity:** How often you need to change your menstrual products.
- **Pain Level:** The severity of any cramping or pelvic pain.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Unexplained spotting or bleeding when you are not menstruating should always be evaluated to rule out infections or structural issues.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several consecutive hours, or passing clots larger than a quarter, seek medical attention.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain
Pain that causes you to miss work, school, or regular activities, and is not relieved by over-the-counter medication, is a red flag.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
Cycles that consistently fall outside the 21-35 day range, or vary wildly in length from month to month.

### 5. Absence of Period
Missing your period for 90 days or more (when not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal) requires investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding through 1 pad/hour. | Seek immediate medical evaluation. |
| Missing 3 or more periods in a row. | Schedule an appointment with your gynecologist. |
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain. | Go to urgent care or the emergency room. |
| Consistent spotting between periods. | Discuss at your next routine checkup, or sooner if accompanied by pain. |
