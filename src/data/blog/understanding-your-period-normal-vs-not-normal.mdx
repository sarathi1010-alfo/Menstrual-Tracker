## Why Knowing Normal Matters

When it comes to the menstrual cycle, "normal" is a broad spectrum. What is typical for one person might be highly unusual for another. However, there are established medical baselines that help distinguish between natural variations and potential health issues.

Knowing these baselines—and more importantly, establishing your own personal baseline through consistent tracking—is essential for early detection of issues like Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS), endometriosis, thyroid dysfunction, or fibroids. Your period is a vital sign, and significant changes should not be ignored.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine if your period is normal, you need to assess four main factors:

1. **Length:** How many days are there from the start of one period to the start of the next? (Normal: 21-35 days).
2. **Duration:** How many days do you bleed? (Normal: 2-7 days).
3. **Flow Intensity:** How much blood do you lose? (Normal: 2 to 3 tablespoons, or needing to change a regular pad/tampon every 3-4 hours).
4. **Pain Level:** Is the discomfort manageable with over-the-counter medication, or is it debilitating?

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding between periods (intermenstrual bleeding) can be caused by hormonal fluctuations, but it can also indicate polyps, fibroids, or infections.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours in a row, or passing large blood clots, you are experiencing heavy menstrual bleeding which can lead to anemia.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain
While mild cramping (dysmenorrhea) is common, pain that causes nausea, vomiting, or prevents you from functioning normally is a red flag for conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
If your cycles jump unpredictably from 20 days to 45 days, or if you regularly go months without a period, this indicates irregular ovulation.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
If you have missed three or more periods in a row (and are not pregnant or menopausal), it requires medical investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Bleeding through 1 pad/hour | See a doctor promptly |
| Pain that stops daily activities | Schedule an appointment |
| Missing periods for 3+ months | Consult a healthcare provider |
| Sudden, unexplained cycle changes | Mention at your next checkup |
