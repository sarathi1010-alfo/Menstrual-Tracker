A regular period occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has moderate flow. Deviations can signal underlying conditions.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what constitutes a 'normal' period is critical for early detection of potential health issues. Your menstrual cycle is often considered a vital sign, reflecting the complex interplay of hormones and your overall health. When you know your baseline—what your average cycle length is, how heavy your flow typically is, and what symptoms you usually experience—you are better equipped to notice when something changes. By tracking with [LunaCycle](/), you can easily establish this baseline. Recognizing deviations early means you can seek medical advice sooner, leading to quicker diagnoses and better management of conditions like PCOS, endometriosis, or thyroid disorders. For more context, read [Menstrual Cycle 101](/blog/menstrual-cycle-101-beginners-guide), [How to Track Your Cycle](/blog/how-to-track-your-cycle-step-by-step), and the [Ultimate Cycle Tracking Guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide).

## How to Assess Your Period

To assess your period, you need to track several key factors over time:
- **Length:** Count the days from the first day of bleeding to the day before your next period starts.
- **Flow:** Note whether your flow is light, medium, or heavy, and how many products you use per day.
- **Pain Level:** Rate your cramps and other discomfort on a scale from mild to severe.
- **Regularity:** Check if your cycles are roughly the same length each month.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

While every woman's cycle is different, certain signs warrant a conversation with a healthcare provider.

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods

Also known as spotting, bleeding when you are not expecting your period can be caused by hormonal fluctuations, stress, or more serious issues that need evaluation.

### 2. Heavy Bleeding

Menorrhagia, or abnormally heavy bleeding, is characterized by soaking through products very quickly, needing to double up on protection, or passing large blood clots.

### 3. Severe Pain

While some cramping is common, dysmenorrhea—pain so severe it disrupts your daily life and isn't helped by typical painkillers—is a sign that something may be wrong, such as endometriosis.

### 4. Irregular Cycles

If your cycles are consistently shorter than 21 days, longer than 35 days, or vary wildly in length from month to month, it could indicate an ovulatory issue.

### 5. Absence of Period

Amenorrhea, or the absence of a period for three or more months (when not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal), is a significant indicator that your body is under stress or experiencing a hormonal imbalance.

## When to See a Doctor

Use this guide to determine when to consult a healthcare professional.

| Symptom | Description | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Missing Periods | No period for 90 days or more. | Consult a doctor for evaluation. |
| Severe Pain | Pain that prevents daily activities. | Schedule an appointment to discuss pain management and causes. |
| Extremely Heavy Flow | Soaking a pad/tampon every hour for >2 hours. | Seek immediate medical advice. |
| Cycles > 35 days | Consistently long cycles. | Discuss with your doctor, as this may indicate anovulation. |
| Sudden Changes | A regular cycle suddenly becomes unpredictable. | Monitor for a couple of months and consult a doctor if it persists. |
