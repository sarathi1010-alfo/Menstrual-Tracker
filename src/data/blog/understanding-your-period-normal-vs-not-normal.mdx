## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what constitutes a "normal" period is essential for proactive health management. Because every woman's body is different, your version of normal might slightly differ from the textbook definition. However, knowing the established medical baselines helps you identify when your body is signaling that something might be wrong. Early detection of abnormalities can lead to quicker diagnoses of conditions like endometriosis, PCOS, or uterine fibroids, preventing long-term complications and improving your quality of life.

## How to Assess Your Period

Assessing your period requires observation over several months. You can use the [LunaCycle](/) tracker to monitor these four key metrics:

1.  **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of your period to Day 1 of your next period. Average is 28 days, but 21-35 is normal.
2.  **Flow Duration:** How many days you bleed. 2 to 7 days is the standard range.
3.  **Flow Intensity:** Are you soaking through products rapidly? Do you see large clots?
4.  **Pain Level:** Note when cramps occur and how severe they are on a scale of 1-10.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or bleeding outside of your expected menstrual window can be a sign of hormonal imbalance, polyps, or infection.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for consecutive hours, this is Menorrhagia and can lead to anemia.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain
Pain that causes you to miss work or school, or involves vomiting and fainting (Dysmenorrhea), is not normal and could indicate Endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
Cycles that frequently fluctuate by more than 7-9 days month-to-month, or cycles longer than 35 days, warrant investigation.

### 5. Complete Absence of Period
Missing three or more periods in a row (Amenorrhea) when not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal requires medical attention.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Potential Cause | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding > 7 days | Fibroids, Hormones | Schedule a gynecologist appointment |
| Clots larger than a quarter | Heavy bleeding/Menorrhagia | Seek medical evaluation |
| Sudden cycle irregularity | Stress, PCOS, Thyroid | Monitor for 2 cycles, then consult doctor |
| Pain not helped by NSAIDs | Endometriosis, Adenomyosis | Consult a specialist |
