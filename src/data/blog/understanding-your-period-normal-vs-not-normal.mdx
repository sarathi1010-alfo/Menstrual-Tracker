## Why Knowing Normal Matters

What is considered a "normal" period covers a broad spectrum. However, knowing the general parameters of a healthy cycle can help you identify when something might be wrong. Early detection of abnormalities can lead to quicker diagnosis and treatment of conditions like PCOS, endometriosis, or fibroids.

## How to Assess Your Period

To understand what is normal for you, track the following:

* **Cycle Length:** The number of days between the first day of one period and the first day of the next.
* **Duration:** How many days the bleeding lasts.
* **Flow Intensity:** How heavy or light the bleeding is (e.g., how often you change menstrual products).
* **Pain Levels:** Note the severity of cramps or other discomfort.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Spotting or bleeding between periods should always be evaluated by a healthcare provider.

### 2. Heavy bleeding
Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours is a sign of heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia).

### 3. Severe pain
Cramps that debilitate you and aren't helped by standard pain relief could indicate conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles that are consistently shorter than 21 days, longer than 35 days, or highly unpredictable.

### 5. Absence of period
Missing three or more periods in a row (when not pregnant or breastfeeding) is a condition called amenorrhea.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding through products rapidly | Seek medical evaluation promptly. |
| Severe, unmanageable pain | Schedule an appointment with a gynecologist. |
| Missing periods for 3+ months | Consult a healthcare provider to investigate causes. |
