## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Because menstrual health is often not discussed openly, many people suffer through abnormal periods thinking their experience is typical. Knowing what constitutes a "normal" period empowers you to advocate for your health. If you can identify when something falls outside the normal range, you can seek medical advice earlier, potentially catching issues like endometriosis, fibroids, or PCOS before they cause severe complications.

## How to Assess Your Period

To know what is normal *for you*, you have to track it.

Using [LunaCycle](/tracker), you should consistently note:
*   **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next.
*   **Period Length:** How many days you actually bleed.
*   **Flow:** Keep mental track of how many pads/tampons you use.
*   **Pain:** Note if cramps are manageable with over-the-counter medication or if they disrupt your daily life.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting mid-cycle can occasionally happen around ovulation, but consistent bleeding or spotting between periods should always be evaluated by a doctor.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour, or passing clots larger than a quarter, this is considered menorrhagia (heavy bleeding) and requires medical attention.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain
Mild cramping is normal. Cramps that cause vomiting, fainting, or require you to miss work or school are not normal and could indicate conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
While a cycle can vary by a few days, cycles that are consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days (or vary wildly month to month) warrant investigation.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
If you are not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal, and you miss three or more periods in a row, consult a healthcare provider.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **No period for 90 days** | Schedule an appointment to check for hormonal imbalances or pregnancy. |
| **Sudden, severe pelvic pain** | Seek immediate medical attention. |
| **Bleeding after menopause** | Contact a doctor immediately. |
| **Periods consistently lasting >7 days** | Discuss with your gynecologist at your next visit. |
| **Fever or unusual discharge** | See a doctor promptly to rule out infection. |
