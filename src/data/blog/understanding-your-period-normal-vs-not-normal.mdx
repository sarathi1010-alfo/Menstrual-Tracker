## Why Knowing Normal Matters
Understanding what constitutes a 'normal' period is crucial because your menstrual cycle is a key indicator of your overall health. When you know what to expect from your body, it becomes much easier to identify when something is amiss. Early detection of abnormalities can lead to quicker diagnosis and treatment for a variety of health conditions, from hormonal imbalances to structural issues in the reproductive organs.

## How to Assess Your Period
To determine if your period is normal, you should track four main components over several months:
- **Length:** How many days pass from the start of one period to the start of the next? (Normal: 21-35 days).
- **Duration:** How many days does the bleeding last? (Normal: 2-7 days).
- **Flow:** How heavy is the bleeding? (Normal flow varies, but needing to change a high-absorbency pad or tampon every hour is considered heavy).
- **Pain Level:** Some mild cramping is typical, but severe pain that interferes with daily activities is not.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch
If you experience any of the following, it may be time to consult a healthcare professional:

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Also known as spotting, this can be caused by hormonal fluctuations, but it can also indicate infections, polyps, or other issues that need medical evaluation.

### 2. Heavy bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours in a row, or if you are passing clots larger than a quarter, this is considered menorrhagia and should be checked.

### 3. Severe pain
Dysmenorrhea (severe menstrual cramps) that does not improve with over-the-counter pain relief and disrupts your life could be a sign of endometriosis or fibroids.

### 4. Irregular cycles
While occasional variations are normal, cycles that consistently fall outside the 21-35 day range, or cycles that fluctuate wildly in length from month to month, warrant investigation.

### 5. Absence of period
If you go more than 90 days without a period (and you are not pregnant, breastfeeding, or going through menopause), this is known as amenorrhea and requires medical attention.

## When to See a Doctor
| Symptom | Action |
|---|---|
