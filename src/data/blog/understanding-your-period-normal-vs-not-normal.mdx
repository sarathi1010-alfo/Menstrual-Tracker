A regular period occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has moderate flow. Deviations can signal underlying conditions. Establishing your baseline by tracking with [LunaCycle](/) is the best way to know when something is off.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters

"Normal" is a range, but it's important to know *your* normal. A cycle that suddenly shifts from 28 days to 40 days, or a period that becomes extremely heavy, is your body's way of communicating that something has changed. To see how tracking helps monitor this over time, review our [Ultimate Cycle Tracking Guide](/blog/ultimate-cycle-tracking-guide) and [How to Track Your Cycle](/blog/how-to-track-your-cycle-step-by-step).

## How to Assess Your Period

When you log your cycle in [LunaCycle](/), pay attention to four key metrics:
1.  **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of your period to Day 1 of your *next* period.
2.  **Period Length:** How many days are you actively bleeding?
3.  **Flow Intensity:** Are you soaking through products rapidly?
4.  **Pain Levels:** Are cramps manageable or debilitating?

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Spotting outside of menstruation (or ovulation) can be a sign of infection, polyps, or hormonal imbalances. (Read more about [Hormones and Your Cycle](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained)).

### 2. Heavy bleeding
If you need to change a pad or tampon every hour for several consecutive hours, or if you are passing large clots, seek medical advice.

### 3. Severe pain
Dysmenorrhea (severe cramps) that disrupts your daily life is not something you just have to "live with."

### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles that consistently fall outside the 21-35 day range, or vary wildly in length month-to-month.

### 5. Absence of period
Missing a period (amenorrhea) when you are not pregnant or menopausal warrants investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Bleeding through >1 pad/hour | See a doctor promptly |
| No period for 90 days | Schedule an appointment |
| Pain not managed by OTC meds | Consult a healthcare provider |
| Sudden cycle irregularity | Track for 3 months, then consult doctor |
