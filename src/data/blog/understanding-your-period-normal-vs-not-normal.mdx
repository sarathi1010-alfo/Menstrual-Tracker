## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what constitutes a "normal" period is crucial for your overall health. The menstrual cycle is often considered a "fifth vital sign" because its regularity—or lack thereof—can provide significant clues about your physical well-being.

*   **Early Detection:** Spotting abnormalities early can lead to faster diagnosis and treatment of conditions like endometriosis, PCOS, or fibroids.
*   **Peace of Mind:** Knowing your own baseline (what's normal *for you*) reduces anxiety when minor variations occur.
*   **Effective Communication:** When you understand your cycle, you can communicate more clearly with your healthcare provider.

## How to Assess Your Period

The best way to determine what is normal for you is to [track your cycle consistently](/blog/how-to-track-your-cycle-step-by-step) using a tool like [LunaCycle](/). Key metrics to assess include:

1.  **Cycle Length:** Count from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next. Normal is typically 21-35 days.
2.  **Period Length:** Bleeding usually lasts 2-7 days.
3.  **Flow Volume:** Assess how many products you use. Changing a pad/tampon every 3-4 hours is generally average.
4.  **Pain Levels:** Rate your cramps from mild discomfort to severe, debilitating pain.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods

Any spotting or bleeding that occurs outside of your normal menstrual phase should be noted and discussed with a doctor.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding

If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for multiple hours, or passing clots larger than a quarter, seek medical attention.

### 3. Severe Pain

While some cramping is common due to prostaglandins, pain that prevents you from working, going to school, or sleeping is abnormal and needs evaluation.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles

If your cycle length varies wildly from month to month (e.g., 25 days one month, 45 days the next), it may indicate a hormonal issue.

### 5. Absence of Periods (Amenorrhea)

If you miss three or more periods in a row (and are not pregnant, breastfeeding, or in menopause), consult a doctor.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Sudden, severe pelvic pain** | Seek immediate medical attention. |
| **Bleeding through products hourly** | Contact your doctor or visit an urgent care center. |
| **No period for 90 days** | Schedule an appointment with your healthcare provider. |
| **Periods consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days** | Discuss at your next appointment. |
| **Bleeding after sex or between periods** | Schedule an evaluation. |
