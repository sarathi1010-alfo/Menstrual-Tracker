## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what constitutes a "normal" period is crucial for your overall health awareness. A regular, moderate period is generally a good indicator that your reproductive system and hormonal balance are functioning correctly. Recognizing the boundaries of normality allows for the early detection of potential health issues, from hormonal imbalances to conditions like PCOS or endometriosis.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine if your period is normal, you need to track several key factors over a few months:
*   **Cycle Length:** Count from the first day of bleeding to the day before your next period starts. It should generally fall between 21 and 35 days.
*   **Duration of Bleeding:** Note how many days you bleed. 2 to 7 days is typical.
*   **Flow Intensity:** Assess how frequently you need to change your sanitary products.
*   **Pain Level:** Monitor the severity of cramping and whether it interferes with your daily activities.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### Bleeding between periods
Also known as intermenstrual bleeding or spotting, this is not a typical part of a regular cycle and should be evaluated to rule out infections or structural issues.

### Heavy bleeding
Menorrhagia, or excessively heavy menstrual bleeding (e.g., soaking a pad/tampon every hour), can lead to anemia and requires medical attention.

### Severe pain
While mild cramps (dysmenorrhea) are common, pain that causes you to miss work or school, or doesn't respond to over-the-counter medication, is a red flag.

### Irregular cycles
Cycles that constantly shift in length (e.g., 25 days one month, 45 the next) indicate inconsistent ovulation and potential hormonal issues.

### Absence of period
Missing a period for several months (amenorrhea) when not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal requires investigation by a healthcare professional.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Action |
|---|---|
| Soaking through >1 pad/hour for hours | See a doctor promptly |
| Missing period for 3 or more months | Schedule an appointment |
| Bleeding lasting longer than 7 days | Consult your healthcare provider |
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain | Seek immediate medical care |
