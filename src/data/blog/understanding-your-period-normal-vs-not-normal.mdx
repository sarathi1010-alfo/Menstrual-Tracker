## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what is 'normal' for your period is essential for early detection of potential health issues. The menstrual cycle is considered a vital sign; significant deviations from normal parameters can indicate underlying conditions such as endometriosis, fibroids, PCOS, or thyroid dysfunction. By knowing the typical ranges and tracking your personal baseline, you can advocate for yourself more effectively if something feels wrong.

## How to Assess Your Period

Assessing your period involves tracking several key parameters using a tool like [LunaCycle](/):

*   **Cycle Length:** Count from the first day of bleeding to the day before your next period begins. (Normal: 21-35 days)
*   **Period Duration:** Note how many days you experience bleeding. (Normal: 2-7 days)
*   **Flow Volume:** Estimate how heavy the bleeding is based on how often you change menstrual products.
*   **Pain Levels:** Track the intensity of cramps or other pain on a scale from mild to severe.
*   **Regularity:** Notice if your cycle length varies significantly from month to month (variation of more than 7-9 days).

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

While variations happen, certain signs warrant closer attention.

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Also known as intermenstrual bleeding or spotting, this can be caused by hormonal fluctuations, cervical polyps, or infections.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours in a row, or if you are passing large blood clots, this is considered menorrhagia and needs medical evaluation.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain
While mild cramping (dysmenorrhea) is common, pain that stops you from participating in your normal daily activities or requires strong medication is not normal and could indicate conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
If your cycles are consistently shorter than 21 days, longer than 35 days, or if the length varies dramatically from month to month, it could signal an ovulation issue.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing periods for three or more consecutive months (when you are not pregnant or menopausal) requires a medical diagnosis to determine the underlying cause.

## When to See a Doctor

Use this table as a quick reference for when to seek professional medical advice.

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **No period for 90+ days** | Consult a doctor to check for pregnancy or underlying hormonal issues. |
| **Bleeding that lasts > 7 days** | Schedule an appointment to rule out fibroids, polyps, or bleeding disorders. |
| **Severe pain not relieved by meds** | Seek evaluation for possible endometriosis or pelvic inflammatory disease (PID). |
| **Bleeding after menopause** | Contact a doctor immediately, as this requires prompt investigation. |
| **Sudden, unexplained changes** | Discuss any dramatic shifts in your typical cycle pattern with a healthcare provider. |
