## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Knowing what is "normal" for a menstrual cycle matters for the early detection of health issues. Your menstrual cycle is often considered a vital sign. When things are functioning correctly, it typically follows a predictable pattern. When that pattern breaks significantly, it can be your body's way of signaling that something requires attention, whether it's stress, a thyroid issue, or a reproductive condition.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine what is normal for *you*, use [LunaCycle](/tracker) to track your baseline over several months. Focus on:
- **Length:** The number of days from the start of one period to the start of the next.
- **Duration:** How many days you actually bleed.
- **Flow:** How many menstrual products you use per day.
- **Pain Level:** Whether cramps are manageable with over-the-counter medication or if they disrupt your life.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Unexplained spotting or bleeding when you do not have your period.

### 2. Heavy bleeding
Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour, or passing large blood clots.

### 3. Severe pain
Cramps that cause nausea, vomiting, or prevent you from going to work or school.

### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles that are consistently shorter than 21 days, longer than 35 days, or highly unpredictable.

### 5. Absence of period
Missing three or more periods in a row (if you are not pregnant).

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bleeding through products hourly | See a doctor promptly. |
| Sudden, severe pelvic pain | Seek immediate medical care. |
| No period for 90 days | Schedule an appointment with your OBGYN. |
| Consistently irregular cycles | Discuss with your doctor at your next visit. |
