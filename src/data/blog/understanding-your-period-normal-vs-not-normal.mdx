## Why Knowing Normal Matters

Understanding what is 'normal' for your period is critical because your menstrual cycle is a vital sign. Deviations from your established baseline can be the first indicator of underlying health issues, from stress and nutritional deficiencies to conditions like PCOS or endometriosis.

## How to Assess Your Period

Establishing your personal baseline requires consistent tracking over several months using a tool like [LunaCycle](/). You should monitor:

*   **Cycle Length:** The number of days from the start of one period to the start of the next (average is 21-35 days).
*   **Bleeding Duration:** How many days you bleed (average is 2-7 days).
*   **Flow Intensity:** Light, medium, or heavy, and how often you need to change products.
*   **Pain Levels:** The severity of cramping or other discomfort.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
Spotting or full bleeding outside of your normal menstruation window should always be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours, or passing large clots, seek medical advice.

### 3. Severe, Debilitating Pain
Cramps that prevent you from attending school or work, or that aren't managed by standard pain relief, are not normal and could indicate endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
Cycles that frequently jump between very short (under 21 days) and very long (over 35 days) or miss months entirely.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row (when not pregnant or menopausal) requires investigation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Soaking >1 pad/tampon per hour | Seek immediate medical evaluation. |
| Debilitating cramps | Schedule an appointment with an OB/GYN. |
| No period for 90 days | Consult a doctor for testing. |
| Bleeding post-menopause | Seek immediate medical evaluation. |
