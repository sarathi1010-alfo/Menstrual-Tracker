## Why Knowing Normal Matters

When it comes to the menstrual cycle, "normal" exists on a spectrum. What is typical for one person may be irregular for another. However, there are established medical baselines. Knowing these baselines is crucial for the early detection of health issues like PCOS, endometriosis, fibroids, or thyroid disorders.

Understanding your baseline empowers you to advocate for your health and recognize when something needs a doctor's attention.

## How to Assess Your Period

To determine what is normal for you, track these four key metrics in [LunaCycle](/tracker):

1. **Cycle Length:** Measure from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next (average is 21-35 days).
2. **Period Duration:** The number of days you bleed (average is 2-7 days).
3. **Flow Intensity:** Keep track of how often you change menstrual products.
4. **Pain Levels:** Note the severity of cramps or pelvic pain.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding Between Periods
While light spotting during ovulation can occur, unpredictable bleeding or spotting between periods should be evaluated.

### 2. Extremely Heavy Bleeding
If you are soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours, or passing large blood clots, seek medical advice.

### 3. Severe Pain
Mild cramping is common due to prostaglandins, but pain that is debilitating, causes nausea, or prevents you from daily activities is a red flag for conditions like endometriosis.

### 4. Highly Irregular Cycles
Cycles that frequently vary by more than 7-9 days month-to-month, or fall outside the 21-35 day range consistently, warrant investigation.

### 5. Absence of Period (Amenorrhea)
Missing three or more periods in a row (when not pregnant, breastfeeding, or menopausal) requires a doctor's evaluation.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Cycle < 21 days or > 35 days | Schedule a routine check-up |
| Heavy bleeding soaking 1+ pad/hour | Seek immediate medical evaluation |
| Debilitating pelvic pain | Consult a gynecologist |
| Bleeding after menopause | See a doctor immediately |
| Sudden, unexplained cycle changes | Schedule an appointment to review hormonal health |
