A regular period occurs every 21-35 days, lasts 2-7 days, and has moderate flow. Deviations can signal underlying conditions.

## Why Knowing Normal Matters
Knowing what is normal for your body is the first step in early detection of health issues. Many reproductive health conditions, such as endometriosis or PCOS, are often dismissed or diagnosed late because women are conditioned to accept severe pain or irregularities as 'normal.' By establishing a baseline through tracking, you empower yourself to advocate for your health when things change.

## How to Assess Your Period
To understand your unique baseline, track these four key metrics for at least three months:
1. **Cycle Length:** The number of days from Day 1 of one period to Day 1 of the next.
2. **Flow Duration:** How many days you bleed.
3. **Pain Levels:** Rate your cramping and discomfort on a scale of 1-10.
4. **Regularity:** How much your cycle length varies from month to month.

## Top 5 Warning Signs to Watch

### 1. Bleeding between periods
Spotting or bleeding at times other than your period or ovulation window should be evaluated.

### 2. Heavy bleeding
Soaking through feminine hygiene products every hour or passing clots larger than a quarter.

### 3. Severe pain
Cramping that does not respond to over-the-counter medication and causes you to miss work or school.

### 4. Irregular cycles
Cycles that are consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or highly unpredictable lengths.

### 5. Absence of period
Going 90 days or more without a period (amenorrhea) when you are not pregnant or menopausal.

## When to See a Doctor

| Symptom | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Heavy bleeding (soaking pad/hr) | Seek immediate medical care |
| Missed 3+ periods in a row | Schedule an appointment |
| Severe, debilitating cramps | Consult a gynecologist |
| Bleeding after menopause | Seek immediate medical evaluation |
