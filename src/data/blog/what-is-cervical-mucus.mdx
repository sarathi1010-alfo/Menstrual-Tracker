# What is Cervical Mucus?

Cervical mucus (also called cervical fluid) is a hydrogel produced by the cervix. Its primary purpose is to either prevent or facilitate the passage of sperm into the uterus, depending on where you are in your cycle.

## How Does Cervical Mucus Work?
As your body prepares for ovulation, estrogen levels rise, causing the cervix to produce more fluid. This fluid becomes more watery and alkaline to help sperm survive and swim. After ovulation, progesterone causes the mucus to thicken and become acidic, creating a "plug" that blocks sperm.

## Real-World Example
Throughout a cycle, a user might notice these stages:
1. **Dry/Sticky:** (Just after period) Low fertility.
2. **Creamy/Lotion-like:** (Approaching fertile window) Rising fertility.
3. **Egg-White (EWCM):** (At ovulation) Peak fertility.
4. **Dry:** (After ovulation) Non-fertile.

## Why is it Important?
Tracking cervical mucus is one of the most reliable ways to identify your fertile window in real-time. Unlike calendar predictions, which are based on averages, mucus is a direct reflection of your current hormonal state.

## Cervical Mucus Phases

| Phase | Consistency | Fertility Level |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| Early Follicular | Dry or Sticky | Low |
| Late Follicular | Creamy | Increasing |
| Ovulation | Clear & Stretchy | Peak |
| Luteal | Thick or Dry | Low |
