**Cervical mucus** (or cervical fluid) is a hydrogel produced by the cervix. Its consistency, color, and volume change throughout your menstrual cycle in response to hormonal shifts.

## How Does Cervical Mucus Work?
As you approach ovulation, rising estrogen levels make the mucus more fluid and alkaline to help sperm survive and swim. After ovulation, progesterone makes the mucus thick and acidic to block sperm and bacteria.

## Real-World Example
During your most fertile days, you may notice mucus that looks and feels like raw egg whites—this is known as "Egg White Cervical Mucus" (EWCM) and is the most conducive to conception.

## Why is it Important?
Cervical mucus is the most reliable "real-time" indicator of your fertile window opening. Unlike BBT (which confirms ovulation after it happens), mucus tells you that ovulation is *approaching*.

## Mucus Types by Cycle Phase

| Phase | Mucus Type | Fertility Level |
|-------|------------|-----------------|
| **Post-Period** | Dry or Scant | Low |
| **Follicular** | Sticky or Tacky | Moderate |
| **Approaching Ovulation** | Creamy or Lotion-like | High |
| **Ovulation** | Clear, Stretchy, Slippery | Peak |
| **Luteal Phase** | Sticky or Dry | Low |
