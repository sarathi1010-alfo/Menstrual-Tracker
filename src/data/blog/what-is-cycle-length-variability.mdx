**Cycle length variability** refers to the natural fluctuations in the total number of days in your menstrual cycle from one month to the next.

## How Does Variability Work?
While the textbook cycle is 28 days, most people experience some shift. If your cycle is 26 days one month and 30 days the next, that is a variability of 4 days. These changes are usually caused by delayed ovulation during the follicular phase.

## Real-World Example
A student might have a very regular 29-day cycle, but during final exam week, the stress delays her ovulation, resulting in a 35-day cycle for that specific month.

## Why is Variability Important?
Understanding your variability helps you:
1. **Reduce Anxiety:** Knowing that a "late" period of a few days is normal for you.
2. **Improve Predictions:** Apps use your average variability to provide "ranges" rather than fixed dates.
3. **Identify Health Shifts:** Significant, persistent changes in variability can signal hormonal imbalances.

## Normal vs. Irregular Variation

| Category | Typical Variation | Description |
|----------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Normal** | < 8 days | Slight shifts due to lifestyle factors. |
| **Moderate** | 8 - 20 days | May indicate stress or perimenopause. |
| **Irregular** | > 20 days | Often associated with conditions like PCOS. |

---

*Medical Disclaimer: If your cycle length consistently varies by more than 8-10 days, or if your cycles are shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, consult a healthcare provider.*
