The fertile window refers to the specific days during the menstrual cycle when intercourse can result in pregnancy. It is determined by the lifespan of both the sperm and the egg.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

While an egg only survives for 12 to 24 hours after it is released during ovulation, sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract for up to five days under the right conditions (facilitated by fertile cervical mucus). Therefore, the fertile window spans the five days leading up to ovulation, plus the day of ovulation itself—a total of about six days.

## Real-World Example of the Fertile Window in Tracking

When you use [LunaCycle](/tracker), the app estimates your fertile window based on your average cycle length. It assumes a standard luteal phase and calculates backwards to estimate when ovulation might occur, highlighting those surrounding days as potentially fertile.

## Fertile Window vs. Safe Days

| Phase | Description | Pregnancy Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Fertile Window** | The days around ovulation when sperm can fertilize an egg. | High |
| **Safe Days** | Days outside the fertile window (e.g., during or immediately after a period). | Low |

*Note: Calendar-based estimations of the fertile window should not be relied upon as a primary method of birth control.*
