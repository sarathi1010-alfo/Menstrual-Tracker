The fertile window represents the narrow timeframe during a menstrual cycle when sexual intercourse can result in pregnancy. It is the biological intersection of two lifespans: the survival time of sperm inside the female reproductive tract, and the lifespan of the egg once it has been released from the ovary.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

To understand the fertile window, you have to look at the biology of the gametes (sperm and egg):

1.  **Sperm Survival:** Under the right conditions—specifically, in the presence of fertile, egg-white-like cervical mucus—sperm can survive in the fallopian tubes for up to five days, waiting for an egg to be released.
2.  **Egg Survival:** Conversely, once ovulation occurs, the egg must be fertilized within 12 to 24 hours. After this brief period, the egg degrades and can no longer be fertilized.

Therefore, the fertile window is generally calculated as the **five days leading up to ovulation, plus the day of ovulation**. Intercourse that occurs on the day *after* ovulation carries a significantly lower chance of conception, and the probability drops to near zero shortly thereafter.

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

If you are trying to conceive, timing is everything. By logging your cycle data into [LunaCycle](/), the application calculates your average cycle length to estimate your upcoming ovulation date. It then highlights the preceding days on your visual calendar, providing a clear map of your fertile window so you can plan accordingly.

## The Fertile Window vs. Ovulation Day

| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation Day |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timeframe** | Approximately 6 days. | A single 12-24 hour event. |
| **Mechanism** | Driven by sperm longevity + egg lifespan. | The physical release of the egg. |
| **Tracking Focus** | Identifying rising estrogen and fertile mucus. | Identifying the LH surge or tracking BBT shifts post-ovulation. |
