## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

The concept of the fertile window relies on a fascinating biological timing mismatch between sperm and the egg.

While a released egg has a very short lifespan—surviving for only 12 to 24 hours after ovulation—sperm are much more resilient. Under the right conditions (specifically, in the presence of fertile cervical mucus), healthy sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract for up to five days.

Therefore, the fertile window spans a total of six days: the five days leading up to ovulation, and the day of ovulation itself. If intercourse occurs during the five days before ovulation, sperm can wait in the fallopian tubes for the egg to be released. If intercourse occurs on the day of ovulation, the freshly introduced sperm can fertilize the newly released egg before it degrades.

Once the egg dies (usually 24 hours after ovulation), the fertile window closes until the next cycle.

## Calculating the Fertile Window

Determining your fertile window requires knowing when you ovulate. Since ovulation generally occurs about 14 days before the start of the next period, you can estimate it based on your average cycle length.

For example, in a standard 28-day cycle:
1.  Next period expected on Day 29.
2.  Estimated ovulation: 28 - 14 = Day 14.
3.  Fertile Window: Days 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, and 14.

*Note: Because cycles can be irregular, manual calendar calculations are just estimates. For quick insights, you can use the [LunaCycle Safe Days Calculator](/tools/safe-days-calculator).*

## Real-World Example: Timing Intercourse

Let's look at a practical example. Maya is trying to conceive and tracks her cycle using [LunaCycle](/). She identifies her fertile window as occurring between Tuesday and Sunday, with Sunday being her predicted day of ovulation.

If Maya and her partner have intercourse on Wednesday, those sperm can survive in her reproductive tract until Sunday. When she ovulates on Sunday, the sperm are already present and capable of fertilizing the egg. The highest probability of conception occurs when intercourse happens 1 to 2 days prior to ovulation (Friday or Saturday in this scenario).

## The Fertile Window vs. Safe Days

For those using cycle tracking to avoid pregnancy (Fertility Awareness Method), understanding the difference is crucial.

| Concept | The Fertile Window | Safe Days (Infertile Phase) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pregnancy Risk** | High. This is the only time conception can occur. | Extremely low to zero (if calculated perfectly). |
| **Duration** | About 6 days per cycle. | The remainder of the cycle outside the fertile window. |
| **Cervical Mucus** | Wet, slippery, clear, stretchy (egg-white). | Dry, sticky, thick, or cloudy. |
| **Timing** | The 5 days before ovulation + the day of ovulation. | After ovulation is confirmed to have passed, until the next fertile window opens. |
