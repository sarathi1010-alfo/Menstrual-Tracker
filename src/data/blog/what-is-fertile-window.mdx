The fertile window is the specific timeframe during the menstrual cycle when a person is most likely to conceive. It is determined by the lifespan of both the sperm and the egg.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

While an egg only lives for 12-24 hours after ovulation, sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract for up to five days in the right conditions (specifically, in fertile cervical mucus). Therefore, the fertile window spans the five days leading up to ovulation, plus the day of ovulation itself—a total of about six days.

## Real-World Example

If ovulation is expected to occur on Day 14 of your cycle, your fertile window roughly spans from Day 9 to Day 14. Intercourse on any of these days carries a chance of pregnancy, with the highest probability occurring on the days immediately preceding ovulation (Days 12 and 13).

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying the fertile window is crucial whether you are actively trying to conceive or trying to avoid pregnancy. It helps couples time intercourse optimally for conception.

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation

| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | The period when conception is possible. | The exact event of egg release. |
| **Duration** | About 6 days. | 12-24 hours. |
| **Focus** | Lifespan of sperm + egg. | Lifespan of the egg only. |
