The fertile window is the specific timeframe during the menstrual cycle when a woman can get pregnant. It typically spans six days: the five days leading up to ovulation, plus the day of ovulation itself.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

While an egg only survives for 12 to 24 hours after being released during ovulation, sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract for up to five days. Therefore, having intercourse in the days *before* ovulation ensures sperm are present when the egg is released.

## Real-World Example

If ovulation occurs on Day 14 of a cycle, the fertile window would be days 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, and 14. Intercourse on any of these days carries a chance of conception, with the highest probability on days 12, 13, and 14.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying this window is essential for family planning. Couples Trying To Conceive (TTC) should time intercourse during this window, while those avoiding pregnancy must use protection or abstain during these days (though calendar tracking alone is not reliable birth control).

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation

| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | ~6 days | 12-24 hours |
| **Components** | Sperm lifespan + egg lifespan | Egg release |
| **Purpose** | The timeframe for conception | The event of egg release |
