The fertile window is the timeframe during the menstrual cycle when intercourse can result in pregnancy, typically spanning the five days leading up to ovulation plus the day of ovulation itself.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

While an egg only survives for 12-24 hours after ovulation, sperm can live inside the female body (specifically in fertile cervical mucus) for up to five days. Therefore, having intercourse in the days *leading up to* ovulation ensures sperm are waiting when the egg is released. Understanding [how to track your cycle](/blog/how-to-track-your-cycle-step-by-step) is key to finding this window.

## Real-World Example

If ovulation is expected on Day 14 of a cycle, the fertile window would roughly span from Day 9 through Day 14. Intercourse on Day 11 could result in pregnancy if the sperm survive until the egg is released on Day 14.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying this window is critical for family planning. For those trying to conceive, timing intercourse during these six days maximizes the chances of success. For those avoiding pregnancy (using fertility awareness methods), this is the time to abstain or use barrier methods. Apps like [LunaCycle](/) help predict this window based on past cycle data.

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation Day

| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation Day |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Duration | ~6 days | 12-24 hours |
| Significance | The entire period when conception is possible. | The specific release of the egg. |
| Sperm Survival | Accommodates the lifespan of sperm. | The narrow window of egg viability. |

### Core Principles
*   Sperm lifespan defines the start of the window.
*   Egg lifespan defines the end of the window.
*   Timing varies based on individual cycles.
*   Tracking cervical mucus is a reliable indicator.
