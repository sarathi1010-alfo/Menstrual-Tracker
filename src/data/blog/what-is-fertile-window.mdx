## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

The fertile window exists because of the different lifespans of the egg and the sperm.

An egg lives for only 12 to 24 hours after being released during ovulation. If it is not fertilized within this time, it dissolves.

Sperm, however, can survive in the female reproductive tract for up to five days in the right conditions (specifically, in the presence of fertile-quality cervical mucus).

Therefore, intercourse that occurs in the days *leading up to* ovulation can result in pregnancy, because the sperm can wait in the fallopian tubes for the egg to be released. The highest chance of conception is 1-2 days before ovulation.

## Real-World Example of Identifying the Fertile Window

If a woman typically ovulates on Day 14 of her cycle, her fertile window would roughly be Days 9 through 14. If she wants to get pregnant, having intercourse during these days maximizes her chances. Tools like [LunaCycle](/) help estimate this window based on average cycle lengths, though physical signs (like cervical mucus changes) offer the most accurate daily confirmation.

## Fertile Window vs. Ovulation

| Term | Definition | Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fertile Window** | The ~6-day period when intercourse can result in conception. | Sperm survival + Egg survival |
| **Ovulation** | The single event of releasing the egg. | Egg release |
