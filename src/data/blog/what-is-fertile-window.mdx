The fertile window is the timeframe during a menstrual cycle when intercourse is most likely to result in pregnancy, typically spanning about six days ending on the day of ovulation.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

The fertile window exists because of the differing lifespans of the egg and sperm. An egg only survives for 12-24 hours after it's released (ovulation). However, sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract for up to 5 days in the presence of fertile cervical mucus. Therefore, the window opens 5 days before ovulation and closes about 24 hours after.

## Real-World Example

If a woman ovulates on Day 14 of her cycle, her fertile window is roughly Days 9 through 14. If she has intercourse on Day 11, the sperm can survive until Day 14 to fertilize the newly released egg.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying the fertile window is the most critical factor for couples Trying To Conceive (TTC). Conversely, understanding this window is also the basis for Natural Family Planning (NFP) or fertility awareness methods used to avoid pregnancy.

## Fertile Window vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Fertile Window | Luteal Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Before and during ovulation | After ovulation |
| **Fertility** | Highest | Almost zero |
| **Hormone** | Driven by Estrogen | Driven by Progesterone |
