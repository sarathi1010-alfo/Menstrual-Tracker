The fertile window is the timeframe during the menstrual cycle when intercourse can result in pregnancy. It typically spans six days: the five days leading up to ovulation, plus the day of ovulation itself.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

While an egg only lives for 12-24 hours after ovulation, sperm can survive in the female reproductive tract for up to five days under the right conditions (facilitated by fertile cervical mucus). Therefore, intercourse in the days *before* ovulation can lead to pregnancy.

## Real-World Example

If ovulation occurs on Day 14 of a cycle, the fertile window would generally span from Day 9 to Day 14. Intercourse on Days 12 or 13 would carry the highest probability of conception.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying the fertile window is crucial for anyone trying to conceive (TTC) to maximize their chances. It is also important knowledge for those avoiding pregnancy using natural methods.

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation

| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | ~6 days | 12-24 hours |
| **Concept** | Timeframe when sperm can survive to fertilize an egg. | The actual release of the egg. |
| **Highest Fertility** | The days immediately preceding ovulation. | The day of release. |
