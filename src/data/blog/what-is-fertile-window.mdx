The fertile window is the specific timeframe during a woman's menstrual cycle when sexual intercourse is most likely to result in pregnancy. It generally spans about six days per cycle.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

The fertile window is determined by the lifespan of both the sperm and the egg:
- **Sperm** can survive inside the female reproductive tract (specifically in fertile cervical mucus) for up to 5 days.
- The **egg** can only survive for 12 to 24 hours after being released (ovulation).
Therefore, the fertile window is the 5 days *before* ovulation, plus the day of ovulation itself.

## Real-World Example

If a woman ovulates on Day 14 of her cycle, her fertile window is approximately Days 9 through 14. Intercourse on Day 11, for example, could lead to pregnancy because the sperm can survive until the egg is released on Day 14.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying the fertile window is essential for family planning. Couples Trying To Conceive (TTC) should time intercourse during this window to maximize their chances. Conversely, those using Fertility Awareness Methods (FAM) to avoid pregnancy must abstain or use barrier methods during this time.

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation Day

| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation Day |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | About 6 days | 12-24 hours |
| **Nature** | A period of time | A specific biological event |
