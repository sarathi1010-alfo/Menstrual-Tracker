# What is the Fertile Window?

The fertile window is the biological timeframe during a menstrual cycle when a woman can get pregnant. It spans approximately six days: the five days leading up to ovulation and the day of ovulation itself. Intercourse outside this window rarely results in conception.

<MedicalDisclaimer />

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

This window exists due to the differing lifespans of sperm and eggs. Sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract (in fertile cervical mucus) for up to 5 days waiting for an egg. The egg, however, only survives for 12 to 24 hours after release. Therefore, having sperm already present *before* ovulation increases the chances of conception.

## Real-World Example

If ovulation occurs on Day 14, the fertile window technically begins around Day 9 and closes on Day 14 (or early Day 15). Intercourse on Day 12 has a high chance of success because the sperm will survive until the egg is released on Day 14.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying this specific window is the core focus of those Trying to Conceive (TTC) to time intercourse effectively. Conversely, those practicing natural family planning use this information to determine which days require abstinence or backup contraception.

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation Day

| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation Day |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | ~6 days | 12-24 hours |
| **Highest Chance of Conception** | 1-2 days before ovulation | The day of ovulation |
| **Purpose** | When intercourse can lead to pregnancy | The actual release of the egg |
