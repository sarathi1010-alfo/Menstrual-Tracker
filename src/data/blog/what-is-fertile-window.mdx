The fertile window is the timeframe during the menstrual cycle when sexual intercourse is most likely to result in pregnancy. It generally spans about six days: the five days preceding ovulation and the day of ovulation itself.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

The fertile window exists because of the different lifespans of the egg and the sperm. An egg only survives for 12 to 24 hours after being released (ovulation). However, healthy sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract for up to five days, provided there is fertile (stretchy, egg-white) cervical mucus.

## Real-World Example

If ovulation occurs on Day 14 of your cycle, your fertile window is roughly Days 9 through 14. Having intercourse on Day 11 means sperm can wait in the fallopian tubes for the egg to be released on Day 14.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying your fertile window is critical if you are trying to conceive (TTC) to time intercourse effectively. Conversely, it is essential for natural family planning (NFP) to know when to avoid unprotected intercourse.

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation

| Concept | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Fertile Window** | A 6-day period defined by sperm lifespan + egg lifespan. |
| **Ovulation** | A 1-day event (the release of the egg). |
