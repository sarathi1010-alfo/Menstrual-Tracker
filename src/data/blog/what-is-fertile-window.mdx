## Definition

The fertile window represents the specific, roughly six-day timeframe during a menstrual cycle when sexual intercourse can result in conception. It is determined by the lifespan of both sperm and the egg. It encompasses the five days leading up to ovulation and the day of ovulation itself.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

The biological basis of the fertile window relies on cellular survival times. An unfertilized egg only lives for 12 to 24 hours after it is released during ovulation. However, healthy sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract—specifically in fertile cervical mucus—for up to five days. Therefore, having sex in the days prior to ovulation means sperm are waiting in the fallopian tubes for the egg to arrive.

## Real-World Example

If a woman with a 28-day cycle ovulates on day 14, her fertile window officially opens around day 9. If she has unprotected intercourse on day 10, the sperm can survive until day 14 to fertilize the newly released egg. By day 16, the window is completely closed, and conception is highly unlikely until the next cycle.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying the fertile window is critical for family planning. For couples trying to conceive (TTC), knowing this window allows them to time intercourse to maximize their chances of pregnancy. Conversely, those practicing fertility awareness-based methods of contraception use this timeframe to abstain or use barrier methods to prevent pregnancy.

## Fertile Window vs Menstruation

| Feature | Fertile Window | Menstruation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | Maximizes chances of conception. | Sheds unneeded uterine lining. |
| **Hormones** | High estrogen leading to LH surge. | Low estrogen and progesterone. |
| **Timing** | Middle of the cycle. | Beginning of the cycle. |
