## How Does the Fertile Window Work?
The fertile window is determined by the biological lifespan of both the sperm and the egg. While an egg only survives for 12 to 24 hours after it is released from the ovary, sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract for up to five days under the right conditions (specifically, in the presence of fertile cervical mucus). Therefore, the fertile window opens five days before ovulation and closes about 24 hours after ovulation occurs. Intercourse during this roughly six-day period is the only time pregnancy can result from a given cycle.

## Real-World Example
Imagine you ovulate on Day 14 of your cycle. Your fertile window would theoretically begin on Day 9. If you have intercourse on Day 10, the sperm can survive in your reproductive tract until the egg is released on Day 14, resulting in fertilization. However, if you wait until Day 16 to have intercourse, the egg will have already dissolved, and pregnancy is no longer possible for that cycle.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?
Understanding your fertile window is the cornerstone of fertility awareness methods. If you are actively trying to conceive, timing intercourse during these days maximizes your chances of success. Conversely, if you are practicing natural family planning to avoid pregnancy, identifying this window tells you when you must abstain or use barrier methods.

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation
| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | A 6-day period | A single event (12-24 hrs) |
| Definition | The time frame when intercourse can lead to pregnancy | The actual release of the egg |
| Focus | Sperm and egg lifespan combined | Egg release only |
