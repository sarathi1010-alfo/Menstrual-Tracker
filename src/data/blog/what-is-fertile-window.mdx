# What is the Fertile Window?

The fertile window refers to the specific timeframe during the menstrual cycle when a woman is most likely to become pregnant. It generally spans six days: the five days leading up to ovulation and the day of ovulation itself. This is because sperm can survive in the female reproductive tract for up to five days, waiting for the egg.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

As ovulation approaches, the body produces more estrogen, which changes cervical mucus to a sperm-friendly environment. Intercourse during these days allows sperm to travel and wait in the fallopian tubes for the egg to be released during [ovulation](/what-is-ovulation).

## Real-World Example

If ovulation is expected on Day 14 of the cycle, the fertile window would be from Day 9 through Day 14. Intercourse on Day 12 or 13 carries the highest probability of resulting in conception.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying the fertile window is crucial for family planning. It helps couples time intercourse optimally when trying to conceive, and is equally important for those using fertility awareness methods to avoid pregnancy.

## Fertile Window vs Luteal Phase

| Feature | Fertile Window | Luteal Phase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timing | Before and during ovulation | After ovulation |
| Fertility | Highest | None (egg has dissolved) |
| Goal | Facilitate fertilization | Prepare uterus for implantation |
