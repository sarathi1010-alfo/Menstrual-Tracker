Definition: The fertile window is the specific timeframe during the menstrual cycle when intercourse can lead to pregnancy. It spans roughly six days: the five days leading up to ovulation and the day of ovulation itself.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

The fertile window exists because of the lifespan of sperm and the egg. Sperm can survive in the female reproductive tract for up to five days under the right conditions (aided by fertile cervical mucus). The egg, however, only lives for 12 to 24 hours after release. Therefore, having intercourse before ovulation ensures sperm are waiting when the egg is released.

## Real-World Example

If a woman ovulates on Day 14 of her cycle, her fertile window is approximately Days 9 through 14. Intercourse on any of these days carries a chance of conception, with the highest probability on Days 12, 13, and 14.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying the fertile window is crucial for family planning. Couples trying to conceive should aim to have intercourse during this time. Conversely, those practicing natural family planning use this knowledge to avoid intercourse during these days. Tools like [LunaCycle](/) help estimate this window based on your cycle history.

## Fertile Window vs Luteal Phase

| Fertile Window | Luteal Phase |
|----------------|--------------|
| High chance of conception | Zero chance of conception |
| Precedes and includes ovulation | Follows ovulation |
| Driven by rising estrogen | Driven by progesterone |
