The fertile window is the specific timeframe during a menstrual cycle when a woman can get pregnant. It typically lasts for about six days: the five days leading up to ovulation, plus the day of ovulation itself.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

The fertile window exists because of the different lifespans of the egg and sperm. While an egg survives for only 12-24 hours after being released, sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract in fertile cervical fluid for up to 5 days. Therefore, intercourse before ovulation can result in conception.

## Real-World Example

If a woman ovulates on Day 14 of her cycle, her fertile window is roughly from Day 9 through Day 14. Intercourse on any of these days carries a chance of pregnancy, with the highest probability on Days 12, 13, and 14.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying this window is crucial for anyone trying to conceive (TTC). Timing intercourse within this timeframe maximizes the chances of pregnancy. Conversely, those avoiding pregnancy use this information for natural family planning.

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation

| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | About 6 days. | 12-24 hours. |
| **Concept** | The timeframe when conception is possible. | The event of the egg being released. |
| **Tracking** | Tracked via cervical mucus and LH surges. | Confirmed via BBT rise or ultrasound. |
