## What is the Fertile Window?

The fertile window is the specific timeframe during the menstrual cycle when pregnancy is possible. It typically lasts for about six days: the five days leading up to ovulation, plus the day of ovulation itself.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

The length of this window is determined by the lifespans of sperm and the egg. Sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract for up to five days under the right conditions (specifically, in fertile cervical mucus). The egg, however, only survives for 12 to 24 hours after being released. Therefore, intercourse leading up to ovulation provides the highest chance of the sperm meeting the egg.

For a deeper dive into the phases surrounding this window, read [Cycle Phases Explained](/blog/cycle-phases-explained-follicular-ovulation-luteal).

## Real-World Example

If a person ovulates on day 14 of their cycle, their fertile window would roughly be from day 9 to day 14. Intercourse on day 12 or 13 would carry a high probability of conception.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Knowing your fertile window is essential for family planning. If you are trying to conceive, timing intercourse during these days maximizes your chances. Conversely, if you are avoiding pregnancy using natural family planning methods, you must avoid unprotected intercourse during this time.

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation Day

| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation Day |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Duration | ~6 days | 12-24 hours |
| Description | The entire period when conception is possible due to sperm survival. | The single event of the egg being released. |
| Purpose | Maximizes the chance of sperm being present when the egg is released. | The release of the egg for fertilization. |
