The fertile window is the timeframe of about six days during the [menstrual cycle](/blog/what-is-menstrual-cycle) when unprotected intercourse can result in pregnancy. It relies on the differing lifespans of sperm and the egg to create a window of opportunity for conception.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

The fertile window is calculated based on two biological facts. First, healthy sperm can survive in hospitable cervical mucus for up to five days. Second, a released egg only survives for 12 to 24 hours after [ovulation](/blog/what-is-ovulation). Therefore, the fertile window opens five days before ovulation occurs and closes roughly 24 hours after the egg is released.

## Real-World Example

If a woman is predicted to ovulate on Day 14 of her cycle, her fertile window begins on Day 9. If she has intercourse on Day 11, the sperm can survive in her reproductive tract until Day 14, at which point it can fertilize the newly released egg. ## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying the fertile window is crucial whether a person is actively trying to conceive (TTC) or actively trying to prevent pregnancy. For those TTC, timing intercourse during this window maximizes the chances of success. For those practicing fertility awareness for birth control, identifying this window dictates when to abstain or use barrier methods.

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation Day

It's common to conflate the entire window with the exact day of ovulation.

| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation Day |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | Up to 6 days. | 12-24 hours. |
| **Biology** | Dictated by the lifespan of sperm. | Dictated by the lifespan of the egg. |
| **Pregnancy Chance** | Varies; highest 1-2 days before ovulation. | Highest chance if intercourse occurred prior. |
| **Tracking Method** | Primarily tracked via cervical mucus changes. | Confirmed via basal body temperature (BBT) shift. |
