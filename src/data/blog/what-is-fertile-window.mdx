The fertile window is the timeframe during a woman's menstrual cycle when intercourse can result in pregnancy. It typically lasts for about six days: the five days leading up to ovulation and the day of ovulation itself.

## How Does the Fertile Window Work?

While an egg only survives for 12 to 24 hours after being released, sperm can survive inside the female reproductive tract for up to five days under the right conditions (fertile cervical mucus). Therefore, having intercourse in the days just *before* ovulation ensures sperm are waiting when the egg is released.

## Real-World Example

If ovulation is expected on Day 14 of a cycle, the fertile window would roughly be Days 9 through 14. Intercourse on Day 12 or 13 carries the highest probability of conception.

## Why is the Fertile Window Important?

Identifying this window is critical for family planning. Couples trying to conceive need to time intercourse during these days, while those avoiding pregnancy naturally must abstain or use barrier methods during this time.

## Fertile Window vs Ovulation

| Feature | Fertile Window | Ovulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | About 6 days. | 12-24 hours. |
| **Timing** | Before and during ovulation. | A specific event midway through the cycle. |
| **Mechanism** | Accounts for sperm lifespan + egg lifespan. | The release of the egg only. |
