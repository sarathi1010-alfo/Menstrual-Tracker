The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, characterized by the production of progesterone to prepare the uterine lining for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty follicle turns into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 through 28. During this time, you might experience PMS symptoms like bloating or mood changes due to fluctuating progesterone.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (at least 10-12 days long) is necessary to sustain a pregnancy in its early stages.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|--------------|------------------|
| Post-ovulation. | Pre-ovulation. |
| Dominated by progesterone. | Dominated by estrogen. |
