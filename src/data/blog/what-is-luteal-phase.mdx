# What is the Luteal Phase?

**Definition:** The luteal phase is the stage of the menstrual cycle that begins after ovulation and ends with the start of the next period. It is dominated by the hormone progesterone.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a fertilized egg. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, a woman might experience PMS symptoms like breast tenderness or mood changes.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is crucial for maintaining a pregnancy in its earliest stages. If the phase is too short (less than 10 days), the uterine lining may not be thick enough for an egg to implant.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Primary Goal** | Support potential pregnancy | Mature an egg |
