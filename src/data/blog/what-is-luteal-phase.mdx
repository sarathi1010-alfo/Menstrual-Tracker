**The luteal phase is the latter portion of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending the day before the next period starts. It prepares the reproductive system for potential pregnancy.**

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After releasing an egg, the ruptured ovarian follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes progesterone, a hormone that thickens and stabilizes the uterine lining, making it receptive to a fertilized egg.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14, the luteal phase spans from day 15 to day 28. During the late luteal phase, as progesterone levels decline (if there is no pregnancy), women often experience Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) symptoms like bloating and mood changes.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy, adequately long luteal phase (usually 10-16 days) is vital for fertility, as it allows enough time for a fertilized egg to implant in the uterus. If it's too short, the lining sheds before implantation can occur.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Goal** | Prepare for implantation | Mature an egg |
