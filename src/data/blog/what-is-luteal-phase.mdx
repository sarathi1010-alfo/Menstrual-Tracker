The luteal phase is the latter part of the menstrual cycle that occurs after ovulation and before the next period starts. It typically lasts between 12 and 14 days and is the most consistent phase in length.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?
After ovulation, the empty follicle turns into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If an egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle, the luteal phase usually spans from Day 15 to Day 28. This is when many people experience Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) symptoms like bloating or mood changes.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?
A healthy luteal phase of at least 11 days is necessary to maintain a uterine lining long enough for a fertilized egg to implant.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Timing | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
