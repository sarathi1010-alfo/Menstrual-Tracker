## What is the Luteal Phase?

**Definition:** The luteal phase is the post-ovulatory phase of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after an egg is released and ends when menstruation begins.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?
After ovulation, the empty follicle turns into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum shrinks, progesterone drops, and your period begins.

## Real-World Example
In a cycle where ovulation happens on day 14 and the next period starts on day 29, the luteal phase spans days 15 to 28. During this time, you might experience breast tenderness or mood shifts.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?
A sufficient luteal phase length (at least 10 days) is necessary for an embryo to implant. A short luteal phase can be a sign of low progesterone and a cause of infertility.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase
| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| Main Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
