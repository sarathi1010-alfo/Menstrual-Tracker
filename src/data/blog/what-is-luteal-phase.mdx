The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, occurring after ovulation and before the next period starts. It prepares the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the ovary releases an egg, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes progesterone, which thickens and stabilizes the uterine lining, making it a hospitable environment for a fertilized egg to implant. If implantation doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels plummet, and the lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 to 28. During the first week of this phase, a woman might feel calm and grounded as progesterone rises. During the second week, as hormones begin to fall, she might experience PMS symptoms like bloating or moodiness.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is essential for maintaining early pregnancy. A luteal phase that is too short (under 10 days) may not give an embryo enough time to implant securely. It's also the phase where premenstrual syndrome (PMS) occurs, deeply impacting daily life for many women.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Purpose** | Prepare uterus for pregnancy | Prepare egg for release |
