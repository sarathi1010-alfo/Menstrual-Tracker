## What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the second main half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. Its primary purpose is to prepare the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the ovary releases an egg, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes high levels of progesterone (and some estrogen). Progesterone thickens the uterine lining, creating a hospitable environment for a fertilized egg to implant. If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, hormone levels drop, and this drop triggers menstruation.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on day 14, the luteal phase spans from day 15 to day 28. During the later part of this phase (days 21-28), many people experience PMS symptoms like bloating, mood changes, or breast tenderness due to the fluctuating hormones.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A sufficiently long luteal phase (usually at least 11 days) is necessary for a fertilized egg to successfully implant. If the luteal phase is too short (luteal phase defect), the uterine lining may shed before an egg has a chance to implant, leading to early miscarriage.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Primary Goal | Prepare uterus for implantation. | Develop and mature an egg. |
