## What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the latter half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. During this time, the body prepares the uterine lining for the potential implantation of a fertilized egg.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the egg is released during ovulation, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes progesterone (and some estrogen), which thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase will last from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, you might experience PMS symptoms like breast tenderness or mood changes due to the hormonal shifts.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (usually 12-16 days) is crucial for maintaining a pregnancy, as it ensures the uterine lining is adequately prepared. You can customize your luteal phase length in the settings of [LunaCycle](/tracker) for more accurate predictions.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Timing | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| Variability | Very consistent (~14 days) | Highly variable |
