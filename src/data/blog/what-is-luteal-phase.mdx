## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the ruptured follicle left on the ovary transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a fertilized egg. If fertilization doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the lining sheds as a period.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase covers Days 15 through 28. During this time, a person might notice breast tenderness, slight bloating, or mood changes as progesterone peaks and then falls.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

The luteal phase is crucial for early pregnancy because it provides the structural support (a thick uterine lining) needed for an embryo to implant. Its length is usually very consistent for each individual, making it a reliable metric in cycle tracking.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---------|--------------|------------------|
| Timing | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| Key Hormone | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| Function | Prepares uterus for implantation. | Matures the egg. |
