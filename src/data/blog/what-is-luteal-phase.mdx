## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?
After the egg is released, the empty follicle turns into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. Progesterone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and this triggers your period.

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle with ovulation on Day 14, the luteal phase runs from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, many women experience premenstrual symptoms (PMS) like bloating or breast tenderness as hormone levels fluctuate.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?
The luteal phase is critical for maintaining early pregnancy. If it is too short (less than 10 days), the uterine lining might shed before a fertilized egg has time to implant, which can affect fertility. You can track this phase using [LunaCycle](/tracker).

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| Main Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Length | Very consistent (12-14 days) | Highly variable |
