The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, occurring after ovulation and before the next period starts. It prepares the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?
After the egg is released, the ruptured follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the uterine lining sheds, starting a new cycle.

## Real-World Example
In a standard 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Day 15 to Day 28. It is generally the most consistent phase of the cycle, usually lasting exactly 12-14 days.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?
A healthy luteal phase is essential for maintaining a pregnancy. If the phase is too short (a 'luteal phase defect'), the uterine lining may begin to shed before a fertilized egg has time to implant. The hormonal drops in this phase are also responsible for PMS symptoms.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Length** | Usually fixed (12-14 days) | Variable length |
