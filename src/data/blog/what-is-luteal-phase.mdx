The luteal phase is the latter portion of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. It is named after the corpus luteum, the structure that forms in the ovary after an egg is released.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

Once the egg is released, the empty follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes massive amounts of progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining to prepare it for a fertilized egg. If pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase will typically run from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, you might notice your basal body temperature (BBT) remains elevated by about half a degree, and you may experience PMS symptoms like breast tenderness or bloating as the phase concludes.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase of at least 11 days is necessary to sustain early pregnancy. Additionally, the hormonal crash at the end of this phase is the primary driver of Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS).

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Timing | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| Length | Usually fixed (12-14 days) | Highly variable |
