**Definition:** The luteal phase is the stage of the menstrual cycle that begins immediately after ovulation and ends with the start of your period. It is characterized by the production of progesterone to prepare the uterine lining for a fertilized egg.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty follicle becomes the *corpus luteum*, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase runs from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, a woman might experience PMS symptoms like bloating or mood changes as hormones fluctuate.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy, adequately long luteal phase (usually 11-17 days) is essential for maintaining a pregnancy. If it's too short (Luteal Phase Defect), the uterine lining may shed before a fertilized egg can implant.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation. | Pre-ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Purpose** | Prepare uterus for implantation. | Mature an egg for release. |
