## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty follicle turns into a temporary endocrine structure called the *corpus luteum*. This structure produces progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining to support a fertilized egg. If no egg implants, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a 32-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 18, the luteal phase runs from Day 19 to Day 32. During this time, you might experience breast tenderness or slight bloating due to elevated progesterone.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is required for successful embryo implantation. If the phase is too short (under 10 days), the uterine lining may shed before a fertilized egg has a chance to attach.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Length** | Usually fixed (12-14 days) | Variable (determines cycle length) |
