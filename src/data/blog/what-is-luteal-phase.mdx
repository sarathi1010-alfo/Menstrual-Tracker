The luteal phase is the second half of the [menstrual cycle](/what-is-menstrual-cycle), occurring after [ovulation](/what-is-ovulation) and before the next period starts. It is characterized by the production of progesterone.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the egg is released, the ruptured follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining, preparing it for a fertilized egg. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, if ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, a woman might notice a slight elevation in her basal body temperature and experience PMS symptoms like bloating or mood changes as progesterone peaks and then falls.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is essential for maintaining early pregnancy. If it is too short (less than 10 days), the uterine lining may shed before a fertilized egg has time to implant.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | After ovulation, before menstruation. | Before ovulation, starts with menstruation. |
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| Length | Usually a consistent 12-14 days. | Varies greatly between women. |
