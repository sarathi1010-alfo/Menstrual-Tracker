The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending with the start of your next period. Its primary purpose is to prepare the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. Progesterone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, hormone levels drop, and the lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 to 28. During the final days of this phase, premenstrual symptoms (PMS) like bloating or mood changes may begin.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is necessary for a fertilized egg to successfully implant in the uterus. Tracking this phase helps you anticipate PMS and understand your fertility window.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation (second half). | Before ovulation (first half). |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Primary Function** | Preparing uterus for pregnancy. | Preparing egg for release. |
