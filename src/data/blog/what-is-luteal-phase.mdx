## What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the final stage of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending the day before your next period starts. During this time, the body prepares the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty follicle turns into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a standard 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 to 28. During this time, a woman might notice breast tenderness, slight bloating, or mood changes, particularly in the days right before her period.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A sufficiently long luteal phase (at least 10-12 days) is necessary for a fertilized egg to implant successfully in the uterus. If the phase is too short, early miscarriage can occur. It's also the phase where Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) occurs due to hormonal fluctuations.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Purpose | Prepare for pregnancy | Prepare an egg for release |
