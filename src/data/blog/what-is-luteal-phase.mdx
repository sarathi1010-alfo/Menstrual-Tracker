# What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the time between ovulation and the start of your next period. During this phase, the body focuses on preparing the uterus to receive a fertilized egg.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the egg is released, the ruptured follicle transforms into the 'corpus luteum,' which secretes progesterone. Progesterone thickens the uterine lining and prevents the release of more eggs. If pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum withers, hormone levels drop, and the lining is shed (menstruation).

## Real-World Example of the Luteal Phase

In a [LunaCycle chart](/tracker), the luteal phase is often marked by a sustained rise in basal body temperature. If you ovulated on Day 14 and your period starts on Day 28, your luteal phase was 14 days long.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

The length of the luteal phase is critical for fertility; it must be long enough for an embryo to implant. Additionally, many people experience PMS during this phase due to the shifting balance of estrogen and progesterone.

## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Body Temp** | Higher | Lower |
| **Timing** | Post-Ovulation | Pre-Ovulation |
