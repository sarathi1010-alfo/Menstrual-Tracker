The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending the day before a menstrual period starts. It is characterized by the dominance of the hormone progesterone.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?
After the egg is released, the empty follicle turns into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a potential embryo. If no pregnancy occurs, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on day 14, the luteal phase encompasses days 15 through 28. During this time, a woman might experience changes in mood or energy due to hormonal shifts.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?
A healthy luteal phase is critical for establishing a pregnancy. If it is too short, the uterine lining may shed before an embryo has a chance to implant. Tracking its length consistently in [LunaCycle](/) can provide valuable reproductive health insights.

## Follicular Phase vs Luteal Phase
| Feature | Follicular Phase | Luteal Phase |
|---------|------------------|--------------|
| Timing | Before ovulation | After ovulation |
| Main Hormone | Estrogen | Progesterone |
