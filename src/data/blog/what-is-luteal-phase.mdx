## What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. During this time, the body prepares the uterus for the possible implantation of a fertilized egg.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After releasing an egg, the ruptured ovarian follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. The corpus luteum secretes large amounts of progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining and slightly raises basal body temperature. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels plummet, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, if ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans from Day 15 to Day 28. Unlike the follicular phase, which can vary greatly in length, the luteal phase is usually very consistent for each individual, almost always lasting 12 to 14 days.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is crucial for maintaining a pregnancy in its earliest stages. If the luteal phase is too short (less than 10 days), the uterine lining may begin to shed before a fertilized egg has a chance to implant, which can lead to early miscarriage or difficulty conceiving.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Duration** | Consistent (12-14 days) | Variable |
