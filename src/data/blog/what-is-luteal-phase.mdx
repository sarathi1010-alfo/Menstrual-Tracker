The luteal phase is the post-ovulation portion of the menstrual cycle. During this time, the body produces progesterone to thicken the uterine lining, preparing it for a fertilized egg.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?
After releasing an egg, the ovarian follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum, which pumps out progesterone. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Day 15 through Day 28.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?
A healthy luteal phase is necessary for a fertilized egg to implant. It's also the phase where Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) occurs.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| Main Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
