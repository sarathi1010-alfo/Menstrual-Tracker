## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the ovary releases an egg, the empty follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the lining sheds, starting the next period.

## Real-World Example

A woman tracking her Basal Body Temperature (BBT) notices her temperature spikes on Day 15. This confirms ovulation. She then experiences 13 days of elevated temperatures before her period begins on Day 29. Those 13 days constitute her luteal phase.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (usually 11-17 days) is vital for pregnancy, as it gives a fertilized egg time to implant. The drop in hormones at the end of this phase is also what causes Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) symptoms like mood swings, bloating, and fatigue.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Length** | Usually consistent (12-14 days) | Highly variable |
