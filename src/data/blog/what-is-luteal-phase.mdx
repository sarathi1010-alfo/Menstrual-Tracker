Definition: The luteal phase is the final stage of the menstrual cycle. It begins right after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts, lasting roughly 12 to 14 days.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the egg is released, the ruptured follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum, which pumps out progesterone. Progesterone thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a fertilized egg. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels plummet, and the uterine lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 to 28. During Days 24-28, as hormones drop, you might notice classic PMS symptoms like bloating or moodiness.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

The luteal phase is crucial for maintaining a healthy uterine environment for pregnancy. Its length is a strong indicator of overall hormonal health and fertility.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| Focus | Preparing for pregnancy | Maturing an egg |
