**Definition:** The luteal phase is the latter part of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. Its primary function is to prepare the uterus for a possible pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After releasing an egg, the empty ovarian follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a standard cycle, the luteal phase covers days 15 through 28. During this time, the rise in progesterone might cause your basal body temperature to remain elevated. As the phase ends and hormones drop, you might experience PMS symptoms like bloating or mood swings. Track these changes in [LunaCycle](/).

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (usually 12-14 days) is necessary to sustain early pregnancy. A short luteal phase (less than 10 days) can make it difficult for a fertilized egg to implant and may indicate a hormonal imbalance.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Primary Goal | Prepare uterus for pregnancy | Mature the egg |
