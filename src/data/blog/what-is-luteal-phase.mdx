The luteal phase is the final phase of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending with the start of your period. It is primarily focused on preparing the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the egg is released, the ruptured follicle transforms into a temporary endocrine gland called the corpus luteum. This gland secretes high levels of progesterone (and some estrogen). Progesterone thickens and stabilizes the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels plummet, and the lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a standard 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase runs from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, a woman might notice her basal body temperature remains slightly elevated compared to the first half of her cycle.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy, sufficiently long luteal phase (11-14 days) is necessary to sustain early pregnancy. The drop in hormones at the end of this phase is also what causes Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) symptoms in many women.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| Primary Function | Prepare for implantation. | Mature the egg. |
