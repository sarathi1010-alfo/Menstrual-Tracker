The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, occurring after ovulation and before the start of the next period. It typically lasts 12-14 days and is dominated by the hormone progesterone.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining in preparation for a fertilized egg to implant.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans from Day 15 to Day 28.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is essential for early pregnancy. If it is too short (less than 10 days), a fertilized egg may not have time to implant before menstruation begins.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| Dominant hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
