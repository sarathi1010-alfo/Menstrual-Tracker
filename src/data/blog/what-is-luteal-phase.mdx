The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending when your next period starts. It is named after the corpus luteum, the structure that forms in the ovary after the egg is released.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

The corpus luteum secretes progesterone, which stabilizes the uterine lining. If the egg isn't fertilized, the corpus luteum stops producing progesterone after about 10-16 days. This drop in hormones causes the lining to break down, triggering menstruation and the start of a new cycle.

## Real-World Example of the Luteal Phase

If you have a 28-day cycle and ovulate on Day 14, your luteal phase lasts from Day 15 to Day 28. If you notice your mood becomes more introspective or you experience breast tenderness during these two weeks, you are feeling the effects of the luteal phase.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase must be long enough (at least 10 days) to allow a fertilized egg to implant in the uterus. It is also the phase associated with Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) as the body reacts to the rising and falling of progesterone.
