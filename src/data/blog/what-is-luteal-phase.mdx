## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the ruptured follicle turns into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. Progesterone thickens the uterine lining to support an embryo. If fertilization doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels plummet, and the lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a standard 28-day cycle, if ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 through 28. During the late luteal phase (e.g., Days 24-28), many experience Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) due to shifting hormones.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A sufficiently long luteal phase (at least 10-12 days) is necessary for a fertilized egg to implant. If the luteal phase is too short (Luteal Phase Defect), it can lead to early miscarriage or difficulty conceiving.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation. | Pre-ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Main Function** | Preparing the uterus for implantation. | Preparing the egg for release. |
