The luteal phase is the latter half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. During this time, the body is primed for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the ovary releases an egg during ovulation, the empty follicle transforms into a temporary endocrine structure called the corpus luteum. The corpus luteum secretes large amounts of progesterone (and some estrogen). Progesterone stops the uterine lining from thickening further and instead matures it, making it receptive to a fertilized egg.

If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum degenerates, progesterone levels drop sharply, and the uterine lining begins to shed (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle and ovulate on Day 14, your luteal phase spans Days 15 through 28. During the late luteal phase (e.g., Days 24-28), the drop in hormones often triggers Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) symptoms like bloating, breast tenderness, and mood changes.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is crucial for fertility. If it is too short (less than 10 days), the uterine lining may begin to shed before a fertilized egg has time to implant, which can lead to early miscarriage.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
