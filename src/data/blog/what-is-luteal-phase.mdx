# What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the latter stage of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending with the start of your next period. It is a critical phase for reproductive health and early pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

Once an egg is released during ovulation, the ruptured follicle transforms into a temporary endocrine gland called the corpus luteum. The corpus luteum secretes large amounts of progesterone (and some estrogen). Progesterone thickens the uterine lining (endometrium), making it receptive to a fertilized egg.

If fertilization and implantation do not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, causing progesterone and estrogen levels to fall. This drop in hormones triggers the shedding of the uterine lining, which is your period. The hormonal fluctuations during this phase are also responsible for Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) symptoms like mood swings, bloating, and breast tenderness.

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

Tracking the length of your luteal phase is important. Unlike the follicular phase, which can vary greatly in length, the luteal phase is usually consistent for each individual (typically 12-14 days). ## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Primary Goal** | Prepare uterus for pregnancy. | Mature an egg for ovulation. |
| **Length** | Usually consistent (11-17 days). | Highly variable. |
