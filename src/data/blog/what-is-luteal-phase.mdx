The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, occurring immediately after ovulation and ending the day before your next period starts. Its primary function is to prepare the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes large amounts of progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels fall, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle with ovulation on Day 14, the luteal phase spans from Day 15 to Day 28. Unlike the follicular phase, which can vary in length, the luteal phase is usually a consistent 12-14 days for most women.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is necessary for a fertilized egg to implant and grow. Furthermore, the hormonal shifts during this phase are responsible for premenstrual syndrome (PMS) symptoms.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Goal** | Maintain uterine lining | Mature an egg |
