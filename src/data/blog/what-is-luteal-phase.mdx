The luteal phase is the latter part of the menstrual cycle that begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. During this time, the body produces progesterone to thicken the uterine lining.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?
The ruptured follicle that released the egg turns into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. If no pregnancy occurs, the corpus luteum degrades, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example
In a typical cycle, if you ovulate on day 14 and your period starts on day 29, your luteal phase spans days 15 through 28 (14 days long).

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?
A healthy luteal phase is necessary to sustain early pregnancy. It's also when PMS symptoms typically occur.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase
| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| Main Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
