## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the ruptured follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes large amounts of progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining to support a potential pregnancy. If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and the uterine lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the luteal phase begins right after ovulation on Day 15 and continues until Day 28. During the first week (Days 15-21), you might feel calm and grounded as progesterone peaks. During the second week (Days 22-28), as hormones crash, you may experience PMS symptoms like bloating or moodiness.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (at least 10-12 days long) is vital for sustaining an early pregnancy. A "luteal phase defect" (a very short phase) can be a cause of early miscarriage or difficulty conceiving.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Length Variability** | Very stable (12-14 days) | Highly variable |
