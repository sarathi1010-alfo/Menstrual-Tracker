## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If an egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the luteal phase typically spans from Day 15 to Day 28. If a woman has a 30-day cycle but ovulates on Day 16, her luteal phase is still 14 days (Days 17-30).

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (at least 10-12 days long) is necessary for a fertilized egg to successfully implant in the uterus. If it's too short, it can cause fertility issues.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Body Goal** | Prepare for implantation | Develop an egg |
