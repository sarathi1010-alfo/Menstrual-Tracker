The luteal phase is the latter half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. During this time, the body is preparing for a possible pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After releasing an egg, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining to support an embryo. If fertilization doesn't happen, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 28-day cycle and ovulates on Day 14, her luteal phase lasts from Day 15 to Day 28. Unlike the first half of the cycle, the luteal phase length is usually very consistent for a given individual, typically lasting 12 to 14 days.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy, adequately long luteal phase is necessary to sustain a pregnancy in its earliest stages. If the phase is too short (a "luteal phase defect"), the lining may shed before an embryo has time to implant. It is also the phase where Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) occurs due to fluctuating hormones.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Primary Goal** | Prepare uterus for pregnancy. | Mature an egg for release. |
