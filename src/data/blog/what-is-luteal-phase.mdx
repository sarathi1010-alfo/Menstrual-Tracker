The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. During this time, the body prepares for a potential pregnancy by thickening the uterine lining.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the ovary releases an egg, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone, a hormone that stabilizes the uterine lining. If the egg is fertilized, progesterone levels stay high. If not, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone and estrogen levels drop, and the uterine lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle and ovulate on Day 14, your luteal phase spans from Day 15 to Day 28. During the final days (Days 24-28), as hormones drop, you might experience PMS symptoms like bloating or mood changes.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

The luteal phase is critical for maintaining an early pregnancy. If the phase is too short (less than 10 days), the uterine lining may begin to shed before a fertilized egg has a chance to implant. It is also the phase responsible for Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS).

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Length** | Consistently 12-14 days. | Varies greatly (usually 10-16 days). |
