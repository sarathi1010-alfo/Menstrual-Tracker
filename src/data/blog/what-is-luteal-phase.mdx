## What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the latter half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. During this phase, the body is actively preparing the uterus for the possible implantation of a fertilized egg.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

Once the egg is released, the empty follicle transforms into a temporary structure called the *corpus luteum*.
- The corpus luteum secretes **progesterone**, which thickens and stabilizes the uterine lining.
- If pregnancy occurs, the corpus luteum continues producing progesterone.
- If pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop rapidly, and this drop triggers menstruation.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14:
- The luteal phase runs from Day 15 to Day 28.
- During days 24-28, as hormones drop, a woman might experience PMS symptoms like bloating, breast tenderness, or mood swings.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is crucial for fertility. It must be long enough (at least 10-12 days) and have sufficient progesterone to support a pregnancy until the placenta takes over. For those not trying to conceive, the luteal phase is significant because the hormonal drop at the end is the primary cause of PMS.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation. | Pre-ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Primary Goal** | Maintain uterine lining. | Mature an egg and build lining. |
