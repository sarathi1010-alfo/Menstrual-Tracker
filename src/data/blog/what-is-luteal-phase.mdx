The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends when the next menstrual period starts. This phase is characterized by the production of progesterone, which prepares the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?
After the egg is released, the ruptured follicle turns into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. If the egg isn't fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle, if you ovulate on Day 14, your luteal phase spans from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, you might experience symptoms like breast tenderness, bloating, or mood changes, commonly known as PMS.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?
A healthy luteal phase (usually 12-14 days long) is necessary for a fertilized egg to successfully implant in the uterine lining. A phase shorter than 10 days (luteal phase defect) can make it difficult to sustain a pregnancy.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Purpose | Prepare for implantation | Prepare an egg for release |
