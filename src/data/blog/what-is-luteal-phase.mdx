Definition: The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, starting immediately after ovulation and ending the day before your next period begins. Its primary purpose is to prepare the uterus for a possible pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

The empty follicle that released the egg turns into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone to thicken and stabilize the uterine lining.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle with ovulation on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 to 28.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is necessary for a fertilized egg to implant. It is also the phase where Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) occurs. You can track its length using [LunaCycle](/).

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| Main Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
