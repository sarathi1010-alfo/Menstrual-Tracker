# What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the latter part of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending the day before your next period starts. During this time, the body prepares for a possible pregnancy. It typically lasts between 12 and 14 days and is relatively consistent in length for most people.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After releasing an egg, the empty ovarian follicle turns into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining to support a fertilized egg. If pregnancy doesn't happen, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the lining sheds, starting the [menstrual cycle](/what-is-menstrual-cycle) over again.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans from Day 15 to Day 28. During the later days of this phase, a person might experience PMS symptoms like bloating or mood changes as hormone levels begin to decline.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is essential for maintaining early pregnancy. A phase that is too short may prevent a fertilized egg from successfully implanting.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timing | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| Main Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Purpose | Prepare for implantation | Prepare an egg |
