The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, occurring immediately after ovulation and ending when the next period begins. It typically lasts between 12 and 16 days.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?
After the egg is released, the empty follicle turns into a structure called the corpus luteum, which pumps out progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a fertilized egg. If pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and the lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 to 28. During this time, you might experience breast tenderness, bloating, or mood changes as a result of fluctuating progesterone levels.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?
A healthy luteal phase is vital for early pregnancy, as it ensures the uterine lining is adequately prepared for implantation. It is also the phase most associated with Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) due to the sharp drop in hormones right before bleeding begins.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---------|--------------|------------------|
| **Timing** | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Length** | Usually fixed (12-16 days) | Can vary widely |
