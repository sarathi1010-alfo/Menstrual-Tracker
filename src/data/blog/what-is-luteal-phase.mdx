The luteal phase is the latter part of the menstrual cycle, beginning after ovulation and ending with the start of the next period. During this time, the body prepares for a potential pregnancy by thickening the uterine lining.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the ruptured follicle turns into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes progesterone, which stabilizes the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14, the luteal phase spans days 15 through 28. During the final days of this phase (often called the late luteal phase), women may experience PMS symptoms like bloating or mood swings.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (at least 11 days long) is necessary for a fertilized egg to successfully implant in the uterus. If it is too short (Luteal Phase Defect), it can lead to early miscarriage.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
