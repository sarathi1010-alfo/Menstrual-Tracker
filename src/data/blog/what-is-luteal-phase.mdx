The luteal phase is the stage of the menstrual cycle that begins after ovulation and ends with the start of your next period. During this time, the body prepares for a possible pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After releasing an egg, the follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. Progesterone keeps the uterine lining thick and receptive. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the lining sheds (your period).

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans from Day 15 to Day 28. Unlike the follicular phase, the luteal phase is usually very consistent in length, lasting 12-14 days for most women.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is necessary to sustain early pregnancy. It is also the phase associated with Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS). Tracking your cycle with [LunaCycle](/) helps you anticipate this phase.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
