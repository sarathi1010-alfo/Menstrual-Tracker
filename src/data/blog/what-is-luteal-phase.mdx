## What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, starting after ovulation and ending with the first day of your period. It is characterized by high levels of progesterone, which prepare the body for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty follicle becomes a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase would last from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, you might experience PMS symptoms like bloating or mood changes.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (usually 12-14 days) is necessary to sustain early pregnancy. A very short luteal phase (less than 10 days) can make it difficult for an embryo to implant.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Purpose** | Prepare for pregnancy. | Prepare to release an egg. |
