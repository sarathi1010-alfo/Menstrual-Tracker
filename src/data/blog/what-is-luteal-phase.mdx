# What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the latter half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends on the first day of your next period. During this time, the body prepares for a potential pregnancy by thickening the uterine lining.

<MedicalDisclaimer />

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After releasing an egg, the ruptured ovarian follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum, which pumps out progesterone. Progesterone keeps the uterine lining thick and healthy. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop sharply, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a cycle, if you ovulate on Day 16 and your period starts on Day 30, your luteal phase lasted 14 days. During these 14 days, you might experience lower energy levels and PMS symptoms like breast tenderness or cravings in the days just before Day 30 due to the changing hormones.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (usually 12-14 days) is crucial for fertility; if it's too short, a fertilized egg won't have enough time to implant securely before the uterine lining sheds. It is also the phase where Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) and PMDD occur.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Energy Levels** | Often lower, inward-focused | Often higher, outward-focused |
