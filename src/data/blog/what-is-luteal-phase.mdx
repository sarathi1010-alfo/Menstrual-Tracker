## What is the Luteal Phase?

The luteal phase is the latter part of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. Its primary purpose is to prepare the uterine lining (endometrium) for a fertilized egg to implant.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the egg is released during ovulation, the empty follicle on the ovary transforms into a structure called the *corpus luteum*. This structure secretes large amounts of progesterone. Progesterone stops the uterine lining from growing further and instead stabilizes it, making it receptive to implantation. If pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels plummet, and the lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 to 28. During this time, basal body temperature rises slightly due to progesterone, and you might experience PMS symptoms around Days 24-28.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A sufficient luteal phase is critical for early pregnancy. If the phase is too short or lacks sufficient progesterone (a "luteal phase defect"), a fertilized egg may not have enough time or the right environment to implant successfully before the lining sheds.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | *After* ovulation | *Before* ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Primary Goal** | Prepare uterus for implantation | Mature an egg for release |
