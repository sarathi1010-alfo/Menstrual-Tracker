The luteal phase is the latter half of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending with the start of the next period.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After releasing an egg, the empty follicle on the ovary transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure produces large amounts of progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a fertilized egg. If pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, hormone levels plummet, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a cycle with ovulation on day 14, the luteal phase would span days 15 through 28. During this time, a person might experience breast tenderness or mood changes due to rising progesterone.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase of sufficient length (at least 10-12 days) is necessary to sustain early pregnancy. It's also the phase where Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) occurs.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Primary Goal** | Support early pregnancy | Prepare egg for ovulation |
