## Definition
The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending the day before the next period starts.

## How Does It Work?
The empty follicle that released the egg becomes the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining to support a potential fertilized egg.

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle, the luteal phase typically lasts from day 15 to day 28. If pregnancy doesn't occur, progesterone drops, triggering the next period.

## Why Is It Important?
A healthy luteal phase (usually 10-14 days) is necessary to sustain early pregnancy. A short luteal phase can cause fertility issues.
