## Definition
The luteal phase is the final portion of the menstrual cycle, beginning the day after ovulation and ending the day before your next period begins. Its primary function is to prepare the uterus for the possible implantation of a fertilized egg.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?
After the egg is released, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and the lining is shed (menstruation).

## Real-World Example
In a 30-day cycle where ovulation happens on day 16, the luteal phase lasts from day 17 to day 30.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?
A healthy luteal phase is essential for maintaining early pregnancy. It is also the phase most associated with Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS), making it an important time for self-care and symptom tracking.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase
| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Duration | Consistent (12-14 days) | Variable |
