The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, occurring after ovulation and before the next period starts, characterized by the dominance of the hormone progesterone.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After ovulation, the empty follicle on the ovary transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. Progesterone thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a fertilized egg. If pregnancy does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the lining sheds (menstruation). Learn more about [hormones and your cycle](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained).

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, if ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase will span from Day 15 to Day 28. During these weeks, a woman might notice a sustained higher basal body temperature and potential PMS symptoms like bloating or mood changes as Day 28 approaches.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase of sufficient length (12-14 days) is necessary to support early pregnancy. If the phase is too short (Luteal Phase Defect), the uterine lining may shed before a fertilized egg can implant. Tracking with [LunaCycle](/) can help identify a short luteal phase.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | Post-ovulation. | Pre-ovulation. |
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| Goal | Prepare uterus for pregnancy. | Mature an egg for ovulation. |

### Core Principles
*   It is the post-ovulatory phase of the cycle.
*   Progesterone is the key hormone.
*   Length is generally consistent (12-14 days).
*   It ends with either pregnancy or menstruation.
