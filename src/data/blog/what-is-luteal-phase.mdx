The luteal phase is the latter half of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending the day before your next period starts. Its primary function is to prepare the uterine lining for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the ovary releases an egg, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the lining is shed (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle with ovulation on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 to 28. During this time, a woman might notice her basal body temperature remains slightly elevated due to the increased progesterone.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is essential for maintaining a pregnancy in its earliest stages. If the luteal phase is too short (less than 10 days), the uterine lining may not develop sufficiently to support an embryo, a condition known as Luteal Phase Defect.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Length** | Usually consistent (12-14 days). | Highly variable. |
