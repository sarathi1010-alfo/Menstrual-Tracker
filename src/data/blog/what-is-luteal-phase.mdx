## Definition

The luteal phase is the latter portion of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next menstrual period starts. This phase is characterized by the dominance of the hormone progesterone, which prepares the body for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an ovary releases an egg, the empty follicle transforms into a temporary endocrine structure called the corpus luteum. The corpus luteum secretes high levels of progesterone (and some estrogen), which thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is fertilized and implants, progesterone levels remain high. If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels plummet, and this drop triggers the shedding of the uterine lining (your period).

## Real-World Example in Cycle Tracking

Unlike the follicular phase, which can vary wildly in length from month to month, the luteal phase is relatively stable for each individual, usually lasting 12 to 14 days. By tracking your cycle on [LunaCycle](/), if you know your luteal phase is consistently 13 days, you can accurately pinpoint when you ovulated in hindsight once your period starts. The hormonal shifts during this phase are also responsible for PMS symptoms like bloating, mood swings, or tender breasts.

## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation to next period. | Start of period to ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Length** | Usually fixed (12-14 days). | Variable (determines total cycle length). |
