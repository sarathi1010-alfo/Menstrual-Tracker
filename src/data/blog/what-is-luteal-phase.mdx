The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending with the start of the next period. It is characterized by the production of progesterone to prepare the uterine lining for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After releasing an egg, the empty ovarian follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels fall, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle with ovulation on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 to 28. Unlike the follicular phase, which can vary greatly in length, the luteal phase is typically a very consistent 12 to 14 days for most women.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy, sufficiently long luteal phase is required to maintain the uterine lining long enough for a fertilized egg to implant. The hormonal fluctuations in this phase are also the primary cause of Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS).

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Main Function** | Preparing for implantation | Developing the egg |
