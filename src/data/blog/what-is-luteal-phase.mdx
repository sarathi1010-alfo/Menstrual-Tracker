## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

Once an egg is released, the empty follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure acts as a temporary endocrine gland, pumping out progesterone. Progesterone stops further ovulation and thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

If you have a 30-day cycle and ovulate on Day 16, your luteal phase spans from Day 17 to Day 30. During these days, you might experience higher body temperatures and eventually PMS symptoms as the phase nears its end.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (at least 10 days long) is essential for maintaining a pregnancy. If it is too short, the uterine lining may shed before a fertilized egg has a chance to implant. This phase is also responsible for premenstrual syndrome (PMS) due to the sharp drop in hormones right before bleeding starts.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Primary Function** | Maintain uterine lining | Mature an egg |
