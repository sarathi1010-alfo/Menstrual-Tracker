## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released during ovulation, the empty follicle left behind in the ovary transforms into a temporary endocrine structure called the corpus luteum. The primary job of the corpus luteum is to produce progesterone (along with some estrogen).

Progesterone is the dominant hormone of the luteal phase. It signals the endometrium (uterine lining) to thicken and secrete nutrients, creating an ideal environment for a fertilized egg to implant.

If implantation does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down after about 10-14 days. This causes a sharp drop in progesterone and estrogen levels, which triggers the shedding of the uterine lining—your period.

## Real-World Example in Symptom Tracking

The hormonal shifts of the luteal phase—specifically the rise and subsequent drop in progesterone—are responsible for Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS). During this time, you might experience bloating, breast tenderness, mood swings, or fatigue. By tracking these symptoms in [LunaCycle](/), you can recognize that they are a normal part of your luteal phase and plan self-care accordingly.

## Luteal Phase vs. Follicular Phase

| Phase | Timing | Dominant Hormone | Primary Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Luteal Phase** | After ovulation to period. | Progesterone | Prepare the uterus for pregnancy. |
| **Follicular Phase**| Day 1 of period to ovulation. | Estrogen | Mature an egg for release. |
