The luteal phase is the stage of the menstrual cycle that begins after ovulation and ends when your next period starts. During this time, the body prepares for a potential pregnancy by thickening the uterine lining.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty follicle turns into the *corpus luteum*, which secretes the hormone progesterone. Progesterone stabilizes the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a standard cycle, you ovulate on Day 14. Days 15 through 28 constitute your luteal phase. During the late luteal phase (days 24-28), you might experience PMS symptoms like bloating, fatigue, or mood changes due to fluctuating hormones.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

It is crucial for maintaining early pregnancy. A luteal phase that is too short (less than 10 days) may not allow enough time for a fertilized egg to implant, which can be a cause of infertility or early miscarriage.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Primary Function** | Prepare uterus for pregnancy | Mature the egg |
