The luteal phase is the latter portion of the menstrual cycle, beginning the day after ovulation and ending the day before your next period begins. It is a critical time for establishing a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After releasing an egg, the empty ovarian follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg isn't fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, hormone levels drop, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on day 14, the luteal phase occurs from days 15 to 28. During this time, women might experience PMS symptoms like bloating or breast tenderness due to high progesterone.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy, adequately long luteal phase is essential for fertility, as it gives a fertilized egg time to implant in the uterine lining.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| ------- | ------------ | ---------------- |
| Dominant Hormone| Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Function | Prepares for implantation| Prepares egg for release |
