## Definition

The luteal phase is the final phase of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before a menstrual period starts. During this time, the body prepares the uterine lining for a potential fertilized egg.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

Once the egg is released, the empty follicle turns into a structure called the corpus luteum, which pumps out progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg isn't fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and menstruation begins. You can read more about these shifts in [Hormones and Your Cycle](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained).

## Real-World Example

If you have a 28-day cycle and ovulate on Day 14, your luteal phase spans Days 15 through 28. You might experience PMS symptoms like bloating or mood swings towards the end of this phase as hormones drop. You can log these symptoms in [LunaCycle](/).

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy, sufficiently long luteal phase (at least 10 days) is necessary for a fertilized egg to successfully implant in the uterus. If it's too short, the lining sheds before implantation can occur.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Primary Function** | Prepare uterus for pregnancy. | Mature the egg for release. |
