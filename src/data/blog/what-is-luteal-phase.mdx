# What is the Luteal Phase?

**The luteal phase is the final stage of the menstrual cycle, beginning immediately after ovulation and ending with the start of your period. It is characterized by high levels of progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining to prepare for a possible pregnancy.**

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the ovary releases an egg during ovulation, the empty follicle turns into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes progesterone. Progesterone's main job is to mature the uterine lining, making it receptive to a fertilized egg. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels plummet, and the uterine lining sheds, resulting in menstruation.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle, the luteal phase typically spans from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, a woman might notice her basal body temperature remains slightly elevated compared to the first half of her cycle, due to the thermogenic effect of progesterone.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is essential for maintaining early pregnancy. A short luteal phase (less than 10 days) can make it difficult for a fertilized egg to implant. This phase is also when Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) occurs, caused by the sharp decline in hormones before menstruation.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation (Days 15-28 approx) | Before ovulation (Days 1-13 approx) |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Body Goal** | Prepare for possible pregnancy | Develop a mature egg |
