The luteal phase is the final phase of the menstrual cycle, occurring after ovulation and before the start of a new period. During this time, the body prepares for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the egg is released, the empty follicle turns into a structure called the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and the lining sheds as your period.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase spans from Day 15 to Day 28. During the latter half of this phase, many women experience Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) due to hormonal shifts.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase (typically 12-14 days) is necessary to support a pregnancy. Tracking your cycle with [LunaCycle](/) helps ensure this phase is a healthy length.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Body's Goal** | Prepare for pregnancy | Prepare to release an egg |
