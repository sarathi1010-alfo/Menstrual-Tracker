The luteal phase is the latter portion of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. Its primary function is to prepare the uterus for a possible pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the ruptured follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. Progesterone thickens the uterine lining. If the egg is fertilized and implants, progesterone levels remain high. If not, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and this drop triggers menstruation.

## Real-World Example

In a typical 28-day cycle, the luteal phase spans roughly from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, a person might experience PMS symptoms like breast tenderness, mood changes, or bloating due to the fluctuating progesterone levels.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is crucial for fertility. It must be long enough (usually 12-14 days) to allow a fertilized egg to implant in the uterine wall. A shortened luteal phase can be a cause of infertility or early miscarriage.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Purpose | Prepare uterus for pregnancy | Develop a mature egg |
