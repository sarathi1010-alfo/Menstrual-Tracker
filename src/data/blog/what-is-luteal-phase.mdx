## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released during ovulation, the ruptured follicle transforms into a structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes progesterone, which thickens the uterine lining to prepare it for a fertilized egg.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14, the luteal phase spans days 15 to 28. If no egg is fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop, and this drop triggers the start of your period.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is necessary to sustain a pregnancy. If it is too short (less than 10 days), the uterine lining might not have enough time to develop, which can lead to early miscarriage or difficulty conceiving.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation | Pre-ovulation |
| **Dominant Hormone**| Progesterone | Estrogen |
| **Main Function** | Prepare uterus for pregnancy | Mature an egg for release |
