The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, occurring after ovulation and ending when your period begins. It is a critical time when the body prepares for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the follicle that held it turns into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone. Progesterone keeps the uterine lining thick and receptive to a fertilized egg. If fertilization doesn't happen, progesterone levels drop, triggering menstruation.

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle where ovulation happens on Day 14, the luteal phase lasts from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, you might experience breast tenderness, bloating, or mood changes as hormone levels fluctuate.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy, adequately long luteal phase is essential for maintaining a pregnancy in its early stages. It's also the phase responsible for Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS).

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| Main Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Purpose | Prepare for implantation | Prepare the egg |
