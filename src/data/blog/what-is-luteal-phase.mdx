The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after ovulation and ends the day before your next period starts. During this time, the body prepares the uterus for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the empty ovarian follicle turns into a temporary endocrine gland called the corpus luteum. This gland secretes high levels of progesterone. Progesterone thickens and stabilizes the uterine lining. If an egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone levels drop sharply, and the uterine lining sheds (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a 28-day cycle with ovulation on Day 14, the luteal phase spans Days 15 to 28. During this time, you might notice your basal body temperature remains slightly elevated and you may experience PMS symptoms like bloating or mood swings as hormones shift.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A sufficiently long luteal phase (at least 11 days) is necessary for a fertilized egg to successfully implant in the uterus. If it is too short, the lining may shed before implantation can occur.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Concept | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Luteal Phase** | Post-ovulation, progesterone-dominant, consistent length (12-14 days). |
| **Follicular Phase** | Pre-ovulation, estrogen-dominant, variable length. |
