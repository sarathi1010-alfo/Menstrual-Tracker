## Definition
The luteal phase is the final stage of the menstrual cycle, occurring after ovulation and before your period starts. During this time, the body prepares for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?
After the egg is released, the empty follicle turns into the *corpus luteum*, which secretes progesterone. This hormone thickens the uterine lining. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone drops, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example
In a 28-day cycle with ovulation on Day 14, the luteal phase lasts from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, you might experience PMS symptoms like bloating or mood changes.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?
A healthy luteal phase is vital for pregnancy, as it gives a fertilized egg time to implant in the uterus. Its length is usually very consistent, making it a reliable marker in your cycle.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---------|--------------|------------------|
| Timing | After ovulation | Before ovulation |
| Main Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
