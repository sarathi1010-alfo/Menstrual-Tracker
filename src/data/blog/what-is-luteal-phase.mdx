The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle. It begins immediately after [ovulation](/what-is-ovulation) and ends the day before your next period starts. Its primary biological function is to prepare the uterus for the possible implantation of a fertilized egg.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

Once the ovary releases an egg, the empty follicle transforms into a temporary endocrine structure called the *corpus luteum*. This structure acts like a hormone factory, primarily pumping out progesterone (and some estrogen). Progesterone stabilizes and thickens the uterine lining.

If the egg is fertilized and implants, the body produces hCG (the hormone detected by pregnancy tests), which tells the corpus luteum to keep producing progesterone. If the egg is *not* fertilized, the corpus luteum breaks down after about 12-14 days. This causes a steep drop in progesterone and estrogen, which triggers the shedding of the uterine lining (your period).

For a complete overview of the hormones involved, read our guide to [hormones and your cycle](/blog/hormones-and-your-cycle-explained).

## Real-World Example

If a woman has a 30-day cycle and ovulates on Day 16, her luteal phase runs from Day 17 to Day 30. During the first week of this phase (Days 17-23), she might feel calm or slightly sluggish as progesterone peaks. During the second week (Days 24-30), as hormones crash, she may experience PMS symptoms like bloating, mood swings, and breast tenderness.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is crucial for fertility; it must be long enough (at least 10-12 days) to allow a fertilized egg time to implant. Furthermore, understanding this phase is key to managing PMS. ## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation to menstruation. | Menstruation to ovulation. |
| **Primary Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Duration** | Usually fixed (12-14 days). | Highly variable (can be 10 to 20+ days). |
| **Physical Effect** | Thickens uterine lining; slightly raises BBT. | Matures an egg; produces fertile cervical mucus. |

- The luteal phase prepares the body for pregnancy after an egg is released.

- It is typically the most consistent phase of the cycle (12-14 days).
- The drop in hormones at the end of this phase causes PMS and triggers your period.
