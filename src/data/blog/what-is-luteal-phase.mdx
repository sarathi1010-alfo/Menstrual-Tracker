## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After the ovary releases an egg, the ruptured follicle transforms into a temporary endocrine gland called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes large amounts of progesterone (and some estrogen) to thicken and maintain the uterine lining, making it receptive to a fertilized egg. If fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, hormone levels plummet, and menstruation begins.

## Real-World Example

In a typical cycle, if a woman ovulates on Day 14, her luteal phase runs from Day 15 to Day 28. During this time, she may experience higher basal body temperatures, increased appetite, and, toward the end, symptoms of PMS like bloating or mood swings.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy luteal phase is essential for fertility, as it provides the necessary environment for implantation. The hormonal shifts during this phase also heavily influence physical and emotional well-being, explaining why many women feel different in the week leading up to their period.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Post-ovulation (second half). | Pre-ovulation (first half). |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Primary Goal** | Maintain uterine lining. | Mature an egg. |
