The luteal phase is the second half of the [menstrual cycle](/blog/what-is-menstrual-cycle), occurring directly after [ovulation](/blog/what-is-ovulation) and continuing until the first day of the next period. It is characterized by the dominance of the hormone progesterone, which prepares the body for a potential pregnancy.

## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

Once an egg is released, the empty follicle on the ovary transforms into a temporary endocrine structure called the corpus luteum. The corpus luteum pumps out progesterone, which causes the uterine lining to thicken and mature, making it receptive to a fertilized egg. If the egg is not fertilized, the corpus luteum naturally breaks down after about 12-14 days. This causes progesterone levels to plummet, which in turn triggers the shedding of the uterine lining (menstruation).

## Real-World Example

In a cycle tracked with [LunaCycle](/), a user observes a clear temperature shift indicating she ovulated on Day 15. The days following this temperature spike (Days 16 through 28) constitute her luteal phase. During this time, she may notice a shift from high energy to a more relaxed, inward-focused state due to rising progesterone.

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

The luteal phase is vital for early pregnancy. A sufficiently long luteal phase (at least 10 days) with adequate progesterone production is necessary to allow a fertilized egg time to travel down the fallopian tube and implant securely in the uterine wall. It is also the phase where individuals experience PMS symptoms as hormones fluctuate.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

The cycle is divided into these two distinct halves, separated by ovulation.

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | After ovulation. | Before ovulation. |
| **Dominant Hormone** | Progesterone. | Estrogen. |
| **Length** | Generally consistent (12-14 days). | Highly variable (can range from 10 to 20+ days). |
| **Primary Goal** | Prepare uterus for implantation. | Mature an egg for release. |
