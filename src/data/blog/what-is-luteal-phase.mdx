## How Does the Luteal Phase Work?

After an egg is released, the ruptured follicle transforms into a temporary structure called the corpus luteum. This structure secretes high levels of progesterone, which thickens and stabilizes the uterine lining, creating an ideal environment for a fertilized egg to implant. If implantation does not occur, the corpus luteum breaks down.

## Real-World Example

During the days following ovulation (e.g., days 15-28), a person might experience physical changes driven by high progesterone, such as breast tenderness, mild bloating, or mood fluctuations, commonly referred to as Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS).

## Why is the Luteal Phase Important?

A healthy, sufficiently long luteal phase (typically 10-14 days) is necessary to sustain early pregnancy. If the phase is too short, the uterine lining may shed before a fertilized egg has a chance to implant.

## Luteal Phase vs Follicular Phase

| Feature | Luteal Phase | Follicular Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant Hormone | Progesterone | Estrogen |
| Primary Goal | Prepare for implantation | Mature the egg |
