# PCOS and Your Menstrual Cycle

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal condition that affects how the ovaries work. One of the most frequent symptoms is irregular or missing periods.

## How PCOS Affects the Cycle
In a typical cycle, an egg is released every month. With PCOS, the hormonal signals can get crossed, leading to:
- **Anovulation:** The body doesn't release an egg.
- **Irregular Timing:** Periods may come every few months or twice a month.
- **Varying Flow:** Bleeding might be very light or very heavy.

## Why Tracking is Essential for PCOS
When your cycle doesn't follow a "standard" 28-day pattern, data becomes your best friend.
1. **Identify Patterns:** Even irregular cycles often have some rhythm.
2. **Fertility Insights:** Tracking cervical mucus or BBT alongside your period can help you see if you are ovulating.
3. **Medical Preparation:** Bringing 6 months of data to your endocrinologist or OBGYN helps them make better treatment decisions.

## Tracking Tips for PCOS
- **Don't Get Discouraged:** Your calendar might look different from others, and that's okay.
- **Log Everything:** Track skin changes, hair growth, and energy levels.
- **Use the Notes:** Record changes in diet or stress to see how they impact your cycle length.
