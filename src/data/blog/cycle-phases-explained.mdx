The menstrual cycle consists of four distinct phases: menstruation, the follicular phase, ovulation, and the luteal phase, each with unique hormonal profiles.

## Why Cycle Phases Matter for Your Health

Your menstrual cycle is more than just your period. It's a continuous internal process that affects your energy, mood, and even your metabolism. By understanding the four phases, you can better predict how you'll feel and optimize your lifestyle accordingly.

## How the 4 Phases Work (Step-by-Step)

1. **The Menstrual Phase**: Day 1 of your cycle is the first day of your period. This is when the uterine lining sheds.
2. **The Follicular Phase**: Overlapping with menstruation, this phase involves the maturation of follicles in the ovaries.
3. **The Ovulation Phase**: The release of a mature egg from the ovary, triggered by a surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH).
4. **The Luteal Phase**: The period after ovulation where the body prepares for potential pregnancy via progesterone.

## Detailed Breakdown of Each Phase

### 1. Menstruation (Day 1-7)
The shedding of the uterine lining. Progesterone and estrogen are at their lowest levels, which can lead to lower energy and cramping.

### 2. Follicular Phase (Day 1-14)
FSH (Follicle-Stimulating Hormone) prompts ovaries to prepare eggs. Estrogen begins to rise, leading to improved mood and energy.

### 3. Ovulation (Day 14)
A 24-48 hour window where the egg is released. This is the peak of your fertile window.

### 4. Luteal Phase (Day 15-28)
Progesterone rises to thicken the uterine lining. If no pregnancy occurs, levels drop, triggering the next period.

## Common Cycle Phase Variations

| Phase | Normal Length | Variation Cause |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| Menstrual | 3-7 days | Stress, nutrition |
| Follicular | 10-22 days | Age, PCOS |
| Luteal | 12-16 days | Hormone imbalances |

### 5 Key Takeaways
- **Cycles are dynamic**: Hormones shift every single day.
- **Follicular is variable**: This phase determines your overall cycle length.
- **Luteal is stable**: Usually stays around 14 days for most women.
- **Energy follows estrogen**: You likely feel best during the follicular phase.
- **Progesterone is calming**: Dominates the luteal phase, often leading to PMS.
