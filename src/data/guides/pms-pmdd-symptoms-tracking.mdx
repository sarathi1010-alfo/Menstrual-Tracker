# The Complete Guide to PMS/PMDD and Cycle Tracking

Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) and Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD) are conditions that affect individuals during the luteal phase of their cycle, with symptoms resolving shortly after the period begins.

## What PMS/PMDD is and How it Affects Your Cycle

While most people experience some mild symptoms before their period (PMS), PMDD is a much more severe form characterized by significant mood disturbances and physical symptoms that can interfere with work, school, and relationships. Both are thought to be related to the brain's sensitivity to normal hormonal fluctuations.

## How Cycle Tracking Can Help Manage PMS/PMDD

Tracking is the "gold standard" for diagnosing PMDD. By logging your symptoms daily for at least two cycles, you can provide your healthcare provider with clear evidence that your symptoms are tied to your cycle and not a different underlying mood disorder.

## What to Look for in Your Cycle Data

- **Timing:** Symptoms should appear 7-14 days before your period and disappear within a few days of your period starting.
- **Severity:** Note which symptoms are "mild" vs. "disrupting your life."
- **Consistency:** Do the same symptoms appear at the same time every month?

## When to Consult a Healthcare Provider

If your premenstrual symptoms make you feel hopeless, out of control, or unable to function in your daily life, it is important to seek professional medical help. Bring your LunaCycle symptom logs to your appointment to facilitate a faster diagnosis.

> ### Quick Reference: PMS vs. PMDD
> | Feature | PMS | PMDD |
> |---------|-----|------|
> | Mood Symptoms | Mild irritability, sadness | Severe depression, anxiety, anger |
> | Physical Symptoms | Bloating, breast tenderness | Severe fatigue, pain, sleep issues |
> | Impact on Life | Annoying but manageable | Debilitating, affects relationships |
> | Timing | Luteal phase | Luteal phase |
> | When to See Doctor | If symptoms bother you | As soon as possible |
