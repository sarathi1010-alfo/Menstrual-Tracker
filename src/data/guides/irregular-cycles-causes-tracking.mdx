# Irregular Cycles: Causes, Tracking, and What to Know

An irregular cycle is generally defined as one that is consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or one that varies significantly from month to month. While common, irregular cycles can be frustrating when you're trying to predict your next period.

## Common Causes of Irregularity

### 1. Lifestyle Factors
- **Stress:** High stress can suppress the hypothalamus, the part of the brain that regulates hormones.
- **Extreme Exercise:** Intense athletic training can lead to amenorrhea (absence of periods).
- **Weight Changes:** Rapid weight loss or gain can disrupt hormone balance.

### 2. Medical Conditions
- **Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS):** One of the most common causes of irregular periods due to hormonal imbalances.
- **Thyroid Disorders:** Both an overactive and underactive thyroid can affect cycle regularity.
- **Endometriosis:** While more known for pain, it can also impact cycle patterns.

## How Cycle Tracking Helps

Even if your cycle is irregular, tracking is the first step toward understanding your health.
- **Find the "New Normal":** Tracking helps you see if your "irregularity" actually follows a broader pattern.
- **Identify Triggers:** By logging moods or stress levels alongside your period, you may find correlations.
- **Empower Your Doctor:** Bringing 3-6 months of cycle data to your physician can significantly speed up a diagnosis.

## What to Look for in Your Data
- **Cycle Length Variability:** How many days does your cycle vary by each month?
- **Flow Intensity:** Are your periods getting heavier or lighter over time?
- **Intermenstrual Bleeding:** Note any spotting that occurs between your main periods.

## AEO Box: When to See a Doctor
- If your cycle is consistently less than 21 days or more than 35 days.
- If you have gone more than 3 months without a period.
- If you experience heavy bleeding that requires changing a pad/tampon every hour.
- If your period suddenly becomes irregular after being regular for years.
