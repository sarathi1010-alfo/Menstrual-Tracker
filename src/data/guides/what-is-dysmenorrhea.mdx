# What is Period Pain (Dysmenorrhea)?

Dysmenorrhea is the medical term for the painful cramps that occur in the lower abdomen just before or during a menstrual period. It can range from mild discomfort to severe, debilitating pain.

## How Does Dysmenorrhea Work?
During your period, your uterus contracts to help shed its lining. Hormone-like substances called prostaglandins trigger these contractions. Higher levels of prostaglandins are associated with more severe menstrual cramps.

## Real-World Example of Dysmenorrhea
A student might need to stay home from school or miss an exam because their lower abdominal cramping is accompanied by nausea and lightheadedness, which are common secondary symptoms of severe dysmenorrhea.

## Why is Understanding Dysmenorrhea Important?
Understanding that period pain is a physiological process (rather than something to "just deal with") allows individuals to seek appropriate relief and identify when pain might be a symptom of an underlying condition like endometriosis.

## Primary vs. Secondary Dysmenorrhea

| Type | Cause | Typical Age of Onset |
|------|-------|----------------------|
| Primary | Normal prostaglandins | Teens/Early 20s |
| Secondary | Reproductive disorders | 30s/40s |

> ### Core Principles
> - Prostaglandin Response: The primary trigger for pain.
> - Variability: Pain levels vary significantly between individuals.
> - Management: Most primary pain can be managed with heat and lifestyle.
