# Understanding Your Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle is a natural, continuous process that prepares the body for a potential pregnancy each month. While everyone's cycle is unique, it generally consists of four main phases.

## 1. Menstruation (The Period)

This is the start of your cycle (Day 1). If a pregnancy has not occurred, the body sheds the lining of the uterus. This phase typically lasts between 3 to 7 days. You might experience common symptoms like cramping, bloating, or fatigue.

## 2. The Follicular Phase

Overlapping with menstruation and continuing until ovulation, this phase involves the brain sending signals to the ovaries to prepare an egg. Estrogen levels begin to rise, which often brings an increase in energy and improved mood.

## 3. Ovulation

Usually occurring around the middle of your cycle (often around day 14 of a 28-day cycle), an ovary releases a mature egg. This is a very short window, typically lasting 12 to 24 hours. Your fertile window includes the days leading up to and the day of ovulation.

## 4. The Luteal Phase

Following ovulation, the body prepares for a potential pregnancy. Progesterone levels rise, which keeps the uterine lining thick. If the egg is not fertilized, hormone levels eventually drop, triggering the start of a new menstrual cycle. This phase is when Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) symptoms most commonly occur.
